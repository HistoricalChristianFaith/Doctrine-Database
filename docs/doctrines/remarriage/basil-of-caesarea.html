Basil of Caesarea on remarriage

Dates: c. 375 (the Canonical Epistles) · Wikipedia
Summary: Basil’s first canonical letter records a year’s penance for a second marriage and three or four years for a third, which he says “is no longer described as marriage at all, but as polygamy; nay rather as limited fornication.”

Claims & evidence

Terms of penance for second and third marriages (Letter 188, canon 4)

…one year for digamy (some authorities say two years); for trigamy men are separated for three and often for four years; but this is no longer described as marriage at all, but as polygamy; nay rather as limited fornication.

— Basil, Letter 188 (First Canonical Epistle), canon 4, NPNF2 vol. 8. The term “limited fornication” is applied to the third marriage. Basil adds that in cases of trigamy “we have accepted a seclusion of five years, not by the canons, but following the precept of our predecessors,” the offenders to be “only restored to the place of communion after showing some fruit of repentance.”

Placement on the timeline

  • Basil’s canons are cited by name in the canons of the Council in Trullo. Penance for digamists is also set by Neocaesarea and Laodicea.
  • The Encyclopédie article “Polygamie” quotes Basil’s phrase as castigatam fornicationem and counts it, with Athenagoras’s “adultere honorable,” among “expressions trop fortes”; see Does Athenagoras’s phrase represent common Christian opinion independently of the New Prophecy?
