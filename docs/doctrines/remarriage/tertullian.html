Tertullian on remarriage

Dates: c. 210 (the monogamist treatises) · Wikipedia
Summary: Tertullian’s early To His Wife urges widowhood while granting that the apostle “permits marrying”; his later works of the New Prophecy, On Exhortation to Chastity and On Monogamy, forbid a second marriage, term it “a species of fornication,” and ask why the Paraclete may not have “abrogated an indulgence which Paul granted.”

Claims & evidence

Early (To His Wife, c. 203): remarriage is permitted, but the single marriage is better

…the Apostle… permits marrying indeed, but prefers abstinence; the former on account of the snares of temptations, the latter on account of the straits of the times.

— Tertullian, To His Wife I.3, ANF vol. 4. Written to his own wife against the day of his death, the treatise urges her to remain a widow, grants that the apostle permits a second marriage, and speaks of the disability of the twice-married in church office and the widows’ order.

Later (On Exhortation to Chastity 9, c. 208–212): a second marriage is “a species of fornication”

If we look deeply into his meanings, and interpret them, second marriage will have to be termed no other than a species of fornication.

— Tertullian, On Exhortation to Chastity 9, ANF vol. 4. Addressed to a recently widowed friend to dissuade him from remarrying, the chapter reasons from the concupiscence marriage shares with fornication. Here and in On Monogamy the remarriage of a widow or widower is classed as stuprum.

The Paraclete and Paul’s “indulgence” (On Monogamy 14)

…why may not the Paraclete, too, have abrogated an indulgence which Paul granted — because second marriage withal ‘was not from the beginning’…?

— Tertullian, On Monogamy 14, ANF vol. 4. Tertullian presents the Paraclete as the restitutor rather than the institutor of monogamy (ch. 4). At On Monogamy 11 he holds that in the Greek original 1 Cor. 7:39 concerns a woman widowed before her conversion, and that the form in common use has come about “through the either crafty or simple alteration of two syllables”; the readings of the verse are set out at Does 1 Corinthians 7:39 free every Christian widow to remarry, or only one widowed before her conversion?

Reception and debate

  • Augustine, cataloguing Tertullian among the heresies (De haeresibus 86), names his condemnation of second marriages “against apostolic teaching, as though they were fornication”; whether the ban was a matter of discipline or of doctrine is the disputed reading Was Tertullian’s ban on second marriage a matter of discipline or of doctrine?
  • His wider place in the church of Carthage is the subject of the “Was Tertullian a Heretic?” timeline.
  • Athenagoras had called a second marriage “a specious adultery” about 177; how that phrase relates to the New Prophecy is the disputed reading Does Athenagoras’s phrase represent common Christian opinion independently of the New Prophecy? Jerome names Tertullian among earlier writers on the subject (Against Jovinianus I.13) and wrote, “I do not condemn second, nor third, nor, pardon the expression, eighth marriages.”
