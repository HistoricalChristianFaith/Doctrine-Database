Origen of Alexandria on remarriage

Dates: c. 245 (Commentary on Matthew) · Wikipedia
Summary: Origen reads Romans 7 as allowing that there is “no reproach” for a widow who remarries, and reads the Pastoral Epistles as barring the twice-married from church office, a rule he writes that he found perplexing, since “some who have been married twice may be much better than those who have been married once.”

Claims & evidence

“No reproach” for the widow who remarries (Commentary on Romans)

Commenting on the law of marriage in Romans 7 — the wife bound only while her husband lives — Origen grants that “there is no reproach for a woman whose first husband has died if she enters a second marriage,” reading the passage’s deeper sense of the soul freed to be joined to Christ.1

The twice-married and church office (Commentary on Matthew XIV.22)

…for Paul wishes no one of those of the church, who has attained to any eminence beyond the many, as is attained in the administration of the sacraments, to make trial of a second marriage.

— Origen, Commentary on Matthew XIV.22, ANF vol. 9. Origen grounds the disqualification in the “husband of one wife” of the Pastorals (1 Tim. 3:2; see Paul) and reads the apostolic writings as “prohibit[ing] those who have been twice married from ruling over the church.”

Origen’s perplexity over the rule

Now, when we saw that some who have been married twice may be much better than those who have been married once, we were perplexed why Paul does not at all permit those who have been twice married to be appointed to ecclesiastical dignities…

— Origen, Commentary on Matthew XIV.22, ANF vol. 9. The next section (XIV.23), headed in the ANF translation “Some Laws Given by Concession to Human Weakness,” continues that “some of the laws were written not as excellent, but as by way of accommodation to the weakness of those to whom the law was given,” citing Matthew 19:8 on Moses and divorce.

Reception and debate

  • The rule against ordaining the twice-married is renewed at the Council in Trullo (canon 3).
  • Origen’s Homily on Jeremiah 20.4, which uses digamos, is read differently: Antonio Grappone reports that Pierre Nautin (Sources Chrétiennes 238) and Giulia Sfameni Gasparro (Dizionario di Origene) take it to concern the second marriage of a widow, and Luciana Mortari (Collana di Studi Patristici 123) divorced people who remarry. ⚠ unverified: the editions themselves are not opened here.2
