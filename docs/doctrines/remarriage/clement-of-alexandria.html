Clement of Alexandria on remarriage

Dates: c. 200 (Stromateis III) · Wikipedia
Summary: In Stromateis III Clement writes against those who forbade marriage and those who held conduct indifferent, and speaks of second marriage as what “the apostle allows” to one who “burns with passion,” quoting Paul that the widow is “happier” if she remains as she is.

Claims & evidence

Two opposed teachings on marriage (Stromateis III.5.40)

…teach that one ought to live on the principle that it is a matter of indifference whether one does right or wrong…

— Clement, Stromateis III.5.40, trans. Oulton & Chadwick, Alexandrian Christianity (LCC 2). Clement sorts the teachings he opposes into two groups — those who make right and wrong indifferent, and those who “impiously affect continence.” (Book III is the one book the old Ante-Nicene Fathers left untranslated in Latin; the standard English is the Oulton–Chadwick version cited here.)

Second marriage and the apostle’s allowance (Stromateis III.12.82)

…if from sympathy the apostle allows a man a second marriage because he cannot control himself and burns with passion…

— Clement, Stromateis III.12.82, trans. Oulton & Chadwick, Alexandrian Christianity (LCC 2). He then quotes the apostle:

The wife is bound to her husband so long as he is alive, but if he dies, she is free to marry, only in the Lord. But she is happier… if she remains as she [is].

— Clement, Stromateis III.12.82 (quoting 1 Cor. 7:39–40), trans. Oulton & Chadwick.

Reception and debate

  • Clement’s position is read differently. Fr. John H. Erickson (Orthodox) calls him “comparatively mild” among the Fathers on second marriage; Fr. Shenouda Maher Ishak (Coptic Orthodox, 2019) lists him with Athenagoras among the Fathers opposed to second marriage, writing that for Clement “even death does not dissolve this union completely and for this reason Clement is against any second marriage.”1 See also the disputed reading Does Athenagoras’s phrase represent common Christian opinion independently of the New Prophecy?
