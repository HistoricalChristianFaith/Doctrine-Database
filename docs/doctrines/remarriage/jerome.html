Jerome on remarriage

Dates: c. 393 (Against Jovinianus) · Wikipedia
Summary: In Against Jovinianus Jerome argues for virginity over marriage, names Tertullian among earlier writers on the subject, and writes: “I do not condemn second, nor third, nor… eighth marriages.” After objections to the book, Pammachius withdrew copies from circulation, and Jerome defended it in Letter 49.

Claims & evidence

Jerome names Tertullian (Against Jovinianus I.13)

At all events Tertullian, while still a young man, gave himself full play with this subject. And my teacher, Gregory of Nazianzus, discussed virginity and marriage in some Greek verses.

— Jerome, Against Jovinianus I.13, NPNF2 vol. 6. Jerome names Tertullian and Gregory of Nazianzus among earlier writers on virginity and marriage.

“I do not condemn second, nor third… marriages” (I.15)

‘All things are lawful, but not all things are expedient.’ I do not condemn second, nor third, nor, pardon the expression, eighth marriages… Things that are equally lawful must be weighed in an even balance.

— Jerome, Against Jovinianus I.15, NPNF2 vol. 6. Jerome sets the sentence under Paul’s “All things are lawful, but not all things are expedient.”

The reception of Against Jovinianus (Letters 49 and 54)

Read, I pray you, and diligently consider the words of the apostle, and you will then see that — with a view to avoid misrepresentation — I have been much more gentle towards married persons than he was disposed to be.

— Jerome, Letter 49 (to Pammachius), 3, NPNF2 vol. 6. When Against Jovinianus was read as condemning marriage outright, Pammachius withdrew copies from circulation; Jerome answered that “words once uttered cannot be recalled” (Letter 49.2) and insisted he had been gentler to the married than Paul was. In Letter 54, urging the widow Furia to remain unmarried, he still calls the point of law “the apostle’s concession of second marriages” (Letter 54.18).

Reception and debate

  • Jerome’s words are cited on the disputed reading Was Tertullian’s ban on second marriage a matter of discipline or of doctrine? Philip Schaff writes that Jerome “represented the prevailing view of the Nicene age”; see Does Athenagoras’s phrase represent common Christian opinion independently of the New Prophecy?
  • Jovinian’s teaching on marriage and virginity was the first of the four propositions Against Jovinianus set out to refute. His other propositions — including the equal-recompense thesis, which Jerome presents as a parity of sins, and his denial of Mary’s virginity in the birth — is treated as one controversy on Jovinian’s page, and the parity charge is a disputed reading at did Jovinian teach that all sins are equal?
