John Calvin on the resurrection of the body

Dates: c. 1559 · Wikipedia
Summary: Calvin, in the final (1559) Latin Institutes III.25 ("Of the Last Resurrection"), states the same-flesh hope for the Reformed churches. The risen body is numerically this body — God will "fabricate" no new one — changed in quality but not in substance; and he rejects as "monstrous" the error of those (he names the ancient Manichees and the "spiritualist" deniers of his own day) who imagine the soul receives a new and different body.

Claims & evidence

The same flesh we now bear rises — "no new bodies are to be fabricated"

"This corruptible (says Paul) must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality," (1 Cor. 15:53.) If God formed new bodies, where would be this change of quality? … but here pointing with the finger to the bodies with which we are clothed, and promising that they shall be incorruptible, he very plainly affirms that no new bodies are to be fabricated.

— Institutes of the Christian Religion III.25.7 (Beveridge trans.), source. Calvin reads Paul's demonstratives, as Cyril had: "this corruptible" is a finger laid on the body the reader now stands in, so a change of quality in this flesh, not the substitution of another, is what Scripture promises.

The error of a new and different body is "monstrous"

Equally monstrous is the error of those who imagine that the soul, instead of resuming the body with which it is now clothed, will obtain a new and different body.

— Institutes III.25.7, source. The target, on Calvin's account, is twofold: the ancient Manichees, who despised the flesh as too impure to rise (the same dualist denial Irenaeus and Tertullian had fought), and the "fanatical" spiritualizers of his own century who read the resurrection as the soul's release into a wholly new mode of being. Against both Calvin holds that the flesh is not replaced but "divested of corruption" and "clothed with incorruption" — the very body, remade.

Christ's own body is the pattern: the same body he had before, not a substitute

The mortal body which he had formerly carried he again received; for it would not have availed us much if a new body had been substituted, and that which had been offered in expiatory sacrifice been destroyed.

— Institutes III.25.7 (with the appeal to John 2:19, "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up"), source. Calvin argues, as Justin and John of Damascus had, from the palpable risen Christ: our resurrection mirrors his, and his was the raising of the identical body that hung on the cross, not the forming of a new one — else the sacrifice offered in that body would be undone. He closes the section: "I am ashamed to waste so many words on so clear a matter; but my readers will kindly submit to the annoyance, in order that perverse and presumptuous minds may not be able to avail themselves of any flaw to deceive the simple" (III.25.8).
