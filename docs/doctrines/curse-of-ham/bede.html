Bede on the Curse of Ham

Dates: c. 673–735 (Commentary on Genesis, c. 720) · Wikipedia
Summary: Bede gives the fullest patristic synthesis of the passage: a typological reading (Ham = the unbelieving Jews who mocked Christ’s passion, Shem and Japheth = the Church that veils it) laid over a literal-historical one in which Canaan is cursed because his line — the Sodomites and the Canaanites — would prove the most wicked of Ham’s descendants and be subdued by Israel. He is explicit that the other lines of Ham (Cush/the Ethiopians, Mizraim/the Egyptians, Phut/the Libyans) are not under the curse.

Claims & evidence

Canaan cursed for the foreseen wickedness of his line — not the rest of Ham’s sons

“It should also be noted according to the literal sense that not in vain is Canaan cursed when Ham sinned, not he himself but his son, especially when he was neither the firstborn of Ham but his youngest son… Indeed, he foresaw equally in spirit that the progeny of Canaan would sin much more than the rest of the offspring of Ham, and therefore be worthy either to perish by curse or to groan under subjugation; which was particularly proven and shown by the wickedness of the Sodomites, who came from the lineage of Canaan… and the extermination or subjugation of the Canaanites, which they suffered when the Israelite people, descended from the lineage of Shem, came out of Egypt. For from Cush came the Ethiopians, from Mizraim the Egyptians, from Phut came the Libyans… among whom there is absolutely nothing of such wickedness or vengeance as Scripture relates about the Sodomites and Canaanites.”

— Commentary on Genesis (on Genesis 9:27). This is decisive against the later racial reading: Bede knows Ham’s African descendants perfectly well (Ethiopians, Egyptians, Libyans) and pointedly exempts them — the curse is on Canaan alone, and for his line’s wickedness, discharged in the conquest.

The curse fulfilled in the subjugation of the Canaanites

“God also expanded Japheth, so that he dwelt in the tents of Shem, and that Canaan was his servant, when the Greeks or Romans, certainly descended from the line of Japheth, possessed the kingdoms of Asia, in which the descendants of Shem lived, and among other things made the Canaanites tributary to themselves.”

— Commentary on Genesis (on Genesis 9:27). The servitude is territorial-political history — Israel, then the Greeks and Romans, subjecting the Canaanite peoples.

The typological reading: Ham the mocker, Shem and Japheth the Church

“Ham, who saw his father’s nakedness and laughed, signifies the Jewish people, who, contradicting and incredulous, rather despised the passion of our Lord and Savior than honored it… the two brothers, the eldest and the youngest, signify those to whom it was subsequently added: both Jews and Gentiles are called to Christ… carrying the sacrament of the Lord’s past and completed passion as if it were a single cloak from behind, not looking at their father’s nudity because they do not consent to Christ’s killing.”

— Commentary on Genesis (on Genesis 9:22). The allegory Bede inherits from Cyprian and Jerome: Noah’s exposure prefigures the passion; the sons’ response divides those who mock the cross from those who honour it.

Notes

  • Bede’s explicit exemption of Cush/Mizraim/Phut is the strongest single patristic datum against the racial curse: the curse is Canaan’s, and its scope is a named people, not a skin colour.
  • His “Canaan cursed for his own line’s foreseen wickedness” answer to the why-Canaan puzzle combines Chrysostom’s individual-desert principle with a prophetic-foresight reading.
  • Bede appears elsewhere on the wiki as a careful reader of the Nimrod crux (another of Ham’s line).
