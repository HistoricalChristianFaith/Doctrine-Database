Theophylact of Ohrid on the death of Judas

Dates: c. 1050–after 1107 (his Commentary on Acts, largely epitomized from earlier Greek exegesis) · Wikipedia
Summary: The archbishop of Ohrid’s Commentary on Acts reads the two death-notices as one death — Judas hanged himself but was cut down before he strangled and lived on — and, to fill out the manner of his end, quotes Papias’s swelling fragment at length, joining all three accounts into one sequence.

Claims & evidence

“Judas did not die in the noose, but lived on”

Observe: he elaborated not on the transgression, but on the punishment for it, because Judas did not die in the noose, but lived on even after, since he was taken down before he strangled to death. Papias, a disciple of John, relates this more clearly in the fourth book of the Exposition of the Sayings of the Lord.

— Theophylact, Commentary on Acts, on 1:18. Where Oecumenius joined the notices by a snapped rope, Theophylact joins them by a failed hanging: the noose did not finish Judas, who was cut down alive, so that Peter in Acts can speak of what befell him afterward. He then reaches back past both canonical notices to Papias for the “punishment” itself — the swelling, the sightless eyes, the death on the deserted estate — transcribing the fragment (quoted in full on the Papias page).

Notes

  • The three accounts as one sequence. In Theophylact’s account Matthew’s hanging is retained but not fatal, Acts’ bursting is the sequel, and Papias’s swelling supplies the drawn-out death in between, so that all three notices are kept and told as one continuous end.
  • Other timelines. Theophylact appears also as a witness on the perpetual-virginity timeline; his commentaries largely epitomize Chrysostom and the earlier Greek fathers, and here he preserves older material, notably the long Papias fragment.
