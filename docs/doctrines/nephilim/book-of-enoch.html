Book of Enoch on the Nephilim

Dates: c. 300 BC Summary: 1 Enoch sets out the angelic reading at length. The "sons of God" are the Watchers — two hundred angels under Semjaza who descend on Mount Hermon, take human wives, and beget giants of monstrous height. The text adds that when the giants die, the spirits that come out of their bodies become the evil spirits (demons) that afflict humanity until the final judgment.

Claims & evidence

The Watchers descend, take wives, and beget giants

And the angels, the children of the heaven, saw and lusted after them, and said to one another: 'Come, let us choose us wives from among the children of men and beget us children.' … And they were in all two hundred; who descended in the days of Jared on the summit of Mount Hermon … And they became pregnant, and they bare great giants, whose height was three thousand ells: Who consumed all the acquisitions of men.

— 1 Enoch, Chapters 6-8, 15, 16, ccel.org. The begetters are "the angels, the children of the heaven," who father a race of devouring giants.

The giants' spirits become evil spirits upon the earth

And now, the giants, who are produced from the spirits and flesh, shall be called evil spirits upon the earth, and on the earth shall be their dwelling. Evil spirits have proceeded from their bodies; because they are born from men and from the holy Watchers is their beginning and primal origin; they shall be evil spirits on earth, and evil spirits shall they be called.

— 1 Enoch, Chapters 6-8, 15, 16, ccel.org. This demonological etiology — demons as the disembodied spirits of dead giants — recurs in later Jewish and Christian writers (see the Notes).

The archangels intervene and the Watchers are bound

And they have gone to the daughters of men upon the earth, and have slept with the women, and have defiled themselves … And the women have borne giants, and the whole earth has thereby been filled with blood and unrighteousness.

— 1 Enoch, Chapters 9-11, ccel.org. Michael, Uriel, Raphael and Gabriel bring the case before God, who orders the Watchers bound and their giant sons destroyed.

Notes

  • In 1 Enoch the sons of God are angels (the Watchers), not the line of Seth.
  • The Watchers' forbidden arts. Between the descent and the demonology, chapter 8 has the Watchers teach humanity a syllabus of forbidden arts — Azazel metallurgy and cosmetics, and, by name, "Baraqijal (taught) astrology, Kokabel the constellations… Sariel the course of the moon." The claim that the arts of civilization were not discovered but taught by the fallen angels recurs in patristic writers on this timeline, among them Clement of Alexandria, Tertullian, and Commodian.
  • A close cousin text, the Book of Giants — preserved in Aramaic among the Dead Sea Scrolls — takes up the same Watcher-and-giants story from the giants' own side and names Gilgamesh and Humbaba among them.
  • Earliest manuscripts. The date above is not read off the late Ethiopic text alone: the oldest surviving copies of 1 Enoch are the Aramaic fragments recovered from Qumran Cave 4 and edited by J. T. Milik. The Book of the Watchers (chapters 1–36, the source of the material above) survives in 4Q201 (4QEna), whose Hasmonean script Milik dated c. 200–150 BC and judged a copy of a still older third-century exemplar; the Astronomical Enoch fragments (4Q208) are older again — Milik reckoned them the oldest of the Qumran Enoch manuscripts, of the late third or early second century BC. Henryk Drawnel's critical editions treat these seven Aramaic manuscripts as the earliest textual witness to the Enochic corpus. On Milik's dating, the manuscripts place the Watcher-and-giants reading of Genesis 6 in written form in Judaea by the second century BC. — J. T. Milik, The Books of Enoch: Aramaic Fragments of Qumrân Cave 4 (Oxford: Clarendon, 1976), archive.org, with Henryk Drawnel, Qumran Cave 4: The Aramaic Books of Enoch (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2019), academia.edu.
  • 1 Enoch 15 is the earliest text on this timeline to identify demons with the spirits of the dead giants; the identification recurs in Jubilees 10, Justin, Athenagoras, Commodian, and Lactantius.
  • Transmission: widely used in the early churches, 1 Enoch lost credit in the fourth century and — after a last allusion by George Syncellus (c. 810) — dropped from view in the West, surviving as Scripture only in the Ethiopian (Ge'ez) tradition. James Bruce brought manuscripts back from Abyssinia in 1773, and a Greek fragment of chapters 1–32 later surfaced at Akhmîm, Egypt — the recovery behind the modern text. Whether its early use amounted to reception as Scripture is set out on the interpretation page Was 1 Enoch received as Scripture in the early churches? — "Book of Henoch," Catholic Encyclopedia.
