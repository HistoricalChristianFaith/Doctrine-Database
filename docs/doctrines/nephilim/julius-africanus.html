Julius Africanus on the Nephilim

Dates: c. 240 Summary: Julius Africanus (d. c. 240) gives the earliest extant Christian statement of the Sethite reading of Genesis 6: the "sons of God" are the righteous descendants of Seth and the "daughters of men" are the line of Cain. He notes a textual variant ("the sons of God" versus "the angels of heaven") and also sets out the angelic alternative — "if it is thought that these refer to angels" — describing those angels as practitioners of magic who taught women celestial lore. The fragment thus preserves both readings, giving the Sethite one as his own opinion.

Claims & evidence

The sons of God are the descendants of Seth; the seed of men are the descendants of Cain

When men multiplied on the earth, the angels of heaven came together with the daughters of men. In some copies I found "the sons of God." What is meant by the Spirit, in my opinion, is that the descendants of Seth are called the sons of God on account of the righteous men and patriarchs who have sprung from him, even down to the Saviour Himself; but that the descendants of Cain are named the seed of men, as having nothing divine in them, on account of the wickedness of their race and the inequality of their nature, being a mixed people, and having stirred the indignation of God.

— Fragment 2, source. This fragment of Africanus's Chronography survives only through its quotation by the Byzantine chronicler George Syncellus (AD 810); Africanus also registers the textual variant "sons of God" against "angels of heaven."

But if these are angels, they were teachers of magic who fathered the giants

But if it is thought that these refer to angels, we must take them to be those who deal with magic and jugglery, who taught the women the motions of the stars and the knowledge of things celestial, by whose power they conceived the giants as their children, by whom wickedness came to its height on the earth, until God decreed that the whole race of the living should perish in their impiety by the deluge.

— Fragment 2, source. Africanus sets this out conditionally, after giving the Sethite reading "in my opinion"; the passage is again preserved via Syncellus.

Notes

  • The fragment is the earliest extant explicit statement of the Sethite reading, and it also records the angelic reading as current. Its date relative to rabbinic sources is one of the points discussed on the interpretation page Did the Sethite reading originate as a rabbinic reaction against the Enochic reading?
  • In setting out the angelic alternative, Africanus describes the angels as teachers of magic and of "the motions of the stars," "by whose power" the women conceived the giants.
  • The same Chronography is the earliest extant Christian universal chronology: Africanus fixed the Flood at Anno Mundi 2262 and the Incarnation at Anno Mundi 5500. His whole-span reckoning, and its later use in the Byzantine world eras, are treated on his Reckoning the Age of the World detail page.
  • De Young on Africanus (Lord of Spirits). De Young identifies Africanus (b. 160 – d. 240) as the first Christian to adopt the Sethite reading, which he traces to Rabbinic Judaism after the synagogue rejected the Enochic literature as "too Christian." The George Syncellus who preserved this fragment (c. 810) also transcribes the Enochic Watchers narrative into his Chronography as antediluvian history; see George Syncellus on the Nephilim. (De Young credits the description of 1 Enoch as an "apostolic apocryphon" to Patriarch Nikephoros I, not to Syncellus; see the Syncellus page.)
