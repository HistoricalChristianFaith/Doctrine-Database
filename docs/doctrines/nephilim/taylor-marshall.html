Dr. Taylor Marshall on the Nephilim

Dates: 2018–2023 · "Angels having Relations with Humans within Jude and 2 Peter and 1 Enoch" (2018) · Podcast ep. 968 (2023) (YouTube)
Summary: Dr. Taylor Marshall (b. 1978), a Roman Catholic writer and podcaster of a Thomist cast, sets out the Enochic Watcher reading and its New Testament use. In a 2018 essay he summarizes the ancient supernatural reading: 1 Enoch's Watchers copulate with human women and beget the Nephilim, Jude directly quotes the book, and 2 Peter borrows its word "Tartarus" — with the Wisdom of Solomon reading the Flood as a divine destruction of "the proud giants." He does not firmly commit, but presents the Sethite reading as "the accepted tradition of Catholicism" while treating the angelic reading as the older, near-unanimous pre-Augustinian one, and poses his framing question: would Augustine and Aquinas have revised their Sethite reading had they had access to 1 Enoch and the Dead Sea Scrolls? He calls the Sethite reading "plausible," stresses that evil is not genetic, and rests his own case on the New Testament's use of the Enochic tradition rather than on the literal history behind it. In the 2023 podcast he reads the Watcher episode as a Maccabean allegory of the Greek Titanomachy, with the Nephilim as the children of Greek–Jewish mixed marriages.

Claims & evidence

1 Enoch, the Watchers, and the begetting of the giants

Sometime around the 200s BC a Jewish document called "Book of Enoch" or 1 Enoch was produced.

These Watchers corrupt humanity by teaching them evil arts… These angels also seduce human women and copulate with them. The women give birth to nephilim or giants. God causes these giants to fight and die.

— Angels having Relations with Humans within Jude and 2 Peter and 1 Enoch, taylormarshall.com. Marshall summarizes the Enochic Watcher-legend — the angelic descent, the corrupting arts, the union with women, and the giant offspring — as the background the New Testament writers inherit.

Jude quotes 1 Enoch; 2 Peter borrows its "Tartarus"

It's not only this direct quotation of 1 Enoch by Jude, but Jude (and 2 Peter) allude to the fantastical events of 1 Enoch, namely the sexual encounters of fallen angels with human women, which gives birth to the nephilim or "giants." The birth of the giants, according to 1 Enoch, is the reason for the Noah's Flood.

— Angels having Relations with Humans…, taylormarshall.com. He notes that Jude 14–15 directly quotes 1 Enoch (1:9), and that 2 Peter 2:4 "explicitly uses Enoch's word 'Tartarus'" (the verb tartaróō) for the imprisonment of the fallen angels — the same two texts De Young and Wilson cite. (Marshall further holds that 2 Peter's "Tartarus" is a deliberate borrowing from the Greek Titanomachy; the readings of the word are set out on Does 2 Peter's "Tartarus" (tartaróō) borrow from the Greek Titanomachy?)

The Wisdom of Solomon on the giants who perished

And from the beginning also when the proud giants (γιγάντων) perished, the hope of the world fleeing to a vessel, which was governed by thy hand, left to the world seed of generation. (Wisdom 14:6)

— Angels having Relations with Humans…, taylormarshall.com, quoting Wisdom 14:6. Marshall reads the Wisdom of Solomon as itself presupposing the giant-tradition — "the proud giants" (τῶν γιγάντων) perishing in the Flood while Noah's vessel preserves the "seed of generation" — and adds that St. Paul, who quotes Wisdom "about seven times," likely shared the belief and "considered Wisdom as part of his Bible." (How the New Testament's allusions to the deuterocanon bear on its standing is set out on What does the New Testament's use of the deuterocanon, by allusion but never by formal citation, show about its standing? De Young cites the same book, Wisdom 12, for a cannibal cult.)

Sirach on the giants toward whom God "was not pacified"

He was not pacified toward the old giants, who fell away in the strength of their foolishness. (Ecclesiasticus 16:7)

In the 2023 podcast Marshall adduces Sirach 16:7 as a second witness, beside Wisdom, to the Genesis-6 giants, tying it to the 1 Enoch scene in which Enoch's plea for the antediluvian rebels is refused: on his reading the "old giants" toward whom God "was not pacified" are a trace of that unforgiven judgment, and the verse, like the Wisdom of Solomon passage above, treats Genesis 6's gigantes as historical figures destroyed for their strength-trusting pride. (See the Sirach page.)

Jude and 2 Peter as using the legend as a teaching paradigm

Regardless of whether Jude and 2 Peter believed the Enochic "Watcher-Angel legend," we know for a fact that they applied it as a teaching paradigm for their contemporary Apostolic Church.

— Angels having Relations with Humans…, taylormarshall.com. Marshall holds it "a fact" that the two epistles wield the Watcher-legend rhetorically against the false teachers of their day, and leaves open whether they took it as literal history; he commends the angelic reading's antiquity without resting his case on angel–human breeding. In the 2023 podcast he draws a point-for-point mapping (the Watchers as appointed guides who fell, the false teachers as fallen church leaders) and holds that the letters "only make sense if their audience understood the background story." On the use of the legend as a judgment-paradigm against the false teachers see the note below.

The framing question: would Augustine and Aquinas have revised the Sethite reading?

In the 2023 podcast Marshall surveys the Second Temple and patristic witnesses and, presenting the Sethite reading as "the accepted tradition of Catholicism," poses his central question — whether Augustine and Aquinas, the two writers he names for that tradition, would have read Genesis 6 differently had they possessed 1 Enoch and the Dead Sea Scrolls (both recovered in the West long after their day). He treats the angelic reading as the older and near-unanimous pre-Augustinian one, calls the Sethite reading "plausible" rather than certain, and cautions that evil is a matter of the will, not of genetics, so that on his account the supernatural reading does not make wickedness heritable.

Saint Augustine and Saint Thomas Aquinas (both had not read 1 Enoch) interpretted the passages in Jude and 2 Peter in this way…

— Angels having Relations with Humans…, taylormarshall.com (the same premise underlies the 2023 podcast's framing question). On Augustine and the Enochic tradition see the note below.

Reception and debate

  • Marshall writes from a Roman-Catholic, Thomist vantage. Among the other modern writers on this timeline, Fr. Stephen De Young (Orthodox) and Doug Wilson (Reformed) hold the angelic reading and each proposes a mechanism for the begetting (De Young's sacral-kingship rite, Wilson's literal embodiment); Marshall proposes none. He presses the New Testament's own use of the Enochic tradition, calls the Sethite reading "plausible" and "the accepted tradition of Catholicism," and asks whether it would have survived contact with 1 Enoch and Qumran; in 2023 he reads the Watcher legend as a Maccabean allegory (below).
  • The epistles' use of the legend as a judgment-paradigm. Jude 5–7 sets three judgment-examples side by side and turns them on "these" intruders, and Jude quotes Enoch as one who "prophesied" (v. 14). Richard Bauckham (Word Biblical Commentary, 1983) reads Jude 4 as Jude applying Enoch's judgment-prophecies to the false teachers. Bauckham also reads 2 Peter as reworking Jude's material, dropping the direct 1 Enoch quotation of Jude 14–15 and generalizing the "angels that sinned" notice.1 Both letters stand in a Second Temple series of judgment-examples that pairs the giants or Watchers with Sodom (Sirach 16; 3 Maccabees 2; the Damascus Document).
  • Augustine, Aquinas, and 1 Enoch. Marshall writes that Augustine and Aquinas "both had not read 1 Enoch." Augustine's own discussion of the Enochic writings in City of God XV.23 is quoted on his page, and the recovery history of 1 Enoch and the readings of Augustine's case, Marshall's among them, are set out on Is Augustine's case against the angelic reading (City of God XV.23) circular?
  • Marshall's paraphrase of Margaret Barker. In the 2023 podcast Marshall surveys other modern views and paraphrases Margaret Barker as reading the Nephilim symbolically, as a metaphor for the wickedness and violence of the pre-flood world. In the essays quoted on her page Barker reads the "sons of God" of Genesis 6 as fallen angels — the "fuller version" of the creation-and-fall story of which Genesis preserves only a fragment — and does not call them a "symbol," "metaphor," or "figure" ("The fallen angels were known as the sons of God. That is how they are described in Genesis 6"). She also reads Genesis 3 and Genesis 6 as two fragments of a single fallen-angel myth about corrupting knowledge. Her position is set out on her own page.
  • The Wisdom of Solomon and the Protestant Bibles. Marshall holds that "St. Paul the Apostle quotes the Book of Wisdom about seven times, so clearly … he … considered Wisdom as part of his Bible," and that Wisdom is absent from Protestant Bibles because "Martin Luther threw it out, so you won't find it in a King James or a Lutheran Bible." The Pauline allusions include Romans 1 ≈ Wisdom 13–14; Bruce Metzger found "little doubt" that Paul "made a close study" of the book. Luther's handling of Wisdom in his 1534 Bible is set out on Luther's page. How the New Testament's allusions bear on the books' standing is set out on What does the New Testament's use of the deuterocanon, by allusion but never by formal citation, show about its standing?
  • The Maccabean-allegory reading. In the 2023 podcast Marshall holds that the Enochic Watcher-episode was written "in the Maccabean period" as a "judaized" retelling of the Greek Titanomachy allegorizing the Hellenistic invasion (Watchers = Hellenizers, Nephilim = Greek–Jewish mixed-marriage offspring, the Flood/war = the Maccabean purge). The datings of the Book of the Watchers (J. T. Milik's palaeographic dating of 4QEna/4Q201; George Nickelsburg's) and the other readings of the Watcher myth (Nickelsburg's, as reflecting the wars of Alexander's successors; David Suter's, as a polemic about priests and their marriages) are set out on Is 1 Enoch's Watcher legend a Maccabean allegory modelled on the Greek Titanomachy?
  • 2 Peter's "Tartarus" and the Titanomachy. Marshall holds that 2 Peter 2:4's tartaróō ("cast into Tartarus") deliberately borrows the Greek Titanomachy, so that the New Testament writers map the Watchers onto the Titans; Michael Heiser holds a similar reading. Jerome Neyrey and Bradly Billings read the word as a deliberate echo of the Titans' punishment for Greek-speaking readers, and J. N. D. Kelly and Richard Bauckham as the Greek name for the deepest prison, which on Bauckham's reading reached 2 Peter "through the mediation of Jewish apocalyptic"; Greek 1 Enoch 20:2 sets the archangel Uriel "over Tartarus." Jude 6, the parallel verse, has only "everlasting chains under darkness." The readings are set out on Does 2 Peter's "Tartarus" (tartaróō) borrow from the Greek Titanomachy?
