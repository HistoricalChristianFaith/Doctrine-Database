Jerome on confirmation (the bishop's hand-laying after baptism)

Dates: c. 380 · Wikipedia
Summary: In the Dialogue Against the Luciferians the Luciferian speaker, Helladius, appeals to the post-baptismal hand-laying as a custom of the churches with warrant in Acts; Jerome’s speaker Orthodoxus grants that it is the practice of the churches for the bishop to visit those baptized by presbyters and deacons far from the towns and invoke the Holy Ghost on them by the laying on of hands. He grounds the reservation to the bishop in the descent of the Holy Ghost upon the Apostles, and describes the practice as kept “more by way of honouring the episcopate than from any compulsory law,” and as a safeguard of church order, lest there be “as many schisms in the Churches as there are priests.”

Claims & evidence

The Luciferian speaker’s appeal to the custom of the churches (§8)

The work is a dialogue between “Helladius the Luciferian” (L.) and Orthodoxus (O.). The following words are spoken by L.:

Don't you know that the laying on of hands after baptism and then the invocation of the Holy Spirit is a custom of the Churches? Do you demand Scripture proof? You may find it in the Acts of the Apostles. And even if it did not rest on the authority of Scripture the consensus of the whole world in this respect would have the force of a command.

— Jerome, The Dialogue Against the Luciferians, §8 (L.; the speaker is named in §2), trans. W. H. Fremantle (Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, 2nd ser., vol. VI), source.

Orthodoxus’s reply: the bishop’s visitation (§9)

I do not deny that it is the practice of the Churches in the case of those who living far from the greater towns have been baptized by presbyters and deacons, for the bishop to visit them, and by the laying on of hands to invoke the Holy Ghost upon them.

— Dialogue Against the Luciferians, §9 (O.), source.

The reservation to the bishop: the Spirit's descent on the Apostles, and the honour of the episcopate

But if you now ask how it is that a person baptized in the Church does not receive the Holy Ghost, Whom we declare to be given in true baptism, except by the hands of the bishop, let me tell you that our authority for the rule is the fact that after our Lord's ascension the Holy Ghost descended upon the Apostles. And in many places we find it the practice, more by way of honouring the episcopate than from any compulsory law. Otherwise, if the Holy Ghost descends only at the bishop's prayer, they are greatly to be pitied who in isolated houses, or in forts, or retired places, after being baptized by the presbyters and deacons have fallen asleep before the bishop's visitation.

— Dialogue Against the Luciferians, §9, source. He grounds the reservation in the good order of the church:

The well-being of a Church depends upon the dignity of its chief-priest, and unless some extraordinary and unique functions be assigned to him, we shall have as many schisms in the Churches as there are priests.

— Dialogue Against the Luciferians, §9, source.

Notes

  • The grounds Jerome gives. Jerome’s speaker Orthodoxus grants the bishop's hand-laying after baptism as the practice of the churches.
