Jerome on confirmation (the bishop's hand-laying — honour, not necessity)

Dates: c. 380 · Wikipedia
Summary: In the Dialogue Against the Luciferians Jerome defends the post-baptismal hand-laying as an apostolic custom of the churches — but candidly explains its reservation to the bishop as done “more by way of honouring the episcopate than from any compulsory law,” since presbyters truly baptize in remote places and the Spirit is given in true baptism. He is the honest early witness that the bishop-reserved shape is a matter of church order, not of the Spirit's refusing to come except at a bishop's prayer.

Claims & evidence

The hand-laying and invocation of the Spirit after baptism is a custom of the churches

Don't you know that the laying on of hands after baptism and then the invocation of the Holy Spirit is a custom of the Churches? Do you demand Scripture proof? You may find it in the Acts of the Apostles. And even if it did not rest on the authority of Scripture the consensus of the whole world in this respect would have the force of a command.

— Jerome, The Dialogue Against the Luciferians, §8, trans. W. H. Fremantle (Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, 2nd ser., vol. VI), source.

Reserved to the bishop to honour the episcopate, not by compulsory law

But if you now ask how it is that a person baptized in the Church does not receive the Holy Ghost, Whom we declare to be given in true baptism, except by the hands of the bishop, let me tell you that our authority for the rule is the fact that after our Lord's ascension the Holy Ghost descended upon the Apostles. And in many places we find it the practice, more by way of honouring the episcopate than from any compulsory law. Otherwise, if the Holy Ghost descends only at the bishop's prayer, they are greatly to be pitied who in isolated houses, or in forts, or retired places, after being baptized by the presbyters and deacons have fallen asleep before the bishop's visitation.

— Dialogue Against the Luciferians, §9, source. He grounds the reservation in the good order of the church:

The well-being of a Church depends upon the dignity of its chief-priest, and unless some extraordinary and unique functions be assigned to him, we shall have as many schisms in the Churches as there are priests.

— Dialogue Against the Luciferians, §9, source.

Notes

  • A concession that cuts both ways. Jerome affirms the bishop-reserved rite (the Western shape) yet denies it rests on any “compulsory law” that the Spirit comes only at the bishop's prayer — the very point the Eastern presbyteral practice presses. His candour is load-bearing on both the bishop-reserved and presbyteral minister arguments.
  • Jerome across the wiki. He is a witness on many timelines — the perpetual virginity of Mary, the Old Testament canon, and the Origenist controversy, among others.
