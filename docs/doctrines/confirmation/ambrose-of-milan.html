Ambrose of Milan on confirmation (the spiritual seal and the sevenfold gift)

Dates: c. 390 · Wikipedia
Summary: Ambrose's catecheses for the newly baptized expound the post-baptismal anointing as “the seal of the Spirit” — the sevenfold gift of Isaiah 11 poured into the heart, with the Father sealing and Christ strengthening. It is the classic Latin statement of the “spiritual seal” (signaculum spiritale) the West would develop into the rite of confirmation.

Claims & evidence

The anointing on the head, that we may be a priestly and chosen race

Consider now why this is done, for “the eyes of a wise man are in his head;” therefore the ointment flows down to the beard, that is to say, to the beauty of youth; and therefore, Aaron's beard, that we, too, may become a chosen race, priestly and precious, for we are all anointed with spiritual grace for a share in the kingdom of God and in the priesthood.

— Ambrose, On the Mysteries (De Mysteriis), ch. VI, §30, trans. H. de Romestin (Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, 2nd ser., vol. X), source.

The seal of the Spirit — the sevenfold gift; the Father seals, Christ strengthens

And then remember that you received the seal of the Spirit; the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and strength, the spirit of knowledge and godliness, and the spirit of holy fear, and preserved what you received. God the Father sealed you, Christ the Lord strengthened you, and gave the earnest of the Spirit in your heart, as you have learned in the lesson from the Apostle.

— On the Mysteries, ch. VII, §42, source. De Romestin's note observes that this passage “evidently refers to confirmation, and to the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit received therein.” Ambrose ties it to the Song of Songs:

Place Me as a seal upon thine heart, that thy faith may shine forth in the fulness of the sacrament.

— On the Mysteries, ch. VII, §41, source.

Notes

  • “Strengthened” — the germ of confirmatio. Ambrose's pairing (the Father seals, Christ “strengthened” / confirmavit) supplies the very word and idea — a strengthening for witness — that the later Western rite of confirmation would take as its keynote, echoed at Florence (“the Holy Spirit is given for strength”).
  • Ambrose across the wiki. He is also a witness on the sacraments in general (the same catecheses, on the uncounted cluster of font, seal, and altar), on penance, on the Real Presence, and elsewhere.
