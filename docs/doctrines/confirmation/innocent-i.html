Pope Innocent I on confirmation (the forehead-sealing reserved to bishops)

Dates: 416 · Wikipedia
Summary: Answering a query from Decentius, bishop of Gubbio, Innocent rules that presbyters may anoint the baptized with chrism, but the signing of the forehead with that chrism — the conferring of “the Spirit, the Paraclete” — belongs to bishops alone. It is the earliest extant papal text reserving the post-baptismal forehead-sealing to bishops.

Claims & evidence

Presbyters anoint with chrism; only bishops sign the forehead and confer the Paraclete

For it is permitted presbyters, when they baptize either without a bishop or in the presence of a bishop, to anoint the baptized with chrism, but with chrism which has been consecrated by a bishop; they are not permitted, however, to sign the forehead with that same oil, which signing pertains to bishops only, when they confer the Spirit Paraclete.

— Innocent I, Letter to Decentius of Gubbio (Ep. 25, “Si instituta ecclesiastica,” 19 March 416), as given in Denzinger, The Sources of Catholic Dogma (Enchiridion Symbolorum), no. 98, trans. R. J. Deferrari, source. In the fuller translation of this section (“On the Signing of Infants”) that Kimberly Hope Belcher and Nathan P. Chase quote from Whitaker and Johnson’s Documents of the Baptismal Liturgy, Innocent grounds the reservation in two warrants: “not only is it ecclesiastical custom that shows this should be done only by pontiffs” but also “that reading in the Acts of the Apostles that describes Peter and John being ordered to give the Holy Spirit to those who had already been baptized [Acts 8:14–17]” (Theological Studies 86/2, 2025, 256, source).

Notes

  • Presbyteral anointing and episcopal forehead-seal. Innocent distinguishes the presbyter's anointing with chrism from the bishop's signing of the forehead. The Catechism of the Catholic Church (§1290) traces the Western separation of confirmation from baptism to “the desire to reserve the completion of Baptism to the bishop”; in the Eastern churches the presbyter chrismates with myron a bishop has consecrated. How the Western separation arose is discussed under Did early churches share one order of initiation, and how does the Latin West's separate confirmation relate to it?
  • Echoed almost verbatim by Bede. Three centuries later Bede's Commentary on Acts 8:17 restates Innocent's rule almost word for word: presbyters may anoint with chrism “but only with chrism consecrated by the bishop; however, they are not to anoint the forehead with the same oil, which is reserved to bishops alone when they impart the Paraclete Spirit.”
