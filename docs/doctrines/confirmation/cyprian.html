Cyprian of Carthage on confirmation (the hand-laying and the Lord's seal)

Dates: c. 255 · Wikipedia
Summary: In the rebaptism controversy Cyprian describes the church's standing practice: the baptized are brought to the “prelates of the Church,” and by prayer and the imposition of hands receive the Holy Spirit and are “perfected with the Lord's seal.” He grounds the two-step in the Samaritans of Acts 8, distinguishing the new birth of baptism from the reception of the Spirit at the hand-laying.

Claims & evidence

The baptized are brought to the church's prelates for the hand-laying, and “perfected with the Lord's seal” (Acts 8)

But in respect of the assertion of some concerning those who had been baptized in Samaria, that when the Apostles Peter and John came, only hands were imposed on them, that they might receive the Holy Ghost, yet that they were not re-baptized… And therefore, because they had obtained a legitimate and ecclesiastical baptism, there was no need that they should be baptized any more, but only that which was needed was performed by Peter and John; viz., that prayer being made for them, and hands being imposed, the Holy Spirit should be invoked and poured out upon them, which now too is done among us, so that they who are baptized in the Church are brought to the prelates of the Church, and by our prayers and by the imposition of hands obtain the Holy Spirit, and are perfected with the Lord's seal.

— Cyprian, Epistle LXXII (to Jubaianus), §9 (trans. Ante-Nicene Fathers, vol. V), source.

Baptism gives the new birth; the imposition of hands, the Spirit

But further, one is not born by the imposition of hands when he receives the Holy Ghost, but in baptism, that so, being already born, he may receive the Holy Spirit, even as it happened in the first man Adam. For first God formed him, and then breathed into his nostrils the breath of life.

— Cyprian, Epistle LXXIII (to Pompey), §7, source. The two moments are held together but distinguished: the font births, the hand-laying seals.

Notes

  • The Samaritan two-step (Acts 8) as the pattern. Cyprian reads Acts 8:14–17 — baptism by Philip, then the apostles' hand-laying for the Spirit — as the template for the church's own post-baptismal sealing. Whether that passage warrants a distinct standing rite is weighed on the Acts 8/19 argument page (Chrysostom, by contrast, reads the Samaritan gift as the extraordinary “Spirit of miracles,” not a repeatable sacramental grace).
  • “The prelates of the Church.” Cyprian has the baptized brought to the bishop for the hand-laying — an early anchor for the Western reservation of confirmation to the bishop, later stated as law by Innocent I. It is weighed on the minister argument.
  • Cyprian across the wiki. He is also a witness on penance (the lapsed) and on infant baptism, among others.
