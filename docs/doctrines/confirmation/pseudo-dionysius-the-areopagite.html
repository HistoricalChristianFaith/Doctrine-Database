Pseudo-Dionysius on chrismation (the rite of the Muron / ointment)

Dates: c. 500 · Wikipedia
Summary: In the first systematic treatise on the church's rites, Pseudo-Dionysius counts the “Initiation of Muron” (the consecration of the ointment / chrism) as one of the perfecting sacred mysteries, on a par with baptism and the eucharist. The perfecting unction of the myron, he teaches, confers “the abiding of the supremely Divine Spirit” — the Eastern seal.

Claims & evidence

The “Initiation of Muron” is a distinct perfecting rite

But there is another perfecting Service of the same rank, which our Leaders name “Initiation of Muron,” by contemplating whose parts in due order, in accordance with the sacred images, we shall thus be borne, by hierarchical contemplations, to its Oneness through its parts.

— Pseudo-Dionysius, The Ecclesiastical Hierarchy, ch. IV, trans. John Parker, source.

The perfecting unction of the myron gives the abiding of the Divine Spirit

But further, the perfecting unction of the Muron gives to him who has been initiated in the most sacred initiation of the Birth in God, the abiding of the supremely Divine Spirit; the sacred imagery of the symbols, portraying, as I think, the most Divine Spirit abundantly supplied by Him, Who, for our sakes, has been sanctified as man by the supremely Divine Spirit…

— The Ecclesiastical Hierarchy, ch. IV, source. The sealing follows the font directly:

…they lead him again to the Hierarch, who, when he has sealed the man with the most Divinely operating Muron, pronounces him to be henceforward partaker of the most Divinely initiating Eucharist.

— The Ecclesiastical Hierarchy, ch. II, source.

Notes

  • The chrism counted among the mysteries. That Pseudo-Dionysius lists the consecration of the ointment as one of his six sacred rites (baptism, eucharist, the ointment, ordination, monastic consecration, and the rites over the dead) is one of the standing proofs that no fixed sevenfold count yet existed — but it is also the plainest early witness that the chrism-seal was reckoned a distinct mystery in its own right. His sixfold catalogue is discussed on the seven-sacraments timeline.
  • Sealed by the hierarch, with baptism. In his Eastern rite the same hierarch who baptizes seals with the myron in one continuous initiation — the unbroken shape the Greek East keeps, against the Western separation stated by Innocent I.
