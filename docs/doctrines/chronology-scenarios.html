Biblical chronology: the anchors under competing systems

The companion timeline, Reckoning the Age of the World, tracks who reckoned the age of the world across time. This page does something different: it fixes each of the historic reckoning systems as a whole bundle of commitments — a "scenario" — and reads the same shelf of chronological anchors (Creation, the Flood, the Exodus, the Egyptian sojourn, the pyramids, the Egyptian dynastic spine, the Persian period, and Daniel's Seventy Weeks) straight through under each, to see what that system gets for free, what it must pay for, and what it simply cannot absorb. The three systems weighed here are the mainstream historic ones — the Masoretic "short" reckoning behind Ussher, the Septuagint "long" reckoning, and the rabbinic Anno Mundi of Seder Olam; the revised-Egyptian-chronology schools (Velikovsky, Rohl, Courville) are out of scope. The conventional secular chronology is not a scored column but the neutral baseline the linked pages already establish, against which the three bundles are measured.

Granting a system is not proving it. The discipline is the same as the other scenario ledgers on this wiki: hold a bundle fixed, force it to account for all the anchors at once, and publish the bill — how many independent datings (or texts) it must overturn, and what residue it leaves unexplained even on its own terms. A system can come out carrying more than it can pay. Each anchor is scored under every system into one of four buckets, and the base facts and neutral ratings live on the linked argument and witness pages — this page holds only the delta and the bill.

Related doctrines: Reckoning sacred time — see also Reckoning the Age of the World (the reception-history timeline of the reckoners and systems scored here).

The scenarios

Each scenario is a spelled-out set of commitments with a bill — the specific results (dates or texts) it must hold true and what would break it — not a mood or a label. All three read the same genealogies and the same secular king-lists; they differ on which text-tradition fixes the primeval interval and on how the sacred reckoning locks onto the datable ground at the Egyptian and Persian anchors. The conventional secular chronology is folded into the neutral baseline (see "Where the secular baseline lives" below), not carried as a fourth column.

Scenario A — Masoretic short / Ussher

Grants: the received Hebrew (Masoretic) figures of Genesis 5 and 11 as original — Creation c. 4004 BC, the Flood 2348 BC (1,656 years from Creation), and the whole span reckoned down through Abraham, the Exodus, and the Temple to a Persian period that agrees with the conventional Persian king-list.1 Bill: the compressed primeval and post-Flood intervals leave almost no room before the first datable civilizations — the pyramids (c. 2600–2500 BC) and predynastic Egypt fall before a 2348 BC Flood, so A must compress the radiocarbon-anchored Egyptian dynastic spine forward or absorb those horizons, and any human horizon before 4004 BC (Göbekli Tepe) is impossible on its face. Its one clean gain is at the far end: at the Persian lock A needs no compression, agreeing with the conventional chronology that C must overturn.

Scenario B — Septuagint long

Grants: the Greek figures of Genesis 5 and 11 as original — Creation c. 5500 BC, the Flood c. 3300–3000 BC — the older Christian reckoning of Eusebius and the Byzantine Anno Mundi, weighed on this wiki as arguably the stronger of the primeval reckonings though still weak as a bare calendar date.2 Bill: the extra ~1,000 years relieve the impossibly compressed post-Flood interval and land the Flood before the literate king-lists the short reckoning collides with — so the pyramids fall comfortably after the Flood (a ✓ where A takes a ✗/△) — but the long figures may themselves be inflated to seat Christ at Anno Mundi 5500, and even c. 3300 BC the continuous Egyptian and Mesopotamian sequences run across the Flood horizon with no rupture. Like A, it agrees with the conventional Persian chronology at the far end.

Scenario C — Rabbinic Anno Mundi / Seder Olam

Grants: the Masoretic primeval figures (so it shares A's short Creation-to-Abraham interval, with the traditional Hebrew-calendar Creation epoch of 3761 BC) and the rabbinic reckoning of the later span fixed by Seder Olam Rabbah — whose distinctive commitment is a compressed Persian period: it allots only about 52 years to Persian rule from the Temple's rebuilding to Alexander, some 165 years fewer than the conventional reckoning.3 Bill: it inherits A's whole primeval/pyramids/deep-time cost (same short Genesis figures), and adds its own signature residue at the other end — the Persian-period "missing years." Seder Olam's ~52-year Persia contradicts the independently anchored Persian king-list (Cyrus through Darius III), which Babylonian astronomical tablets, the Canon of Ptolemy, and the Greek historians fix within a year or two; to keep its reckoning C must overturn that secular anchor — a textual/computational move (△/✗) at the very point where sacred and secular chronology otherwise lock.

Where the secular baseline lives

This page carries no naturalistic-null column. The conventional reading of every anchor — the radiocarbon-anchored Egyptian dynastic spine, the astronomically fixed Persian king-list, the deep dates of the first Neolithic horizons — is exactly what the neutral pages this ledger links already establish (the dating of the Flood and the forthcoming anchor argument pages). That reading is the backdrop against which the three systems are measured here, not a further contestant: a cell's △ or ✗ is precisely its distance from what those pages conclude on their own evidence. Folding the baseline in keeps the comparison where the interest is — among the total reckoning systems that read the same anchors differently.

Each row links the full argument or witness page, which keeps its own neutral assessment; the cells report only how that anchor reads conditional on each system.

The bill (per scenario)

The honesty floor of a scenario ledger: for each system, what it still cannot explain (✗) and how many independent datings or textual readings it must overturn (△) — and whether those failures share one mechanism (correlated) or are ad hoc. The friendly bundle is always shown beside its bill, and a scenario ledger may conclude against a bundle. The tallies below are read straight from the scored rows above.

Scenario A — Masoretic / Ussher

✗ residue: the pyramids. The Giza Fourth Dynasty (c. 2589–2504 BC) predates a 2348 BC Flood by some 150 years, a collision A cannot absorb even granting its own premises. △ count: the short interval must move the whole third-millennium Egyptian record down below the Flood, and that single ambition cashes out as four independent datings overturned at once — the Illahun lunar series, dead-reckoning from the king-lists, the 763 BC-anchored Near-Eastern synchronisms, and (decisively) radiocarbon, which dates the pyramids older, not younger. The failures do not share one mechanism, so the bill is ad hoc, not a single correlated compression. ✓ gain: the far end is clean — A's sacred timeline meets the independently-fixed Persian chronology from the Exile onward with no adjustment, the one anchor where sacred and secular positively converge (and the one C must break). Net: a hard Creation-end residue and a four-currency compression bill, against a single clean historical-period win.

Scenario B — Septuagint long

✗ residue: the continuous record. The extra ~1,000 years buy B out of the pyramids collision, but even at c. 3300 BC the unbroken Naqada/Predynastic and Uruk sequences straddle the Flood horizon with no rupture — a residue the long bundle leaves unexplained, since it (rightly) claims no Egyptian compression. △ count: two, and both textual rather than dating — it must hold the longer Septuagint begetting-figures original against the received Hebrew (Genesis 5 & 11), and the “and in Canaan” expansion of Exodus 12:40 against the Masoretic text and the Qumran scroll. These are correlated (both are the same preference for the fuller Greek/expansive text), and the same generosity raises the worry that the figures were inflated to seat Christ at Anno Mundi 5500. ✓ gains: the pyramids fall comfortably after the Flood, and B keeps A's clean Persian convergence; its Egyptian spine needs no move at all. Net: the strongest of the three on the material shelf — it trades A's hard pyramids ✗ for a milder continuity ✗ and pays in text-critical, not dating, currency — but it buys its relief with two contested textual bets.

Scenario C — rabbinic / Seder Olam

✗ residue (two): C is A plus one. It shares A's short Masoretic interval, so it inherits the whole pyramids-before-the-Flood residue at the Creation end; and it adds its own signature failure at the far end — the Persian “missing years,” a ~52-year Persia that contradicts the astronomically-fixed king-list (the Cambyses eclipse of 523 BC, Ptolemy's Canon, the continuous dated tablet archives, the Elephantine double dates, VAT 4956), ~165 years unrecoverable without overturning four independent anchors. △ count: A's whole Egyptian-compression bill (shared) plus the far-end Persian one — so C carries costs at both ends where A carries them at one. ✓ gain: one, and it is internal — the Seventy-Weeks arithmetic (70 + 420 = 490, destruction to destruction) is exact and self-consistent, the fit the system was built to deliver; but it is reverse-engineered from the same compression scored ✗ at the Persian row, so the win and the residue are one commitment seen from two sides. Net: the ledger concludes most heavily against C — strictly dominated by A on the shared shelf and carrying an extra far-end ✗ that A does not, its only positive an internally-designed exegetical fit rather than an external corroboration.
