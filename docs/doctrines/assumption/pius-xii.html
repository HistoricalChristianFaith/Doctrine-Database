Pius XII on the Assumption / Dormition of Mary

Dates: 1 November 1950 (the apostolic constitution Munificentissimus Deus) · Eugenio Pacelli, Pope Pius XII, 1876–1958; reigned 1939–1958 · Wikipedia
Summary: Pius XII defined the Assumption as dogma for the Catholic Church (1950). He declared that Mary, “having completed the course of her earthly life,” was assumed body and soul into heavenly glory; the definition does not state whether she died first.

Claims & evidence

The dogmatic definition

“we pronounce, declare, and define it to be a divinely revealed dogma: that the Immaculate Mother of God, the ever Virgin Mary, having completed the course of her earthly life, was assumed body and soul into heavenly glory.”

— Munificentissimus Deus §44, Vatican. The constitution surveys the liturgical feast, the medieval theologians, and the “fittingness” arguments — among them the reasoning of John of Damascus — before issuing the definition. It grounds the definition on the “concordant teaching of the Church’s ordinary doctrinal authority and the concordant faith of the Christian people” (§12) and holds that the theologians’ proofs “are based upon the Sacred Writings as their ultimate foundation” (§38).1

Whether Mary died

The phrase “having completed the course of her earthly life” (expleto terrestris vitae cursu) does not state whether Mary died before her assumption or was taken up without death. Elsewhere the constitution reports the faithful’s belief that she “had actually passed from this life” (§14) and speaks of the moment when she “passed from this earthly exile to heaven” (§17).1 John Paul II later said that “Pius XII did not intend to deny the fact of her death, but merely did not judge it opportune to affirm solemnly the death of the Mother of God as a truth to be accepted by all believers”;2 Stephen Shoemaker writes that “at the urging of Jugie in particular, the 1950 definition deliberately left open the question of the Virgin’s actual death.”3 The question is the one Epiphanius of Salamis (c. 377) had left undecided.

Notes

  • How the definition relates to the Eastern Dormition is read differently. John Paul II (1997) held that “a common tradition actually exists which sees Mary’s death as her entry into heavenly glory” and that Pius XII did not intend to deny it.2 Kallistos Ware (Orthodox, 1984) contrasts the Orthodox view that Mary “underwent physical death in the usual way” with the definition, “stating merely with studied vagueness expleto terrestris vitae cursu,” and reports that “while most Orthodox are firmly convinced of the reality of the bodily Assumption, they do not consider that it is desirable or even possible to define it as a dogma”; he quotes the Greek theologian Panagiotis Trembelas that the 1950 dogma “is based on apocryphal renditions dating from the fourth century,” adding that Trembelas “does not actually say that he disbelieves the doctrine.”4
  • The constitution argues from the feast (§§16–19) but adds that “the liturgy of the Church does not engender the Catholic faith, but rather springs from it” (§20). Whether the feast led to the definition is taken up on the lex orandi timeline at Did the Marian feasts lead the Church to define the Immaculate Conception and the Assumption?
  • Pius XII appears on this wiki also as a witness on the ministerial priesthood timeline (the encyclical Mediator Dei, 1947), and on the lex orandi timeline, where Mediator Dei reverses the adage: “let the rule of belief determine the rule of prayer.”
