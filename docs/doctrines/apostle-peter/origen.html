Origen on Simon Peter

Dates: c. 230 (quoted by Eusebius, c. 325) · Wikipedia
Summary: The earliest named writer to record that Peter was crucified head-downward. His notice, from a lost commentary and preserved by Eusebius, also places Peter's mission among the Jews of the dispersion before his death at Rome.

Claims & evidence

Peter's mission field, and his crucifixion “head-downwards”

Peter appears to have preached in Pontus, Galatia, Bithynia, Cappadocia, and Asia to the Jews of the dispersion. And at last, having come to Rome, he was crucified head-downwards; for he had requested that he might suffer in this way.

— Origen, Commentary on Genesis book 3, quoted in Eusebius, Ecclesiastical History 3.1. The field Origen assigns Peter matches the recipients of 1 Peter (“Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia”), and the request — that Peter asked to be inverted — also appears in the apocryphal Acts of Peter. Origen gives no reason for the request.

Reception and debate

  • Origen and the Acts. Origen is the earliest datable writer to name the head-downward posture; Tertullian, who mentions the crucifixion, does not. A. C. McGiffert (1890) cites that silence in judging the tradition “exceedingly doubtful” (note on Church History 3.1). Sean McDowell notes that Origen omits the speech that accompanies the scene in the Acts of Peter and calls it “uncertain whether Origen derived this from an independent tradition or from the Acts of Peter” (“Was Peter Crucified Upside Down?”). See Was Peter crucified head-downward?
  • Eusebius quotes the passage in his account of the apostles' mission fields — Thomas to Parthia, Andrew to Scythia, John to Asia, Peter to the dispersion and Rome; see the apostles hub.
