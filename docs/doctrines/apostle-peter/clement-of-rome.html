Clement of Rome on Simon Peter

Dates: c. 96 · Wikipedia
Summary: The earliest extant witness after the New Testament to Peter's martyrdom. Writing from Rome to Corinth, Clement sets the deaths of Peter and Paul among the examples “furnished in our own generation” and names neither the manner nor the place.

Claims & evidence

Peter and Paul, “the greatest and most righteous pillars,” put to death

But not to dwell upon ancient examples, let us come to the most recent spiritual heroes. Let us take the noble examples furnished in our own generation. Through envy and jealousy, the greatest and most righteous pillars [of the Church] have been persecuted and put to death. Let us set before our eyes the illustrious apostles. Peter, through unrighteous envy, endured not one or two, but numerous labours and when he had at length suffered martyrdom, departed to the place of glory due to him.

— First Epistle to the Corinthians 5, source. Clement pairs Peter with Paul, who “after preaching both in the east and west … came to the extreme limit of the west, and suffered martyrdom under the prefects.”

Reception and debate

  • The place. Clement gives no place by name. A. C. McGiffert (1890) holds that “he evidently assumes the place as well known” (note on Church History 2.25), and J. P. Kirsch (1911) counts the letter among the testimonies to Peter's death at Rome (“St. Peter, Prince of the Apostles”). See Did Peter lead and die at Rome?
  • The date. The letter is traditionally dated c. 96. Otto Zwierlein dates it about 120–125 and holds that it knows nothing of Peter at Rome (“Has St. Peter ever been in Rome?”, p. 3).
  • The manner. Clement gives no method of execution and does not mention a cross or its posture.
