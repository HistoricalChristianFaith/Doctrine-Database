Tertullian on Simon Peter

Dates: c. 200 · Wikipedia
Summary: The earliest extant writer to state that Peter died by crucifixion at Rome. He names the cross but not its posture.

Claims & evidence

“Where Peter endures a passion like his Lord's”

Since, moreover, you are close upon Italy, you have Rome, from which there comes even into our own hands the very authority (of apostles themselves). How happy is its church, on which apostles poured forth all their doctrine along with their blood! where Peter endures a passion like his Lord's! where Paul wins his crown in a death like John's where the Apostle John was first plunged, unhurt, into boiling oil, and thence remitted to his island-exile!

— Prescription against Heretics 36, source. “A passion like his Lord's” is read by J. P. Kirsch (1911) and A. C. McGiffert (1890) as death by crucifixion; Tertullian sets it beside Paul's “death like John's,” which McGiffert reads as Paul's beheading, and John's escape from the boiling oil. Elsewhere he writes, reading John 21:18 of the crucifixion: “Then is Peter girt by another, when he is made fast to the cross” (Scorpiace 15).

Reception and debate

  • Carthage, c. 200. Tertullian writes from North Africa a generation before Origen. Wolfgang Dieter Lebek, as summarised by Markus Bockmuehl, finds Tertullian linking Peter's martyrdom to the Neronian persecution in Prescription 36 and Scorpiace 15 “without dependence on the Petrine Acts” (⚠ unverified: Lebek's essay not opened; review in TC 17 (2012), ¶22). See Did Peter lead and die at Rome?
  • The posture. Tertullian names no inversion. McGiffert cites the absence of the detail in the earlier Fathers, “even by Tertullian, who mentions the crucifixion,” in judging the head-downward tradition “exceedingly doubtful” (note on Church History 3.1). See Was Peter crucified head-downward?
  • The New Prophecy. Tertullian spent his later years in the Montanist “New Prophecy”; the later judgements on his standing are set out on the timeline “Was Tertullian a heretic?”
