Jerome on Simon Peter

Dates: c. 393 · Wikipedia
Summary: Jerome's compact Latin life of Peter gives in one paragraph his origin, Antioch, Rome, the twenty-five-year chair, the head-downward crucifixion and the Vatican grave, and lists the writings under Peter's name that were received and those Jerome reports as rejected.

Claims & evidence

The whole life in one paragraph

Simon Peter the son of John, from the village of Bethsaida in the province of Galilee, brother of Andrew the apostle, and himself chief of the apostles, after having been bishop of the church of Antioch and having preached to the Dispersion—the believers in circumcision, in Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia and Bithynia—pushed on to Rome in the second year of Claudius to over-throw Simon Magus, and held the sacerdotal chair there for twenty-five years until the last, that is the fourteenth, year of Nero. At his hands he received the crown of martyrdom being nailed to the cross with his head towards the ground and his feet raised on high, asserting that he was unworthy to be crucified in the same manner as his Lord.

— On Illustrious Men 1, source. The details also appear in earlier sources: the mission field is Origen's, Peter's coming to Rome under Claudius is in Eusebius (Ecclesiastical History 2.14), and J. P. Kirsch (1911) dates the twenty-five-year notice to a third-century entry in the Roman bishop-lists (“St. Peter, Prince of the Apostles”). Origen reports the inverted crucifixion at Peter's request; the reason that Peter was unworthy to die as his Lord appears in the Greek Acts of Peter and Paul (41).

The Petrine writings and the Vatican grave

He wrote two epistles which are called Catholic, the second of which, on account of its difference from the first in style, is considered by many not to be by him. … the books, of which one is entitled his Acts, another his Gospel, a third his Preaching, a fourth his Revelation, a fifth his “Judgment” are rejected as apocryphal. Buried at Rome in the Vatican near the triumphal way he is venerated by the whole world.

— On Illustrious Men 1, source. Jerome reports 2 Peter's authorship as doubted “by many,” and lists the Acts, Gospel, Preaching, Revelation, and Judgment of Peter as rejected; the Acts of Peter is the earliest extant source for the head-downward scene he reports above.

Reception and debate

  • The head-downward posture. Whether the posture has a source independent of the Acts of Peter is discussed at Was Peter crucified head-downward?
  • The twenty-five years. J. P. Kirsch (Catholic, 1911) writes that the notice of “a twenty-five years’ pontificate” entered the Roman bishop-list in the third century, as Eusebius and the “Chronograph of 354” show, and that “we are unable to trace its origin” (“St. Peter, Prince of the Apostles”). A. C. McGiffert (1890), who rejects it, reports that since Jerome the tradition “has been almost universally accepted in the Roman Catholic Church” (note 153 on Eusebius, Ecclesiastical History 2.14, in Church History, Book 2). The First Vatican Council (1870) speaks of the Roman see as one Peter “founded and consecrated with his blood” (Pastor aeternus, ch. 2); John Calvin and Philip Schaff reject a long Roman episcopate. See Did Peter lead and die at Rome?
  • Jerome is also a witness on other timelines — on the Antioch clash (Jerome and Augustine) and the Origenist controversy (Jerome and Rufinus).
