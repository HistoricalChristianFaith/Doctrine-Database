The New Testament on Matthias

Dates: c. 30 (the election) · c. 62–90 (the writing of Acts) · Wikipedia
Summary: Matthias appears in one passage only, Acts 1:15–26, and is the single apostle whose calling the New Testament narrates in full. Between the Ascension and Pentecost the Eleven, on Peter’s motion, filled the place left empty by Judas: the candidate had to have followed Jesus through the whole of the public ministry and to be a witness of the resurrection; two men were put forward; the lot fell on Matthias. He is not named again, nor are most of the Eleven listed in Acts 1:13.

Claims & evidence

Peter proposes to fill Judas’s place

Luke sets the scene in the upper room, in the ten days between the Ascension and Pentecost, before some hundred and twenty believers. Peter rises and reads Judas’s fall as the fulfilment of Scripture, then states the vacancy to be filled:

And in those days Peter stood up in the midst of the disciples, and said, (the number of names together were about an hundred and twenty,) Men and brethren, this scripture must needs have been fulfilled, which the Holy Ghost by the mouth of David spake before concerning Judas, which was guide to them that took Jesus… For he was numbered with us, and had obtained part of this ministry.

— Acts 1:15–17. Peter grounds the replacement in the Psalms — “Let his habitation be desolate… and his bishoprick (episkopē) let another take” (v. 20, citing Psalm 69:25 and 109:8) — so that Peter presents the filling of the Twelve as the fulfilment of prophecy.

The qualification: an eyewitness from the baptism of John to the Ascension

Peter states the requirement for the replacement:

Wherefore of these men which have companied with us all the time that the Lord Jesus went in and out among us, Beginning from the baptism of John, unto that same day that he was taken up from us, must one be ordained to be a witness with us of his resurrection.

— Acts 1:21–22. The twelfth man must have accompanied the apostles from John’s baptism to the Ascension, and is to be “a witness with us of his resurrection.”

Two candidates, prayer, and the lot

Two men were put forward, and the choice between them was referred to God by lot — the last casting of lots recorded in the New Testament:

And they appointed two, Joseph called Barsabas, who was surnamed Justus, and Matthias. And they prayed, and said, Thou, Lord, which knowest the hearts of all men, shew whether of these two thou hast chosen, That he may take part of this ministry and apostleship, from which Judas by transgression fell, that he might go to his own place. And they gave forth their lots; and the lot fell upon Matthias; and he was numbered with the eleven apostles.

— Acts 1:23–26. The company prays to the Lord “which knowest the hearts of all men” to “shew whether of these two thou hast chosen,” and then casts the lot (compare Proverbs 16:33). With verse 26 Matthias is “numbered with the eleven apostles,” and the New Testament’s account of him is over.

Matthias after the election

The New Testament records no saying of Matthias, and no deed, mission or death after the election. Paul hands on the creed that the risen Christ “was seen of Cephas, then of the twelve” (1 Corinthians 15:5). Of the Eleven listed in Acts 1:13, most are likewise not named again.

Reception and debate

  • The one full call-narrative. Matthias is the only apostle whose appointment the New Testament describes step by step, and the passage is its only notice of him.
  • Joseph Barsabbas Justus. The other candidate bears three names. Later identifications connect Matthias with Barnabas, whose given name was also Joseph, or with Zacchaeus; these are set out on the identity page.
