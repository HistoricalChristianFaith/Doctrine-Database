Eusebius on Matthias

Dates: c. 325 (Eusebius of Caesarea, Ecclesiastical History) · Church History
Summary: Eusebius gives Matthias two short notices. In Book 1 he records the tradition, in its earliest extant form, that Matthias and his fellow candidate had first been among the Seventy. In Book 3, sorting the writings in circulation, he lists a “Gospel of Matthias” among the works he calls “the fictions of heretics.”

Claims & evidence

Matthias among the Seventy

Discussing the disciples of Jesus beyond the Twelve, Eusebius notes that no proper catalogue of the Seventy survives, then relays what tradition preserved — including a placement of Matthias:

Matthias, also, who was numbered with the apostles in the place of Judas, and the one who was honored by being made a candidate with him, are likewise said to have been deemed worthy of the same calling with the seventy.

— Eusebius, Ecclesiastical History 1.12.3. Peter’s requirement in Acts 1 was that the replacement have “companied with us all the time that the Lord Jesus went in and out among us”; Luke 10:1 records Jesus sending out the Seventy. Eusebius reports the placement as something “said.” This is its earliest extant record, and later writers (Epiphanius, Nicephorus) repeat it.

The “Gospel of Matthias” among the rejected books

In his sorting of the New Testament writings into accepted, disputed, and rejected, Eusebius reaches a last class — works cited by the heretics under apostolic names — and Matthias’s name is on the list:

…those that are cited by the heretics under the name of the apostles, including, for instance, such books as the Gospels of Peter, of Thomas, of Matthias, or of any others besides them, and the Acts of Andrew and John and the other apostles… the character of the style is at variance with apostolic usage, and both the thoughts and the purpose of the things that are related in them are so completely out of accord with true orthodoxy that they clearly show themselves to be the fictions of heretics. Wherefore they are not to be placed even among the rejected writings, but are all of them to be cast aside as absurd and impious.

— Eusebius, Ecclesiastical History 3.25.6–7. The Catholic Encyclopedia judges this Gospel “probably the document whence Clement of Alexandria quoted several passages” (“St. Matthias”) — the Traditions claimed by the Basilidians (see the Clement page). Eusebius classes all the works of this last group as “to be cast aside as absurd and impious.”

Reception and debate

  • Two notices of one name. In the same work Eusebius places the man among the Seventy and rejects the book that bears his name.
  • The Seventy. The placement of Matthias among the Seventy is first recorded here, c. 325, and Eusebius reports it as something “said.”
  • Beside other apostolic names. Matthias’s name sits here beside Peter’s and Thomas’s in the list of gospels cited under apostolic names.
