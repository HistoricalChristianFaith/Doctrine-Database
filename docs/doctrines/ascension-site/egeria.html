Egeria on the Imbomon (the place of the Ascension)

Dates: c. 384 (the Itinerarium Egeriae, c. 381–384) · Wikipedia
Summary: The Western pilgrim Egeria, whose diary records the 4th-century Jerusalem liturgy at first hand, gives the earliest extant naming, among the sources on this timeline, of the Imbomon — “the place whence the Lord ascended into heaven” — as a fixed liturgical station, distinct from the Eleona lower down. She calls it a “place” and names no church there; the round church on the site is dated c. 384–390.

Claims & evidence

The Imbomon named — “the place whence the Lord ascended” (c. 384)

And when the ninth hour approaches they go up with hymns to the Imbomon, that is, to the place whence the Lord ascended into heaven, and there they sit down, for all the people are always bidden to sit when the bishop is present; the deacons alone always stand.

— The Pilgrimage of Egeria (the Palm Sunday afternoon procession, ch. 31), trans. M. L. McClure & C. L. Feltoe. The gloss — “the Imbomon, that is, the place whence the Lord ascended into heaven” — recurs verbatim each time she names it (Palm Sunday, Holy Thursday, the Easter octave, and Pentecost), a fixed liturgical formula by the 380s.

Distinguished from the Eleona; the Ascension Gospel read there at Pentecost

…both in the church which is on Eleona… and also in the Imbomon, that is, in the place whence the Lord ascended into heaven.

…they first enter the Imbomon, that is, the place whence the Lord ascended into heaven… The passage from the Gospel is also read where it speaks of the Lord's Ascension, also that from the Acts of the Apostles which tells of the Ascension of the Lord into heaven after His Resurrection.

— The Pilgrimage of Egeria, the Easter-octave and Whitsunday (Pentecost) offices (chs. 39 and 43), trans. McClure & Feltoe. Egeria distinguishes the two Olivet sites — “the church which is on Eleona” (Constantine's teaching-cave church) and “the Imbomon” (the higher ascension-spot) — and it is at the Imbomon, on Pentecost, that the Gospel and Acts passages on the Ascension are read there.

Placement on the timeline

Eusebius's Proof of the Gospel places the teaching, the prayer and the Ascension at one cave; Egeria names a distinct upper station, the Imbomon, within a stational liturgy that reads each scene at its own place. She calls it a “place,” never a church; the round church on the Imbomon is dated c. 384–390 and attributed to the pilgrim Poemenia. Egeria's vigil for the fortieth day after Easter is kept at Bethlehem, and the Ascension narrative is read at the Imbomon on Pentecost.

Notes

  • Egeria is also a witness on the timelines of the Garden of Gethsemane (her Holy-Thursday procession “down from the Imbomon”), at Bethany (the Lazarus-Saturday liturgy), and at the tomb of Jesus.
