Natural evil as the penalty of the human Fall — did death and predation enter through Adam’s sin?

Type: supporting argument (off-timeline) · Assessment: weak as a theodicy of the whole natural order — the reading is ancient, genuinely advocated, and this wiki does not fault it for taking Scripture’s Fall narrative at its word; but as an explanation of animal predation, disease, and death it founders on the antiquity of natural evil. Predation, parasitism, and mass extinction are attested in the fossil record for hundreds of millions of years before any human, so a human sin cannot be their cause — unless the whole conventional dating is overturned, which throws the theodicy onto a young-earth chronology this wiki rates weak. The two escapes each cost it its target: restrict the penalty to human death (the strongest exegesis of Romans 5) and it no longer answers natural evil at all; extend it to all creation and it must deny the record. Where it retains real force — as an account of human mortality and its scriptural warrant — it is a doctrine of the Fall, not a theodicy of the predatory world. The claim

The evolutionary theodicy takes the predatory order as intrinsic to creation; the fallen-powers reading blames rebel spirits. The oldest Christian answer to natural evil makes a third move: it pins death, decay, and the whole train of natural suffering on the human Fall. Creation as God made it was “very good” and deathless; death and its train entered as the penal consequence of Adam’s sin, a just sentence inherited by his stock. Augustine states it flatly:

the first men were indeed so created, that if they had not sinned, they would not have experienced any kind of death; but that, having become sinners, they were so punished with death, that whatsoever sprang from their stock should also be punished with the same death.

— Augustine, The City of God XIII.3.1 John of Damascus gathers the Greek verdict into a formula: “God did not create death… its origin is in Adam’s transgression.”2 The scriptural spine is Paul: “by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men” (Romans 5:12); “by man came death” and “in Adam all die” (1 Corinthians 15:21–22); and the groaning creation “made subject to vanity… by reason of him who hath subjected the same” (Romans 8:20–22), read as the whole cosmos falling with its head.3 In its modern young-earth form the claim is sharpened into a pillar: there was no death at all — not even animal death — before the Fall, since a creation containing death and predation could not be called “very good”; Genesis 1:29–30 has humans and animals originally vegetarian, and the fossil record of death must therefore post-date Adam, not pre-date him.4

Who advances it

Two tiers, again to be kept apart. The patristic tier — Augustine, John of Damascus, and much of the tradition after them — teaches that human death is penal, the wage of Adam’s sin, without committing to a fixed age of the earth or pronouncing on the antiquity of the animal fossil record (a question the ancients had no way to raise). The modern young-earth tier — Answers in Genesis, Creation Ministries International — presses the reading to its literal limit: all biological death entered at the Fall, so the earth is young and the fossil-bearing strata are post-Fall (chiefly Flood) deposits, not a record of ages of pre-human death. This tier treats “no death before the Fall” as foundational — if death is “the last enemy” (1 Corinthians 15:26) and the wage of sin, they argue, it cannot have been woven into a creation God called good. The strong form thus stands or falls with young-earth chronology; the modest patristic form does not.

The evidence for

It has the plainest scriptural warrant of the three readings. No rival natural-evil theodicy can point to texts as direct as Romans 5:12 and 1 Corinthians 15:21–22. That death is bound up with sin, and entered “by one man,” is not read into Paul but stated by him; and Romans 8’s groaning, “subjected” creation gives the move a cosmic reach. Where the evolutionary and fallen-powers readings must supply their mechanism by inference, this one claims to be simply following the apostle.

It preserves the goodness of the original creation and gives natural evil a moral cause. Like the fallen-powers reading — and unlike the evolutionary one — it refuses to make God the direct designer of a predatory order. The world’s brutality is not what God made but what sin marred; the “very good” of Genesis 1 is protected, and natural evil is given a morally-responsible author (the human will) other than God. For a reader who holds a historical Adam and a real Fall on other grounds, extending the corruption to the creature’s whole environment costs little extra.

Adversarial assessment

The decisive defeater is the antiquity of natural evil. Predation, parasitism, disease, and mass extinction are attested in the fossil record for hundreds of millions of years — on the mainstream dating, long before any human being existed to sin. A human Fall therefore cannot be the cause of the animal suffering that most needs explaining: the carnivore’s tooth, the parasite, the predator’s prey are older than humanity by orders of magnitude.5 This is the same antiquity that defeats the retrospective, no-death-before-Adam form of the fallen-powers reading, and it is exactly what the evolutionary theodicy is built to accommodate. Two escapes are available, and each is costly:

  • Deny the antiquity (young-earth). The strong “no death before the Fall” form escapes the defeater by rejecting the conventional dating outright: the fossils are post-Fall, the earth is young. But this does not rescue the theodicy so much as fold it into young-earth chronology — which this wiki weighs on its Flood-date and material-record pages and finds weak on the dating evidence (the continuous, un-ruptured stratigraphic and radiometric sequences that run straight across any young horizon). The reading cannot be stronger than the chronology it now depends on. (Per this wiki’s method, the point is not that a young earth is impossible — it is that the dating evidence, weighed on its own merits, tells heavily against it; possibility is not proof.)
  • Restrict the penalty to human death. The stronger exegesis of Romans 5 — and the reading many old-earth Christians adopt — is that Paul speaks of human death: “death passed upon all men” (5:12), “in Adam all die” (1 Corinthians 15:22). Neither Genesis 3, Romans 5, Romans 8, nor 1 Corinthians 15 says anything explicit about animal death; and much of the “death” Paul has in view is arguably spiritual (separation from God) as much as biological. This reading is exegetically defensible — but it concedes the whole predatory order to the pre-Fall world, and so stops being a theodicy of natural evil: it explains human mortality, not the fawn in the fire or the ichneumon wasp.6

The strong form leans on further strained claims. To keep a deathless original creation the young-earth tier is driven to auxiliary moves — that plants are not “living” in the biblical sense and so their being eaten is not “death,” that Romans 8’s “bondage to decay” is a physical corruption of natural law at the Fall — which strain both the text (Genesis uses the same “living”/“kind” language of plants and animals) and the biology. Each patch defends the pillar at the price of new implausibilities.

What holds. The reading is not unsupported: it has the directest proof-texts of any natural-evil theodicy, it honours the “very good” of Genesis and keeps God clear of designing predation, and as an account of human mortality it is on firm and ancient ground. Nor does the wiki fault it for taking Scripture’s Fall at face value. But as an answer to the problem it was invoked to solve — the suffering of the animal world across deep time — it does not hold on its best available case: it must either surrender that world to the pre-Fall era (and answer only human death) or deny a dating record the evidence strongly supports.

Assessment

Weak as a theodicy of natural evil in general. As an account of human mortality — that death for humankind is bound up with sin, on the warrant of Romans 5 and 1 Corinthians 15 — the reading is scripturally grounded and belongs to the doctrine of the Fall. But as an explanation of the whole natural order — animal predation, parasitism, disease reaching back across deep time — it founders on the antiquity of that suffering: a human sin cannot cause a carnage that predates humanity, and the only way to make it do so is to overturn the conventional dating and adopt a young-earth chronology this wiki rates weak on the evidence. Restrict it to save the exegesis and it stops answering natural evil; extend it to answer natural evil and it must deny the record. Genuinely advocated and anciently held, but it does not hold on its best case — weak. On Augustine’s page and any modern proponent’s, flag the slide from “human death is penal” (defensible) to “all death, including the pre-human animal record, entered at the Fall” (the overreach the evidence does not support).
