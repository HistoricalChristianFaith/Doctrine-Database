The Flood and the Nephilim: the evidence under competing scenarios

The other Genesis 6–9 pages on this wiki either ask who believed what (the sons of God and the Nephilim, the historicity of the Flood) or weigh each artifact on its own merits with the premises held open (the material record, the dating of the Flood). This page does something different: it fixes a whole bundle of presuppositions — a "scenario" — and then reads the same body of evidence straight through under each, to see what that scenario gets for free, what it must pay for, and what it simply cannot explain.

Granting a scenario is not proving it. The point is the opposite of apologetics: hold a bundle fixed, force it to account for all the evidence at once, and publish the bill — how many independent datings it must overturn, and what residue it leaves unexplained even on its own terms. A scenario can, and here one does, come out carrying more than it can pay. Each item is scored under every scenario into one of four buckets, and the base facts and neutral ratings live on the linked pages — this page holds only the delta and the bill. The ledger is being extended across the full shelf of evidence; the rows below are the worked core.

The scenarios

Each scenario is a spelled-out set of commitments with a bill — the specific results that must hold true for it, and what would break it — not a mood or a label. Three are carried here; a fourth (a Samaritan reckoning, or a regional-Flood-with-inheritance hybrid) may earn a column as the ledger is worked.

Scenario A — Long chronology + Watcher-inheritance

Grants: a historical Flood with a real event-kernel, dated on the Septuagint long chronology (Flood c. 3300–3000 BC, Creation c. 5500 BC) — the reckoning weighed here as arguably the stronger of the three.1 The Nephilim were a real phenomenon, and the antediluvian "Watcher-arts" of 1 Enoch 7–8 (metallurgy, monumental stonework, astronomy) were genuine technical knowledge carried through the Flood into post-Flood heirs — the inheritance steelman this wiki applies to any "beyond-native-capacity" claim.2 It stays deliberately agnostic between a global and a regional/anthropological Flood except where a particular artifact forces the question (flagged in the row). Bill: it must place deep-time monuments before its own Creation horizon, reconcile the continuous civilization sequences that run across c. 3300–3000 BC with whatever extent it adopts, and show the inherited capacity in the material record, not merely in the texts.

Scenario B — Short chronology / young-earth

Grants: a literal, global Flood c. 2348 BC (the Masoretic reckoning behind Ussher),3 eight human survivors, most of the geologic column laid down by the Flood, and the independent dating apparatus (radiocarbon calibration and, with it, the deep dendrochronology / varve / ice-core / speleothem chains) systematically compressible — accelerated decay or a foreshortened calibration spine.4 Bill: it must overturn essentially that entire dating edifice, and every civilization dated before 2348 BC (Göbekli Tepe, predynastic Egypt, Uruk, Liangzhu) becomes impossible on its face until redated forward by millennia.

Scenario C — Mainstream naturalistic null

Grants: no global Flood and no Nephilim or Watcher-tech; the flood- and giant-traditions are memories of local inundations plus independent invention and diffusion; all conventional datings stand; monumental capacity is ordinary human cultural development.5 Bill: it must carry the convergence of flood and giant traditions on independent seeding and diffusion alone. It is a null for the material question only; the theological claims lie outside its scope.

The bill (per scenario)

The honesty floor of a scenario ledger: for each bundle, what it still cannot explain (✗), and how many independent datings it must overturn (△) — and whether those failures share one mechanism (correlated) or are ad hoc. The tallies below are read from the worked rows above and grow as the ledger is extended; the friendly bundle is shown beside its bill, not apart from it.

Scenario A

Across the ledger so far, Scenario A leaves a real ✗ residue: no authenticated Nephilim remains despite its own prediction, and a deep-time monument (Göbekli Tepe) sitting before its Creation horizon. Its △ cost is, so far, isolated rather than one correlated mechanism — a single contested radiocarbon horizon plus a global-extent sub-model that fails against the continuous sequences (a failure this wiki scopes to the global variant, not to the bundle, since the regional reading absorbs it). Its wins are chronological fit (Liangzhu) and the flood-tradition convergence; what it does not yet have is a positive material trace of the inherited capacity it posits.

Scenario B

Scenario B's ✗ residue is the largest: every civilization dated before 2348 BC is impossible on its face. Its △ cost is enormous but concentrated — it rests on a single correlated claim (the whole radiometric and calibration apparatus is compressible), so it is not ad hoc so much as all-or-nothing: the bill is the size of that one claim, and the continuous records that run unbroken through its Flood year are the sharpest thing it must absorb.

Scenario C

Scenario C carries no material ✗ in the worked rows and overturns no datings (△ = 0). Its whole burden is to carry the convergence of flood and giant traditions on diffusion and independent invention (the ○ cells) — a burden the linked arguments judge it can bear for the material question, though it answers the theological claims not at all, being a null on that axis by construction.
