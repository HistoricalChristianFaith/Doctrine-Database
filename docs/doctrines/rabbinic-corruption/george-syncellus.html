George Syncellus on the Rabbinic Corruption of Scripture

Dates: c. 810 (the Ecloga Chronographica written c. 808–810, left unfinished at his death, c. 810, and continued by Theophanes) · Wikipedia
Summary: The Byzantine monk and chronographer — once synkellos ("cell-mate," a senior ecclesiastical aide) to the patriarch of Constantinople — whose Ecloga Chronographica ("Selection of Chronography") carries the Eusebian chronology argument into a Byzantine universal history. Setting the Septuagint, the Jewish Hebrew, and the Samaritan reckonings of Genesis 5–11 side by side after Eusebius, Syncellus states in his own voice that the "text of scripture used by the Jews has erred in its chronology from Adam to Abraham," that "the Hebrew text used by the Jews is wrong," that the Septuagint was made from "an ancient and a strictly accurate Hebrew text," and that "it is this version that we too have used in our own chronography." His language is chiefly that of error; at the recapitulation he suspects that the Jews "devised" the shorter figures. The line "a criminal act of the Jews," quoted in his name, is Gustav Seyffarth's nineteenth-century rendering of that passage.

Claims & evidence

The Jewish Hebrew text "has erred in its chronology from Adam to Abraham"

"According to the most ancient Hebrew text, which is to this day still in fact preserved by the Samaritans, there is, then, clear agreement with the Septuagint that the patriarchs from the Flood up to Abraham procreated after the passing of one hundred years plus some additional period of time. By what reasoning, therefore, would one say that their predecessors, even though they lived much longer than their posterity, proceeded with the utmost alacrity to procreate, and not rather according to the years marked down in the Septuagint? On this issue, then, careful reflection suggests this conclusion: there is no choice but to acknowledge that, with the exception of Jared and two generations after him, the text of scripture used by the Jews has erred in its chronology from Adam to Abraham, whereas the Samaritan text is in error only for the years from Adam to the Flood."

— George Syncellus, Ecloga Chronographica, trans. William Adler & Paul Tuffin, The Chronography of George Synkellos (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2002), pp. 123–124, source.1 The reasoning follows Eusebius: the tables in this section of the Ecloga are headed "From Eusebios," and Syncellus's argument runs along the same lines — the patriarchs after the Flood are agreed on all sides to have fathered children past a hundred, so the much longer-lived antediluvians can hardly have rushed to father them earlier, and the shorter Hebrew begetting-ages must therefore be the error. Syncellus states the conclusion as his own ("there is no choice but to acknowledge…") and applies it to the Jewish text by name.

"The Hebrew text used by the Jews is wrong": the Septuagint as original and as the Church's text

"But it is abundantly clear that the Hebrew text used by the Jews is wrong. … There is thus complete confirmation that the Septuagint translation was translated from, so it would seem, an ancient and a strictly accurate Hebrew text of scripture. So for good reason it is this version that we too have used in our own chronography, especially insofar as the Church of Christ diffused over the whole earth is devoted to this version alone, the apostles and disciples of our Saviour having handed down a tradition from of old that it be used."

— Ecloga Chronographica, trans. Adler & Tuffin (2002), p. 124, source.2 Here Syncellus adopts the Septuagint as his own working text ("it is this version that we too have used"), and grounds that choice, as Philoxenus and Julian of Toledo do, in the Church's reception and apostolic use of the Septuagint. The traditions about that version's origin are traced on the companion doctrine, the Origin and Inspiration of the Septuagint.

Reception and debate

  • Following Eusebius. The chronology argument in this part of the Ecloga is Eusebian material that Syncellus transmits and affirms — the tables headed "From Eusebios," the reasoning attributed to him. The Ecloga is one of the channels through which much of Eusebius's lost Greek Chronicon survives. On this timeline Syncellus (c. 810) is the Greek witness to the chronology strand after Julian in the Latin West (c. 686).
  • Error and suspicion. Syncellus's usual language is that the Jewish Hebrew "has erred" / "is wrong." At the recapitulation (Paris p. 84) he suspects that the Jews "devised" (excogitatos) the shorter antediluvian begetting-numbers to license early marriage. Jacob of Edessa names an anti-messianic motive, and Agapius narrates a plot. The line often quoted in Syncellus's name — "I concur entirely in the opinion, that this… was a criminal act of the Jews" — comes from the nineteenth-century Egyptologist Gustav Seyffarth (1859), citing "Syncell. p. 84 Ed. Paris." That page of Goar's Paris edition (1652) says that George "suspects" (suspicatur) that the Jews "devised" (excogitatos) the shorter Hebrew begetting-numbers, and calls Eusebius's Hebrew a "bad copy"; the phrase "a criminal act of the Jews" is Seyffarth's wording. Henry B. Smith Jr., who quotes the line, reports that he could not find it in Adler & Tuffin's 725-page translation.3
  • The textual question. Syncellus is a witness to the textual half of the chronology charge — that the short Hebrew numbers are the errant ones and the long Greek the original. The readings of that question, including those of Ronald Hendel, Emanuel Tov and John Wevers, who trace the hundred-year differences to the Greek side, are set out on the Genesis 5/11 chronology page. His appeal to the Samaritan agreement with the Greek for Adam-to-Flood, and his exception of "Jared and two generations after him," concern the same patterned hundred-year differences. He shares the antediluvian genealogy with the Flood doctrine's timeline.
  • Sourcing. Cited from the standard English translation, William Adler & Paul Tuffin, The Chronography of George Synkellos: A Byzantine Chronicle of Universal History from the Creation (Oxford, 2002), pp. 123–124, from Alden Mosshammer's critical edition of the Greek; the comparison of the three chronologies runs pp. 115–125, the book's index entering it under "Jews: corruptions of biblical text." Syncellus is also named in the deflation-witness chain on p. 122 of Smith's ICC 2018 paper.
