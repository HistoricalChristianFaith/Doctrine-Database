Egeria on the tomb of Jesus

Dates: pilgrimage c. 381–384 · Wikipedia
Summary: A western nun or laywoman whose travel journal is the fullest early description of the Jerusalem liturgy — showing the site by the 380s fully woven into daily and Holy-Week worship as three linked stations (the Martyrium basilica, Golgotha/“the Cross,” and the Anastasis over the tomb).

Claims & evidence

Constantine's church “in Golgotha behind the Cross”

the greater church, built by Constantine, which is situated in Golgotha behind the Cross, where the Lord suffered.

— Itinerarium XXV.1, source.

The Anastasis, “where the Lord rose after His Passion”

the holy church also which is at the Anastasis, that is, in the place where the Lord rose after His Passion, was consecrated to God on that day… because the Cross of the Lord was found on this same day.

— Itinerarium XLVIII.1, source (the Feast of the Dedication / Encaenia). The evening lucernare was sung “in the Anastasis and at the Cross.”

What it attests

Egeria is evidence of established veneration by the 380s — the tomb (Anastasis rotunda), Golgotha (“the Cross”), and the Martyrium basilica functioning as a single liturgical complex — not of the original identification, which was already a half-century old when she arrived. She also reports the finding of the True Cross, a tradition absent from Eusebius and grown up over the later fourth century: a legendary overlay on the fourth-century excavation that should be kept distinct from the identification of the site itself — the historicity of that discovery narrative is weighed on its own argument page.
