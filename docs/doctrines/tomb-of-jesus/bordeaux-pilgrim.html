The Bordeaux Pilgrim on the tomb of Jesus

Dates: 333 · Wikipedia
Summary: An anonymous traveller from Bordeaux whose Itinerarium (333) is the earliest surviving pilgrim itinerary to name Golgotha and the tomb — describing the site as already identified and Constantine's basilica already under construction.

Claims & evidence

Golgotha, the tomb, and Constantine's basilica — already marked in 333

On the left hand side is the little hill of Golgotha, where the Lord was crucified. About a stone's throw from there is the crypt where his body was buried and on the third day he rose; at the same place, on the command of the Emperor Constantine, a basilica was built (that is, a church), of marvelous beauty, having reservoirs on the side… and a bath behind in which infants are bathed.

— Itinerarium Burdigalense, trans. A. Jacobs. (Latin of the first clause: A sinistra autem parte est monticulus Golgotha, ubi dominus crucifixus est.)

What it attests — and what it does not

Two things enter the record with this pilgrim. He is the earliest source to call Golgotha a “little hill” (monticulus): the Gospels never call it a hill, so the whole later “Mount Calvary” image traces to here, not to Scripture. And he witnesses the state of the site in 333, mid-construction — the basilica (dedicated 335) was already going up — not an independent rediscovery. The pilgrim is famously credulous elsewhere (shown all manner of relics), so he is a reliable witness to what was pointed out in 333, not a critic of whether the identification was right.

Notes

  • Places the identified site a generation before Cyril and contemporary with Eusebius; together they show the location fixed and monumentalized by the 330s.
  • Alternative full texts: the 1887 Palestine Pilgrims' Text Society translation (archive.org) and ToposText (work 231).
