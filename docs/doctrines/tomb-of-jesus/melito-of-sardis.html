Melito of Sardis on the tomb of Jesus

Dates: d. c. 180 · Wikipedia
Summary: Bishop of Sardis who travelled to Palestine and whose Paschal homily locates the crucifixion “in the centre of Jerusalem.” He is the earliest extant non-biblical writer read as pointing to the site; whether his phrases carry topographical weight is disputed. The homily names no tomb or Golgotha.

Claims & evidence

The killing happened “in the centre of Jerusalem”

This one was murdered. And where was he murdered? In the very center of Jerusalem!

— On the Passover §72, source.

An extraordinary murder has taken place in the center of Jerusalem, in the city devoted to God's law, in the city of the Hebrews, in the city of the prophets, in the city thought of as just… but it was in the middle of the main street, even in the center of the city, while all were looking on, that the unjust murder of this just person took place.

— On the Passover §94, source.

Reception and debate

The homily names no tomb, no Golgotha, and no shown site. Melito visited Palestine (he tells us elsewhere he went east to establish the Old Testament canon). Joan Taylor, in “Golgotha: A Reconsideration of the Evidence for the Sites of Jesus’ Crucifixion and Burial” (New Testament Studies 44, 1998), reads “in the middle of the city” as reflecting the post-Hadrianic topography: after Hadrian's building enclosed the old quarry, a site that had been outside the earlier wall now lay inside the city, and a spot in the middle of Jerusalem was pointed out to Melito as the place where Jesus died. She adds that his words fit her New Testament-based placement of the crucifixion “if Melito’s plateia is, in fact, the Decumanus.” On this reading Melito is an indirect second-century witness that the location was already remembered. A. E. Harvey is reported as an earlier holder of the same reading ⚠ unverified.

U. C. von Wahlde is reported as reading the phrases as rhetoric — the homily's force being the public enormity of killing the just one in the holy city, paralleling the prophets slain in Jerusalem — without topographical weight ⚠ unverified.

Notes

  • Placement on the timeline: the earliest dated entry after Hadrian's building on the summary timeline.
  • Translation quoted is the Kerux/Kline rendering; the Greek of §72–94 reads epi mesēs plateias kai en mesō poleōs (“in the middle of the street and in the centre of the city”).
