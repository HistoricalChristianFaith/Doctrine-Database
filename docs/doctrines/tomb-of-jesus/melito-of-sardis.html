Melito of Sardis on the tomb of Jesus

Dates: d. c. 180 · Wikipedia
Summary: Bishop of Sardis who travelled to Palestine and whose Paschal homily locates the crucifixion “in the centre of Jerusalem.” He is the earliest non-biblical writer sometimes read as pointing to the site — but his language is rhetorical, names no tomb or Golgotha, and reaches less than later apologists claim.

Claims & evidence

The killing happened “in the centre of Jerusalem”

This one was murdered. And where was he murdered? In the very center of Jerusalem!

— On the Passover §72, source.

An extraordinary murder has taken place in the center of Jerusalem, in the city devoted to God's law, in the city of the Hebrews, in the city of the prophets, in the city thought of as just… but it was in the middle of the main street, even in the center of the city, while all were looking on, that the unjust murder of this just person took place.

— On the Passover §94, source.

How far this reaches

Melito visited Palestine (he tells us elsewhere he went east to establish the Old Testament canon), and one scholarly reading — A. E. Harvey's, taken up cautiously by Joan Taylor — holds that “in the middle of the city” reflects the post-Hadrianic topography: after Hadrian's forum enclosed the old quarry, a memory-site that had been outside the earlier wall now lay inside the city, so a spot “in the centre” could plausibly have been shown to Melito. On this reading Melito is an indirect second-century witness that the location was already remembered.

The counter-reading (U. C. van Wahlde and others) is that the phrases are pure rhetoric — the homily's whole force is the public enormity of killing the just one in the holy city, paralleling the prophets slain in Jerusalem — and carry no topographical weight at all. The decisive honest point either way: Melito names no tomb, no Golgotha, and no shown site. He is the earliest possible external anchor for the tradition, not proof of it; “Melito confirms the Holy Sepulchre” is an inference by later readers, not a statement Melito makes.
