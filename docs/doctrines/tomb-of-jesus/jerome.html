Jerome on the tomb of Jesus

Dates: c. 347–420 (Letter 58 c. 395) · Wikipedia
Summary: The Bethlehem scholar who reports Hadrian's pagan occupation of the site and places Golgotha at the execution ground outside the gate. In the same letter he discourages pilgrimage to Jerusalem.

Claims & evidence

A statue of Jupiter over the tomb, of Venus over the cross-rock, for 180 years

From the time of Hadrian to the reign of Constantine—a period of about one hundred and eighty years—the spot which had witnessed the resurrection was occupied by a figure of Jupiter; while on the rock where the cross had stood, a marble statue of Venus was set up by the heathen and became an object of worship. The original persecutors, indeed, supposed that by polluting our holy places they would deprive us of our faith in the passion and in the resurrection.

— Letter 58.3 to Paulinus, source.

Golgotha is the execution ground “without the city outside the gate”

A plausible interpretation [that Golgotha = Adam's grave]… yet not a true one. Without the city outside the gate are the places where criminals are executed, and these have got the name of Calvary, that is, of the beheaded. And Jesus was crucified there…

— Commentary on Matthew 27:33 (this translation as preserved in Aquinas, Catena Aurea). Jerome here rejects the identification of Golgotha as Adam's grave and cites instead “without the gate” (Heb 13:12). His reasons are the sense of the name, that the two thieves were crucified on the same spot, and the scriptural burial of Adam at Hebron (Josh 14:15). The Adam's-grave tradition is reported also by Origen ⚠ unverified.

Features of the letter

Jerome's account of Hadrian's temple differs from Eusebius's: Jerome names a Jupiter over the tomb and a Venus over the cross, where Eusebius, writing some sixty years earlier, names only the Venus/Aphrodite shrine over the cave. The burden of Letter 58 is to dissuade Paulinus from pilgrimage — “Access to the courts of heaven is as easy from Britain as from Jerusalem.”

Notes

  • Jerome appears on many timelines; on this one he figures for the Hadrianic-suppression report and the “outside the gate” passage on the summary.
  • Jerome reports a comparable Hadrianic Adonis grove over the Bethlehem cave; see the birthplace of Christ.
