Jerome on the tomb of Jesus

Dates: c. 347–420 (Letter 58 c. 395) · Wikipedia
Summary: The Bethlehem scholar who supplies both the fullest report of Hadrian's pagan occupation of the site and a plain affirmation of the topographical test — that Golgotha was the execution ground outside the gate. A pilgrimage-sceptic, which makes his witness the more disinterested.

Claims & evidence

A statue of Jupiter over the tomb, of Venus over the cross-rock, for 180 years

From the time of Hadrian to the reign of Constantine—a period of about one hundred and eighty years—the spot which had witnessed the resurrection was occupied by a figure of Jupiter; while on the rock where the cross had stood, a marble statue of Venus was set up by the heathen and became an object of worship. The original persecutors, indeed, supposed that by polluting our holy places they would deprive us of our faith in the passion and in the resurrection.

— Letter 58.3 to Paulinus, source.

Golgotha is the execution ground “without the city outside the gate”

A plausible interpretation [that Golgotha = Adam's grave]… yet not a true one. Without the city outside the gate are the places where criminals are executed, and these have got the name of Calvary, that is, of the beheaded. And Jesus was crucified there…

— Commentary on Matthew 27:33 (this translation as preserved in Aquinas, Catena Aurea). Jerome here rejects the pious “Adam's skull” legend and affirms instead the Gospel requirement (“without the gate,” Heb 13:12).

Weighing the witness

Two things to hold in view. Jerome's account of Hadrian's temple differs from Eusebius's: Jerome names a Jupiter over the tomb and a Venus over the cross, where the earlier, contemporary Eusebius names only the Venus/Aphrodite shrine over the cave. Writing some sixty years later and from Bethlehem, Jerome does not have priority on that detail; the discrepancy should be flagged, not silently harmonized. But his value is real precisely because he is no promoter of the site: the whole burden of Letter 58 is to talk Paulinus out of pilgrimage — “Access to the courts of heaven is as easy from Britain as from Jerusalem” — so his testimony to the remembered location is not special pleading for a shrine he wanted people to visit.
