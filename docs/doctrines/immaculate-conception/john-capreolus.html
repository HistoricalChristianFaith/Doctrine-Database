John Capreolus on the Immaculate Conception

Dates: c. 1380–1444 · Dominican; Defensiones Bk III written c. 1426–32 · Wikipedia
Summary: John Capreolus (Jean Cabrol), called the princeps thomistarum, the “Prince of Thomists,” devoted his one major work — the Defensiones theologiae Divi Thomae Aquinatis — to defending Aquinas against fourteenth-century critics, among them Scotus and Peter Auriol. On Mary’s conception he holds, with Aquinas, that she was conceived in original sin and sanctified after her soul was infused — claim (a), not (b).

Claims & evidence

He concludes that the Blessed Virgin was conceived in original sin

…beata Maria fuit concepta in peccato originali. Hanc conclusionem tenet beatus Thomas…

— Capreolus, Defensiones theologiae Divi Thomae Aquinatis, Bk III, dist. 3, q. 1 (“the Blessed Mary was conceived in original sin; this conclusion St Thomas holds”), Paban–Pègues edition (Tours, 1900–08), vol. 5, p. 26.1 He sets it out as the single conclusion (unica conclusio) of the article and then marshals Aquinas’s reasoning behind it, adding that this is why “blessed Bernard wrote to the people of Lyons that her conception was not to be celebrated” — the Bernard letter of c. 1140 reported earlier on this timeline.

The structure of the Defensiones: Aquinas’s teaching, the objections of Scotus and Auriol, and replies

The Defensiones is structured as a running rebuttal: for each distinction of the Sentences Capreolus states Aquinas’s teaching, then the objections of Scotus, Auriol, Durandus and others, then his replies. On Mary’s sanctification (Bk III, d. 3) he takes up the immaculist arguments — including the fittingness reasoning that God could and therefore did preserve her — and answers them from the universality of original sin and of Christ’s redemption, from which, on his account, an exemption at conception would remove Mary. This is the objection of Aquinas, restated against the Scotist reply to it.1
