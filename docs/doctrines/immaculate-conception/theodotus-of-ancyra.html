Theodotus of Ancyra on the Immaculate Conception

Dates: d. c. 446 (Theodotus, bishop of Ancyra; a quasi-official theologian and anti-Nestorian preacher at the Council of Ephesus, 431; the Marian-purity text is his Oration on the Mother of God) · Wikipedia
Summary: Theodotus, bishop of Ancyra and an anti-Nestorian preacher at Ephesus, praises Mary in his Oration on the Mother of God as “a virgin innocent, without spot, void of culpability, holy in body and in soul… untaught the ills of Eve,” and adds that she, “when not yet born, was consecrated to God.” Frederick Holweck (Catholic, Catholic Encyclopedia, 1910) quotes the passage among his texts on “the absolute purity of Mary”; Martin Jugie (Catholic, 1952) concludes from Theodotus’s homilies that he saw Mary as free of the original stain from the first instant of her existence, while noting a comparison in another homily that he finds hard, at first sight, to reconcile with that reading.

Claims & evidence

“A virgin innocent, without spot… untaught the ills of Eve”

A virgin innocent, without spot, void of culpability, holy in body and in soul, a lily springing among thorns, untaught the ills of Eve, nor was there any communion in her of light with darkness, and, when not yet born, she was consecrated to God.

— Oration on the Mother of God (§11), as quoted in the Catholic Encyclopedia’s catalogue of witnesses to “the absolute purity of Mary.”1 The sentence sets Mary against Eve, “a lily springing among thorns,” “untaught the ills of Eve,” and closes with a consecration before her birth. Elsewhere Theodotus greets her as “the immaculate Mother of holiness.”2

The purification at the Annunciation (Homily IV)

In his fourth homily, On the Holy Mother of God and Simeon, Theodotus describes a “transformation of sanctity” that the Virgin underwent when the Spirit came upon her, by two comparisons: a piece of black, dross-laden iron cast into the fire that sheds its foreign matter and takes the purity of its nature, and a man standing under a waterfall who is wetted from head to foot. Jugie, who translates the passage into French, notes that the Greek text as published is “fort défectueux” (very defective).3

Reception and debate

  • Holweck (1910). Holweck lists Theodotus among the Fathers who compare Mary with Eve and quotes him on Mary’s purity. Of such passages generally he writes that “the rhetorical character, however, of many of these and similar passages prevents us from laying too much stress on them,” and that “the Greek Fathers never formally or explicitly discussed the question of the Immaculate Conception.”4
  • Jugie (1952). Martin Jugie (Assumptionist, L’Immaculée Conception dans l’Écriture sainte et dans la tradition orientale) devotes a section to Theodotus. Of the iron-in-the-fire comparison he writes that it “suggèrerait, au premier abord, quelque chose de difficilement conciliable avec la sainteté originelle de Marie” (would suggest, at first sight, something hard to reconcile with Mary’s original holiness), and asks whether the preacher was carried by his comparison “au delà de sa véritable pensée” (beyond his true thought); he inclines to the second alternative “à cause de la sainteté parfaite et perpétuelle que Théodote attribue à Marie” in the sixth homily. He concludes: “De l’ensemble de ces expressions il paraît bien ressortir que Théodote voyait Marie exempte de la souillure originelle et sainte dès le premier instant de son existence” (From all these expressions together it appears that Theodotus saw Mary as free of the original stain and holy from the first instant of her existence).3
  • The prenatal-consecration clause names a sanctification before birth. Bernard of Clairvaux (c. 1140) granted Mary a sanctification in the womb on the model of Jeremiah and John the Baptist while denying that her conception was holy; the readings of the Greek “prepurified / all-holy” vocabulary are set out on the disputed-reading pages Does Gregory Nazianzen’s prokathartheisa testify to the Immaculate Conception? and Does the Eastern “all-holy” vocabulary (Panagia) testify to the Immaculate Conception?
  • The quoted Oration is also catalogued in Michael O’Carroll, Theotokos, s.v. “Theodotus of Ancyra”; the Greek is in Migne, Patrologia Graeca 77. His fellow Ephesus-era preacher Proclus of Constantinople also appears on this timeline, and Theodotus is a witness on the Theotokos timeline as an anti-Nestorian at Ephesus.
