John of Damascus on the Immaculate Conception

Dates: c. 730 (John of Damascus, monk of Mar Saba, c. 675/676–749; the key text is Homily I on the Nativity of the Theotokos, one of his mature Palestinian sermons, with supporting matter from An Exposition of the Orthodox Faith) · Wikipedia
Summary: Preaching on Mary’s own nativity, John praises not only her life but her conception and her parents — the “wholly unblemished seed” of Joachim, the “all-holy infant” grown in Anne’s womb, a birth in which, in Holweck’s rendering, “nature did not dare to anticipate the germ of grace.” He frames the homily in terms of God’s foreknowing love and predestination of Mary. John Janaro (Catholic, 2006) reads these praises as corresponding “essentially to the doctrine of the Immaculate Conception”; Craig Truglia (Orthodox, 2020) cites John for a purification of Mary at the Annunciation. The Orthodox reception of the 1854 definition is reported on its own page.

Claims & evidence

The “unblemished seed” of Joachim and the “all-holy infant” in Anne’s womb

O most all-blessed loins of Joachim, from which a wholly unblemished seed was sent forth! O renowned womb of Anna, in which slowly, with additions from her, an all-holy infant grew, and once it had taken shape, was born!

— Homily I on the Nativity of the Theotokos, §2, trans. Mary B. Cunningham, source.1 The praise here extends to the act of Mary’s conception and to her parents.

“Nature did not dare to anticipate the germ of grace”

Nature has been defeated by grace and stands trembling, no longer ready to take the lead.

— Homily I on the Nativity of the Theotokos, §2, trans. Cunningham, source. Frederick Holweck (Catholic, Catholic Encyclopedia, 1910) renders the fuller sentence — “when the Virgin Mother of God was to be born of Anne, nature did not dare to anticipate the germ of grace, but remained devoid of fruit” — and reads John as extending God’s influence to Mary’s parents, so that “the material of which she was formed, was pure and holy” — “this opinion of an immaculate active generation and the sanctity of the ‘conceptio carnis’,” he adds, “was taken up by some Western authors.”2

God foreknew and predestined her

The God of all things, having known in advance your worth, loved you; and because of this love, he predestined you, and “at the end of times” he brought you into being and revealed you as Theotokos…

— Homily I on the Nativity of the Theotokos, §7, trans. Cunningham, source. Here the homily speaks of God’s foreknowing love and predestination: God loves Mary in advance and brings her into being for her office as Theotokos.

Her flesh “pure and immaculate” in the Exposition

…the Son of God and God in that He was made flesh and became man from her pure and immaculate flesh and blood…

— An Exposition of the Orthodox Faith, IV.14, trans. S. D. F. Salmond, source. In his dogmatic handbook Mary’s flesh is “pure and immaculate,” the flesh from which the incarnate Word takes his humanity.

Reception and debate

  • Catholic readings. Holweck cites the “germ of grace” line among the patristic passages on Mary’s purity. John Janaro (Catholic, The Saint Anselm Journal, 2006) calls John “arguably, the first theologian to draw together the whole rich tradition of the Christian East regarding the ‘All-Holy’ Theotokos and express it in terms that we can clearly recognize today as corresponding fully to the doctrine of the Immaculate Conception,” and writes that the later Greek Fathers, “with perhaps the exception of Saint John of Damascus,” wrote “somewhat more vaguely regarding the issues that would become the focus of Latin theologians.” Reading the homily’s praises through what he calls a “hermeneutic of doxology,” he holds that John’s “Marian praises” “correspond essentially to the doctrine of the Immaculate Conception.”3
  • Orthodox readings. Craig Truglia (Orthodox, 2020) quotes John’s Exposition of the Orthodox Faith — “after the assent of the Holy Virgin, the Holy Spirit descended on her” — on the Spirit “purifying her,” and reads John as placing Mary’s prepurification at the Annunciation rather than at her own conception.4 The readings of the Greek “all-holy” vocabulary by Vladimir Lossky and Kallistos Ware are set out on the disputed-reading page Does the Eastern “all-holy” vocabulary (Panagia) testify to the Immaculate Conception?
  • Elsewhere on this wiki. John appears also as a witness to Mary as the New Eve, to the Assumption / Dormition, to the Theotokos, and to the perpetual virginity.
