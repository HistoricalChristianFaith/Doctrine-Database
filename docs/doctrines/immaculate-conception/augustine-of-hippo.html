Augustine of Hippo on the Immaculate Conception

Dates: c. 415 (On Nature and Grace) · c. 354–430 · Wikipedia
Summary: Answering Pelagius’s list of saints said to have lived without sin, Augustine excepts Mary from the question of sin “out of honour to the Lord,” citing the “abundance of grace for overcoming sin in every particular” conferred on her. Frederick Holweck (Catholic, Catholic Encyclopedia, 1910) lists the passage among the patristic testimonies to Mary’s purity. Augustine’s teaching on original sin, transmitted to every descendant of Adam, is the teaching Kallistos Ware (Orthodox) calls “the idea of ‘original guilt,’ put forward by Augustine.”

Claims & evidence

Mary excepted from the question of sin

We must except the holy Virgin Mary, concerning whom I wish to raise no question when it touches the subject of sins, out of honour to the Lord; for from Him we know what abundance of grace for overcoming sin in every particular was conferred upon her who had the merit to conceive and bear Him who undoubtedly had no sin.

— On Nature and Grace, ch. 36 (42), source. Augustine is answering Pelagius, who had drawn up a list of Old- and New-Testament saints said to have “lived without sin.” Augustine holds that all of them, if asked, would confess with the Apostle John, “If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves.” Mary alone he declines to submit to that question, on the ground of the “grace for overcoming sin” given to the mother of the sinless Lord.1

Reception and debate

  • Holweck (Catholic Encyclopedia, 1910), listing patristic testimonies, renders the passage: all the just have known sin “except the Holy Virgin Mary, of whom, for the honour of the Lord, I will have no question whatever where sin is concerned.”2 How this and other first-millennium texts on Mary’s purity bear on the 1854 definition is the subject of a disputed-reading page.
  • Augustine taught that original sin is contracted by every human being through generation from Adam. Medieval writers who held that Mary was sanctified after her conception — Bernard, Bonaventure, Aquinas — argued from the universality of original sin and of Christ’s redemption; Duns Scotus answered with an account of Mary’s redemption as preservative. Kallistos Ware writes that “most Orthodox theologians reject the idea of ‘original guilt,’ put forward by Augustine” (see the Eastern Orthodox response).3
