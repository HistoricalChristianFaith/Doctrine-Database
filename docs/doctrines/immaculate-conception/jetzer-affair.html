The Jetzer affair (Bern, 1507–1509)

Dates: 1507–1509 (executions 31 May 1509) · Wikipedia
Summary: In the Dominican convent at Bern, apparitions of the Virgin Mary were staged before a lay brother, Hans Jetzer, in which “Mary” declared she had been conceived in original sin, the position the Bern Dominicans held with their order against the Franciscan teaching on the Immaculate Conception. After trials that relied on confessions extracted under torture, four Dominicans were burned at Bern on 31 May 1509. Who devised the apparitions is disputed among historians: the Bern verdict held the four friars responsible; Nikolaus Paulus (1897) presented Jetzer as the sole guilty party; Rudolf Steck (1904) called the verdict judicial murder.

Claims & evidence

The events at Bern, 1507–1509

Hans Jetzer (c. 1483–c. 1514), a tailor’s assistant, entered the Dominican convent at Bern as a lay brother in 1507. From early that year he was subjected to a series of staged supernatural manifestations — a ghost, then apparitions of the Virgin Mary (with St Barbara), false stigmata, and a weeping image of the Virgin with painted “bloody tears.” The scripted “revelation” inverted the immaculist position: the Bern Dominicans held, with their order, that Mary “like all humans except Jesus Christ, was born into original sin,” and the staged Virgin was made to say she was distressed “at being said to have been born without sin, when that honor belonged to her son alone.”1

The matter went to trial. There were, in effect, three proceedings between 1507 and 1509, culminating in a retrial authorized by Pope Julius II; on 23 May 1509 the four accused friars were defrocked and handed to the secular arm. On 31 May 1509 the prior Johann Vatter, the subprior Franz Ueltschi, the lector Stephan Boltzhurst, and the steward Heinrich Steinegger were burned at the Schwellenmatte in Bern.2 Jetzer himself was acquitted of the friars’ crimes, banished, and escaped from prison; he is thought to have died around 1514 ⚠ unverified.

The historiography of the verdict

The verdict rested in significant part on confessions extracted under torture: Jetzer was tortured in February 1508 and under torture accused his superiors, and the friars too were tortured. Nikolaus Paulus (1897) presented Jetzer as the sole guilty party, orchestrating the fraud himself, and the four friars as victims of “judicial murder.” Rudolf Steck (1904), who published the records of all three trials, also described the verdict as judicial murder, but laid responsibility on the pope.3 Kathrin Utz Tremp, Warum Maria blutige Tränen weinte (2022), is reported as arguing that the illiterate Jetzer was more likely the dupe than the mastermind (the theological script drawing on learned Mariological sources he could not have read), and as reading his 1509 acquittal as the judgment of contemporaries who had the full evidence before them ⚠ unverified.4
