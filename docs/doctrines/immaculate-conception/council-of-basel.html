The Council of Basel on the Immaculate Conception

Dates: 17 September 1439 (Session 36) · Wikipedia
Summary: On 17 September 1439 the Council of Basel declared the doctrine that Mary was never subject to original sin “pious, and consonant to the Catholic faith,” and forbade preaching against it. By then Pope Eugene IV had transferred the council to Ferrara, and the assembly remaining at Basel had purported to depose him; its sessions from this period are not counted as those of an ecumenical council in the Catholic reckoning, and Frederick Holweck (Catholic Encyclopedia, 1910) writes that for this reason it “could not pronounce with authority.”

Claims & evidence

Basel declares the doctrine pious and consonant to the faith

This doctrine asserting that the glorious Virgin Mary, Mother of God, through the special preventing and operating grace of the divine Being, was never actually subjected to original sin, but was always exempt from original and actual sin, holy and immaculate, we define and declare as pious, and consonant to the Catholic faith, to right reason, and to Sacred Scripture… that it is not lawful for anyone to preach or to teach anything to the contrary.

— Council of Basel, Session 36 (17 September 1439), a standard English rendering.1 The decree speaks of preservation from original sin by a “preventing” grace, the position Scotus had argued at the start of the fourteenth century.

The standing of the session

By 1437 Eugene IV had transferred the council to Ferrara (and thence to Florence); the members who remained at Basel did not follow, and on 25 June 1439 — less than three months before this session — they purported to depose the reigning pope as a heretic, later electing Felix V. The editors of Papal Encyclicals Online write that “all agree that the sessions held at Basel after the 25th session… cannot be regarded as sessions of an ecumenical council,” and the Catholic Encyclopedia’s article on the council calls the post-transfer body a “schismatical conventicle.”2 Holweck writes: “As the council at the time was not ecumenical, it could not pronounce with authority.”3 In 1483 Sixtus IV stated that “up to this time there has been no decision made by the Roman Church and the Apostolic See.” In 1515 Cajetan, writing against the doctrine, called the Basel assembly “schismatic” and its decree the act of “the synagogue of Satan.”

Reception and debate

  • Basel’s decree is the conciliar text that later defenders of the doctrine cited, and that Cajetan rejected for its provenance; Sixtus IV’s constitutions of 1476–1483 and the Council of Trent’s clause of 1546 are recorded on their own pages.
  • The English wording above is well attested but the original Latin of Session 36 was not confirmed here against a primary conciliar edition; for the Latin see Mansi, Sacrorum Conciliorum… Collectio, or Denzinger, Enchiridion Symbolorum.
