The Council of Trent on the Immaculate Conception

Dates: 17 June 1546 (Session 5, Decree Concerning Original Sin) · Wikipedia
Summary: In its decree on original sin the Council of Trent declared that it did not intend to include in the decree “the blessed and immaculate Virgin Mary, the mother of God,” and ordered the constitutions of Pope Sixtus IV observed. What the clause implies about the doctrine is read differently by Catholic and Protestant writers; see the disputed-reading page.

Claims & evidence

The Marian exemption clause

This same holy Synod doth nevertheless declare, that it is not its intention to include in this decree, where original sin is treated of, the blessed and immaculate Virgin Mary, the mother of God; but that the constitutions of Pope Sixtus IV., of happy memory, are to be observed, under the pains contained in the said constitutions, which it renews.

— Council of Trent, Session 5 (17 June 1546), Decree Concerning Original Sin, trans. J. Waterworth (1848), source.1 The clause sets Mary outside the decree on original sin and renews the constitutions of Sixtus IV, which had forbidden either party in the dispute to condemn the other.

Reception and debate

  • Martin Chemnitz (Lutheran) and Pius IX read the clause as favouring Mary’s freedom from original sin; Philip Schaff (Protestant), Frederick Holweck (Catholic) and Hubert Jedin (Catholic historian) read it as leaving the question undecided. Their readings are set out on the disputed-reading page.
  • This is the same council that appears elsewhere on this wiki — on the real presence, the ministerial priesthood, prayer to the saints, and the Old Testament canon; the Marian clause belongs to the fifth session of 1546.
