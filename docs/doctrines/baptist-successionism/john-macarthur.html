John MacArthur on Baptist Successionism

Dates: c. 2018 (1939–2025) · Wikipedia
Summary: John MacArthur, the American Reformed-Baptist pastor of Grace Community Church (Sun Valley, California), spoke of a perpetual remnant outside Rome: that throughout "the years of the Roman Catholic monolith" there were "always dissenters" — Waldensians, Anabaptists, Huguenots — who "really were the remnant of those who held to the true New Testament gospel."

Claims & evidence

An ever-present "remnant" of true-gospel dissenters under Rome

Throughout the years of the Roman Catholic monolith in the Western world there were always dissenters, there were always those who upheld the true faith, whether you were talking about Waldensians or Anabaptists or Huguenots or whatever it was there were always those groups who really were the remnant of those who held to the true New Testament gospel.

— interview clip "Exposing Roman Catholicism (John MacArthur)," recorded for Larry Wessel's documentary crew at a conference, at 0:45–1:07 (YouTube, published 12 Jan 2018).1 MacArthur's wider preaching frames the same history through the lens of the "sacral society" that punished dissenters: in a sermon in his series Explaining the Heresy of Catholicism (Grace to You, dated 30 April 2006) he notes the Donatists and "Anabaptists and others" as those who "protested" the church-state monolith, holding that "there have always been true people of God who challenged the sacral society."2

Reception and debate

  • MacArthur speaks of a “remnant” of those who held the gospel; in the interview he does not name a succession of Baptist churches such as J. M. Carroll's The Trail of Blood (1931) charts.1
  • The Catholic apostolate Catholic Truth answers that the groups MacArthur names held contrary doctrines — the Anabaptists rejecting infant baptism, the Waldensians accepting it — and that the Waldensians of Peter Waldo were Catholics who sought reform and held the sacraments and a priestly hierarchy.3 The readings of the wider question of Baptist origins are set out at Where did the Baptist churches come from: an unbroken succession of churches from Christ, a beginning among English Separatists, or kinship with earlier dissenters and the Anabaptists?
  • Provenance note: the quoted span is transcribed from the interview video itself, the firsthand source. The phrasing does not appear verbatim in MacArthur's published Grace to You sermons surveyed here (the Explaining the Heresy of Catholicism series and the Catholic-Mass sermons), which carry the cognate "sacral society / dissenters" material cited above rather than this exact sentence.
