Charles Spurgeon on Baptist Successionism

Dates: c. 1861 (1834–1892) · Wikipedia
Summary: Charles Haddon Spurgeon, the English Particular Baptist preacher, stated Baptist successionism from the pulpit — that Baptists are "the original Christians," who "never came from the Church of Rome, for we were never in it," with "an unbroken line up to the Apostles themselves." He framed it in an 1861 address welcoming the Baptist churches of London to the newly opened Metropolitan Tabernacle.

Claims & evidence

Baptists are "the original Christians" with "an unbroken line up to the apostles"

We believe that the Baptists are the original Christians. We did not commence our existence at the Reformation, we were Reformers before Luther or Calvin were born; we never came from the Church of Rome, for we were never in it. We have an unbroken line up to the Apostles themselves! We have always existed from the very days of Christ, and our principles, sometimes veiled and forgotten like a river which may travel underground for a little season, have always had honest and holy adherents.

— The New Park Street Pulpit, Vol. VII (1861), Sermon No. 376, "Public Meeting of Our London Baptist Brethren" (delivered 2 April 1861), p. 225, full text at ccel.org (also at Answers in Genesis). The setting is a welcome address, not a history lecture: Spurgeon had invited the Baptist churches of London to gather at the just-completed Metropolitan Tabernacle, telling them the building belonged not to him or his congregation especially but "to all the Baptist denomination," and that he felt as if he were "rendering up the trust deeds to the proper proprietors" — those "who hold the pure primitive ancient apostolic faith." The successionist claim comes at the climax of that welcome.

Reception and debate

  • The address is cited on both sides of the debate over Baptist origins. The Wikipedia article on the Baptists lists Spurgeon among holders of the successionist view, and the successionist writer R. L. Vaughn cites him as a holder who was not a Landmarker, unlike J. R. Graves and J. M. Carroll.1 The readings of the question are set out at Where did the Baptist churches come from: an unbroken succession of churches from Christ, a beginning among English Separatists, or kinship with earlier dissenters and the Anabaptists?
  • Textual note: the CCEL text reads “for we were never in it. We have an unbroken line up to the Apostles themselves!” and “for a little season”; the Answers in Genesis transcription reads “for we were never in it, but we have an unbroken line up to the apostles themselves” and “for a little time.” The passage is at NPSP/MTP Vol. VII, p. 225 (sermon 376).
