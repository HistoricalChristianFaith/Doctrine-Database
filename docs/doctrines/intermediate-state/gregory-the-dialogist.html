Gregory the Dialogist on the Intermediate State

Dates: c. 594 (Dialogues, Bk. IV) · Wikipedia
Summary: Pope Gregory I (c. 540–604), "the Great" or "the Dialogist," gives an extended Latin account of the soul between death and resurrection. In his Forty Gospel Homilies he reads Abraham's bosom (Luke 16:22–26) as the "secret rest of the fathers" — a real place where the blessed dead repose, visible to the damned in the deep but unreachable, and held back from the kingdom only until the Mediator's coming. In Dialogues Book IV he argues at length that the soul survives the body, that souls of the perfect enter heaven's joys at once at death, and that for certain lighter faults there is a cleansing fire before the last judgment.

Claims & evidence

Abraham's bosom as the "secret rest of the fathers"

Now it happened that both died. The rich man, who was clothed in purple and fine linen, was buried in hell; but Lazarus was led by angels into Abraham's bosom. What does Abraham's bosom signify but the secret rest of the fathers? Of which the Truth says: "Many will come from the East and the West, and will recline with Abraham and Isaac and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven; but the children of the kingdom will be cast out into outer darkness."

— Forty Gospel Homilies, Homily 40, historicalchristian.faith. Gregory identifies "Abraham's bosom" not as final heaven but as a hidden place of rest holding the fathers, the destination of the blessed dead "after this life."

The lost in the deep and the blessed above them, before the day of the last judgment

But being afar off, the rich man lifted up his eyes to behold Lazarus, because the unbelievers while they suffer the sentence of their condemnation, lying in the deep, fix their eyes upon certain of the faithful, abiding before the day of the last Judgment in rest above them, whose bliss afterwards they would in no wise contemplate. But that which they behold is afar off, for thither they cannot attain by their merits.

— Catena Aurea (citing Gregory's Morals on Job, lib. 4 c. 29), historicalchristian.faith. The two parties of the dead are already separated and already in their states — punishment and rest — yet "before the day of the last Judgment," i.e. in the intermediate state, with a fixed gulf between them.

The just held back in remote places of rest before the Mediator's coming

Now if Abraham sate below, the rich man placed in torments would not see him. For they who have followed the path to the heavenly country, when they leave the flesh, are kept back by the gates of hell; not that punishment smites them as sinners, but that resting in some more remote places, (for the intercession of the Mediator was not yet come,) the guilt of their first fault prevents them from entering the kingdom.

— Catena Aurea (citing Gregory's Morals on Job, lib. 4 c. 29), historicalchristian.faith. In Gregory's account the just who died before Christ rest in remote places, "kept back by the gates of hell," not punished as sinners but not yet admitted to the kingdom, "for the intercession of the Mediator was not yet come."

The soul's life after the death of the body

the soul doth live after the death of the body, reason doth teach us, assisted and holpen with faith

— Dialogues, Book IV, ch. 3, tertullian.org. Dialogues IV argues at length, against doubters, that the soul is a conscious substance that outlasts the body.

The souls of the perfect and the joys of heaven at death

the souls of them that be perfect, do, straight after death, possess the joys of heaven

— Dialogues, Book IV, ch. 25, tertullian.org. In Gregory's account the perfect do not wait for the general resurrection to enjoy heaven but receive its joys at once; of the wicked he writes (ch. 28) that "from the day of their departure, fire doth afflict and burn the reprobate," tertullian.org.

A cleansing fire before judgment for lighter faults

Each one will be presented to the Judge exactly as he was when he departed this life. Yet, there must be a cleansing fire before judgment, because of some minor faults that may remain to be purged away.

— Dialogues, Book IV, ch. 39 (on "whether there be any fire of purgatory in the next world"), tertullian.org. Resting the claim on 1 Corinthians 3:12–15 ("wood, hay, stubble") and Matthew 12:32 (sins forgiven "in the world to come"), Gregory states that "slight transgressions" not purged in life are purged after death by a fire before the last judgment. The passage is cited on the purgatory and prayer-for-the-dead timeline.

Summary of the passages and sources

  • The passages above describe three features of Gregory's account: (1) the dead separated into rest or punishment at death; (2) a place of rest for the pre-Christian just (Abraham's bosom, the "secret rest of the fathers"), held back from the kingdom until the Mediator's coming; and (3) a cleansing fire before the last judgment for lighter faults.
  • The Latin homily quotes are firsthand from his Forty Gospel Homilies; the Morals on Job passage is preserved in Aquinas's Catena Aurea and the Dialogues IV passages are cited from the standard tertullian.org edition. The cleansing-fire sentence is given here in Odo Zimmerman's rendering of Dialogues IV.39.
  • In Gregory's account the coming of the Mediator opens the kingdom to the just "kept back by the gates of hell." John of Damascus (c. 749) writes that Christ's soul descended into Hades and "freed those who had been bound for ages."
