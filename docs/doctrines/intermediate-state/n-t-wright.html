N. T. Wright on the Intermediate State

Dates: c. 2008 · Wikipedia
Summary: N. T. Wright (b. 1948), New Testament scholar and former Bishop of Durham, writes in Surprised by Hope (2007) of a conscious intermediate state — "paradise," a "blissful garden" of rest where the Christian dead are in "restful happiness," consciously with Christ — and holds that this interim is not the Christian hope. On his account the hope is bodily resurrection in God's renewed creation: not merely "life after death" but "life after life after death." He criticizes the popular summary of the gospel as "going to heaven when you die."

Claims & evidence

Wright on "paradise" in Luke 23:43

Paradise is, rather, the blissful garden where God's people rest prior to the resurrection.

— Surprised by Hope (SPCK / HarperOne, 2007), p. 41, archive.org. Commenting on Luke 23:43, Wright argues that the paradise Jesus promises the thief "clearly cannot be their ultimate destination" — the next chapter's empty tomb is — but is the temporary, restful place where God's people await the resurrection. He restates this at p. 150: paradise is "not a final destination but the blissful garden, the parkland of rest and tranquility, where the dead are refreshed as they await the dawn of the new day."

Wright on the state of the Christian departed

All the Christian departed are in substantially the same state, that of restful happiness. … they are held firmly within the conscious love of God and the conscious presence of Jesus Christ while they await that day.

— Surprised by Hope, p. 172, archive.org. Wright describes a single, undifferentiated interim, distinct both from soul-sleep and from purgatory: the dead are conscious, at rest, and "with Christ" (Phil 1:23), but not yet raised. He rejects purgatory and any further sorting of the saved; in rejecting purgatory he agrees with the Westminster Confession, while describing the interim as a conscious rest before the resurrection.

"Life after life after death": Wright on the resurrection hope

Resurrection [is] a way of talking about a new bodily life after whatever state of existence one might enter immediately upon death. It [is], in other words, life after life after death.

— Surprised by Hope, p. 151, archive.org. Wright distinguishes "life after death" (the conscious interim), which he holds is real but penultimate, from the Christian hope proper, "life after life after death" — the resurrection of the body into God's new creation. He argues that "as long as we see salvation in terms of going to heaven when we die," the Church mislocates its mission; for Wright the resurrection, not the intermediate state, is the gospel's horizon.

Context

  • Wright affirms conscious survival between death and resurrection, against soul-sleep, and on the structure of the interim describes one undifferentiated state, without compartments or purgatory.
  • He subordinates the intermediate state to the bodily resurrection, treating "where do souls go" as secondary to "what is our final hope?"
  • Earlier witnesses on this timeline also describe an interim rest for the righteous under the figure of Abraham's bosom: Gregory of Nyssa as "the good state of the soul" at rest, and Bede as "the rest of the blessed." Wright's account of paradise as a temporary garden of rest includes no limbus patrum emptied at the descent and no divided Hades.
