Doug Wilson on the Intermediate State

Dates: c. 2021 · Wikipedia
Summary: Douglas Wilson (b. 1953), a Reformed/CREC pastor in Moscow, Idaho, in the expository sermon "The Nephilim, Hades, and Other Oddments" describes a two-compartment Hades — a structured underworld in which a chasm divides Tartarus (the prison of the rebel angels) from Paradise / Abraham's Bosom (the righteous dead). On this reading the descended Christ heralded his victory to the imprisoned angels rather than offering them the gospel (1 Pet 3:18–20).

Claims & evidence

Hades as a structured place: a chasm between Tartarus and Paradise

There was a chasm between Tartarus on the one side, and Paradise on the other. … The lowest pit of Hades there was called Tartarus, and that is where the angels were locked up—the ones that Jesus preached to.

— The Nephilim, Hades, and Other Oddments, dougwils.com. Wilson reads Hades as a divided geography: at the lowest pit is Tartarus, the prison of the rebel angels (2 Pet 2:4, tartaróō); on the other side of the chasm is Paradise / Abraham's Bosom, where the righteous dead rest (Luke 16:22–26, the rich man and Lazarus separated by "a great gulf fixed"). It is to this Paradise that Christ tells the penitent thief he will come — "that day" (Luke 23:43). In Wilson's account the intermediate state is a peopled place between death and the resurrection, neither an undifferentiated sleep nor an immediate final heaven or hell.

Wilson describes the chasm in the past tense ("there was a chasm"), of Hades at the time of Christ's descent. In a companion sermon he states that the righteous compartment has since been emptied: "the part of Hades that contained the saints of God has been emptied out" (Apostles Creed 11; see below).

1 Peter 3:18–20 read as a herald of victory to imprisoned angels

The word rendered as preached here is not the word for preaching the gospel, which means that this was not an offer of a second chance. The word means to "herald," or to "announce." … The Lord was declaring that He was now the final victor, and that He now held the keys to Death and Hades (Rev. 1:18).

— The Nephilim, Hades, and Other Oddments, dougwils.com. On Wilson's reading the descended Christ goes to Hades and proclaims victory to the disobedient angels of Noah's day locked in Tartarus — distinguishing kēryssō ("to herald / announce") from euangelizō ("to preach the gospel"), so that the proclamation is one of conquest, not salvation: Christ now "held the keys to Death and Hades" (Rev 1:18). This locates the "spirits in prison" among the rebel angels rather than the human dead. Earlier witnesses on this timeline identify them with human dead: Clement of Alexandria with "those that perished in the flood," and Augustine with those preached to "in the days of Noah."

The question bears both on who the spirits are and on what Christ proclaimed to them. Clement of Alexandria, on this timeline, describes the preaching in Hades as the gospel, offered so that some might repent; Wilson describes it as an announcement of victory, not "an offer of a second chance."

Reception and context

  • Wilson's fuller treatment of these texts — the angelic reading of Genesis 6, the embodiment mechanism, and the same Hades and 1 Peter material in their original Nephilim context — is on his Doug Wilson on the Nephilim page. This page carries only the part of his sermon that bears on the intermediate state.
  • Second Temple texts on this timeline also describe divisions among the dead awaiting judgement (see 1 Enoch 22).
  • Responding to the charge that the structured-Hades reading is Greek underworld cosmography (the Aeneid's Tartarus/Elysium) in Jewish dress, Wilson argued in Hell and Hellenism: the "Hellenism" label "is not enough to condemn it," Peter himself uses Tartarus (2 Pet 2:4), and "why did God switch to Greek for the New Testament?" (dougwils.com). Andrew Perriman replied point by point (postost.net), asking of the Elysium = Abraham's bosom equation, "Where is the evidence for that?", and arguing that in 2 Peter "only the fallen angels are kept here, and there is no reference to torment," so that Tartarus there is not the rich man's place of torment.
  • Tertullian and Hippolytus, earlier on this timeline, also describe a structured Hades. The Westminster Confession (32.1), a Reformed confessional standard, states that the souls of the righteous are received at death "into the highest heavens" and those of the wicked cast into hell.
  • In a companion sermon Wilson describes the descent's effect on the human dead: "all the saints in the Old Testament who had died and gone to Abraham's bosom were transferred when Paradise was moved … the part of Hades that contained the saints of God has been emptied out" (The Apostles Creed 11: He Descended Into Hades, dougwils.com). In the same passage he cites Ephesians 4:8 ("He led captivity captive") for the risen Lord's leading out of the saints, Matthew 27:52 for the moving of Paradise, and 2 Corinthians 12:4 for Paradise being above "by the time of Paul."
