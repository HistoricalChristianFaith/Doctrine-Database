2 Maccabees on the Intermediate State

Dates: c. 124 BC · Wikipedia
Summary: 2 Maccabees 12:39–45 is the earliest extant Jewish text on this timeline to state that the living can aid the dead. Finding idolatrous tokens on his fallen soldiers, Judas Maccabeus takes up a collection and sends a sin offering to Jerusalem for them, reasoning from the resurrection that to "pray for the dead" is "an holy and good thought" — making "a reconciliation for the dead, that they might be delivered from sin." Augustine, in On Care to Be Had for the Dead, cites the passage for sacrifice offered for the dead, and it is the opening witness on the purgatory and prayer-for-the-dead timeline.

Claims & evidence

A sin offering is sent to Jerusalem for the slain

And when he had made a gathering throughout the company to the sum of two thousand drachms of silver, he sent it to Jerusalem to offer a sin offering, doing therein very well and honestly, in that he was mindful of the resurrection:

— 2 Maccabees 12:43 (KJV/Apocrypha), Wikisource. After idolatrous amulets are found under the coats of the dead (vv. 39–40), Judas does not write them off: he gathers silver and sends it to the temple as an offering on their behalf, and the author commends the act as done "very well and honestly, in that he was mindful of the resurrection."

Prayer for the dead presupposes the resurrection

For if he had not hoped that they that were slain should have risen again, it had been superfluous and vain to pray for the dead.

— 2 Maccabees 12:44 (KJV/Apocrypha), Wikisource. The author grounds the practice in the resurrection hope: it would be "superfluous and vain to pray for the dead" had Judas not hoped "that they that were slain should have risen again."

A reconciliation is made for the dead, that they might be delivered from sin

And also in that he perceived that there was great favour laid up for those that died godly, it was an holy and good thought. Whereupon he made a reconciliation for the dead, that they might be delivered from sin.

— 2 Maccabees 12:45 (KJV/Apocrypha), Wikisource. The dead "that died godly" have "great favour laid up" for them, and the offering is "to make a reconciliation for the dead, that they might be delivered from sin." Augustine, in On Care to Be Had for the Dead §3, cites it: "In the books of the Maccabees we read of sacrifice offered for the dead" (historicalchristian.faith).

Reception and context

  • On this timeline it is the earliest extant text to speak of the dead being helped by the prayers, alms, and sacrifice of the living. It is also the opening witness on the purgatory and prayer-for-the-dead timeline.
  • Canonical status. 2 Maccabees is received in the Catholic canon (as one of the deuterocanonical books) and in the Eastern Orthodox canon; it is not in the Jewish canon, and Protestants class it among the Apocrypha. How much weight the passage carries for doctrine is read differently according to that status.
  • On this timeline it stands with 1 Enoch 22 and Wisdom 3 among Second Temple texts that speak of the dead awaiting the resurrection or the judgement.
