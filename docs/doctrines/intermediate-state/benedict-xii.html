Benedict XII on the Intermediate State

Dates: c. 1336 · Wikipedia
Summary: Pope Benedict XII, in the constitution Benedictus Deus (1336), addressed the question of when the blessed see God — the question raised in the controversy over his predecessor John XXII's teaching that the souls of the blessed departed do not see God until after the Last Judgment. The constitution states that these souls, immediately after death (and, for those who need it, after purification), before they resume their bodies and before the general judgment, see the divine essence "with an intuitive vision and even face to face."

Claims & evidence

The purified souls see the divine essence at once, before the resurrection and the general judgment

… all these souls, immediately (mox) after death and, in the case of those in need of purification, after the purification mentioned above, since the ascension of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ into heaven, already before they take up their bodies again and before the general judgment, have been, are and will be with Christ in heaven, in the heavenly kingdom and paradise, joined to the company of the holy angels.

— Benedictus Deus (1336), papalencyclicals.net (Denzinger 1000–1002). The words "before they take up their bodies again and before the general judgment" place the vision within the interval between death and resurrection. According to the Catholic Encyclopedia (1910), "Pope John XXII", John XXII had written before his election that "the souls of the blessed departed do not see God until after the Last Judgment," "advanced the same teaching in his sermons" as pope, and before his death affirmed that separated souls "enjoyed in heaven the Beatific Vision." The fuller description of his view (that the souls of the just rest "under the altar," seeing only Christ's humanity, until the resurrection) is ⚠ unverified.

The vision is intuitive and face to face, and they are truly blessed

… these souls have seen and see the divine essence with an intuitive vision and even face to face, without the mediation of any creature … moreover, by this vision and enjoyment the souls of those who have already died are truly blessed and have eternal life and rest.

— Benedictus Deus (1336), papalencyclicals.net. In the constitution's terms, the souls already see "the divine essence … without the mediation of any creature" and are "truly blessed and have eternal life and rest" before the resurrection.

Purification after death, then the vision

… if they then needed or will need some purification, after they have been purified after death …

— Benedictus Deus (1336), papalencyclicals.net. The constitution speaks of "purification after death" for those who need it, after which they attain the vision. It thus states both a post-mortem purification and an immediate vision for the purified.

Reception and context

  • Thomas Aquinas, earlier on this timeline, holds that the perfectly cleansed righteous enter the vision of God immediately. Benedictus Deus states the immediate vision for the purified and dates it from Christ's ascension.
  • Essence and energies. Vladimir Lossky (Orthodox, The Vision of God, 1963) sets Benedictus Deus beside the Councils of Constantinople of 1341, 1351 and 1368, which in his account affirm that God's essence "cannot be the object of knowledge or vision even for the blessed and the angels, to whom the Divine Being is revealed and has become knowable in His uncreated and deifying energies." He reads the two as "two formulae neatly opposed" on the object of the vision, a question distinct from the timing Benedict XII addressed, and leaves open whether the difference "is due simply to a difference of terminology, or if there is after all a basic difference in theological conception" (Lossky, The Vision of God, trans. Asheleigh Moorhouse, London: Faith Press; Clayton, Wis.: American Orthodox Press, 1963, pp. 9–10). Joseph Hontheim (Catholic, Catholic Encyclopedia (1910), "Heaven") writes: "That the blessed see God is a dogma of faith, expressly defined by Benedict XII (1336)"; he adds: "Although the blessed see God, they do not comprehend Him, because God is absolutely incomprehensible to every created intellect."
