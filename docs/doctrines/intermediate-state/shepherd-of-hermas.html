The Shepherd of Hermas on the Intermediate State

Dates: c. 150 · Wikipedia
Summary: The Shepherd pictures the righteous dead who fell asleep before Christ — the just of earlier generations — still awaiting the gospel in the realm below, reached there by the apostles and teachers who, after their own deaths, preached the name of the Son of God and "sealed" them so that they too could be built into the tower (the Church) and enter the kingdom. It is among the earliest extant Christian texts to describe a preaching to the dead.

Claims & evidence

Similitude 9.16: the apostles and teachers preach to those who had fallen asleep

"Because," he said, "these apostles and teachers who preached the name of the Son of God, after falling asleep in the power and faith of the Son of God, preached it not only to those who were asleep, but themselves also gave them the seal of the preaching. Accordingly they descended with them into the water, and again ascended. [But these descended alive and rose up again alive; whereas they who had previously fallen asleep descended dead, but rose up again alive.] By these, then, were they quickened and made to know the name of the Son of God. For this reason also did they ascend with them, and were fitted along with them into the building of the tower, and, untouched by the chisel, were built in along with them. For they slept in righteousness and in great purity, but only they had not this seal.

— The Shepherd of Hermas, Similitude 9.16, historicalchristian.faith. In the vision the tower is the Church, built of stones drawn up "out of the pit"; the forty stones that ascend already sealed are the apostles and teachers. Because "before a man bears the name of the Son of God he is dead," the just who slept "in righteousness and in great purity" before Christ "had not this seal" and could not enter the kingdom unsealed. So the dead apostles and teachers descend to them in the place below, preach the name, and "seal" them — and those who "had previously fallen asleep descended dead, but rose up again alive," quickened to "know the name of the Son of God."

Context

  • The Shepherd pictures a post-mortem preaching and "sealing" of the dead: the righteous who died before the gospel are reached in the realm below by the apostles and teachers after their own deaths, not by Christ in person. 1 Pet 3:19 and 4:6 ("the gospel preached … to them that are dead") also speak of a preaching to the dead; in the Shepherd the preachers are the apostles and teachers.
  • The passage concerns how the pre-Christian just are brought into the Church and the kingdom. It assumes a place "below" where the dead remain, and does not describe the layout of that place or the soul's state of waiting.
  • Clement of Alexandria, who likewise has the apostles (following Christ) preach to the dead in Hades, quotes this passage of Hermas twice: in Stromata II ("the Shepherd, speaking plainly of those who had fallen asleep, recognises certain righteous among Gentiles and Jews"; historicalchristian.faith) and in Stromata VI ("it is well said by the Shepherd"; historicalchristian.faith).
