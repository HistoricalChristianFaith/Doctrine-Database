Eusebius of Caesarea on the Intermediate State

Dates: c. 324 · Wikipedia
Summary: Eusebius of Caesarea, in the Church History, gives in his account of Abgar of Edessa a summary of preaching in which Christ went down to Hades, burst its ancient bars, and raised the dead — "he descended alone, but rose with many." It is a fourth-century text on the harrowing of Hades, set within a narrative report.

Claims & evidence

Christ descended into Hades, burst its eternal bars, and raised the dead — "descended alone, but rose with many"

and concerning his new preaching, and his abasement and humiliation, and how he humbled himself, and died and debased his divinity and was crucified, and descended into Hades, and burst the bars which from eternity had not been broken, and raised the dead; for he descended alone, but rose with many, and thus ascended to his Father.

— Church History (Historia Ecclesiastica) I.13.19, historicalchristian.faith. The line sits within Eusebius's report of the Edessa/Abgar correspondence: it is the preaching the apostle Thaddeus promises to deliver before the citizens of Edessa, summarizing the gospel from the incarnation to the ascension. In the formula "descended alone, but rose with many," Christ enters the place of the dead by himself and, having "raised the dead," rises with many.

Reception and context

  • This is a brief credal-style descent statement, not a developed treatment of Hades or of the soul's interim condition. Eusebius presents the Abgar documents as taken "from the archives" of Edessa and "translated from the Syriac literally"; the line is part of the preaching attributed there to Thaddeus, not an argument in Eusebius's own voice. In it the descent appears as one item in a summary of the gospel from the incarnation to the ascension.
  • The sequence — descent into Hades, the breaking of its "bars which from eternity had not been broken," and the raising of the dead — treats the place of the dead as holding captives whom Christ releases.
  • Irenaeus, earlier on this timeline, reads Christ's descent as preaching to the prophets and patriarchs below.
