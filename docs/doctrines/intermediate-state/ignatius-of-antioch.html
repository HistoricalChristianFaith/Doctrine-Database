Ignatius of Antioch on the Intermediate State

Dates: c. 107 · Wikipedia
Summary: Ignatius of Antioch, writing to the churches while in chains on his way to martyrdom (d. c. 107), writes that the prophets who awaited Christ as their Teacher were, at his coming, "raised … from the dead." The line is placed on this timeline beside later accounts of Christ's descent to the righteous dead; Ignatius does not say where the prophets were or how they were raised.

Claims & evidence

The prophets who awaited Christ, "raised … from the dead"

that we may be found the disciples of Jesus Christ, our only Master—how shall we be able to live apart from Him, whose disciples the prophets themselves in the Spirit did wait for Him as their Teacher? And therefore He whom they rightly waited for, being come, raised them from the dead.

— Epistle to the Magnesians, ch. 9, historicalchristian.faith. Ignatius's point is christological and polemical (against keeping the Sabbath "after the Jewish manner"): the prophets foresaw Christ "in the Spirit" and waited for Him as Teacher. His closing clause states that at His coming He "raised them from the dead." Ignatius gives no account of where the prophets were before that, or of the structure of the realm of the dead.

Context

  • Ignatius does not narrate a harrowing of Hades; his statement is that the prophets awaited the Teacher, who came and "raised them from the dead." Later witnesses on this timeline, among them Melito of Sardis and Irenaeus, describe Christ's descent to the dead.
  • Ignatius writes within years of the apostolic age and is roughly contemporaneous with Polycarp of Smyrna, his fellow recipient of an Ignatian letter; both stand at the head of the timeline.
  • The statement is brief. Ignatius nowhere describes the structure of Hades, names a place of waiting, or distinguishes compartments. The text used here is the shorter recension; the longer recension expands the same chapter but adds no further statement on this point.
