The Catechism of the Catholic Church on the Intermediate State

Dates: c. 1992 · Wikipedia
Summary: The Catechism of the Catholic Church (promulgated 1992) sets out the Catholic Church's present teaching on the descent. Under the article "He Descended into Hell" (§§632–635) it teaches that Christ's soul, truly joined to the dead, "descended there as Saviour, proclaiming the Good News to the spirits imprisoned there" (1 Pet 3:18–19) — that he delivered "the just who had gone before him," the holy souls who "awaited their Saviour in Abraham's bosom," but "did not descend into hell to deliver the damned." It thus states both what the descent accomplished and what it did not: the freeing of the just, not a universal deliverance.

Claims & evidence

Christ descended as Saviour, proclaiming the Good News to the imprisoned spirits

It is precisely these holy souls, who awaited their Saviour in Abraham's bosom, whom Christ the Lord delivered when he descended into hell.

— Catechism of the Catholic Church §633, vatican.va. §632 states that Christ "descended there as Saviour, proclaiming the Good News to the spirits imprisoned there" (citing 1 Pet 3:18–19), and §633 identifies those he delivers as the OT righteous held in "Abraham's bosom" — the place that Thomas Aquinas, earlier on this timeline, calls the limbus patrum.

He freed the just who had gone before him — not the damned

Jesus did not descend into hell to deliver the damned, nor to destroy the hell of damnation, but to free the just who had gone before him.

— Catechism of the Catholic Church §633, vatican.va. The Catechism limits the deliverance to "the just who had gone before him."

"The gospel was preached even to the dead"

The gospel was preached even to the dead.

— Catechism of the Catholic Church §634, vatican.va. Quoting 1 Pet 4:6, the Catechism calls the descent "the last phase of Jesus' messianic mission," "the spread of Christ's redemptive work to all men of all times and all places." The risen Christ now holds "the keys of Death and Hades" (Rev 1:18; §635).

Placement on the timeline

  • On this timeline it is the next Catholic magisterial document after Benedict XII's Benedictus Deus (1336). The timeline also carries the Reformed Westminster Confession (1646).
  • The article retains the creed clause "He descended into hell" while explaining that Scripture calls "the abode of the dead, to which the dead Christ went down, 'hell' - Sheol in Hebrew or Hades in Greek" (§633), as distinct from "the hell of damnation." (That the underlying Latin is inferi is ⚠ unverified.) On the clause itself, see the question of the descent clause in the Apostles' Creed.
