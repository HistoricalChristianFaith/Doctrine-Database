The New Testament on the Intermediate State

Dates: c. AD 60–90 · Wikipedia
Summary: The New Testament passages most cited on this timeline for the state of the dead are the parable of the rich man and Lazarus, with "Abraham's bosom," a place of "torments" and "a great gulf fixed" between them (Luke 16); Christ's promise to the thief, "To day shalt thou be with me in paradise" (Luke 23); and Paul's language of being "absent from the body" and "present with the Lord," and of departing "to be with Christ" (2 Cor 5; Phil 1). All quotations are KJV.

Claims & evidence

Lazarus in Abraham's bosom, the rich man in torment (Luke 16:22–23)

And it came to pass, that the beggar died, and was carried by the angels into Abraham's bosom: the rich man also died, and was buried; And in hell he lift up his eyes, being in torments, and seeth Abraham afar off, and Lazarus in his bosom.

— Luke 16:22–23 (KJV), historicalchristian.faith. In the parable both men are described after death and before the resurrection: Lazarus carried "into Abraham's bosom," the rich man "in hell" (Gk. Hades) "in torments." Tertullian (De Anima VII) and Hippolytus draw on this parable for their accounts of a divided Hades; Bede reads Abraham's bosom as "the rest of the blessed."

"A great gulf fixed" (Luke 16:26)

And beside all this, between us and you there is a great gulf fixed: so that they which would pass from hence to you cannot; neither can they pass to us, that would come from thence.

— Luke 16:26 (KJV), historicalchristian.faith. In the parable Abraham tells the rich man that a "great gulf" is "fixed" between them, so that none may pass from one side to the other. 1 Enoch 22, earlier on this timeline, also sets the spirits of the righteous and of sinners in separate places; Hippolytus places "a deep and vast abyss" between the two parts of Hades.

"To day shalt thou be with me in paradise" (Luke 23:43)

And Jesus said unto him, Verily I say unto thee, To day shalt thou be with me in paradise.

— Luke 23:43 (KJV), historicalchristian.faith. Christ promises the penitent thief that "to day" he will be with him "in paradise." Origen reads the promise as the reopening of the paradise Adam had closed; N. T. Wright reads "paradise" here as the temporary rest of God's people before the resurrection.

"Absent from the body … present with the Lord" (2 Corinthians 5:8)

We are confident, I say, and willing rather to be absent from the body, and to be present with the Lord.

— 2 Corinthians 5:8 (KJV), historicalchristian.faith. Paul sets being "absent from the body" beside being "present with the Lord," and says he would "rather" be so. The Westminster Confession (1646) teaches, in its own words, that at death the souls of men "immediately return to God who gave them."

"To depart, and to be with Christ" (Philippians 1:23)

For I am in a strait betwixt two, having a desire to depart, and to be with Christ; which is far better.

— Philippians 1:23 (KJV), historicalchristian.faith. Paul sets the desire "to depart" beside being "with Christ," which he calls "far better" than remaining in the flesh. N. T. Wright describes the Christian departed as consciously "with Christ" while they await the resurrection.

Context

  • The passages above are brief and occasional, and the New Testament gives no systematic description of Hades. Later witnesses on this timeline cite them in fuller accounts of the realm of the dead (Abraham's bosom, the place of torment, the gulf, the descent): see Tertullian, Hippolytus and Thomas Aquinas.
  • 1 Enoch 22, the earliest-dated text on this timeline, also divides the dead into separate places for the righteous and for sinners.
