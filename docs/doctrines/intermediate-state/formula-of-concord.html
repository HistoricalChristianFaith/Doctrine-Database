The Formula of Concord on the Intermediate State (the Descent into Hell)

Dates: 1577 (Solid Declaration; published in the Book of Concord, 1580) · Wikipedia
Summary: The Formula of Concord (1577) is the last of the Lutheran confessional documents, drafted to settle the controversies that divided Lutherans after Luther's death. Its ninth article, "Christ's Descent into Hell," directs the churches to Luther's 1533 Torgau sermon and confesses that the whole person of Christ, God and man, descended after his burial and conquered the devil and destroyed the power of hell. It declines to settle how this happened — that question is to be reserved "until the other world" — and so adopts in confessional form both the victorious descent and the refusal to speculate found in Luther's sermon.

Claims & evidence

The descent of the whole Christ after burial

we simply believe that the entire person, God and man, after the burial descended into hell, conquered the devil, destroyed the power of hell, and took from the devil all his might

— Solid Declaration, Art. IX (Of the Descent of Christ to Hell), bookofconcord.org. The confession distinguishes "the burial and descent of Christ to hell … as different articles," and confesses the descent as an act of "the entire person, God and man," whose content is conquest: the defeat of the devil and the breaking of hell's power. (Calvin, on this timeline, reads the article instead as Christ's soul bearing divine judgment.)

The article's appeal to Luther's 1533 Torgau sermon

we abide … by the simplicity of our Christian faith, whereof Dr. Luther has spoken in his sermon, delivered in the castle at Torgau in the year 1533, where he directs us to this article of the Apostles' Creed

— Solid Declaration, Art. IX, bookofconcord.org. Faced with "dissimilar explanations of the article … found" among "the ancient Christian teachers of the Church, as well as in some among our teachers," the Formula does not adjudicate between them but refers the churches to Luther's Torgau sermon as their guide.

The manner of the descent reserved for the next world

But how this occurred we should … reserve until the other world, where not only this point, but also still others will be revealed

— Solid Declaration, Art. IX, bookofconcord.org. The companion Epitome Art. IX (bookofconcord.org) urges the same restraint — "there should be no disputation concerning it, but … it should be believed and taught only in the simplest manner" — because the article "cannot be comprehended by the senses or by our reason, but must be grasped by faith alone." What the believer needs to know is the benefit: that Christ "destroyed hell for all believers, and delivered them from the power of death and of the devil."

Context and placement

  • The Epitome (Art. IX, bookofconcord.org) names the questions disputed among theologians who subscribed to the Augsburg Confession: when and in what manner Christ descended, whether before or after his death, whether according to the soul alone, the divinity alone, or body and soul, and whether the article belongs to Christ's passion or to his "glorious victory and triumph." The Formula leaves these open and confesses that Christ descended, destroyed hell for all believers, and delivered them, referring the churches to Luther's 1533 sermon.
  • The timeline also carries Calvin's reading of the article (Christ's soul bearing divine judgment) and Aquinas's (the descent to the limbus patrum). The Formula retains the creed's "he descended into hell" and devotes an article to it; whether the clause should be retained is the subject of a disputed-reading page on the creed's descent clause, where Grudem and Piper argue for dropping it.
  • The Solid Declaration is the fuller of the Formula's two parts; the briefer Epitome states the same doctrine. Both were adopted in 1577 and printed in the Book of Concord (1580). English quotations here follow the standard Triglot Concordia rendering.
