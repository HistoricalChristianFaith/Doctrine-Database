Ambrose of Milan on the Intermediate State

Dates: c. 380 · Wikipedia
Summary: The early Latin Easter office hymn Aurora Lucis Rutilat ("Heaven with rosy morn is glowing"), traditionally ascribed to Ambrose, sings the harrowing of hell: as the risen Christ rises "from the deep sepulchral night," "Hell is writhing in despair," and he "leads His saints to life and light." The hymn places the descent and the liberation of the dead in Latin Easter worship. (The ascription to Ambrose himself is traditional; see "Attribution" below.)

Claims & evidence

The risen Christ and the despair of hell in the Easter hymn

Heaven with rosy morn is glowing,
Songs of triumph fill the air,
Strains of praise from earth are flowing,
Hell is writhing in despair.

Earth's great king, in glory springing,
From the deep sepulchral night,
While loud anthems round are ringing,
Leads His saints to life and light.

— Aurora Lucis Rutilat ("Heaven with rosy morn is glowing"), tr. John Williams, hymnary.org. The hymn sets Easter as a contest of realms: as heaven and earth break into triumph, the underworld below recoils — "Hell is writhing in despair." The risen "Earth's great king" rises "from the deep sepulchral night" and leads "His saints" out "to life and light." The descent and the liberation of the dead (the harrowing of hell) are here sung in Easter worship rather than argued.

Attribution and placement

  • Attribution. The ascription of Aurora Lucis Rutilat to Ambrose is traditional. The Cyber Hymnal entry reproduced on hymnary.org lists the English text under "Ambrose of Milan, 340-397; John Williams," while hymnary.org's own record lists only "J. Williams." That the Latin is an anonymous early-medieval office hymn associated with the Ambrosian or early Milanese tradition, rather than Ambrose's own composition, is stated here ⚠ unverified. It is slotted under Ambrose at c. 380 by the traditional ascription; read on that basis, it attests the early Latin liturgical tradition rather than a documented position of Ambrose himself.
  • This is a worship / hymn witness, not a treatise: the descent and the liberation of the dead appear in Easter liturgy, sung rather than argued.
  • The English is a 19th-century text by John Williams, given on hymnary.org with the source Ancient Hymns of the Holy Church (Hartford, Conn., 1845); the underlying Latin is the older text.
