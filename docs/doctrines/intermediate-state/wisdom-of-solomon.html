The Wisdom of Solomon on the Intermediate State

Dates: c. 50 BC · Wikipedia
Summary: Wisdom 3:1–4, a Greek-language Jewish wisdom book from Alexandria (roughly contemporary with Philo), speaks of a peaceful post-mortem rest for the righteous: "the souls of the righteous are in the hand of God, and there shall no torment touch them"; though "in the sight of the unwise they seemed to die," in truth "they are in peace," "their hope full of immortality." On the book's account their death is only apparent, and they await their reward (Wisdom 3:5).

Claims & evidence

"The souls of the righteous are in the hand of God" (3:1)

But the souls of the righteous are in the hand of God, and there shall no torment touch them.

— Wisdom 3:1 (KJV/Apocrypha), Wikisource. The righteous dead are held "in the hand of God," and "no torment" touches them.

"They seemed to die … but they are in peace" (3:2–3)

In the sight of the unwise they seemed to die: and their departure is taken for misery, And their going from us to be utter destruction: but they are in peace.

— Wisdom 3:2–3 (KJV/Apocrypha), Wikisource. The text sets the view that the departure of the righteous is "utter destruction," which it ascribes to "the sight of the unwise," against its own statement that "they are in peace."

"Their hope full of immortality" (3:4)

For though they be punished in the sight of men, yet is their hope full of immortality.

— Wisdom 3:4 (KJV/Apocrypha), Wikisource. The peace of the righteous is joined to a hope "full of immortality," and the next verse speaks of a reward still to come (Wisdom 3:5, "they shall be greatly rewarded").

Context

  • Wisdom's "they are in peace" stands on this timeline beside the New Testament's "with Christ" / "in paradise" (see The New Testament).
  • The same book supplies the giants / cult-of-the-dead data on the Nephilim timeline; see The Wisdom of Solomon on the Giants and the Cult of the Dead. Like 2 Maccabees it is deuterocanonical: received in the Catholic and Eastern Orthodox canons, outside the Jewish canon, and classed among the Apocrypha by Protestants.
