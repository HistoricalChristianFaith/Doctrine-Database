Polycarp of Smyrna on the Intermediate State

Dates: c. 125 · Wikipedia
Summary: Polycarp of Smyrna (c. 69–155), bishop of Smyrna, whom Irenaeus remembered speaking "of his familiar intercourse with John," confesses in his letter to the Philippians that God raised Christ "having loosed the bands of the grave." The phrase quotes Acts 2:24; Polycarp's Greek reads "the pangs of Hades," a reading also found in some witnesses to the text of Acts.

Claims & evidence

God raised Christ, "having loosed the bands of the grave"

… who for our sins suffered even unto death, [but] "whom God raised from the dead, having loosed the bands of the grave."

— Epistle to the Philippians, ch. 1, historicalchristian.faith. Polycarp opens his letter by praising the Philippians' faith, which "bringeth forth fruit to our Lord Jesus Christ, who for our sins suffered even unto death" and whom God raised again. The clause "having loosed the bands of the grave" quotes Acts 2:24 (Peter's Pentecost sermon). The ANF translators note here, "Literally, 'having loosed the pains of Hades'"; the Greek of Polycarp's letter reads λύσας τὰς ὠδῖνας τοῦ ᾅδου, "having loosed the pangs of Hades" (Greek text, greekdoc.com). The text of Acts 2:24 itself varies at this word: the apparatus printed with Bengel's Gnomon lists the codices A, B, C and E for "of death" (θανάτου, the KJV's "having loosed the pains of death") and the witnesses D, the Vulgate, the Memphitic (Coptic) and the Syriac for "of Hades" (ᾅδου), with Irenaeus's Latin inferorum (biblehub.com).

Context

  • Polycarp gives no developed account of the soul between death and resurrection; his witness here is one phrase, quoting Acts 2:24 in its "Hades" form. The reading speaks of Christ loosed from the pangs of Hades; the letter does not go on to describe the descent itself.
  • He does, in the same letter (ch. 9), locate the martyred dead "in their due place in the presence of the Lord, with whom also they suffered" — a passing note that the faithful departed are already with Christ, without a worked-out scheme of the interim state.
  • Polycarp was a contemporary and correspondent of Ignatius of Antioch (Polycarp forwarded Ignatius's letters). Irenaeus's recollection of Polycarp is in his letter to Florinus (historicalchristian.faith).
