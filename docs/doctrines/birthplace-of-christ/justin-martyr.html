Justin Martyr on the birthplace of Christ

Dates: c. 160 · Wikipedia
Summary: Justin, a native of Palestine writing his Dialogue with Trypho around 160, reports the birth in “a certain cave near the village” of Bethlehem as an established fact, grounds it in Isaiah’s “lofty cave of the strong rock,” and contrasts it with the cave in which initiates of Mithras were received — the earliest named father to attest the cave tradition.

Claims & evidence

The child was born in a cave near Bethlehem, and laid in a manger there

But when the Child was born in Bethlehem, since Joseph could not find a lodging in that village, he took up his quarters in a certain cave near the village; and while they were there Mary brought forth the Christ and placed Him in a manger, and here the Magi who came from Arabia found Him.

— Dialogue with Trypho 78, source. Justin gives the cave matter-of-factly, as something known rather than argued — he was born at Flavia Neapolis (Shechem), some forty miles from Bethlehem, and writes within about a century and a quarter of the birth. The manger sits inside the cave; there is no stable and no inn, only a “lodging” the family could not obtain.

The cave was foreshadowed by Isaiah’s “lofty cave of the strong rock”

… he shall dwell in the lofty cave of the strong rock. … I have repeated to you what Isaiah foretold about the sign which foreshadowed the cave.

— Dialogue with Trypho 70 and 78, source (quoting Isaiah 33:16 in the Septuagint). Justin reads the cave out of prophecy, which is a double-edged detail: it shows the cave was already the received setting he had to account for, but it also raises the question — queued as the cave-authentic-memory argument — whether the prophecy generated the cave or merely confirmed a site already remembered.

The Mithras “cave” is a demonic counterfeit of the true one

And when those who record the mysteries of Mithras say that he was begotten of a rock, and call the place where those who believe in him are initiated a cave …

— Dialogue with Trypho 70, source. Justin treats the Mithraic cave as the devil’s imitation of the prophecies about Christ — not as their source. Modern writers sometimes reverse the arrow and argue the Christian cave was borrowed from Mithraism; that this earliest witness runs the influence the other way is a datum for the queued mithras-cave-parallel argument.

Notes

  • Justin also appears on this wiki as a witness on the magi timeline (he places the magi in Arabia). The cave passage and the magi both sit in Dialogue with Trypho 77–78.
  • His cave is the crucial hinge between the apocryphal Protoevangelium of James and the later shown-and-venerated grotto of Origen, Eusebius, and Jerome (queued witnesses): with Justin the cave is no longer only a story-setting but a claim about a real place near Bethlehem.
