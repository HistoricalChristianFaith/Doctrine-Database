Origen on the birthplace of Christ

Dates: c. 248 · Wikipedia
Summary: Origen, writing Against Celsus around 248 while living at Caesarea in Palestine, is the first witness to treat the Bethlehem cave not merely as the setting of a story but as a place publicly shown to visitors — a site so well known, he says, that it is talked of even among the enemies of the faith. His notice predates Constantine’s basilica over the grotto by some eighty years.

Claims & evidence

The cave and the manger are shown at Bethlehem, and the site is talked of even by the faith’s enemies

With respect to the birth of Jesus in Bethlehem, if any one desires, after the prophecy of Micah and after the history recorded in the Gospels by the disciples of Jesus, to have additional evidence from other sources, let him know that, in conformity with the narrative in the Gospel regarding His birth, there is shown at Bethlehem the cave where He was born, and the manger in the cave where He was wrapped in swaddling-clothes. And this sight is greatly talked of in surrounding places, even among the enemies of the faith, it being said that in this cave was born that Jesus who is worshipped and reverenced by the Christians.

— Against Celsus 1.51, source. Origen offers the shown cave as evidence — corroboration of the Gospel narrative that a skeptic could go and verify — not merely as pious lore. The wording marks two firsts: the cave is now a place pointed out to visitors (a shrine, not just a scene), and its fame reaches beyond the church to non-believers “in surrounding places.” The manger sits inside the cave, exactly as in Justin; there is still no stable and no inn.

Bethlehem is grounded on Micah, not Isaiah’s “cave”

In the same stretch of argument Origen anchors the birthplace on Micah 5:2 — “And thou Bethlehem, house of Ephrata, art not the least among the thousands of Judah… out of thee shall He come forth… who is to be Ruler in Israel” — and on the Gospel history, saying the prophecy “could not suit any one of those who… were fanatics and mob-leaders… unless it were clearly shown that He had been born in Bethlehem.”1 Notably he does not reach for Isaiah 33:16 (the “lofty cave of the strong rock”) to justify the cave, as Justin had. Origen’s cave rests on a claim about a visible place, not on a prophetic proof-text — a datum for the queued cave-authentic-memory and isaiah-cave-lxx arguments, since it shows the site being attested independently of the Isaiah reading that Justin used to explain it.

Notes

  • Origen appears on this wiki also as a witness on the magi timeline and elsewhere; he wrote Against Celsus at Caesarea, roughly forty miles from Bethlehem, and could speak of the site as something a reader might go and see.
  • A cave shown to visitors c. 248 predates Constantine and Helena’s Church of the Nativity over the grotto (c. 327–339) by about eighty years — a point that bears on the queued cave-authentic-memory and hadrian-grove arguments (whether the pointed-out site preserves an authentic local memory, and whether Hadrian’s pagan grove over it inadvertently marked the spot).
  • With Origen the tradition moves decisively from the Protoevangelium and Justin’s narrative cave to a venerated place: the next witnesses (Eusebius, then the basilica) describe locals showing the grotto and, soon after, a church built over it.
