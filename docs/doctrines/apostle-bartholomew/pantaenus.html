Pantaenus: Bartholomew’s Gospel in “India”

Dates: c. 180 (Pantaenus’s mission), reported by Eusebius c. 325 · Eusebius, Church History 5.10
Summary: The earliest extant notice of a mission by Bartholomew. Pantaenus, head of the catechetical school of Alexandria, is said to have travelled “as far as India” about the year 180 and to have found Christians there already holding a Gospel of Matthew in Hebrew — left with them, they said, by the apostle Bartholomew. Eusebius relays it as report, and Jerome repeats it. It is earlier than the extant sources that place Bartholomew in Armenia or report his death.

Claims & evidence

Pantaenus sent “as far as India”

Eusebius introduces Pantaenus as a former Stoic turned Christian teacher, so zealous for the gospel that he was made a missionary to the East.

He displayed such zeal for the divine Word, that he was appointed as a herald of the Gospel of Christ to the nations in the East, and was sent as far as India.

— Eusebius, Ecclesiastical History 5.10, 2.

The Hebrew Matthew that Bartholomew left

There, Eusebius reports, Pantaenus found a Hebrew copy of Matthew’s Gospel, said to have been brought by Bartholomew and kept by the Christians there.

Bartholomew, one of the apostles, had preached to them, and left with them the writing of Matthew in the Hebrew language, which they had preserved till that time.

— Eusebius, Ecclesiastical History 5.10, 3. Eusebius gives the whole as what “is reported” and “they say,” not as his own certain knowledge.

Jerome repeats it

Two centuries later Jerome, drawing on Eusebius in his life of Pantaenus, tells the same story — that in India Pantaenus found the Bartholomew tradition and carried the Hebrew Matthew back to Alexandria.

he found that Bartholomew, one of the twelve apostles, had preached the advent of the Lord Jesus according to the gospel of Matthew, and on his return to Alexandria he brought this with him written in Hebrew characters.

— Jerome, On Illustrious Men (De Viris Illustribus) 36.

Reception and debate

  • What “India” meant. J. F. Fenlon (Catholic Encyclopedia, 1907) notes that “‘India’ was a name covering a very wide area, including even Arabia Felix.” Harnack, Medlycott and Mingana, among others listed by the Nasrani.net survey, place Pantaenus’s “India” in Arabia Felix or Ethiopia; A. C. Perumalil and G. M. Moraes, as the same survey summarises them, on the Konkan coast of the subcontinent; their readings are set out on the disputed-reading page.
  • A proposed confusion of names. “Some writers,” according to the Nasrani.net survey, which names none of them, have suggested that Pantaenus, meeting the Thomas Christians of the East, heard their “Mar Thoma” (Saint Thomas) as “Bar Tolmai” (Bartholomew).
  • Placement on the timeline. This is the earliest extant tradition to attach a mission to Bartholomew, earlier than the extant Armenian tradition and the accounts of his flaying. On the early fathers as sources for the apostles, see the apostles hub.
