The apostle-lists: Armenia and a martyr’s death

Dates: uncertain (the apostle-list literature, c. 3rd–8th centuries) · Pseudo-Hippolytus, On the Twelve Apostles
Summary: The sources that give Bartholomew a place of death. The family of related catalogues — Pseudo-Hippolytus, Pseudo-Dorotheus, the Byzantine and Latin lists — keeps the “India” of Pantaenus and adds Armenia, a martyrdom, and a burial place. They are of uncertain date, related to one another (⚠ unverified), and differ on how he died.

Claims & evidence

Pseudo-Hippolytus: India, Armenia, and crucifixion head-downward

The catalogue that goes under the name of Hippolytus joins the eastern mission of Pantaenus’s report to a death and a grave in Armenia, and gives the martyrdom as a crucifixion.

Bartholomew, again, preached to the Indians, to whom he also gave the Gospel according to Matthew, and was crucified with his head downward, and was buried in Allanum, a town of the great Armenia.

— Pseudo-Hippolytus, On the Twelve Apostles, 6 (ANF vol. 5). The place-name appears variously as Allanum, Alban, or Albanopolis of Armenia Major.

The wider catalogue tradition

The same field and death recur, with variations, across the related lists — Pseudo-Dorotheus of Tyre, the Byzantine Chronicon Paschale, the Latin Breviarium Apostolorum, and their kin. Pseudo-Hippolytus gives crucifixion, as does Dorotheus according to the Golden Legend, which cites Theoderus for the flaying and “many books” for beheading alone. In these lists Armenia is the country of his death and Albanopolis the place named. The Armenian Apostolic Church honours Bartholomew, with Thaddaeus, as its patron saint.

Reception and debate

  • Date and relationship of the lists. These lists are of uncertain authorship and date and draw on one another (⚠ unverified). On the whole class, see the apostles hub.
  • Variant accounts of his death. In this layer the manner of death is given as crucifixion, flaying, or beheading. The Golden Legend (c. 1260) sets out these variants and combines them. The readings are set out on the disputed-reading page on his death.
  • India and Armenia. The lists hold both together — a mission to “India” and a death in Armenia — without describing a route between them. The Armenian tradition of Bartholomew, with Thaddaeus, as an apostle of Armenia is also attributed to Movses Khorenatsi (5th c.) ⚠ unverified. The readings of the two places are set out on the disputed-reading page on his mission.
