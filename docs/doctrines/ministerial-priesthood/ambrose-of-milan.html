Ambrose of Milan on the Ministerial Priesthood

Dates: c. 390 AD (On the Mysteries and On the Sacraments, catechetical addresses to the newly baptized) · Ambrose, c. 339 – 397; bishop of Milan from 374 · Wikipedia
Summary: Instructing the newly baptized, he points them to the threefold ministry they have just seen — "the deacon… the priest… the chief priest [i.e. the bishop]" — and grounds the consecration not in the minister's own words but in Christ's: "that sacrament which you receive is made what it is by the word of Christ," so that "when it comes to performing a venerable sacrament, then the priest uses not his own expressions, but he uses the expressions of Christ." In Ambrose's account the consecration is effected by Christ's words of institution, which the priest speaks over the elements.

Claims & evidence

The threefold ministry at the altar (On the Mysteries 2.6)

Reminding the newly baptized of what they beheld at the font and the altar, Ambrose names the three orders in ascending rank, bidding them look past the men to the grace of the mysteries:

You saw there the deacon, you saw the priest, you saw the chief priest [i.e. the bishop]. Consider not the bodily forms, but the grace of the Mysteries. You spoke in the presence of the angels, as it is written: "For the priest's lips keep knowledge, and they seek the law at his mouth, for he is the angel of the Lord Almighty."

— On the Mysteries, ch. 2, § 6, source (Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, ser. 2, vol. 10; cf. New Advent).1 Ambrose names the graded ministry — deacon, priest, bishop — as the order the initiate has just witnessed, and applies to the priest Malachi 2:7, "the priest's lips keep knowledge." He bids the newly baptized look past the ministers to "the grace of the Mysteries."

The consecration is by the word of Christ, spoken by the priest (On the Mysteries 9.52–54; On the Sacraments 4)

The heart of Ambrose's eucharistic teaching is that the elements are changed not by any natural process nor by the minister's own authority, but by the priest uttering Christ's own words of institution over them:

…what are we to say of that divine consecration where the very words of the Lord and Saviour operate? For that sacrament which you receive is made what it is by the word of Christ. … Before the blessing of the heavenly words another nature is spoken of, after the consecration the Body is signified.

— On the Mysteries, ch. 9, §§ 52, 54, source (NPNF ser. 2, vol. 10; cf. New Advent).2 In the parallel catechesis On the Sacraments he says: at the moment of consecration "the priest uses not his own expressions, but he uses the expressions of Christ. Thus the expression of Christ performs this sacrament" (IV.4.14). In these catecheses the priest consecrates by speaking Christ's institution; the words that perform the sacrament are Christ's. Thomas Aquinas (c. 1274) quotes this passage of On the Sacraments 4 when treating the form of the sacrament.3

Notes

  • The passages together. Ambrose names the threefold ministry as the order of the Milanese church and places the eucharistic change in Christ's words of institution, spoken by the priest over the elements.
  • Neighbouring witnesses. Cyprian (c. 253) wrote that the priest "discharges the office of Christ"; Ambrose speaks of the priest using "the words of Christ." Aquinas's Summa (III, Q. 78, a. 1) quotes On the Sacraments 4 on the form of the consecration.3
  • Overlap with the Real Presence. Ambrose's change-of-nature language ("Before the blessing… another nature… after the consecration the Body") is quoted here only for the ministerial point — that the change comes by the priest's speaking Christ's words — and it is also cited on the Real Presence page.
