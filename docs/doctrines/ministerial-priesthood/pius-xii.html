Pius XII on the Ministerial Priesthood

Dates: 20 November 1947 (the encyclical Mediator Dei, on the sacred liturgy) · Eugenio Pacelli, Pope Pius XII, 1876–1958; reigned 1939–1958 · Wikipedia
Summary: In Mediator Dei Pius XII teaches that the ordained priest offers the Eucharistic sacrifice "in the person of Christ" (in persona Christi), because his minister "represents" the sacred Person of Jesus Christ and, by his consecration, "possesses the power of performing actions in virtue of Christ's very person." Pius holds that though the faithful truly "offer" the sacrifice in their own way, they do not thereby possess priestly power: "Only to the apostles, and thenceforth to those on whom their successors have imposed hands, is granted the power of the priesthood." Vatican II (1964) later stated that the two priesthoods differ "in essence and not only in degree."

Claims & evidence

The priest offers in the person of Christ (Mediator Dei 69)

Explaining why the Mass is the same sacrifice as Calvary, Pius locates the identity in the priest, who at the altar acts not in his own name but in Christ's:

The priest is the same, Jesus Christ, whose sacred Person his minister represents. Now the minister, by reason of the sacerdotal consecration which he has received, is made like to the High Priest and possesses the power of performing actions in virtue of Christ's very person.

— Mediator Dei (1947), § 69, source.1 In the encyclical the consecration is Christ's act, performed through a minister who "represents" his Person. Earlier statements on this timeline include Cyprian's "that priest truly discharges the office of Christ" and Ambrose's "the priest… uses the words of Christ."

The power of the priesthood and the imposition of hands (Mediator Dei 40)

Pius grounds that power in the apostolic succession; Clement of Rome and Irenaeus are the earliest appeals to succession on this timeline:

Only to the apostles, and thenceforth to those on whom their successors have imposed hands, is granted the power of the priesthood, in virtue of which they represent the person of Jesus Christ before their people, acting at the same time as representatives of their people before God.

— Mediator Dei (1947), § 40, source.2 The priest is here a double representative — of Christ before the people, and of the people before God.

The ministerial priesthood distinguished from the common priesthood of the faithful (Mediator Dei 43)

The faithful "offer" the sacrifice, Pius teaches, but not by a priestly power of their own; ordination sets the priest apart:

In the same way, actually that baptism is the distinctive mark of all Christians, and serves to differentiate them from those who have not been cleansed in this purifying stream and consequently are not members of Christ, the sacrament of holy orders sets the priest apart from the rest of the faithful who have not received this consecration.

— Mediator Dei (1947), § 43, source.3 Leo the Great (c. 450) had spoken in one sentence of the whole people "consecrated priests by the oil of the Holy Spirit" and of "the special service of our ministry as priests."

Notes

  • The encyclical's terms. Mediator Dei gives the priest's acting "in virtue of Christ's very person" as the reason the power to offer belongs to the ordained; Aquinas (c. 1274) had spoken of the consecration as performed "in the person of Christ."
  • The common priesthood. Pius affirms that the faithful share in Christ's priesthood and "offer" the sacrifice, and teaches that this does not give them priestly power, so that the ordained and the baptized "offer" in two different modes.
  • Provenance. Mediator Dei is quoted from the official English translation published by the Vatican; the section numbering follows that edition.
  • Elsewhere on the site. The same encyclical’s paragraphs on the liturgy and the faith (§§46–48), where Pius reverses the adage lex orandi, lex credendi, are set out on the lex orandi timeline.
