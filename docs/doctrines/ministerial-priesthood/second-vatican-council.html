The Second Vatican Council on the Ministerial Priesthood

Dates: 21 November 1964 (the dogmatic constitution Lumen Gentium, on the Church, promulgated by Pope Paul VI at the twenty-first ecumenical council) · Wikipedia
Summary: In Lumen Gentium 10 the council affirms the common priesthood of all the baptized — "a holy priesthood" who "offer spiritual sacrifices" — and then distinguishes it from the ordained ministry not by degree but by kind: "Though they differ from one another in essence and not only in degree (essentia et non gradu tantum), the common priesthood of the faithful and the ministerial or hierarchical priesthood are nonetheless interrelated." The ministerial priest "acting in the person of Christ… makes present the Eucharistic sacrifice, and offers it to God in the name of all the people."

Claims & evidence

The common priesthood of all the baptized (Lumen Gentium 10)

Before distinguishing the ordained ministry, the council states the priesthood of all the baptized:

Christ the Lord, High Priest taken from among men, made the new people "a kingdom and priests to God the Father." The baptized, by regeneration and the anointing of the Holy Spirit, are consecrated as a spiritual house and a holy priesthood, in order that through all those works which are those of the Christian man they may offer spiritual sacrifices and proclaim the power of Him who has called them out of darkness into His marvelous light.

— Lumen Gentium (1964), § 10, source.1 Earlier statements of a corporate priesthood on this timeline include Justin ("the true high priestly race of God"), Irenaeus ("all the righteous possess the sacerdotal rank"), and Leo the Great.

The two priesthoods differ "in essence and not only in degree" (Lumen Gentium 10)

The council then draws the distinction:

Though they differ from one another in essence and not only in degree, the common priesthood of the faithful and the ministerial or hierarchical priesthood are nonetheless interrelated: each of them in its own special way is a participation in the one priesthood of Christ. The ministerial priest, by the sacred power he enjoys, teaches and rules the priestly people; acting in the person of Christ, he makes present the Eucharistic sacrifice, and offers it to God in the name of all the people. But the faithful, in virtue of their royal priesthood, join in the offering of the Eucharist.

— Lumen Gentium (1964), § 10, source.2 With the phrase essentia et non gradu tantum the council distinguishes the ministerial priesthood from the common priesthood in kind, not only in degree; the ministerial priest acts "in the person of Christ" to make the sacrifice present. Pius XII's Mediator Dei (1947) had used the same in persona Christi language.

Notes

  • The article together. Lumen Gentium 10 states, in one article, the common priesthood of the baptized and the ministerial priesthood, which it says differs "in essence"; the ministerial priest offers the Eucharist in persona Christi in the name of all, and the faithful "join in the offering."
  • Earlier Catholic texts. Trent (1563) had defined a visible priesthood with the power to consecrate and offer; Pius XII (1947) had distinguished the ministerial from the common priesthood. Lumen Gentium speaks of both as participations in Christ's one priesthood.
  • Provenance. Lumen Gentium is quoted from the official English translation published by the Vatican; the Latin essentia et non gradu tantum is from the promulgated Latin text.
  • Elsewhere on the site. The council’s Constitution on the Sacred Liturgy, which calls the liturgy “summit” and “font” and ordered the reform of the Roman rite, is set out on the lex orandi timeline.
