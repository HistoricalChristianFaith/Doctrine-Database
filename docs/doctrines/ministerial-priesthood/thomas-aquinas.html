Thomas Aquinas on the Ministerial Priesthood

Dates: c. 1274 AD (the Summa Theologiae, Third Part, left unfinished at his death) · Thomas Aquinas, c. 1225 – 1274; Dominican friar and scholastic theologian · Wikipedia
Summary: Asking "whether the consecration of this sacrament belongs to a priest alone," he reasons that because the Eucharist "is performed only as in the person of Christ," and "whoever performs any act in another's stead, must do so by the power bestowed by such a one," the priest can consecrate only because "the power of consecrating this sacrament on Christ's behalf is bestowed upon the priest at his ordination" — the very point at which he "is put upon a level with them to whom the Lord said… 'Do this for a commemoration of Me'" (Luke 22:19). He concludes that "it belongs to priests to accomplish this sacrament."

Claims & evidence

"Whether the consecration of this sacrament belongs to a priest alone" (Summa Theologiae III, Q. 82, a. 1)

In the Third Part of the Summa, treating "the minister of this sacrament," Aquinas asks whether the consecration belongs to a priest alone. He answers from the principle that the Eucharist is performed in the person of Christ, and that to act in another's stead one must hold a power bestowed for it:

As stated above, such is the dignity of this sacrament that it is performed only as in the person of Christ. Now whoever performs any act in another's stead, must do so by the power bestowed by such a one.

— Summa Theologiae III, Q. 82, a. 1, source (Fathers of the English Dominican Province translation).1 From this he draws the conclusion that the lay believer is given the power to receive the sacrament in baptism, but the power to consecrate it is given only at ordination:

But as the power of receiving this sacrament is conceded by Christ to the baptized person, so likewise the power of consecrating this sacrament on Christ's behalf is bestowed upon the priest at his ordination: for thereby he is put upon a level with them to whom the Lord said (Luke 22:19): "Do this for a commemoration of Me."

— Summa Theologiae III, Q. 82, a. 1, source.2 The argument rests on the Last-Supper command: the priest may consecrate because ordination places him among those to whom Christ said "Do this" (Luke 22:19), a charge Aquinas reads as committed to the apostles and, through them, to the ordained. He concludes:

Therefore, it must be said that it belongs to priests to accomplish this sacrament.

— Summa Theologiae III, Q. 82, a. 1, source.3

Notes

  • The article's argument. In Aquinas's article the consecration is an act done in persona Christi by a power that Christ confers on the priest at ordination, so that the reservation of the consecration to priests follows, on his account, from what the sacrament is. Earlier on this timeline Cyprian had said the priest "discharges the office of Christ," and Ignatius had tied the proper Eucharist to the bishop.
  • The Luke 22 text. Aquinas rests the priest's power on the "do this in remembrance of me" command (Luke 22:19), read as addressed to the apostles and transmitted by ordination. Paul gives the same command in his account to the Corinthian church (1 Corinthians 11:23–26).
  • Provenance. The Summa Theologiae is quoted from the Fathers of the English Dominican Province translation as published online.
