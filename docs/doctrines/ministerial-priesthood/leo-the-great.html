Leo the Great on the Ministerial Priesthood

Dates: c. 450 AD (the teaching spans his pontificate, 440–461 — the anniversary Sermons 1–4, preached on the day of his consecration in the early 440s, and the Holy-Week Sermon 59 on the Passion; Leo c. 400–461) · Wikipedia
Summary: Preaching year by year on the anniversary of his own consecration, Pope Leo I describes the priesthood as an office which the NPNF translation's heading calls "handed on in perpetuity" from Christ — the eternal High Priest "after the order of Melchizedeck" — through Peter and the ministry, so that it is "not the prerogative of earthly origin" but "the condescension of Divine grace which creates the bishop." Leo holds this together with a statement of the corporate priesthood: all the regenerate are "a royal race and… sharers in the office of the priesthood" — and in the same sentence speaks of "the special service of our ministry as priests." In his Passion preaching "the one offering of… Body and Blood" has replaced "the variety of fleshly sacrifices," and the ministers are "priests of holier anointing."

Claims & evidence

"The condescension of Divine grace which creates the bishop" (Sermon 3)

Leo's anniversary sermon on his elevation to the papacy presents the ministry as a succession from Christ the eternal priest. Reading "Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedeck" of Christ — "in whom was prefigured the eternal High Priest" — he presses that "now that the mystery of this Divine priesthood has descended to human agency, it runs not by the line of birth," but is given to "those men… whom the Holy Ghost prepares":1

…now that the mystery of this Divine priesthood has descended to human agency, it runs not by the line of birth, nor is that which flesh and blood created, chosen, but without regard to the privilege of paternity and succession by inheritance, those men are received by the Church as its rulers whom the Holy Ghost prepares: so that in the people of God's adoption, the whole body of which is priestly and royal, it is not the prerogative of earthly origin which obtains the unction, but the condescension of Divine grace which creates the bishop.

The section in the NPNF translation is headed "From Christ and through S. Peter the priesthood is handed on in perpetuity." Clement and Irenaeus had appealed to an apostolic succession; Leo speaks of the succession through Peter, whose see he holds, and of the bishop as created by "the condescension of Divine grace," not by "the prerogative of earthly origin."

A royal and priestly people and "the special service of our ministry as priests" (Sermon 4)

Preaching again on his consecration day, Leo tells the whole congregation that their baptism has made them royal and priestly, and in the same sentence speaks of "the special service of our ministry as priests":2

For all, regenerated in Christ, are made kings by the sign of the cross; they are consecrated priests by the oil of the Holy Spirit, so that beyond the special service of our ministry as priests, all spiritual and mature Christians know that they are a royal race and are sharers in the office of the priesthood.

The sentence names both the priesthood all the regenerate share and "the special service of our ministry as priests." Justin, Irenaeus, Tertullian, and Augustine likewise speak of the priesthood of the whole people alongside an ordered ministry.

"The one offering of Thy Body and Blood" and "priests of holier anointing" (Sermon 59)

In his Holy-Week preaching on the Passion, where the cross is called "the fount of all blessings," Leo contrasts the old rites with the new:3

…what before was done in the one temple of the Jews in dark signs, was now to be celebrated everywhere by the piety of all the nations in full and open rite. For now there is a nobler rank of Levites, there are elders of greater dignity and priests of holier anointing… Now, too, the variety of fleshly sacrifices has ceased, and the one offering of Thy Body and Blood fulfils all those different victims: for Thou art the true "Lamb of God, that takest away the sins of the world," and in Thyself so accomplishest all mysteries, that as there is but one sacrifice instead of many victims, so there is but one kingdom instead of many nations.

Leo speaks of a fulfilled sacrificial order: the many victims have ceased, "the one offering" of the Lord's Body and Blood is celebrated "everywhere by the piety of all the nations," and there is "a nobler rank of Levites… priests of holier anointing." The worldwide offering recalls the reading of Malachi found from the Didache onward. The Passion sermons are also relevant to the Real Presence timeline.

Notes

  • Dating. The quotations come from sermons preached across Leo's pontificate (440–461), not from a single datable work: the succession sermon and the "royal race" sermon are among the anniversary sermons on his consecration (traditionally assigned to his early years, the first delivered 29 September 440 and its successors on the following anniversaries), and the "one offering" passage is from a Holy-Week Passion sermon of the same period. The timeline anchors him at a representative c. 450.
  • The two priesthoods. Leo's "royal race… sharers in the office of the priesthood" and his "special service of our ministry as priests" stand in one sentence; the passage is quoted both for the priesthood of all the baptized and for the distinct ministry.
  • Texts used. Sermon 3 (on the anniversary of his elevation) and Sermon 59 (on the Passion) are quoted in the NPNF translation (Feltoe, Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, ser. 2, vol. 12); Sermon 4 (on the anniversary of his consecration, Sermo 4.1 in PL 54) is quoted in the modern translation carried in the Roman Office of Readings. Leo also appears on the intermediate state and astrology timelines.
