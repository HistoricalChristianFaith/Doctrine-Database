John of Damascus on the Veneration of the Cross

Dates: c. 675–749 (the Exposition, c. 743) · Wikipedia
Summary: John of Damascus writes on the Cross in the Exposition of the Orthodox Faith IV.11. He holds that the very wood on which Christ died “is to be worshipped,” and that the image of the Cross is worshipped “not honouring the tree… but the image as a symbol of Christ.”

Claims & evidence

The Cross as the most marvellous of Christ's works

“Every action, therefore, and performance of miracles by Christ are most great and divine and marvellous: but the most marvellous of all is His precious Cross. For no other thing has subdued death, expiated the sin of the first parent, despoiled Hades, bestowed the resurrection… save the Cross of our Lord Jesus Christ.”

— An Exposition of the Orthodox Faith IV.11 (NPNF2 vol. 9). John singles out the Cross among Christ's works as that through which death was subdued, sin expiated and the resurrection bestowed.

The wood of the Cross, sanctified by contact with Christ

“So, then, this same truly precious and august tree, on which Christ has offered Himself as a sacrifice for our sakes, is to be worshipped as sanctified by contact with His holy body and blood; likewise the nails, the spear, the clothes… are to be worshipped.”

— Exposition IV.11. John grounds the honour paid the wood in its contact with Christ's body and blood, and extends the same ground to the nails, the spear and the clothes. The Greek verb rendered “is to be worshipped” is proskynēteon, from proskynein (Greek text: “ὡς ἁγιασθὲν τῇ ἁφῇ τοῦ ἁγίου σώματος καὶ αἵματος εἰκότως προσκυνητέον”).

The cross-image, honoured as a symbol of Christ

“Moreover we worship even the image of the precious and life-giving Cross, although made of another tree, not honouring the tree (God forbid) but the image as a symbol of Christ… It behoves us, then, to worship the sign of Christ… But it does not behove us to worship the material of which the image of the Cross is composed, even though it be gold or precious stones, after it is destroyed, if that should happen. Everything, therefore, that is dedicated to God we worship, conferring the adoration on Him.”

— Exposition IV.11. In John's account the image of the Cross is honoured as a sign of Christ, its material is not to be honoured once the image is destroyed, and the adoration is conferred on Christ. He treats images at Exposition IV.16 (“Concerning Images”).

Reception and debate

  • John also appears on this wiki as a witness on image-veneration (the incarnational argument and “the honour given to the image passes over to the prototype”) — see the veneration of images and icons.
  • Calvin (1559) held that the dulia/latria distinction does not keep such honour from being worship; his position is recorded on his page.
