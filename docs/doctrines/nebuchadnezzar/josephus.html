Josephus on Nebuchadnezzar — the great builder

Dates: c. AD 93 (the Antiquities of the Jews 10; Josephus b. c. AD 37, d. c. 100) · Wikipedia
Summary: Josephus retells Daniel at length, reports the king’s seven years in the desert briefly, and adds the external record — the Babylonian historian Berossus on Nebuchadnezzar’s building works, and Megasthenes on his greatness.

Claims & evidence

The reign and death of “an active man”

Now when king Nebuchadnezzar had reigned forty-three years, he ended his life. He was an active man, and more fortunate than the kings that were before him.

— Josephus, Antiquities of the Jews 10.219, trans. William Whiston, source. Josephus’s paraphrase of Daniel runs through the dreams, the great image, and the fiery furnace, and tells the second dream briefly: the king was to “feed among the wild beasts,” live “in the desert for seven years,” and, when the time was past, “he prayed to God that he might recover his Kingdom, and he returned to it” (10.10.6, 10.216–218).1

Berossus and Megasthenes: the walls, the hanging garden, and a king greater than Hercules

He also erected what was called a pensile paradise, because his wife was desirous to have things like her own country, she having been bred up in the palaces of Media… Megasthenes also, in his fourth book of his Accounts of India, makes mention of these things, and thereby endeavors to show that this king exceeded Hercules in fortitude, and in the greatness of his actions.

— Josephus, Antiquities of the Jews 10.225–227, trans. Whiston, source. Josephus quotes Berossus’s “third book of his Chaldaic History” on Nebuchadnezzar’s triple walls and the “pensile paradise” (the “hanging gardens”), and repeats the same material in Against Apion 1.19–20. Josephus’s portrait of the king rests on his conquests and monuments, with Megasthenes’ comparison to Hercules.2

Notes

  • Josephus reports the seven years in the desert in a few sentences of his Daniel paraphrase; the Berossus and Megasthenes passages he quotes on the reign (10.11.1) do not mention it. Whether the account of Daniel 4 concerns an episode of Nebuchadnezzar’s reign is one of the readings on the Nabonidus-tradition interpretation page.
  • Abydenus (via Eusebius, Praeparatio Evangelica 9.41), quoting Megasthenes, reports that Nebuchadnezzar went up to his palace “possessed by some god or other,” prophesying Babylon’s fall to a “Persian mule” before he “had immediately disappeared.” Eusebius quotes the passage because “in the prophecy of Daniel” the king’s boast over Babylon is followed by his catastrophe.3
