Nebuchadnezzar in Jewish tradition — the blasphemer and the interrupted song

Dates: traditions from c. AD 200, redacted in the Babylonian Talmud by c. 500 · Sefaria (topic)
Summary: The Babylonian Talmud makes Nebuchadnezzar a blasphemer — the “king of Babylon” of Isaiah 14 and disciple of Nimrod — and records that had an angel not struck him on the mouth, his praise would have sought to overshadow the Psalms of David.

Claims & evidence

The praise cut short by an angel

Rabbi Yitzḥak says: Molten gold should be poured into the mouth of that wicked person… if an angel had not come and struck him on his mouth to prevent him from continuing his praise, he would have sought to overshadow all the songs and praises that David recited in the book of Psalms.

— Babylonian Talmud, Sanhedrin 92b (William Davidson translation), source. The same passage that calls him “that wicked person” records that his praise (Daniel 3–4) was stopped by an angel before it could overshadow David’s.1

The disciple of Nimrod who said “I will be like the Most High”

The Talmud makes Nebuchadnezzar the boaster of Isaiah 14 — “I will ascend above the heights of the clouds; I will be like the Most High” — and “a disciple in corruption of Nimrod the wicked, who caused the entire world to rebel.” The Divine Voice answers his ambition with the next verse: “Yet you shall be brought down to the netherworld, to the uttermost parts of the pit.” Pesachim 94a–b thus identifies Nebuchadnezzar with the “king of Babylon” of Isaiah 14.2
