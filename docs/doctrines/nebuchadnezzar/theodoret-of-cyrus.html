Theodoret of Cyrus on Nebuchadnezzar — the reason lost, and grace restored

Dates: c. AD 433 (the Commentary on Daniel; Theodoret c. 393–458/466) · Wikipedia
Summary: Theodoret gives the fullest Greek reading of Daniel 4 and lands, against Jerome, on a genuine restoration — the madness a justice fitted to the sin of reaching for heaven, the recovery the work of divine grace.

Claims & evidence

Driven from earth for reaching at heaven

since he was not satisfied with the palace here below, and instead at the same time he insanely hankered after one on high, he was deprived of the one here below; in his wish to snatch heaven he was driven also from earth.

— Theodoret of Cyrus, Commentary on Daniel, on 4:31, trans. Robert C. Hill, shown at historicalchristian.faith, on Daniel 4:31. The punishment is tailored to the crime: bereft of reason, the king “like a wild animal… kept frequenting uninhabited places, filling his stomach with the food of brute beasts.”1

Restored by divine grace, and singing God’s praise

Squalid and dirty, in all likelihood, unkempt and with nails grown long, he washed away the dirt, and thanks to divine grace he was recognized as the person who previously had administered the mighty kingdom.

— Theodoret of Cyrus, Commentary on Daniel, on 4:36, trans. Hill, shown at historicalchristian.faith, on Daniel 4:36. Theodoret puts the closing praise in the king’s own grateful mouth: “I sing the praises of the one who to my advantage brought on me one condition and the other… I know he is the King of heaven and Lord of all… capable of humbling those behaving haughtily.”2

Notes

  • Theodoret stands with Hippolytus and Ephrem on the “genuine restoration” side, against Jerome’s scepticism — see the true-conversion argument.
