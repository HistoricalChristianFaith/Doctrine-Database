Innocent I on the Biblical Canon

Dates: c. 405 (Consulenti tibi, Ep. 6 to Exuperius of Toulouse, dated 20 February 405; Innocent was bishop of Rome 401–417) · Wikipedia
Summary: Answering a query from Exuperius, bishop of Toulouse, Innocent I appends a canon-list that matches the present twenty-seven-book New Testament and the longer Old Testament, with Tobit, Judith, and two books of Maccabees — and then names the books circulating "under the name of Matthias … or of Peter and John … or of Andrew … or of Thomas," which "you should know … are not only to be rejected but also condemned" (non solum repudianda verum etiam … damnanda).

Claims & evidence

The canon-list appended for Exuperius

Which books really are received in the canon, this brief addition shows. These therefore are the things of which you desired to be informed. Five books of Moses, that is, Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, and Deuteronomy, and Joshua the son of Nun, and Judges, and the four books of Kings together with Ruth, sixteen books of the Prophets, five books of Solomon, and the Psalms. Also of the historical books, one book of Job, one of Tobit, one of Esther, one of Judith, two of Maccabees, two of Ezra, two of Chronicles. And of the New Testament: of the Gospels four. Epistles of the apostle Paul fourteen. Epistles of John three. Epistles of Peter two. Epistle of Jude. Epistle of James. Acts of the Apostles. John's Apocalypse.

— Innocent I, Ep. 6 to Exuperius, ch. 7, trans. B. F. Westcott (A General Survey of the History of the Canon of the New Testament, 5th ed., 1881, pp. 570f.).1 Innocent's New Testament is the twenty-seven books of Athanasius's list (367) and the African councils of Hippo (393) and Carthage (397); on the Old-Testament side he lists Tobit, Judith, and the two books of Maccabees among "the historical books." The condemnation of the pseudo-apostolic books follows in the next sentence.

The pseudo-apostolic gospels and acts — "not only to be rejected but also condemned"

But the rest of the books, which appear under the name of Matthias or of James the Less, or under the name of Peter and John (which were written by a certain Leucius), or under the name of Andrew (which were written by the philosophers Xenocharides and Leonidas), or under the name of Thomas, and whatever others there may be, you should know they are not only to be rejected but also condemned.

— Innocent I, Ep. 6 to Exuperius, ch. 7 (Latin: "Caetera autem quae vel sub nomine Matthiae, sive Jacobi minoris, vel sub nomine Petri et Johannis, quae a quodam Leucio scripta sunt, vel sub nomine Andreae, quae a Nexocharide, et Leonida philosophis, vel sub nomine Thomae, et si qua sunt talia, non solum repudianda verum etiam noveris esse damnanda"), trans. Westcott.2 Innocent names the apostolic names Eusebius had listed a century earlier — Peter, Thomas, Matthias, with the Acts of Andrew and John — and assigns authors to some of the books: those under Peter and John to "a certain Leucius," those under Andrew to "the philosophers Xenocharides and Leonidas." His wording moves from repudianda (to be rejected) to damnanda (to be condemned). "Under the name of Thomas" is given without description of contents.

Reception and context

  • "Rejected" and "condemned." Innocent's damnanda concerns the books' standing in the church; the letter gives no instruction about the copies themselves. The Decretum Gelasianum's title, On books to be received and not to be received, uses the same vocabulary of reception. Whether the apocryphal gospels were suppressed is set out at Were the infancy gospels suppressed — banned, voted out, gathered up and burned?
  • Two senses of "apocrypha." The books Innocent condemns here are writings under the names of apostles — gospels and acts — not Tobit, Judith, and Maccabees, which he includes in the canon-list above. His Old-Testament list is treated on the companion Old Testament canon page.
  • The title without contents. Like Origen's and Eusebius's lists, Innocent's "under the name of Thomas" gives no contents; which work of that name he meant the notice does not say.
  • Named authors. Innocent's attributions — the Petrine and Johannine acts to "a certain Leucius" (Leucius Charinus, the name later attached to the apocryphal Acts), the Andrean acts to "Xenocharides and Leonidas" — assign the rejected books to named writers; Eusebius had called the books "fictions of heretics."
