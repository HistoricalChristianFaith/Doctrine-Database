The Council of Laodicea on the Biblical Canon

Dates: c. 363–364 (a regional synod of Phrygia Pacatiana in Asia Minor) · Wikipedia · his Old Testament canon page
Summary: A regional Eastern synod rules that only "the Canonical Books… of the New and Old Testament" may be read in church (canon 59), and — in the much-disputed canon 60 — appends a book-list whose New Testament is the present twenty-seven minus the Apocalypse: four Gospels, Acts, seven Catholic Epistles, and fourteen of Paul, without Revelation — the same twenty-six books Cyril of Jerusalem lists a generation earlier. Percival and Westcott doubt that canon 60 belongs to the synod's original acts.

Claims & evidence

Canon 59 — only "the Canonical Books" read in church

Let no private psalms nor any uncanonical books be read in the church, but only the Canonical Books of the New and Old Testament.

— Council of Laodicea, canon 59, trans. H. R. Percival (NPNF 2.14), CCEL.1 The fifty-ninth canon sets a "read in church" rule but names no books. The catalogue is supplied by canon 60. The African councils of 393–397 frame a similar rule (Hippo and Carthage).

Canon 60 — the New Testament, the Apocalypse omitted

And these are the books of the New Testament: Four Gospels, according to Matthew, Mark, Luke and John; The Acts of the Apostles; Seven Catholic Epistles, to wit, one of James, two of Peter, three of John, one of Jude; Fourteen Epistles of Paul, one to the Romans, two to the Corinthians, one to the Galatians, one to the Ephesians, one to the Philippians, one to the Colossians, two to the Thessalonians, one to the Hebrews, two to Timothy, one to Titus, and one to Philemon.

— Council of Laodicea, canon 60 (New Testament list), trans. H. R. Percival (NPNF 2.14), CCEL.2 The list runs four Gospels, Acts, the seven Catholic Epistles, and "fourteen Epistles of Paul" (Hebrews folded into the Pauline corpus, as Cyril, Origen, and Athanasius also count it) — twenty-six books, closing without the Revelation of John. Athanasius's list of 367 ends "and besides, the Revelation of John."

The genuineness of canon 60

Percival and Westcott doubt that canon 60 belongs to the synod's original acts. Percival prints it under the caption "[N.B.—This Canon is of most questionable genuineness]," and the book-list is missing from a range of early canon-collections of Laodicea (the Latin versions of Dionysius Exiguus and John of Antioch); Westcott judged "the external evidence… decidedly against the authenticity" of the enumeration, which on this view was appended later to spell out the "canonical books" canon 59 leaves unnamed.3 Canon 60 is placed here at the synod's date, c. 363, as the received text gives it; Percival and Westcott raise no such question about canon 59.

Reception and context

  • Revelation in the Eastern lists. Cyril of Jerusalem's list (c. 350) likewise runs four Gospels, Acts, seven Catholic Epistles, and fourteen of Paul, without the Apocalypse; the Apostolic Canons also give a New Testament without it. Athanasius (367) includes it.
  • The same witness, two doctrines. This page treats Laodicea's New-Testament list; the parallel Old Testament canon page treats canon 60's Old Testament — the Hebrew twenty-two, without Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, or Maccabees, but with Baruch and the Epistle of Jeremiah under "Jeremiah." Both rest on the one canon 60.
  • Confirmed at Trullo (692). The Council in Trullo (692), canon 2, ruled that the eighty-five Apostolic Canons "should from this time forth remain firm and unshaken," and set "our seal likewise upon" the canons of Laodicea, Carthage, Athanasius, Amphilochius of Iconium, and others; their book-lists differ on the Old-Testament books and on Revelation. Canon 2 sets out no book-list of its own.4
