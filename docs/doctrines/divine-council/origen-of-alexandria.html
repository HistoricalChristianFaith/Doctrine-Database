Origen of Alexandria on the Divine Council

Dates: c. 248 (Origen, c. 185–253; Wikipedia) Summary: Origen is the strongest patristic witness to a real heavenly administration of the nations. Reading Deuteronomy 32:8–9—the nations apportioned “according to the number of the angels of God,” with Israel as the Lord's own portion—he keeps the divine-council shape intact: every nation stands under a presiding heavenly power. But those powers are created angels set in order by Providence, not an assembly of rival “gods.” He is the mediating position between the Second-Temple council and the later “elohim = men” reading.

Claims & evidence

The nations were divided “according to the number of the angels of God”; Israel is the Lord's portion

…even this point is not to be idly passed over, that even of us human beings certain different orders are mentioned in the words, “The portion of the Lord is His people Jacob; Israel is the cord of His inheritance.” Other nations, moreover, are called a part of the angels; since “when the Most High divided the nations, and dispersed the sons of Adam, He fixed the boundaries of the nations according to the number of the angels of God.”

— De Principiis, Book I, source. Origen builds the Deuteronomy 32:8 structure straight into his hierarchy of rational natures: the nations are apportioned to the angels, while Israel falls directly to the Lord. The heavenly beings over the nations are one of the “orders and offices” of created rational existences, not a class of gods.

The quarters of the earth were allotted to superintending spirits and governing powers

…in all probability, the various quarters of the earth were from the beginning allotted to different superintending spirits, and were thus distributed among certain governing powers, and in this manner the administration of the world is carried on.

— Against Celsus, Book V, source. Answering Celsus on why nations keep their ancestral customs, Origen grounds the arrangement in a real division of the earth among “superintending spirits” and “governing powers”—the council's administrative division of the world—yet frames it as the ordered administration of the one God's providence, not the sway of independent deities.

Moses names the same division: the bounds of the peoples set by the number of the angels of God

…our prophet of God and His genuine servant Moses, in his song in the book of Deuteronomy, makes a statement regarding the portioning out of the earth in the following terms: “When the Most High divided the nations, when He dispersed the sons of Adam, He set the bounds of the people according to the number of the angels of God; and the portion was His people Jacob, and Israel the cord of His inheritance.”

— Against Celsus, Book V, source. Origen cites Deuteronomy 32:8 by name and in full to establish the point against Celsus: the apportioning of the peoples runs by “the number of the angels of God,” with Israel reserved as the Lord's own inheritance. This is the divine-council text read as an angelology.

Notes

  • Origen preserves the divine-council structure of Deuteronomy 32:8—heavenly beings set over the nations, Israel the Lord's portion—more fully than most fathers, and treats those beings as real, personal powers, not literary flourish.
  • But his beings are created angels under God's ordering providence, not a separate class of “gods” and not (in these passages) a set of rebel deities holding the nations against God. This distinguishes his reading from the modern “rebel elohim / gods of the nations” revival: Origen keeps the council's administrative shape while draining it of any rival divinity, making him the mediating patristic position between the Second-Temple council and the later “elohim = men” reading.
