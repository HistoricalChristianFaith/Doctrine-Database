Athanasius of Alexandria on the Divine Council

Dates: c. 340 (Athanasius of Alexandria, c. 296–373; Wikipedia). Summary: Against the Arians, Athanasius reads the "gods" of Psalm 82 (LXX 81) as creatures — the ancient people, and any called sons or gods "in earth or in heaven" — who were "adopted and deified through the Word." They are gods by grace, not by nature; the Word alone is "very God from the very God." For Athanasius Psalm 82 is therefore a text about deification by adoption, not a standing council of independent deities: whatever else is called "god" exists only through the one true Son.

Claims & evidence

All who are called "sons" and "gods" were adopted and deified through the Word

Therefore He was not man, and then became God, but He was God, and then became man, and that to deify us… (and Scripture says of many, 'God stands in the congregation of Gods')… Yet, says He to their brethren the Jews, 'If He called them gods, unto whom the Word of God came.' And if all that are called sons and gods, whether in earth or in heaven, were adopted and deified through the Word, and the Son Himself is the Word, it is plain that through Him are they all, and He Himself before all… and He alone is very God from the very God, not receiving these prerogatives as a reward for His virtue, nor being another beside them, but being all these by nature and according to essence.

— Discourses Against the Arians 1.11.39, source. Athanasius answers Arians who cited Psalm 82 to make the Son "god" only by grace, as creatures are: he grants that many are called gods, but insists they hold the title as adopted, deified creatures — through the Word — whereas the Son alone is God "by nature and according to essence."

Notes

  • The anti-Arian frame is decisive: Athanasius reads the elohim of Psalm 82 as adopted and deified creatures (the ancient people, and any "in earth or in heaven" so called) precisely to protect the Son's unique natural divinity against those who leveled Him with them.
  • So "gods" is for him a title of grace, not the name of a council of independent deities: the sons and gods "exist through Him" and after Him, while He alone is "very God from the very God."
