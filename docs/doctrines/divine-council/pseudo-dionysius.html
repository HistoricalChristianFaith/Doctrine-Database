Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite on the Divine Council

Dates: c. 500 (the works are pseudonymous — written under the name of Paul's Athenian convert of Acts 17:34, but composed c. 500 and first cited in 533, at a colloquy in Constantinople between the Chalcedonian and Severan parties).

Summary: Pseudo-Dionysius' On the Celestial Hierarchy is the classic later development of the council-and-host motif: it takes the loose Old-Testament picture of Yahweh amid "sons of God," "holy ones," and the "host of heaven" and hardens it into a fixed system — nine ranks of heavenly beings arranged in three descending triads, each rank passing the divine light down to the one below. This is the ordering that became standard in Christian angelology (later carried by Gregory the Great, John of Damascus, Aquinas, and Dante). Its value on this timeline is as reception-history: it shows what the biblical council became. And on the specific question of the nations, Pseudo-Dionysius reads the very Deuteronomy 32:8 allotment that anchors the divine-council frame — but, like Origen, as created angels under one Providence, explicitly denying that "certain other gods" preside over the peoples.

Claims & evidence

The heavenly beings are nine, "divided into three threefold Orders"

The Word of God has designated the whole Heavenly Beings as nine, by appellations, which shew their functions. These our Divine Initiator divides into three threefold Orders.

— Celestial Hierarchy VI, tr. John Parker (1897/1899), historicalchristian.faith. The nine names are all drawn from Scripture (Seraphim, Cherubim, Thrones; Dominions/"Lordships," Virtues/"Powers," and "Authorities"; Principalities, Archangels, Angels — gathered chiefly from Isaiah 6, Ezekiel 1–10, and Ephesians 1:21 / Colossians 1:16). Pseudo-Dionysius' innovation is not the names but the system: a fixed nine sorted into three graded triads.

The three triads, highest to lowest

the most Holy Thrones, and the many-eyed and many-winged hosts, named in the Hebrew tongue Cherubim and Seraphim, are established immediately around God, with a nearness superior to all. This threefold order, then, our illustrious Guide spoke of as one, and of equal rank, and really first Hierarchy… But he says, that which is composed of the Authorities, and Lordships, and Powers is second; and, as respects the lowest of the Heavenly Hierarchies, the Order of the Angels and Archangels and Principalities is third.

— Celestial Hierarchy VI, historicalchristian.faith. The first (highest) triad is Seraphim, Cherubim, Thrones; the middle triad the Lordships (Dominions), Powers (Virtues), and Authorities; the last (lowest) triad the Principalities, Archangels, and Angels — the rank that "is more particularly concerned with the things of the world" and so is "more properly named Angels by us than those of higher degree." The word "angel," which in the loose biblical usage covers the whole host, is here narrowed to the bottom rung of a ranked cosmos.

The names decoded from the Hebrew

those who know Hebrew affirm, that the holy designation of the Seraphim denotes either that they are kindling or burning; and that of Cherubim, a fulness of knowledge or stream of wisdom.

— Celestial Hierarchy VII.1, historicalchristian.faith. The etymologies are broadly sound — śārāp ("burn") and the wisdom/knowledge associations of the cherubim — and they show the system being built by reading the biblical names themselves, not by importing pagan ones.

A graded cascade of light: each rank illumines the one below

The middle Order of the Heavenly Minds having these Godlike characteristics, is purified and illuminated and perfected in the manner described, by the Divine illuminations vouchsafed to it at second hand, through the first Hierarchical Order, and passing through this middle as a secondary manifestation.

— Celestial Hierarchy VIII.1, historicalchristian.faith. This is the engine of the whole scheme (Pseudo-Dionysius coined the very word "hierarchy," hierarchia, "sacred order"): the highest triad receives the divine light immediately from God, and "from this Order again, in due degree, the second, and from the second, the third, and from the third, our Hierarchy" (Celestial Hierarchy X.1). The council is no longer a flat assembly of "sons of God" standing together before the throne (as in Job 1 or 1 Enoch 40); it is a stepped conduit, each rank mediating to the next.

The nations under their angels — Deuteronomy 32:8, but as created angels under one God

Hence, the Word of God has assigned our Hierarchy to Angels, by naming Michael as Ruler of the Jewish people, and others over other nations. For the Most High established borders of nations according to number of Angels of God.

— Celestial Hierarchy IX.2, historicalchristian.faith. Pseudo-Dionysius quotes Deuteronomy 32:8 in its Septuagint form — the nations bounded "according to number of Angels of God," with Michael over Israel — the same allotment text the divine-council reading builds on (see the cosmic-geography argument). But he is emphatic that these are created angels serving one Providence, not rival gods: "never must we think… that certain other gods, preside over the other nations… there is one Providence of the whole, superessentially established above all the powers, unseen and seen, and… all the Angels who preside over each nation, elevate… those who follow them… to It as their proper Head" (Celestial Hierarchy IX.4). This is the Origen position — the divine-council structure kept as an angelology under providence, not an assembly of elohim.

Notes

  • The author wrote under the name of Dionysius the Areopagite, the Athenian converted by Paul (Acts 17:34), and was long received as a sub-apostolic authority; the works are in fact c. 500 (they quote Proclus and surface in the 533 Constantinople colloquy), hence "Pseudo-Dionysius." The false attribution lent the nine-rank scheme enormous authority in the medieval West.
  • This page is reception-history, not a proof of the maximal reading. Pseudo-Dionysius shows how the sparse, loosely-ranked council-host of the Hebrew Bible and 1 Enoch was systematized into a fixed celestial bureaucracy — a development the modern divine-council reading (Heiser) generally treats as a later over-precision, preferring the flatter Old-Testament picture of a council of elohim. What Pseudo-Dionysius does confirm is the continuity of the underlying cosmology: a real, populated, ranked heaven of created beings, and the Deuteronomy 32:8 allotment of the nations to angelic princes, both taken as plain fact five centuries into the Christian era.
  • The Celestial Hierarchy also carries a striking anticipation of the "many-eyed and many-winged" throne-beings of Ezekiel and Revelation; its numbering scheme (Cherubim and Seraphim nearest, Angels nearest us) fixed the order later versified by Dante (Paradiso XXVIII) and codified by Aquinas (Summa I q. 108).
