Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite on the Divine Council

Dates: c. 500 (the works are pseudonymous — written under the name of Paul's Athenian convert of Acts 17:34, but composed c. 500 and first cited in 533, at a colloquy in Constantinople between the Chalcedonian and Severan parties).

Summary: Pseudo-Dionysius' On the Celestial Hierarchy sorts the heavenly beings of Scripture — the "sons of God," "holy ones," and "host of heaven" around Yahweh — into a fixed system: nine ranks of heavenly beings arranged in three descending triads, each rank passing the divine light down to the one below. Aquinas (Summa I q. 108) and Dante (Paradiso XXVIII) later set out the same nine orders; Gregory the Great and John of Damascus are also named as carrying the ordering (⚠ unverified). On the nations, Pseudo-Dionysius reads the Deuteronomy 32:8 allotment, like Origen, as created angels under one Providence, explicitly denying that "certain other gods" preside over the peoples.

Claims & evidence

The heavenly beings are nine, "divided into three threefold Orders"

The Word of God has designated the whole Heavenly Beings as nine, by appellations, which shew their functions. These our Divine Initiator divides into three threefold Orders.

— Celestial Hierarchy VI, tr. John Parker (1897/1899), historicalchristian.faith. The nine names are all drawn from Scripture (Seraphim, Cherubim, Thrones; Dominions/"Lordships," Virtues/"Powers," and "Authorities"; Principalities, Archangels, Angels — gathered chiefly from Isaiah 6, Ezekiel 1–10, and Ephesians 1:21 / Colossians 1:16). The names come from Scripture; the arrangement into a fixed nine in three graded triads is the treatise's own.

The three triads, highest to lowest

the most Holy Thrones, and the many-eyed and many-winged hosts, named in the Hebrew tongue Cherubim and Seraphim, are established immediately around God, with a nearness superior to all. This threefold order, then, our illustrious Guide spoke of as one, and of equal rank, and really first Hierarchy… But he says, that which is composed of the Authorities, and Lordships, and Powers is second; and, as respects the lowest of the Heavenly Hierarchies, the Order of the Angels and Archangels and Principalities is third.

— Celestial Hierarchy VI, historicalchristian.faith. The first (highest) triad is Seraphim, Cherubim, Thrones; the middle triad the Lordships (Dominions), Powers (Virtues), and Authorities; the last (lowest) triad the Principalities, Archangels, and Angels — the rank that "is more particularly concerned with the things of the world" and so is "more properly named Angels by us than those of higher degree." Here the word "angel," used in the Bible of the whole host, names the lowest rank.

The names decoded from the Hebrew

those who know Hebrew affirm, that the holy designation of the Seraphim denotes either that they are kindling or burning; and that of Cherubim, a fulness of knowledge or stream of wisdom.

— Celestial Hierarchy VII.1, historicalchristian.faith. The etymologies tie the Seraphim to śārāp ("burn") and the Cherubim to knowledge and wisdom; the ranks are named from the biblical names themselves.

A graded cascade of light: each rank illumines the one below

The middle Order of the Heavenly Minds having these Godlike characteristics, is purified and illuminated and perfected in the manner described, by the Divine illuminations vouchsafed to it at second hand, through the first Hierarchical Order, and passing through this middle as a secondary manifestation.

— Celestial Hierarchy VIII.1, historicalchristian.faith. This is the working of the scheme (the word "hierarchy," hierarchia, "sacred order," is credited to Pseudo-Dionysius, ⚠ unverified): the highest triad receives the divine light immediately from God, and "from this Order again, in due degree, the second, and from the second, the third, and from the third, our Hierarchy" (Celestial Hierarchy X.1). Where Job 1 and 1 Enoch 40 picture the heavenly beings standing together before the throne, here each rank mediates to the next.

The nations under their angels — Deuteronomy 32:8, read as created angels under one God

Hence, the Word of God has assigned our Hierarchy to Angels, by naming Michael as Ruler of the Jewish people, and others over other nations. For the Most High established borders of nations according to number of Angels of God.

— Celestial Hierarchy IX.2, historicalchristian.faith. Pseudo-Dionysius quotes Deuteronomy 32:8 in its Septuagint form — the nations bounded "according to number of Angels of God," with Michael over Israel — the same verse Michael Heiser's divine-council reading cites. He states that these are created angels serving one Providence, not rival gods: "never must we think… that certain other gods, preside over the other nations… there is one Providence of the whole, superessentially established above all the powers, unseen and seen, and… all the Angels who preside over each nation, elevate… those who follow them… to It as their proper Head" (Celestial Hierarchy IX.4). Origen reads the verse the same way, of angels under one Providence.

Reception and debate

  • The author wrote under the name of Dionysius the Areopagite, the Athenian converted by Paul (Acts 17:34), and was long received as a sub-apostolic authority; the works are dated c. 500 (they quote Proclus and surface in the 533 Constantinople colloquy), hence "Pseudo-Dionysius." The attribution to the Areopagite gave the nine-rank scheme authority in the medieval West (⚠ unverified).
  • Pseudo-Dionysius sets the heavenly beings of the Hebrew Bible and 1 Enoch in a fixed ranked order. Pseudo-Dionysius takes a real, populated, ranked heaven of created beings, and the Deuteronomy 32:8 allotment of the nations to angelic princes, as given.
  • The Celestial Hierarchy also draws on the "many-eyed and many-winged" throne-beings of Ezekiel and Revelation. Its numbering scheme (Cherubim and Seraphim nearest God, Angels nearest us) is the order Dante later versified (Paradiso XXVIII) and Aquinas treated (Summa I q. 108). Pseudo-Dionysius is also a witness on the living-heavens timeline, where the nine-rank scheme is discussed in connection with the medieval correspondence of nine orders and nine spheres; his hierarchy is one of illumination, and he assigns no angel to move a heaven.
