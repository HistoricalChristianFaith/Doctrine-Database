Augustine of Hippo on the Divine Council

Dates: c. 415 (Augustine of Hippo, 354–430; Wikipedia). Summary: Expounding Psalm 82 (LXX 81), Augustine gives the most explicit patristic denial that its "gods" are heavenly beings: they are not "the gods of the Gentiles, or idols, or any creature in heaven or earth except men." The psalm itself, he says, explains whom it means — "the children of the Most High," who "shall die like men." These are the people of Israel, made gods by adoptive grace, who fall through pride "like one of the princes, that is, the devil." It is the direct antithesis of a Psalm 82 council of real created elohim.

Claims & evidence

The "gods" are no creature in heaven or earth except men — the people of Israel

For it begins, God stood in the synagogue of gods. Far however be it from us to understand by these Gods the gods of the Gentiles, or idols, or any creature in heaven or earth except men; for a little after this verse the same Psalm relates and explains what Gods it means in whose synagogue God stood, where it says, I have said, You are gods, and you are all the children of the Most High: but you shall die like men, and fall like one of the princes… By the synagogue we understand the people of Israel, because synagogue is the word properly used of them.

— Exposition on Psalm 82, source. Augustine anchors his reading in the psalm's own explanation: those called "gods" are the ones who "shall die like men" — therefore men, the congregation of Israel — and not any heavenly creature.

Made children of the Most High, they fall through pride like the devil

I said, You are gods, and you are all the children of the Most High: that is, to all of you I promised celestial happiness, but you, through the infirmity of your flesh, shall die like men, and through haughtiness of soul, like one of the princes, that is, the devil, shall not be exalted, but shall fall.

— Exposition on Psalm 82, source. The "gods" are men whom God "wished by lowliness to make the children of the Most High" — a promised celestial happiness held by grace — who nonetheless "die like men" and, through pride, "fall like one of the princes, that is, the devil."

Notes

  • Augustine explicitly excludes "any creature in heaven or earth except men" — the strongest patristic denial of a Psalm 82 council of real heavenly elohim. The "gods" are the people of Israel, sons of the Most High by adoptive grace who "die like men."
  • This is the direct antithesis of the reading (revived in modern scholarship by Michael Heiser) that Psalm 82:1 convenes a council of created lesser divine beings: for Augustine the only candidates the psalm allows are human.
