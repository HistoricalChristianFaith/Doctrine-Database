Tertullian on the Divine Council

Dates: c. 197 (The Apology 22–23; On Idolatry 9; Tertullian of Carthage, fl. c. 155–220; Wikipedia) Summary: Tertullian states that the gods of the nations are the fallen angels and the demon-brood sprung from them. Scripture teaches “how from certain angels, who fell of their own free-will, there sprang a more wicked demon-brood.” Those demons crave the “flesh-fumes and blood” of sacrifice and “make themselves gods,” so behind the whole pagan pantheon is “but a single race — the race of demons.” In On Idolatry he adds that the same “deserters from God, the lovers of women” invented astrology. On this timeline he identifies the gods of the nations as fallen angels and their offspring.

Claims & evidence

From the fallen angels sprang a “demon-brood” that feeds on the sacrifices of the nations

We are instructed, moreover, by our sacred books how from certain angels, who fell of their own free-will, there sprang a more wicked demon-brood, condemned of God along with the authors of their race, and that chief we have referred to… of which the worst is that by which these deities are commended to the favour of deceived and deluded human beings, that they may get their proper food of flesh-fumes and blood when that is offered up to idol-images.

— The Apology 22, source. Tertullian grounds the identity of the pagan gods in the Genesis-6 descent: fallen angels beget a “demon-brood,” and those demons, posing as “deities,” feed on the blood and smoke of sacrifice offered at the idols. For Tertullian the powers of the nations are real, and they are rebel spirits.

The demons “make themselves gods”; the pagan pantheon is one race — the race of demons

Will it not then be more reasonable to hold that these spirits make themselves gods, giving as they do the very proofs which raise your gods to godhead, than that the gods are the equals of angels and demons?… But since on beth sides there is a concurrent acknowledgment that they are not gods, gather from this that there is but a single race—I mean the race of demons, the real race in both cases. Let your search, then, now be after gods; for those whom you had imagined to be so you find to be spirits of evil.

— The Apology 23, source. Tertullian appeals to exorcism: brought before a Christian, the “wicked spirit… will as readily make the truthful confession that he is a demon, as elsewhere he has falsely asserted that he is a god.” On his argument the nations' gods are all “a single race — the race of demons.” (The transcription's “beth” is a scanning error for “both.”)

The fallen angels — “the deserters from God, the lovers of women” — invented astrology

One proposition I lay down: that those angels, the deserters from God, the lovers of women, were likewise the discoverers of this curious art, on that account also condemned by God. Oh divine sentence, reaching even unto the earth in its vigour, whereto the unwitting render testimony! The astrologers are expelled just like their angels.

— On Idolatry 9, source. In Tertullian's account the same fallen angels who begat the demons also taught mankind the forbidden arts, here astrology. In 1 Enoch 7–8 the Watchers likewise teach forbidden arts, astrology among them.

Reception and debate

  • With Justin and the Septuagint (Psalm 96:5, “the gods of the nations are demons”), Tertullian holds that the powers of the nations are real and malevolent: for him, the rebel angels of Genesis 6 and their brood.
  • The mechanism is the Genesis-6 / 1 Enoch descent: the fallen angels beget a “demon-brood,” and those demons feed on sacrifice and pose as the gods of the temples. Lactantius (c. 310) gives the same account at length (the “two kinds of demons” and the same appeal to exorcism).
  • Tertullian appears on this wiki also as a witness on the Nephilim, astrology, and Serpent of Eden timelines; the fallen-angels-taught-astrology claim of On Idolatry 9 anchors his witness there as here.
