Jerome on the Divine Council

Dates: c. 400 (Jerome, c. 347–420; Wikipedia). Summary: Preaching on Psalm 82 (LXX 81), Jerome reads its "gods" as men made gods "not by nature, but by grace" — the very ground of adoption. He hears the psalm as a rebuke to Arius and Eunomius, who would make the Son a son "in the same way as we are"; and he insists the "gods" are not an exalted class of kings and princes but all humanity, deified by grace and adoption alike.

Claims & evidence

"You are gods" — not by nature but by grace; and said to all, not to kings and princes

I said: you are gods, and all of you sons of the Most High. Let Eunomius hear, let Arius hear, who say that the Son of God is a son in the same way as we are. That we are gods, we are so not by nature, but by grace… Therefore I made man, that out of men gods might be made… I made you gods, just as I made Moses a god of Pharaoh: that after you have been gods, you may be made worthy to be sons of God… He did not say: I said: you are gods, the kings and the princes, but all…

— Homily 14, on Psalm 81, source. Jerome grounds adoption in grace, not nature: the "gods" of the psalm are men to whom God "bestowed both deity and adoption," made gods as Moses was made "a god of Pharaoh," and the psalm speaks so of all — not of an exalted class.

Notes

  • Jerome explicitly rejects reading the "gods" as an exalted class (kings and princes): "He did not say… the kings and the princes, but all" — they are all humanity, deified by grace.
  • The point is anti-Arian and anti-Eunomian: because we are gods only by grace and adoption, the Son cannot be "a son in the same way as we are" — a title of grace for men, not evidence of independent lesser deities.
