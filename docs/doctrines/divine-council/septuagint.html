The Septuagint on the Divine Council

Dates: c. 250–100 BC (the Septuagint, the Greek translation of the Hebrew Bible made by Alexandrian Jews).

Summary: The wording of the Greek translation gives a cosmology of lesser heavenly beings gathered around Yahweh: an assembly of divine beings among whom God stands to judge, the nations apportioned to heavenly administrators, and the gods of the pagans named as demons. At Deuteronomy 32:8 and Psalm 96:5 its wording differs from the Masoretic Hebrew; which wording is earlier at Deuteronomy 32:8 is disputed.

Claims & evidence

Deuteronomy 32:8 — the nations apportioned "according to the number of the angels of God"

ἤστησεν ὅρια ἐθνῶν κατὰ ἀριθμὸν ἀγγέλων Θεοῦ

he set the bounds of the nations according to the number of the angels of God.

— Deuteronomy 32:8 (Septuagint), Greek text and English at Elpenor. Where the received Hebrew (Masoretic) text reads that God fixed the nations' boundaries "according to the number of the sons of Israel," the Greek reads "angels of God," a Dead Sea Scrolls fragment of the same verse (4QDeutj) reads "sons of God" (Hebrew bene elohim), and a minority of Greek witnesses read "sons of God" (υἱῶν θεοῦ). On this reading the seventy nations of the Table of Nations (Genesis 10) are parcelled out among heavenly beings under the Most High. Michael Heiser's divine-council reading rests on this variant; see Does Deuteronomy 32:8 originally end "sons of God" or "sons of Israel"?

Psalm 82(81):1 — God stands in the assembly of gods

Ο ΘΕΟΣ ἥστη ἐν συναγωγῇ θεῶν, ἐν μέσῳ δὲ θεοὺσ διακρινεῖ.

God stands in the assembly of gods; and in the midst [of them] will judge gods.

— Psalm 81:1 (Septuagint numbering = Hebrew Psalm 82:1), Greek text and English at Elpenor; cf. the verse Psalm 82:1. The translators rendered the Hebrew ‘adat ’el as a "συναγωγῆ θεῶν" — an assembly of gods — within which the God of Israel rises to pass judgment. The plural θεοί ("gods") is kept as the object of that judgment.

Psalm 96(95):5 — the gods of the nations are demons

ὅτι πάντες οἱ θεοὶ τῶν ἐθνῶν δαιμόνια, ὁ δὲ Κύρι&omicromicron;ς τοὺς οὐρανοὺς ἐποίησεν.

For all the gods of the heathen are devils: but the Lord made the heavens.

— Psalm 95:5 (Septuagint numbering = Hebrew Psalm 96:5), Greek text and English at Elpenor. Where the Hebrew has the nations' gods as ’elilim ("worthless idols," "nothings"), the Greek reads δαιμόνια — "demons." The Greek names spiritual beings where the Hebrew word names idols as nothings. 1 Corinthians 10:20 uses the same Greek word of the recipients of pagan sacrifice.

Reception and debate

  • Together, the three renderings give, at the level of the Greek text: an assembly of gods around Yahweh (Ps 82), heavenly beings set over the nations (Deut 32), and the nations' gods understood as real demonic powers (Ps 96).
  • Which wording of Deuteronomy 32:8 is older is disputed. P. W. Skehan, J. W. Wevers, Jeffrey Tigay, Emanuel Tov and Michael Heiser hold that the Septuagint ("angels of God") and 4QDeutj ("sons of God") preserve an older text than the Masoretic "sons of Israel," which Tov calls a theological correction; David E. Stevens (1997) and others hold the Masoretic reading original. Both readings are set out on Does Deuteronomy 32:8 originally end "sons of God" or "sons of Israel"?; see also the survey at TheTorah.com, "The Sons of Israel or God? – Deuteronomy 32:8", and Michael Heiser's text-critical study, "Deuteronomy 32:8 and the Sons of God," Bibliotheca Sacra 158 (2001).
