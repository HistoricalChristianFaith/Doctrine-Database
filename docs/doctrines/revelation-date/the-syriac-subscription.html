The Syriac Subscription on the Date of Revelation

Dates: 6th century (the Syriac Apocalypse; superscription/title) · Wikipedia
Summary: The heading prefixed to the Apocalypse in the Syriac tradition names Nero, not Domitian, as the emperor who banished John to Patmos; Mark Hitchcock calls it "the first clear, accepted, unambiguous witness to the Neronic date" (see the interpretation page). It is a heading of the sixth century or later; the Greek and Latin witnesses on this timeline name Domitian (or, for Epiphanius, Claudius).

Claims & evidence

John was banished to Patmos "by Nero Caesar"

The title-line carried with the Apocalypse in the Syriac version reads, in Moses Stuart's direct quotation of the title page, that "it was written in Patmos, whither John was sent by Nero Caesar." (Stuart, A Commentary on the Apocalypse, 1845, 1:267) Stuart also traces the edition: the Syriac Apocalypse in the printed Syriac New Testament was copied in western Asia by Caspar, passed to Scaliger the younger and the library of Leyden, and was published by L. de Dieu in 1627 — the text the London and Paris Polyglots then took. This is the later (Philoxenian/Harklean) Syriac stratum, not the Peshitta, whose New Testament dates from the beginning of the fifth century and never carried the book. A fuller English form — "The Revelation which was made by God to John the Evangelist on the island of Patmos, into which he was thrown by Nero Caesar" — circulates through the modern early-date literature (Kenneth Gentry). John Gwynn's later critical edition of a different Syriac copy (The Apocalypse of St. John in a Syriac Version Hitherto Unknown, Dublin, 1897, from the Crawford manuscript) prints the Syriac and a reconstructed Greek but supplies no English of the heading — its opening leaves were damaged — so the "by Nero Caesar" title is anchored to the De Dieu/Polyglot edition Stuart used, not to Gwynn's.

Reception and debate

  • A heading that names Nero. Where the Greek and Latin Fathers on this timeline name Domitian (or, for Epiphanius, Claudius), the Syriac heading names Nero, placing the exile before AD 68. Moses Stuart and Kenneth Gentry adduce it as ancient external testimony agreeing with the internal evidence for the early date.
  • Readings of the heading. Stuart and Gentry read the heading as preserving a Syriac tradition of a Neronian exile. Mark Hitchcock, noting that Revelation entered the Syriac New Testament only with the sixth- and seventh-century Philoxenian and Harklean versions, some five centuries after John, reads it as recording the belief of the time it was written.
