Irenaeus on the Date of Revelation

Dates: c. 180 (Against Heresies Book 5) · Wikipedia
Summary: Arguing that the number of the Antichrist's name would have been revealed had it been needful, Irenaeus writes of "him who beheld the apocalyptic vision" that "that was seen… almost in our day, towards the end of Domitian's reign" — the earliest extant external notice bearing on when Revelation was seen, and the chief external testimony on which holders of the late (Domitianic) date rest.

Claims & evidence

"That was seen… towards the end of Domitian's reign"

"We will not, however, incur the risk of pronouncing positively as to the name of Antichrist; for if it were necessary that his name should be distinctly revealed in this present time, it would have been announced by him who beheld the apocalyptic vision. For that was seen no very long time since, but almost in our day, towards the end of Domitian's reign."

— Against Heresies 5.30.3, trans. ANF, source. Domitian reigned 81–96; read as dating the vision, "towards the end of" his reign points to c. 95–96, the date assigned to the book by the commentaries of David Aune, G. K. Beale and Craig Koester (see the late-date commentaries).

Reception and debate

  • The subject of "was seen." The English "that was seen" renders the Greek ἐωράθη (heōrathē), whose unexpressed subject is disputed. Eusebius, who quotes the sentence as his authority for the date, and G. K. Beale take the subject to be the vision; J. A. T. Robinson, who dates the book c. 68, reads the sentence the same way. Kenneth Gentry and Dean Furlong take the subject to be the person who beheld the vision: it was John who "was seen" living on into Domitian's day, which leaves the date of the book itself open. See Does Irenaeus (5.30.3) date the seeing of Revelation to Domitian's reign?
  • Irenaeus and the later witnesses. Irenaeus wrote c. 180, and was a hearer of Polycarp (a disciple of John). Whether the Domitianic dates of Victorinus, Eusebius and Jerome derive from his sentence or attest a broader tradition is disputed; see Do the later Fathers' Domitianic dates confirm Irenaeus independently, or derive from him? Kenneth Gentry gives the book's internal evidence priority over this external testimony; see Gentry.
