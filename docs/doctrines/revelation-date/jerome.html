Jerome on the Date of Revelation

Dates: c. 393 (De viris illustribus / Lives of Illustrious Men) · Wikipedia
Summary: Jerome gives the Domitianic dating with a reckoning of years: John was banished to Patmos and wrote the Apocalypse "in the fourteenth year… after Nero" — i.e. under Domitian — and, freed at Domitian's death, returned to Ephesus and lived until Trajan.

Claims & evidence

John was banished to Patmos and wrote the Apocalypse under Domitian

"In the fourteenth year then after Nero Domitian having raised a second persecution he was banished to the island of Patmos, and wrote the Apocalypse, on which Justin Martyr and Irenaeus afterwards wrote commentaries. But Domitian having been put to death and his acts, on account of his excessive cruelty, having been annulled by the senate, he returned to Ephesus under Pertinax and continuing there until the time of the Emperor Trajan, founded and built churches throughout all Asia, and, worn out by old age, died…"

— Lives of Illustrious Men 9 (on the apostle John), trans. NPNF, source. "The fourteenth year after Nero" and "a second persecution" both place the banishment in Domitian's reign (Nero's persecution being the first), in agreement with the chronology of Eusebius.

Reception and debate

  • A reckoning from Nero. Where Irenaeus wrote "towards the end of Domitian's reign," Jerome reckons from Nero — "the fourteenth year after Nero" — and frames the exile as the second imperial persecution, after Nero's.
  • Jerome's sources. Jerome draws throughout the De viris illustribus on Eusebius, whose dating rests on Irenaeus. Moses Stuart, Thomas Randell, Milton Terry and Kenneth Gentry read Jerome as passing on the Eusebian account rather than adding an independent witness; G. K. Beale and Mark Hitchcock read the reception through Eusebius and Jerome as carrying forward a tradition broader than one source. His notice records the Domitianic date in the Latin West by the end of the fourth century. See Do the later Fathers' Domitianic dates confirm Irenaeus independently, or derive from him?
