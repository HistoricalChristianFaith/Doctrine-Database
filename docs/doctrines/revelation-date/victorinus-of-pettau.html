Victorinus of Pettau on the Date of Revelation

Dates: c. 300 (d. c. 304) · Wikipedia
Summary: In the earliest extant commentary on Revelation, Victorinus states that when John saw the Apocalypse he was on Patmos, "condemned to the labour of the mines by Cæsar Domitian," and that he published it after Domitian's death.

Claims & evidence

John was condemned to the mines on Patmos by Domitian

"He says this, because when John said these things he was in the island of Patmos, condemned to the labour of the mines by Cæsar Domitian."

— Commentary on the Apocalypse of the Blessed John, on Revelation 10:11, trans. ANF, source. Victorinus adds that after Domitian's death John was released and published the Apocalypse he had received — so, on his account, the vision was seen under Domitian and the book issued just after.

Reception and debate

  • The earliest extant Revelation commentary. Writing in Latin at the end of the third century, Victorinus wrote the earliest extant verse-by-verse commentary on the Apocalypse, and sets the vision in Domitian's reign. Where Irenaeus wrote "towards the end of Domitian's reign," Victorinus adds that John was a condemned exile in the Patmos mines under that emperor.
  • Victorinus and Irenaeus. Victorinus knew and used Irenaeus (⚠ unverified). G. K. Beale and Mark Hitchcock read the detail of the mines, absent from Irenaeus, as marking a strand of tradition beyond Irenaeus's words; Moses Stuart, Thomas Randell, Milton Terry and Kenneth Gentry read the later Domitianic notices as derived from Irenaeus.
