Clement of Alexandria on the Date of Revelation

Dates: c. 200 (Quis dives salvetur?) · Wikipedia
Summary: Telling the story of the apostle John and the young robber, Clement places John's return from Patmos to Ephesus "on the tyrant's death." He names no emperor; Eusebius, quoting the passage, identifies the tyrant as Domitian.

Claims & evidence

John returned from Patmos "on the tyrant's death"

"For when, on the tyrant's death, he returned to Ephesus from the isle of Patmos, he went away, being invited, to the contiguous territories of the nations"

— Who Is the Rich Man That Shall Be Saved? 42, trans. ANF, source. The clause comes in passing, within the pastoral tale that follows; Clement does not argue a date, but writes of John's exile as ending when a "tyrant" died.

Reception and debate

  • Which tyrant? Clement leaves the emperor unnamed. Eusebius quotes the passage in his Church History (3.23) as evidence for John's Domitianic exile and release, identifying the tyrant as Domitian.
  • Clement and Irenaeus. Writing c. 200 in Alexandria, Clement tells the exile-and-return story without reference to Irenaeus's Antichrist-number argument. G. K. Beale and Mark Hitchcock read him as an independent Alexandrian witness to the exile on which the Domitianic chronology rests; Moses Stuart, Thomas Randell, Milton Terry and Kenneth Gentry, who read the later Domitianic notices as derived from Irenaeus, note that Clement names no emperor and that the name Domitian is attached to his "tyrant" by Eusebius.
