Gregory the Dialogist on the parity of sins

Dates: c. 591 (the Moralia circulated by 591; Gregory kept revising, and the dedicatory letter is dated 596) · Wikipedia
Summary: Expounding Job, Gregory describes righteousness as a garment: a man who is just in some things and unjust in others has not clothed himself but left one side bare; good deeds, he says, are “defiled” by evil deeds springing up beside them.

Claims & evidence

Gregory’s image of righteousness as a garment

Surely, when we are clothed with a garment, we are surrounded on every side, and so he is “clothed with righteousness as with a garment,” who defends himself on every side with good practice, and leaves no part of his conduct naked to sin; for he that is just in some deeds and unjust in others, it is as if he covered over this side, and exposed that one naked; nor are those henceforth good deeds, which are defiled by other evil deeds springing up.

— Morals on the Book of Job 19.32, source, expounding Job 29:14. In the last clause good deeds are “defiled” by evil ones springing up beside them.

Notes

  • Gregory does not discuss in this passage whether sins differ in gravity. He is a principal witness on the purgatory timeline.
  • Augustine explains “guilty of all” by the love on which all the commandments hang; Gregory’s image speaks of the righteous life as one garment.
