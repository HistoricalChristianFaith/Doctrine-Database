Bede on the parity of sins

Dates: c. 716 (Commentary on the Catholic Epistles) · Wikipedia
Summary: Bede is the earliest witness surveyed here to comment on both James 2:10 and 1 John 5:16–17 in a single work. On James 2:10 he gives Augustine’s solution and Augustine’s answer to the parity objection; on 1 John 5:17 he names the Stoics and Jovinian and rejects the parity thesis, distinguishing small sins “without which this life cannot be lived” from sins that lead to eternal punishment unless corrected; on 1 John 5:16 he assigns the two kinds of sin to different remedies.

Claims & evidence

On James 2:10: guilty of all through love, with degrees of sin

Or perhaps because the fullness of the law is love, by which God and neighbor are loved, upon which commandments of love the whole law and the prophets depend, he rightly becomes guilty of all who acts against that in which all things hang? No one sins except by acting against it. … Why then are not all sins said to be equal? Or perhaps because he acts more against charity who sins more gravely, and less who sins lightly? Yet even if he offends in one, he is guilty of all, because he acts against that in which all things hang.

— Commentary on the Catholic Epistles, on James 2:10, source. Both halves of Augustine’s Letter 167 are here in brief: guilt of the whole from the object violated, gradation of guilt from the degree of violation. Bede keeps Augustine’s interrogative form (“or perhaps because…?”).

On 1 John 5:17: two classes of sin, the Stoics, and Jovinian

Such is the diversity of sins, he says, that everything which deviates from the rule of equity is counted among the sins, although small sins cannot in any way take away or diminish the merit of righteousness from the just, as long as they are those without which this life cannot be lived, and likewise there are certain sins so discordant with all justice, committed with such iniquity, that without any contradiction, unless they are corrected, they lead their perpetrator to eternal punishment. … Blessed John’s statement clearly repudiates the inept argument of the Stoics, who dared to say against all human sense and to affirm that all sins are equal, saying it makes no difference whether a man steals a human being, an ox, or a chicken, because it is not the animal but the intention that constitutes the crime. Jovinian the heretic followed them, asserting that there is no distinction between marriage and virginity

— Commentary on the Catholic Epistles, on 1 John 5:17, source. The passage has three parts: a two-class division of sins by their effect, one class compatible with the merit of the just and one leading to eternal punishment unless corrected; the Stoic thesis with its reason (intention, not object, makes the crime), the reason Cicero gives in Paradoxa III; and a report about Jovinian, who is said to have followed the Stoics and is then described as asserting that there is no distinction between marriage and virginity. Whether Jovinian taught the parity of sins is set out at did Jovinian teach that all sins are equal?

Bede on different remedies for light and grave sins

He speaks, however, of daily and light sins, which as they are difficult to avoid, so also are easily cured. … If, therefore, by speaking, or thinking, or forgetfulness, or ignorance you have perhaps erred, go to your brother, confess to him, ask for intervention. … But these things are said about lighter sins. Moreover, if you have committed something more serious, bring in the elders of the Church, and at their examination, punish yourself.

— Commentary on the Catholic Epistles, on 1 John 5:16, source. Bede assigns light sins to mutual confession among brethren and graver ones to the examination of the elders of the church.

Notes

  • On this timeline the 1 John reading of two classes of sin appears in Clement and Tertullian, and the love-reading of James 2:10 in Augustine; Bede comments on both texts in one work.
  • Aquinas later treats venial and mortal sin in a formal question of the Summa Theologiae (I-II q. 88).
  • Bede is also a witness on the nephilim.
