Clement of Alexandria on the parity of sins

Dates: c. 200 · Wikipedia
Summary: The earliest Christian witness surveyed here to state, in so many words, that Scripture teaches “the differences of sins.” Clement reads the distinction from 1 John 5:16–17, in a discussion of sin and repentance, without naming the Stoics.

Claims & evidence

John “manifestly teaches the differences of sins”

John, too, manifestly teaches the differences of sins, in his larger Epistle, in these words: “If any man see his brother sin a sin that is not unto death, he shall ask, and he shall give him life: for these that sin not unto death,” he says. For “there is a sin unto death: I do not say that one is to pray for it. All unrighteousness is sin; and there is a sin not unto death.”

— Stromata II, source. The context is a discussion of sin, ignorance, and repentance. Clement argues from the text: John distinguishes a sin unto death from a sin not unto death, and gives them different remedies.

Notes

  • Two texts carry the later discussion on this timeline: James 2:10, “guilty of all,” and 1 John 5:16–17, which Clement cites. Bede comments on both in one work.
  • The disciplinary question attached to 1 John 5:16 — whether the church may reconcile those guilty of the gravest sins — is taken up on this timeline by Tertullian, Cyprian and Ambrose; Augustine and Aquinas discuss the classes of sin in their own terms.
