Bonaventure on the parity of sins

Dates: c. 1267 (the Collations on the Ten Commandments, delivered at Paris in Lent) · Wikipedia
Summary: Bonaventure answers James 2:10 with the scholastic distinction between two kinds of punishment: the man who offends in one point incurs the full pain of loss — exclusion from God — but not the full pain of sense. On this distinction “guilty of all” holds of the one penalty and not of the other.

Claims & evidence

Full pain of loss, graded pain of sense

“Whoever shall keep the whole law, yet offend in one point, is become guilty of all”: not that for one transgression he is punished with as much punishment of sense as for the transgression of all; but he will be punished with as much punishment of loss, yet not as severely for one as for all. Therefore, if anyone wishes to attain to true life, it is necessary that he have regard to all the commandments of God in heart, mouth, and deed.

— Collationes de Decem Praeceptis, Collation 6, source. The medieval terms are poena damni (the loss of the vision of God) and poena sensus (positive suffering). Bonaventure applies the first to the whole and the second by degrees: the offender is punished “not as severely for one as for all.”

Fear God and keep all the commandments

You must always fear God, because he who “keeps the whole law but offends in one point has become guilty of all.” And in Deuteronomy it is said: “And now, Israel, what does the Lord your God ask of you, except that you fear him and keep all his commandments and walk in his ways?”

— Collationes de Septem Donis, Collation 2, source. In this collation Bonaventure uses the verse as an exhortation to keep all the commandments, joining it to the words of Deuteronomy.

Notes

  • Augustine had explained “guilty of all” by the love that all the commandments hang on; Bonaventure explains it by the kinds of punishment incurred. Aquinas treats the gravity of sins and the distinction of mortal and venial sin in the Summa Theologiae.
  • Bonaventure is also a witness on the nephilim and the immaculate conception.
