4 Maccabees on the parity of sins

Dates: c. AD 40 (dated between AD 20 and 54 by Moses Hadas, 1953, ⚠ unverified, as reported by Sigrid Peterson, “Fourth Maccabees and the Asia Minor Provenance Hypothesis” (rev. 1995); the Wikipedia article below reports Hadas’s suggestion of c. 40 and proposals ranging from AD 20 to 130) · Wikipedia
Summary: A Greek Jewish martyrdom-homily whose author writes in the vocabulary of reason ruling the emotions (“devout reason is sovereign over the emotions”). In the mouth of the aged priest Eleazar it states that a small transgression is as serious as a great one, because in either case the law itself is despised.

Claims & evidence

Eleazar: small and great transgressions equally serious, because the law is equally despised

Therefore do not suppose that it would be a petty sin if we were to eat defiling food; to transgress the law in matters either small or great is of equal seriousness, for in either case the law is equally despised.

— 4 Maccabees 5:19–21 (NRSVue), source. Eleazar is being pressed to eat pork under Antiochus, and the speech defends his refusal: no concession is small enough to make. The ground given is that the law is equally set at nought in the small transgression and the great.

Notes

  • No later witness on this timeline quotes 4 Maccabees on the point. Compare James 2:10–11, which gives as the ground of “guilty of all” that the one who said “Do not commit adultery” said also “Do not kill.”
  • 4 Maccabees survives chiefly in Christian manuscripts of the Greek Bible. On the canonical status of the Maccabean literature in the different communions, see the Old Testament canon.
