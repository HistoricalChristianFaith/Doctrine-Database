The Babylonian Talmud on How Tall Was Goliath?

Dates: c. 500 (redaction of the Babylonian Talmud) · Wikipedia
Summary: An indirect witness to the "six" reading. The rabbinic Goliath haggadah works from the Hebrew text of 1 Samuel 17 that reads "six cubits and a span," and expounds Goliath's name, lineage, taunts and fall. Its mountain-uprooting giant story is told of Og of Bashan, not Goliath. The Talmud quotes no height figure for Goliath and does not mention the Greek and Qumran "four cubits and a span."

Claims & evidence

The Goliath haggadah is built on the Masoretic text of 1 Samuel 17

In the aggadic sweep of Sotah 42a–42b, the sages read Goliath straight out of the received Hebrew of 1 Samuel 17 and expound it. They derive his name and pedigree from the wording of the chapter, identify his mother with Orpah — Ruth's sister-in-law, who "returned" to her people (playing on oref, the "back of the neck") — and gloss the taunting champion who "presented himself forty days, morning and evening" (1 Sam 17:16) as forty days set against the forty days of the giving of the Torah. Rav reads the word beinayim (1 Sam 17:4) to mean that he was "built perfectly and free of any blemish." See Sotah 42b, Sefaria (Koren–Steinsaltz translation); and the discussion of the passage in Jeremy Brown, "Sotah 42 — Goliath, Polydactyly, and Hereditary Gigantism," Talmudology.

For height, the passage bears on the question by the text it presupposes rather than by anything it states: the exposition rests on the Hebrew of 1 Samuel 17, the text whose verse 4 gives Goliath's stature as six cubits and a span. The sages here expound Goliath's ancestry and downfall, not his measurements.

The Talmud's mountain-scale giant story concerns Og of Bashan

Where the rabbis tell a mountain-scale giant story, the giant is Og, king of Bashan — not Goliath. In Berakhot 54b the sages recount Og resolving to uproot a mountain "three parasangs" long to hurl upon the camp of Israel, only to have it drop over his own head; Moses, himself reckoned ten cubits tall, leaps ten cubits with a ten-cubit axe to strike Og in the ankle. The tradition states that for these dimensions "there is no biblical reference, but rather a tradition was transmitted." See Berakhot 54b, Sefaria (Koren–Steinsaltz translation). No comparable story of Goliath's size is told in the Goliath passage of Sotah.

Reception and debate

  • Relation to the "six" reading. Because the rabbinic tradition transmits and expounds the Masoretic Hebrew, it presupposes the "six cubits and a span" reading of 1 Samuel 17:4; it never cites the measurement.
  • The shorter text not mentioned. The Goliath haggadah does not mention the Septuagint's "four cubits and a span," or of the like reading in the Qumran scroll 4QSama. The rabbinic discussion never registers a textual variant in Goliath's height at all; it works from the Hebrew before it.
  • Haggadic, not metrological. The interest of Sotah 42b is aggadic — Goliath's lineage (his mother Orpah, his hundred fathers), his forty days of blasphemy, and his fall by David's hand. It gives no measurement of his stature; it bears on this timeline as evidence for the text the Hebrew tradition carried, not for a height figure of its own.
