4QSama — the Dead Sea Samuel Scroll — on How Tall Was Goliath?

Dates: copied mid-1st century BC (c. 50–25 BC); the oldest extant Hebrew manuscript of this verse · Wikipedia
Summary: The Qumran Samuel scroll gives Goliath's height as four cubits and a span (~6'9″) at 1 Samuel 17:4. By about a thousand years the oldest extant Hebrew copy of the verse, it agrees with the Septuagint against the "six cubits and a span" (~9'9″) of the medieval Masoretic codices.

Claims & evidence

4QSama reads "four cubits and a span" at 1 Samuel 17:4

The scroll is fragmentary at this point, so the height is read and reconstructed by its editors rather than surviving whole; but their reading is four cubits and a span, not the six of the later Masoretic Text. It is the earliest Hebrew evidence for Goliath's stature by roughly a millennium, and it stands with the Greek against the medieval Hebrew codices. The scroll was first edited by Frank Moore Cross; its bearing on Goliath's height, and the underlying manuscript relations, are set out by Eugene Ulrich, The Qumran Text of Samuel and Josephus (Harvard Semitic Monographs 19; Missoula: Scholars Press, 1978), and surveyed for this verse specifically by J. Daniel Hays, "Reconsidering the Height of Goliath," Journal of the Evangelical Theological Society 48/4 (2005): 701–714, at 703–704 (PDF). See also the overview of the manuscript at the Samuel Scroll.

Reception and debate

  • It aligns the Hebrew with the Greek and Josephus. 4QSama's "four cubits and a span" matches the Septuagint and the height Josephus reports, and stands against the later Masoretic codices. The shorter number is thus attested in Hebrew, in Greek and in Josephus.
  • Its discovery and the question. J. Daniel Hays writes that for much of the modern era the Septuagint manuscripts were viewed as secondary witnesses to the Old Testament text, and that the Dead Sea Scrolls "changed that situation," since in several cases the older Hebrew texts from Qumran supported the Septuagint against the Masoretic Text (Hays, JETS 48/4 (2005), 702). Peter Goeman, following Alexander Rofé's proposal to retitle the scroll Midrash Sefer Shemuel, holds that 4QSama "cannot be thought of as a simple Hebrew textual witness" and that "we should be careful putting too much weight on the four cubit reading" ("How Tall Was Goliath? 9 Feet or 6 Feet?", 2026). William Barrick holds the Qumran reading to be "a deliberate alteration," and Clyde Billington that it was either corrected from the Greek or copied for or by an Alexandrian Jew; see the disputed reading.
  • A text-type close to the Greek and Josephus. Ulrich concluded that the bible Josephus used for Samuel was "a slightly revised form of OG [Old Greek]" and "intimately affiliated with the 4QSama tradition" (The Qumran Text of Samuel and Josephus, 259, as quoted by Hays, 704 n. 6). Hays reads the scroll, the Old Greek and Josephus as one family of Samuel text; Goeman notes that 4QSama "will also at times agree with the MT against the LXX."
