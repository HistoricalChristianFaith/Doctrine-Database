Jerome and the Vulgate on How Tall Was Goliath?

Dates: c. 405 (the Vulgate translation of Samuel–Kings, made from the Hebrew) · Wikipedia
Summary: Translating from the Hebrew of his day, Jerome renders Goliath "six cubits and a span" — the figure of the proto-Masoretic Hebrew, roughly 9'9" — and the Vulgate carried that number to Western readers; the Greek and Qumran witnesses read "four cubits and a span" (roughly 6'9").

Claims & evidence

The Vulgate reading: "six cubits and a span"

"And there went out a man baseborn from the camp of the Philistines named Goliath, of Geth, whose height was six cubits and a span."

— Douay-Rheims, 1 Kings (= 1 Samuel) 17:4, source. The Latin of the Vulgate here reads that Goliath's height was sex cubitorum et palmo — "six cubits and a palm/span." The Douay-Rheims English follows the Vulgate directly, and so preserves Jerome's "six." At roughly a foot and a half to the cubit plus a span, that yields a giant of about nine feet nine inches.

Reception and debate

  • Jerome translated from the Hebrew. For Samuel–Kings Jerome worked from a Hebrew (proto-Masoretic) text, which read "six," and he does not follow the Greek "four" here. J. Daniel Hays describes the Vulgate as appearing "to follow the Hebrew proto-MT faithfully" at this verse ("Reconsidering the Height of Goliath," JETS 48/4 (2005), 705).
  • A note on the book title. In the Vulgate and the Douay-Rheims that follows it, the books we call 1–2 Samuel are titled 1–2 Kings (with our 1–2 Kings becoming 3–4 Kings). So the verse cited above as "1 Kings 17:4" is the same passage as 1 Samuel 17:4.
  • Where his "six" stands in the tradition. Jerome's figure aligns with the later Masoretic codices and against the shorter "four cubits and a span" of the Septuagint, the Qumran scroll 4QSama, and Josephus. Hays writes that the Vulgate "eventually became the 'received text' for the Western churches, thus codifying the 9′9″ giant into Western culture" (705).
