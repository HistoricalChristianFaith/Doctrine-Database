The Masoretic Codices on How Tall Was Goliath?

Dates: Aleppo Codex c. 930; Leningrad Codex 1008/1010 — the earliest complete Hebrew Bibles · Aleppo Codex · Leningrad Codex
Summary: The great medieval Masoretic manuscripts — the Leningrad Codex being the base text of the modern critical editions (Biblia Hebraica) — give Goliath's height as "six cubits and a span" (Hebrew shesh ammot wa-zaret, roughly 9 feet 9 inches). This is the reading standardized in the printed Hebrew Bible and inherited by the English versions that print "six" (usually with a footnote for the ancient "four"). The codices are about a thousand years younger than the Dead Sea Samuel scroll, which reads "four."

Claims & evidence

1 Samuel 17:4 in the Masoretic Text reads "six cubits and a span"

"And there went out a champion out of the camp of the Philistines, named Goliath, of Gath, whose height was six cubits and a span."

— 1 Samuel 17:4, Masoretic Text (Hebrew shesh ammot wa-zaret), Jewish Publication Society translation, source. The medieval Masoretic codices — the Aleppo Codex (c. 930) and the Leningrad Codex (1008/1010), the two oldest surviving near-complete Hebrew Bibles — carry this "six" (shesh); the Leningrad Codex has been the base text of the modern critical editions, Biblia Hebraica, since 1937, while earlier printed Hebrew Bibles were made from other manuscripts ("Leningrad Codex," Wikipedia). As J. Daniel Hays notes, the Masoretic tradition here stands against the older witnesses, which read "four"; Reconsidering the Height of Goliath, Journal of the Evangelical Theological Society 48/4 (2005): 701–714, at 705.

Reception and debate

  • The base of the printed Hebrew Bible. The Leningrad Codex is the manuscript reproduced in the standard scholarly editions of the Hebrew Bible (Biblia Hebraica), and the Aleppo Codex is the model text of others. Hays reports "six cubits and a span" in "all of the manuscripts of the MT" (705), so it is the reading of the printed Hebrew Bible and of the translations made from the Hebrew.
  • Dates of the witnesses. These codices post-date the Dead Sea Samuel scroll 4QSama by roughly a millennium; 4QSama, the Septuagint, and Josephus give Goliath's height as four cubits and a span (about 6 feet 9 inches). Hays notes that Symmachus (c. AD 200), Origen's fifth column and the Vulgate read "six," and holds that the reading stood in the proto-Masoretic text by about AD 200 (Hays, JETS 48/4 (2005), 705–706). Gurry and Meade add the Peshitta and the Targum to the witnesses for "six" ("How Tall Was Goliath? A Textual Dilemma," 2022). Which reading is earlier is set out on the disputed-reading page.
  • The "six" in Jerome and Bede. The same "six" stood in the proto-Masoretic Hebrew Jerome translated (c. 405); Hays describes the Vulgate as appearing "to follow the Hebrew proto-MT faithfully" (705). Bede (c. 716) comments on the Vulgate's "six," reading the six cubits as a figure of the devil's boast "to be similar to the Most High."
