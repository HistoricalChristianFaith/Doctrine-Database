Jerome on the name of Gethsemane

Dates: c. 347 – 420 (the Commentary on Matthew, 398) · Wikipedia
Summary: The great Latin translator, resident in Palestine, who rendered Eusebius's gazetteer into Latin and supplied the church's standard — and arguably mistaken — etymology of the name Gethsemane.

Claim & evidence

“Gethsemane means ‘very fertile valley’” (398)

Then Jesus came with them to a village called Gethsemane and said to his disciples, ‘Sit here while I go over there and pray.’ Gethsemane means ‘very fertile valley,’ in which he ordered the disciples to sit for a little while and wait for the Lord to return, while he prayed for everyone alone.

— Jerome, Commentary on Matthew 26:36. The same rendering — “Gethsemane is interpreted, ‘The rich valley’” — is preserved in Aquinas's Catena Aurea on Matthew 26.

What it attests — and what it does not

Jerome's gloss is philology, not topography. He does not locate the site — he had already carried Eusebius's placement (foot of the Mount of Olives) into Latin when he translated the Onomasticon — but he fixes what the church would take the name to mean. And the reading he fixes is probably the wrong one: a Semitic gat shemanim is far more naturally “oil-press” (the installation you would expect on an olive-covered slope) than “richest valley.” The mistake is instructive, not idle: it is Jerome's “valley of fatness” that the medieval commentators — Bede, Rabanus, the Glossa — run with, reading the place-name allegorically as the valley of humility and the fatness of charity in which Christ prayed before his Passion. The name became a sermon; the location it actually encodes (an oil-press at the foot of the mount) is the one the archaeology of the grotto later recovered.

Notes

  • Jerome is a recurring Passion-topography witness on this wiki: he supplies the fullest report of Hadrian's suppression of the tomb of Jesus and the “without the gate” test for Golgotha.
  • His Latin recension of Eusebius's Onomasticon (the Liber locorum) is how the earliest gazetteer placement of Gethsemane reached the Latin West.
