Bede on Gethsemane “shown to this day”

Dates: c. 672/673 – 735 (the Commentary on Mark, c. 720s–730) · Wikipedia
Summary: The Northumbrian monk of Wearmouth–Jarrow, who never saw the Holy Land, transmits the pilgrim reports of his day and states plainly that the Gethsemane site — with a church over it — was still shown in the eighth century.

Claim & evidence

“The place of Gethsemane is shown to this day… now a church built above it” (c. 720s)

And they come to a place called Gethsemane. The place of Gethsemane is shown to this day, where the Lord prayed, at the foot of the Mount of Olives, now a church built above it. However, Gethsemane is interpreted as the valley of the fat ones or the fatness.

— Bede, Commentary on Mark 14:32 (the abridged form is also carried in Aquinas's Catena Aurea on Mark 14). Bede goes on to read the “valley of fatness” allegorically, following Jerome — a sermon on humility and charity — but the topographical sentence is the datum.

What it attests — and what it does not

Bede is not an eyewitness — he compiled his Holy-Land notices from earlier pilgrim accounts (chiefly Adomnán's De locis sanctis, itself reporting the Gaulish bishop Arculf) — and the value of his line is precisely that it is a report of a report: it shows the identification unbroken from Egeria's fourth-century church down to the eighth. Three things travel together in his sentence and match the older witnesses exactly: the site is shown (a living, pointed-out place), it is at the foot of the Mount of Olives (Eusebius's placement), and a church stands over it (Egeria's ecclesia elegans, still standing). What Bede does not add is any independent verification; he is a faithful conduit, and his own interest, as the second half of the passage shows, is the allegory of the name, not the survey of the ground.

Notes

  • A century later Rabanus Maurus repeats the same report — harmonizing Luke's mount, John's Kidron garden, and Matthew's Gethsemane, and confirming “a Church now built” — showing the memory carried on through the ninth century.
  • Bede appears elsewhere on this wiki as a witness on the perpetual-virginity timeline; here he is a link in the chain of continuity for the Gethsemane site.
