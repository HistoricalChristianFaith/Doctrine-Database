John Chrysostom on the brook Cedron and the garden

Dates: c. 347 – 407 (the Homilies on John, c. 391, preached at Antioch) · Wikipedia
Summary: The great Antiochene preacher, expounding John's account, reads the garden across the Kidron as a real and habitual place — a known haunt of Jesus and his disciples, not a hiding-place — the eastern homiletic witness to John's topography of the arrest.

Claim & evidence

“He… departed beyond the brook Cedron, where was a garden” (c. 391)

“These things,” it saith, “Jesus spake, and departed beyond the brook Cedron, where was a garden, into the which He entered, and His disciples.” He journeyeth at midnight, and crosseth a river, and hasteth to come to a place known to the traitor, lessening the labor to those who plotted against Him, and freeing them from all trouble; and showeth to the disciples that He came willingly to the action… and placeth Himself in the garden as in a prison.

“Judas knew the place… He often resorted thither”

But how came Judas there, or whence had he gained his information when he came? It is evident from this circumstance, that Jesus generally passed the night out of doors… And lest, hearing of a “garden,” thou shouldest think that Jesus hid Himself, it addeth, that “Judas knew the place”; and not simply so, but that He “often resorted thither with His disciples.”… And He did this especially in mountains and gardens, seeking a place free from disturbance, that their attention might not be distracted from listening.

— John Chrysostom, Homilies on John 83 (on John 18:1–2).

What it attests — and what it does not

Chrysostom is expounding the Gospel, not the shown site, and he adds no localization beyond what John supplies — he never names Gethsemane, the Mount of Olives, or a church. What he does add is a preacher's insistence that the garden across the Kidron was a real and habitual place: a spot known to Judas because Jesus “often resorted thither,” passing the night out of doors, choosing “mountains and gardens” for undisturbed teaching. That reading — the garden as a familiar haunt, entered openly “as in a prison,” not a hiding-place — is exactly the picture John 18:2 gives (“Jesus ofttimes resorted thither with his disciples”), and it is the eastern counterpart to the western topographers on this timeline: where Eusebius and the pilgrims fix where, Chrysostom presses that the garden was a known, regularly-used place east of the city across the winter torrent. It is testimony to the character of the place in the Gospel, corroborating John's Kidron-and-garden setting, rather than an independent identification of the plot.

Notes

  • Chrysostom appears elsewhere on this wiki as an Old Testament–canon witness and as a principal of the Theophilus–Chrysostom relationship timeline; here he is a witness to the topography of the arrest.
  • The “brook Cedron” (the Kidron valley) is the watercourse that separates the city from the western foot of the Mount of Olives — the crossing John names and Chrysostom dramatizes as a midnight passage “to a place known to the traitor.”
