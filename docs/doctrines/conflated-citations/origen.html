Origen on Matthew’s “Jeremiah” for words found in Zechariah

Dates: c. 246 (the Commentary on Matthew, written at Caesarea late in life; Origen c. 185–254) · Wikipedia
Summary: Origen is the earliest extant writer on this timeline to comment on Matthew 27:9’s ascription of a Zechariah oracle to “Jeremiah the prophet” and to state that the words are not found in Jeremiah. Commenting on the thirty-pieces passage, he notes that the quotation “is not found to have been prophesied by Jeremiah anywhere in his books,” whether the copies read in the churches or those recited among the Jews, and offers two conjectures: an error in the text (“Jeremiah is put for Zechariah”) or a “secret writing of Jeremiah.” He develops the second.

Claims & evidence

The words not found in Jeremiah: two conjectures

Working through the account of Judas’s remorse and the potter’s field, Origen reaches the fulfilment formula and states that its words cannot be located in the prophet Matthew names. He sets out two possibilities and invites correction (“if anyone can know, let him show where it is written”):

But since what the evangelist says after these things (then was fulfilled that which had been spoken by Jeremiah the prophet, saying the things which are written) — this is not found to have been prophesied by Jeremiah anywhere in his books, whether those which are read in the churches or those which are recited among the Jews — but if anyone can know, let him show where it is written. I suspect, however, that either it is an error of the Scripture and Jeremiah is put for Zechariah, or that there is some secret writing of Jeremiah in which it is written.

Origen then quotes the Zechariah oracle in full (“they appointed my wages, thirty pieces of silver… and I took the thirty pieces of silver, and put them into the house of the Lord”), setting the Zechariah wording before the reader.1

The defence of the “secret writing” option

Of his two conjectures, Origen develops the second — that Matthew drew on a “secret writing of Jeremiah” outside the public books — by appeal to the apostolic precedent of citing such sources:

But if anyone saying this supposes that he stumbles, let him see whether this may be prophesied somewhere in the secret writings of Jeremiah, knowing that the Apostle too brings forward certain writings of the secret books, as he says somewhere: “what eye has not seen nor ear heard”; for in no canonical book is this found set down, except in the secret writings of Elijah the prophet. Likewise what he says: “as Jamnes and Mambres withstood Moses” is not found in the public books, but in the secret book which is entitled the book of Jamnes and Mambres.

— Origen, Commentary on Matthew, Commentariorum Series §117 (on Matthew 27:3–10). He treats Paul’s citation of non-canonical material at 1 Corinthians 2:9 (which he assigns to a “secret writing of Elijah”) and at 2 Timothy 3:8 (Jannes and Jambres) as warrant that an Evangelist could likewise quote a lost book of Jeremiah.2

Placement on the timeline

  • Origen and Eusebius. Origen (c. 246) wrote some two generations before Eusebius (c. 315), who develops the copyist conjecture at length. The lost-book answer appears later, for Matthew 2:23, in Chrysostom and Theophylact. On the summary timeline Origen stands between Irenaeus (c. 180) and Eusebius. See Naming the Prophet.
  • The Matthew 27:9 crux. Matthew’s “Jeremy” is also treated on Judas’s biographical timeline.
