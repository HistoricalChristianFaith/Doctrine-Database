Josephus on the Dating of the Flood

Dates: c. 37–100 (the Antiquities of the Jews composed c. AD 93) · Wikipedia
Summary: Retelling the primeval history in Greek for a Roman readership, Josephus closes the antediluvian genealogy with an explicit interval from Adam to the Flood. The transmitted figure — "two thousand six hundred and fifty-six years" (Antiquities 1.82) — varies in the manuscripts, and Josephus' own summed patriarch-ages come to 2,256, roughly six centuries longer than the Masoretic 1,656 and close to the Septuagint's long chronology. He agrees with the Hebrew and Samaritan against the Greek in omitting the second Cainan of Genesis 11, and his figures do not agree with their own transmitted total.

Claims & evidence

The Flood "two thousand six hundred and fifty-six years from Adam" — a figure in crux

…this flood began on the twenty-seventh [seventeenth] day of the forementioned month: and this was two thousand six hundred and fifty six [one thousand five hundred and fifty six years] from Adam the first man…

— Antiquities of the Jews, I.iii.3 (§82), Whiston translation, source (the bracketed figures are Whiston's note of the manuscript variants). The transmitted 2656 is textually unstable — manuscripts and editors give 2262, 1656, and 1056 as well. Josephus' own list of begetting-ages, when summed, comes to 2,256. Isaac Vossius, in his De vera aetate mundi (1659), emended §82 to 2,256 to bring Josephus into line with the Septuagint against the received text — an emendation with no support in the printed Greek editions, and the figure Josephus' own ages sum to.

The patriarch-ages follow the Septuagint's longer numbers

Whatever total is read, Josephus' individual antediluvian begetting-ages carry the same centenary additions that distinguish the Septuagint's long chronology from the Hebrew: his interval from Adam to the Flood runs roughly six hundred years longer than the Masoretic 1,656 and lands fourteen years from the Septuagint's 2,242. His figures are cited as evidence that the longer Genesis 5 figures were current among first-century Jews, in the dispute over whether the received Hebrew was later shortened (see the transmission-of-the-text page). Felix Schlichter traces both the arithmetic (the ages summing to 2,256) and the early-modern controversy it fuelled — Vossius' campaign to prove Josephus followed the Septuagint throughout — in his study of Josephus in early modern biblical chronology.

The post-Flood line without the second Cainan of Genesis 11

…Sala was the son of Arphaxad: and his son was Heber…

— Antiquities of the Jews, I.vi.4, Whiston translation, source. Josephus runs the post-Flood line straight from Arphaxad to Sala (Salah), with no Cainan between them. This is the Hebrew and Samaritan sequence; the Septuagint's Genesis 11 inserts a second Cainan there, and Luke 3:36 repeats it. Josephus thus follows the Greek's longer numbers and the Hebrew list.

Reception and debate

  • The figures among the reckonings. Josephus' figures are, in Whiston's phrase, "a law unto themselves" (⚠ unverified): they differ from the Septuagint, from the Masoretic Hebrew, and from their own transmitted sum (2656 vs. the 2256 the ages sum to). The Masoretic, Septuagint, and Samaritan reckonings are set out on the timeline, with the Samaritan question on its interpretation page.
  • Josephus appears also as a witness on the Nephilim, First Language, Old Testament Canon, Origin of the Septuagint, and Pillars of Seth timelines — the first four, with this one, all drawn from his primeval history in Antiquities Book I — and on the Star Prophecy timeline, where his referral of the "ambiguous oracle" of a world-ruler to Vespasian (Jewish War 6.312–313) is treated.
