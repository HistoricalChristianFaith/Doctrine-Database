Does the Samaritan Pentateuch preserve the original chronology of Genesis 5 and 11?

Disputed reading · on The Dating of Noah's Flood · witness: the Samaritan Pentateuch's figures for Genesis 5

The question of which text-form is prior also arises on the Septuagint's origin and on the charge that the Hebrew figures were later shortened.

The passage

And Jared lived an hundred sixty and two years, and he begat Enoch:

And Methuselah lived an hundred eighty and seven years, and begat Lamech:

And Lamech lived an hundred eighty and two years, and begat a son:

— Genesis 5:18, 25, 28 (King James Version, from the Hebrew), source.1 The Samaritan Pentateuch reads Genesis 5 like the Hebrew except at these three begetting-ages: Jared 62 rather than 162, Methuselah 67 rather than 187, Lamech 53 rather than 182. Summed with Noah's 600 years at the Flood, its figures give 1,307 years from Creation to the Flood, against the Masoretic 1,656 and the Septuagint's c. 2,242. In Genesis 11 the Samaritan text adds a hundred years to several begetting-ages, as the Septuagint does, so that its Adam-to-Abraham total (about 2,249 years) falls between the Masoretic (c. 2,008) and the Septuagint (c. 3,394). Its text-form is dated to c. 100 BC or earlier by the Armstrong Institute of Biblical Archaeology, which cites its short antediluvian ages in the separate dispute over whether the Hebrew figures were shortened in the rabbinic era.2

The question

Whether the three Samaritan begetting-ages of Genesis 5 preserve the original antediluvian figures, from which the Hebrew (and the Greek) derive, or are a later revision of an older set. The answer bears on which of the three reckonings of the interval from Creation to the Flood is the earliest; none of the scholars named on this page converts the Samaritan 1,307 years into a calendar date for the Flood.

The Samaritan figures are the original system

On this reading the Samaritan numbers are the source, and the Hebrew and Greek figures are later alterations of them.

Held by: Ernst Bertheau (1878); A. Jepsen (1929); Gösta Larsson (1973); the paper "Samaritan Chronology of Antediluvian Patriarchs in Genesis 5 and Prime Numbers" (Academia.edu, 2018).3

Its holders start from the internal order of the Samaritan figures. Bertheau observed that they form a self-consistent arithmetical system: "one adds from the flood the years of age when the first son was born and arrives at the total age of the respective patriarch," a regularity that holds only on the Samaritan numbers, and he inferred that the Samaritan text is prior to the Hebrew. Jepsen argued the same priority, holding that the Hebrew has "the simpler figures." Larsson put the point in terms of direction of change: "an alteration from the almost perfect Samaritan system to the irregular Hebrew system seems quite incredible." On this view a clean, interlocking scheme is the likelier source, and the Hebrew's figures for Jared (162), Methuselah (187) and Lamech (182) are what remains after that scheme was disturbed.4

The 2018 paper restates the case in number-theoretic terms. Counting Adam and Eve's seven years in Eden, it finds that the Samaritan antediluvian dates of birth and death are almost all prime numbers, the Flood year (Anno Mundi 1,307) among them; it holds that "such combination cannot occur by chance" and that the prime scheme "should be assumed as the progenitor one," so that "the chronology of the Masoretic text was developed from the Samaritan one and not vice versa." The paper treats the chronology as a "carefully constructed artificial" system, and reads that design as the mark of the original.5

The Samaritan figures are a later revision

On this reading the three lower Samaritan begetting-ages were cut from an older set of figures so that three patriarchs would die in the year of the Flood.

Held by: Gerhard F. Hasel (Origins, 1980); Henry B. Smith Jr. (Answers Research Journal, 2017); Lita Sanders (Answers Research Journal, 2025).6

Its holders start from where the three departures fall. On the Samaritan figures Jared (born Anno Mundi 460, lifespan 847), Methuselah (born 587, lifespan 720) and Lamech (born 654, lifespan 653) each die in Anno Mundi 1,307, the Flood year. Hasel describes the change: "The Samaritan text reduced the great ages for Jared and Lamech by 100 years when they begat their first sons and by 120 years in the case of Methuselah… most of all to have them die before the flood. In the Samaritan system Jared, Methuselah and Lamech died together in the year of the flood." He applies the principle "that the irregular is prior," since "there is no reason why something regular is to become irregular": "the MT has a non-schematic presentation of figures, whereas the Samaritan Pentateuch and the LXX version give evidence of schematizations," and "the Hebrew text contains the original figures when compared to those in the Samaritan Pentateuch." Hasel reports this as the scholarly consensus, that "the Hebrew text has preserved the original figures in their purest form," and writes that Jepsen's Samaritan-priority view "has not found any following."7

Smith traces the revised figures to the Book of Jubilees (c. 160 BC): "The entire antediluvian chronology of the SP mirrors the chronology found in Jubilees," whose jubilee scheme "also forced the author to alter the remaining years and lifespans" of Jared, Methuselah and Lamech, and "There is no discernable reason for the SP to have been altered exactly in this same manner, except to bring it in line with the antediluvian chronology of Jubilees." Sanders treats the Samaritan text as a recension that truncates the remaining lifespans of the same three patriarchs, and counts it among "secondary texts that are clearly recensions." On this reading the Samaritan figures record a deliberate revision of the primeval chronology, with the earlier numbers kept in the Hebrew (Hasel, Sanders) or in the Greek (Smith).8,9
