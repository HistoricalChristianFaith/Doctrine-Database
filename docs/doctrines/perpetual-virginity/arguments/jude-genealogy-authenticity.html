Does the Enochic structure of the Lukan genealogy link it to Jude’s circle, and does that bear on the letter’s authorship?

Disputed reading · on the perpetual virginity of Mary · witness: the Epistle of Jude and the genealogy of Luke 3:23–38

Related readings: Does the Epistle of Jude’s pesher-style exegesis bear on its authorship by Jude the Lord’s brother? · Does Jude 9’s allusion to the Testament of Moses bear on the letter’s authorship? · Does the letter’s title “Master” (despotēs) for Christ, beside the relatives’ name desposynoi, bear on its authorship?

The passage

And Jesus himself began to be about thirty years of age, being (as was supposed) the son of Joseph, which was the son of Heli, … Which was the son of Mathusala, which was the son of Enoch, which was the son of Jared, which was the son of Maleleel, which was the son of Cainan, Which was the son of Enos, which was the son of Seth, which was the son of Adam, which was the son of God.

— Luke 3:23, 37–38 (KJV), source.1

And the angels which kept not their first estate, but left their own habitation, he hath reserved in everlasting chains under darkness unto the judgment of the great day.

And Enoch also, the seventh from Adam, prophesied of these, saying, Behold, the Lord cometh with ten thousands of his saints,

— Jude 6 and 14 (KJV), source.1

The question

Richard Bauckham argued that Luke’s genealogy of Jesus counts seventy-seven generations from Adam on an Enochic scheme, with Enoch seventh and Jesus last, and that the two Enoch data the scheme needs, the “great day” of the Watchers’ judgment (1 Enoch 10:12; Jude 6) and Enoch “the seventh from Adam” (Jude 14), both appear in the Epistle of Jude, so that the genealogy comes from Jude’s circle. What is disputed is whether the genealogy carries that deliberate seventy-seven-generation structure, linking it to the circle of the Lord’s relatives and through it to the letter, or whether the count of names is too uncertain to bear it. On the letter’s authorship more broadly, Raymond E. Brown (1997) reported “debate about Jas and Jude, with the majority favoring pseudonymity”; a response to the genealogy argument from those who hold the letter pseudonymous was not located.2

A seventy-seven-generation scheme from the circle of Jude

In brief: The genealogy was composed in Jesus’ own generation on an Enochic pattern by the relatives of Jesus, most probably in Jude’s circle, and the letter of Jude supplies the link.

Held by: Richard Bauckham (Anglican, Jude and the Relatives of Jesus in the Early Church, 1990; restated in a 2013 comment).3

Bauckham holds that the genealogy “derives, in virtually the form that Luke has preserved, from the circle of the first generation of the desposynoi.”4 It has seventy-seven generations, a number of “ultimacy” as in the song of Lamech (Genesis 4:24). Enoch, “well-known to be the seventh generation from Adam,” closes the first week; 1 Enoch 10:12 binds the Watchers “for seventy generations… until the great day of their judgment”; so “the whole of world history from Adam to the Last Judgment comprises seventy-seven generations, seven up to and including Enoch, followed by a further seventy,” and Jesus stands in the last. On the seventy-seven-name text Jesus also stands forty-ninth, which “strongly favours the view that the seventy-seven name list was a deliberately composed one.”5 The scheme “must have been composed in this form during the generation of Jesus’ contemporaries,” and since Luke shows no other interest in Enoch, “it is rather doubtful whether he even perceived the Enochic structure of the genealogy.”6

“Only one other extant Christian writing from that period alludes indisputably to the Enoch literature: the letter of Jude,” and “in the mere twenty-five verses of his letter Jude makes contact with the two most essential foundations of the sabbatical structure of the genealogy.” Bauckham concludes: “The letter of Jude, as an authentic work of the brother of Jesus, provides uniquely the link we need to locate the origin of the Lukan genealogy in early Christianity… If the genealogy is not the work of Jude himself, it must certainly come from his circle.”7 In 2013 he described it as “very plausibly a traditional family genealogy that has been manipulated a bit to fit a numerical scheme (as often in OT and other ancient genealogies) of 77 generations,” the genealogy used by the relatives who, according to Julius Africanus, expounded their family record on their travels.3 On this reading Luke preserves a document of the Lord’s own family, and the letter of Jude shows the learning that produced it.

The count of names is a text-critical question first

In brief: The number of names in Luke’s list varies in the manuscripts, and the seventy-seven count that the Enochic scheme requires is one text-form among several.

Held by: Peter M. Head (evangelical, Evangelical Textual Criticism, 2008).8

Head sets out the manuscript evidence: “Sinaiticus has 77 names; Alexandrinus has 74 names (W and 579 omit the whole thing).” The printed critical text follows the longer count: “NA27 prints a text with 77 names, this has the advantage of neatness (significant sevens all over the place; cf. Bauckham in Jude and the Relatives of Jesus); but the disadvantage that this neatness is pretty much imposed on the textual evidence (esp. at 3.33 reading Aminadab, Admin, Arni).” Bauckham notes that the seventy-seven figure “is found in most but not all manuscripts. Some have fewer names, ranging from seventy-two to seventy-six.”6

The earliest extant reader of the count found a different number and a different meaning in it. Irenaeus wrote that “Luke points out that the pedigree which traces the generation of our Lord back to Adam contains seventy-two generations, connecting the end with the beginning, and implying that it is He who has summed up in Himself all nations dispersed from Adam downwards.”9 Head asks whether “the textual evidence is too uncertain to allow far-reaching theological deductions from the ennumeration,” or whether “the theological neatness” should “help us determine the original text.” On this reading the genealogy’s number has to be settled at Luke 3:33 before any scheme, Enochic or other, is read from it.
