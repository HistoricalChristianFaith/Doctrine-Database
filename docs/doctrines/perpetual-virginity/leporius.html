Leporius on the perpetual virginity of Mary

Dates: c. 426 (Leporius, a Gallic monk and priest, fl. early 5th century. Having taught that Christ endured His human life and Passion by the power of His manhood alone, without the help of His deity, he was expelled from Gaul, made his way to Africa, and there was reconciled through Aurelius of Carthage and Augustine of Hippo. His recantation, the Libellus emendationis or Document of Amendment, was drawn up in Africa c. 426 and sent back to the Gallic bishops Proculus of Marseille and Cillenius) · Wikipedia
Summary: Leporius is not arguing the brethren-question, nor even the perpetual virginity as such — his subject is the union of the two natures in Christ. But in the confession of faith that anchors his retraction he names the Lord’s mother “the ever-virgin Mary” (Latin semper virgo), using the title within a confession of faith rather than arguing for it. The document was drawn up in Africa under Aurelius and Augustine and subscribed as the condition of Leporius’s reconciliation.

Claims & evidence

“The ever-virgin Mary” as a creedal clause in a retraction

The Libellus emendationis is a formal recantation: Leporius withdraws his earlier teaching about the person of Christ and sets down, in its place, the confession required of him. Rehearsing the two births of the one Son — eternal from the Father, temporal from Mary — he writes:

We confess, therefore, that our Lord and God, Jesus Christ, the only Son of God, born of the Father before the ages, and in times most recent, made man of the Holy Spirit and the ever-virgin Mary…

— Leporius, Document of Amendment (Libellus emendationis) 3 (c. 426).1 The clause tracks the incarnational formula of the creed (“incarnate by the Holy Spirit of the Virgin Mary”), with “Virgin” given as “ever-virgin,” the perpetual-virginity title.

The setting of the title

The title occurs in a disciplinary document rather than a treatise on Mary: a confession drafted under the supervision of Aurelius and Augustine and offered as proof of a corrected faith. It dates from a generation after Helvidius had raised the question at Rome (c. 383), and from the years of Augustine’s own mature affirmations of the perpetual virginity.

What the confession addresses

Because Leporius affirms only the title and no account of the Lord’s brethren, his witness bears on the perpetual virginity itself and not on the three readings of the brethren. Like Athanasius, Didymus, and Chromatius before him, he attests that Mary was and remained ever-virgin and does not address the Epiphanian/Hieronymian question.

Reception and context

  • A Christological document. The Libellus is a Christological recantation. Its concern is the inseparable union of Christ’s deity and humanity, against the views Leporius had held; the Marian title occurs within that confession.
  • The Latin title. The confession renders the Lord’s mother semper virgo (“ever-virgin”) — the Latin equivalent of the Greek aēiparthenos used of Mary by Athanasius and other Greek writers.
  • Text and date. The Libellus emendationis is printed in Migne, Patrologia Latina 31, cols. 1221–1230, and critically edited by R. Demeulenaere in Corpus Christianorum, Series Latina 64 (Turnhout: Brepols, 1985). Composed in Africa c. 426 during Leporius’ reconciliation under Aurelius and Augustine, it was sent to the Gallic bishops Proculus of Marseille and Cillenius; John Cassian later praised the retraction in his On the Incarnation.
