Eunomius of Cyzicus on the perpetual virginity of Mary

Dates: c. 360 (Eunomius, c. 325–c. 393, was consecrated bishop of Cyzicus about 360 and soon deposed; the “c. 360” anchors his episcopate and the height of his influence, not the report of his Mariology, which reaches us only through a commentary written a generation later) · Wikipedia
Summary: Eunomius of Cyzicus was a leader of the Anomoeans — the party, also called “Neo-Arian,” that held the Son unlike (anomoios) the Father in essence. On the perpetual virginity he is reported to have taught the Helvidian reading — that after Jesus’ birth Joseph “knew” Mary and she bore further children. The report is secondhand and comes from an opponent: it comes from the anonymous, Arian-leaning Incomplete Commentary on Matthew (Opus imperfectum, c. 400), which names the view only to reject it, and nothing on Mary survives from Eunomius’s own hand. His teaching on the point is therefore marked ⚠ unverified. The report places a named fourth-century holder of that reading in the Greek East, beside the Roman Helvidius and the Arabian Antidicomarianites.

Claims & evidence

A Helvidian reading: Mary bore children to Joseph after Jesus (⚠ unverified)

The attribution rests on a single passage. Expounding “he knew her not until she had given birth to a son” (Matthew 1:25), the author of the Opus imperfectum pauses over the inference some drew from “until,” and traces it to Eunomius:

Therefore he said, “But he knew her not until she had given birth to a son.” He knew who she was after his birth. From this statement, some people, following Eunomius, think that until Mary gave birth Joseph did not have carnal relations with her, but afterwards he knew her and she gave birth to children.

— Incomplete Commentary on Matthew (Opus imperfectum), Homily 1, on Matthew 1:25.1 The position described — Joseph abstained only until the birth, then knew Mary, who bore children — is the Helvidian reading, resting on the same reading of Matthew 1:25’s “until” that Helvidius pressed at Rome about the same time. The commentator reports it in order to reject it: he takes “firstborn” in the sense “whom no sibling followed,” and holds that Joseph, once he knew who Mary was, would not have touched her. The notice is thus an opponent’s report; because it is the only witness to Eunomius’ view, and no primary from Eunomius survives, the claim is marked ⚠ unverified.

Eunomius’ career and surviving writings

Eunomius (c. 325–c. 393), a Cappadocian by birth and pupil and secretary of Aetius, became the systematic theologian of the Anomoeans, who taught that the Son is “unlike” the Father in essence. Consecrated bishop of Cyzicus about 360, he was soon accused and deposed, and spent the rest of his life leading his party from exile; his followers were known as Eunomians.2 Basil of Caesarea and Gregory of Nyssa answered him at length — Basil’s Against Eunomius and Gregory’s long work of the same name — but that controversy turned on the doctrine of God, the generation of the Son, and the knowability of the divine essence, not on Mary. Of Eunomius’ own writings the Apology survives entire, the Apology for the Apology only in the fragments Gregory quotes, and a later Confession of Faith; none of them treats the virginity of Mary post partum.2 The Helvidian teaching is thus attached to his name only by the Opus imperfectum, a source written about the time of his death and opposed to the view; the timeline lists him on that report, which cannot be checked against his own words.

Reception and context

  • Secondhand attestation. Everything here rests on one passage in a commentary that names the view to reject it. Because the claim rests only on a later report by an opponent, Eunomius’ teaching on the point is marked ⚠ unverified; what is verified is that the Opus imperfectum ascribes the Helvidian reading of Matthew 1:25 to “those following Eunomius.”
  • Where he stands among the readings. As reported, Eunomius is a Helvidian witness — Mary bore later children, so she was not ever-virgin. He stands, in the Greek East, beside the Roman Helvidius, the Arabian Antidicomarianites, and the Latin Bonosus and Jovinian; most other witnesses on this timeline hold the Epiphanian or Hieronymian readings. The one notice that reports his view does not connect it with his teaching on the Son.
  • The proof-text. The reading, as reported, turns on Matthew 1:25’s “knew her not until” (Greek heōs hou), the text Helvidius also cited.
