Gleason Archer on Ahaziah's age

Dates: 1982 (the Encyclopedia of Bible Difficulties) · Wikipedia
Summary: In the Encyclopedia of Bible Difficulties Archer treats the pair as a numerical copying-error: for him the correct age is the twenty-two of Kings, and the "forty-two" of Chronicles arose in the number-notation of Ezra's day, where a decade-mark could drop out.

Claims & evidence

Archer on the correct age

Archer sets out the arithmetic: 2 Kings 8:17 makes Ahaziah's father Joram thirty-two at his accession, dying eight years later at forty, so Ahaziah "could not have been forty-two at the time of his father's death at age forty." For Archer the lower figure is the correct one, and the "forty-two" of Chronicles a transcription error.1

Archer's proposed mechanism: a lost decade-mark in the old number-notation

Archer grounds the slip in the way numbers were written in the fifth century BC, when Archer dates the composition of Chronicles. In that notation a decade was marked by a horizontal stroke ending in a hook, the units by little vertical strokes; if a hook were "smudged or flaked off" the papyrus, a figure could shift by ten — or, by a related mishap, be multiplied. On this account the copyist did not misread a word but lost or added a decade-mark, turning an original "twenty(-two)" into "forty-two."2

Reception and debate

  • A different mechanism from Keil & Delitzsch. Where Keil & Delitzsch locate the slip in the alphabetic numeral-letters (כ 20 misread as מ 40), Archer locates it in the older stroke-and-hook system. The two accounts differ on how the error happened and agree that it happened and that the original was in the low twenties.
  • Archer's framing. Archer treats numeral corruption as the ordinary hazard of hand-copied figures, recoverable because a second witness with the smaller figure survives — here the parallel in Kings and the ancient versions. He makes the same kind of argument on Solomon's stalls ("multiplication by ten"). Other readings of the figure are set out on the disputed reading page.
