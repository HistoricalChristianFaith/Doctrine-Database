Gleason Archer on Ahaziah's age

Dates: 1982 (the Encyclopedia of Bible Difficulties) · Wikipedia
Summary: Twentieth-century conservative apologetics adopts the textual explanation as its own. Archer treats the pair as a model numerical copying-error: the correct age is the twenty-two of Kings, and the "forty-two" of Chronicles arose in the number-notation of Ezra's day, where a decade-mark could drop out.

Claims & evidence

The correct age is twenty-two; "forty-two" is a copyist's slip

Archer states the arithmetic plainly: 2 Kings 8:17 makes Ahaziah's father Joram thirty-two at his accession, dying eight years later at forty, so Ahaziah "could not have been forty-two at the time of his father's death at age forty." The lower figure is the correct one, and the "forty-two" of Chronicles a transcription error.1

The mechanism: a lost decade-mark in the old number-notation

Archer grounds the slip in the way numbers were written in the fifth century BC, when Chronicles was composed. In that notation a decade was marked by a horizontal stroke ending in a hook, the units by little vertical strokes; if a hook were "smudged or flaked off" the papyrus, a figure could shift by ten — or, by a related mishap, be multiplied. On this account the copyist did not misread a word but lost or added a decade-mark, turning an original "twenty(-two)" into "forty-two."2

Notes

  • A rival mechanism to Keil & Delitzsch, the same verdict. Where Keil & Delitzsch locate the slip in the alphabetic numeral-letters (כ 20 misread as מ 40), Archer locates it in the older stroke-and-hook system. Both are reconstructions of how the error happened; both agree on that it happened and that the original was in the low twenties. The disagreement over mechanism is a minor one beside the settled fact of corruption.
  • The apologetic frame. Archer treats numeral corruption as the ordinary hazard of hand-copied figures, recoverable precisely because a second, un-inflated witness survives — here the parallel in Kings and the ancient versions. It is the same move he makes on Solomon's stalls ("multiplication by ten"); the difference is that here the external witnesses do not split, so the recovery is cleaner. The reading is weighed on the argument page.
