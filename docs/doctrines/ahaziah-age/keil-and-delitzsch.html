Keil & Delitzsch on Ahaziah's age

Dates: 1872 (the Biblical Commentary on the Old Testament) · Wikipedia
Summary: Keil & Delitzsch's commentary, on 2 Chronicles 22:2, calls the "forty-two" an orthographical error for "twenty-two," located in a confusion of the Hebrew numeral-letters.

Claims & evidence

"An orthographical error for 22"

"The number 42 is an orthographical error for 22… 2 Kings 8:26."

— Keil & Delitzsch, Biblical Commentary on the Old Testament, on 2 Chronicles 22:2. They locate the slip in the Hebrew numeral-letters — "twenty" (כ, kaph) written or read as "forty" (מ, mem) — so that the corruption is mechanical, not a matter of the Chronicler holding a different figure. On their reading Kings keeps the original number and Chronicles the copyist's slip.

John Gill on the same verse

"it seems best to acknowledge a mistake of the copier, which might easily be made through a similarity of the numeral letters, forty two, for twenty two."

— John Gill, Exposition of the Old and New Testament, on 2 Chronicles 22:2. Gill, canvassing the same options as Poole, comes down on the textual side: the numeral was miscopied. The Cambridge Bible, on 2 Kings 8:26, says the figure "cannot be correct"; Barnes ("For '42' read '22'") and the Pulpit Commentary ("read, twenty and two") also read "twenty-two."

Reception and debate

  • Jehoram's age. Keil & Delitzsch ground their judgement on Jehoram's age: "As Joram was thirty-two years of age at his accession, and reigned eight years (2 Chronicles 21:20 and 2 Chronicles 21:5), at his death his youngest son could not be older than twenty-one or twenty-two years of age" (on 2 Chronicles 22:2).
  • Other readings. Archer's alternative mechanism, a lost decade-mark in an older stroke notation, and other readings of the figure are set out on the disputed reading page.
