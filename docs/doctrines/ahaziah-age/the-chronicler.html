The Chronicler on Ahaziah's age

Dates: c. 400 BC (the composition of 1–2 Chronicles) · Wikipedia
Summary: The book whose Masoretic text reads that Ahaziah was forty-two at his accession (2 Chronicles 22:2), two verses after giving his father Jehoram an accession at thirty-two and a reign of eight years. The figure has been read as the years of the house of Omri (Poole), as the age at a second accession (the Geneva Bible notes), and as a copyist's corruption of "twenty-two" (Gill, Keil & Delitzsch, Archer).

Claims & evidence

The received Hebrew reads "forty-two"

"Forty and two years old was Ahaziah when he began to reign, and he reigned one year in Jerusalem. His mother's name also was Athaliah the daughter of Omri."

— 2 Chronicles 22:2 (KJV), source. The verse is the Chronicler's parallel to 2 Kings 8:26, which reads "two and twenty years old." Both notices give the same one-year reign and the same mother; only the age differs, and by exactly twenty.

Jehoram's age in Chronicles

"Thirty and two years old was he when he began to reign, and he reigned in Jerusalem eight years, and departed without being desired."

— 2 Chronicles 21:20 (KJV), source, on Ahaziah's father Jehoram — king at thirty-two, reigning eight years. Keil & Delitzsch reason from these figures that "at his death his youngest son could not be older than twenty-one or twenty-two years of age"; the Geneva Bible notes read the "one year in Jerusalem" of 22:2 as "after the death of his father" (both on 2 Chronicles 22:2).

Reception and debate

  • Composition and transmission. The Chronicler wrote after the exile (conventionally c. 400 BC), and his account parallels Samuel–Kings, whose figure is "twenty-two." Gill, Keil & Delitzsch and Archer hold that a copyist of Chronicles, not the Chronicler, produced the "forty-two," citing the father's age and the versions. See the disputed reading page.
  • Other readings. Matthew Poole keeps the "forty-two" as written by taking it to count the years of the Omride dynasty (or, as an alternative, his mother Athaliah's age); the Geneva Bible notes take it as Ahaziah's age at a second accession, after his father's death.
  • Related numeric differences. A figure that differs between Kings and Chronicles — compare Solomon's stalls and who killed Goliath.
