The Chronicler on Ahaziah's age

Dates: c. 400 BC (the composition of 1–2 Chronicles) · Wikipedia
Summary: The book whose Masoretic text carries the disputed figure. Retelling the reign from Kings, 2 Chronicles 22:2 now reads that Ahaziah was forty-two — an impossibility, since the same writer two verses earlier has Ahaziah's father die at forty. On the mainstream reading the Chronicler wrote "twenty-two," and a later hand inflated it.

Claims & evidence

The received Hebrew reads "forty-two"

"Forty and two years old was Ahaziah when he began to reign, and he reigned one year in Jerusalem. His mother's name also was Athaliah the daughter of Omri."

— 2 Chronicles 22:2 (KJV), source. The verse is the Chronicler's parallel to 2 Kings 8:26, which reads "two and twenty years old." Both notices give the same one-year reign and the same mother; only the age differs, and by exactly twenty.

The Chronicler's own arithmetic rules "forty-two" out

"Thirty and two years old was he when he began to reign, and he reigned in Jerusalem eight years, and departed without being desired."

— 2 Chronicles 21:20 (KJV), source, on Ahaziah's father Jehoram — king at thirty-two, reigning eight years, so dead at forty. The Chronicler himself, then, has just made Jehoram forty at his death; he cannot also have meant to make the son forty-two. The contradiction is internal to Chronicles, which is the strongest single reason to read the "forty-two" as a slip in transmission rather than the Chronicler's own figure.

Notes

  • A dependent witness whose text was later damaged. The Chronicler wrote after the exile (conventionally c. 400 BC) and used Samuel–Kings as a written source, so his notice began as a reading of his copy of Kings — which read, and reads, "twenty-two." The impossibility of the received number, together with the unanimous versional witness to "twenty/twenty-two," is why the mainstream verdict is that a copyist of Chronicles, not the Chronicler, produced the "forty-two." The direction of the error is weighed on the argument page.
  • The harmonizer's alternative. A minority route keeps the "forty-two" as written by taking it to count not Ahaziah's age but the years of the Omride dynasty (or, in a variant, his mother Athaliah's age). That reading is weighed on its own page and rated weak: the Hebrew says Ahaziah "was a son of forty-two years," the ordinary idiom for a person's age.
  • Same shape as the Solomon and Goliath cruxes. A figure that differs between Kings and Chronicles, resolved in the text rather than in history — compare Solomon's stalls, where Chronicles carries the number many judge original, and who killed Goliath.
