The ancient versions on Ahaziah's age (Septuagint, Syriac, and Arabic)

Dates: c. 150 BC onward (the Septuagint of Chronicles; the Syriac Peshitta and the Arabic later) · Wikipedia
Summary: The old translations never carry "forty-two." The Septuagint of Chronicles reads "twenty," the Syriac and Arabic read "twenty-two," and Kings reads "twenty-two" in both Hebrew and Greek. The Masoretic Hebrew of Chronicles stands alone with the impossible figure.

Claims & evidence

The Septuagint of Chronicles reads "twenty"

"ὦn eἴkosi ἐtῶn" — "being twenty years old."

— Septuagint (2 Paralipomenon 22:2), as reported by the Cambridge Bible for Schools and Colleges: "LXX., ὦn eἴkosi ἐtῶn agreeing nearly with 2 Kings 8:26, 'two-and-twenty years old.'" The Greek translator's Hebrew copy of Chronicles, then, did not read "forty-two."

The Syriac and Arabic read "twenty-two"

"An error of transcription. 2 Kings 8:26, twenty and two; and so the Syriac and Arabic: the LXX. has 'twenty.'"

— Ellicott's Commentary for English Readers, on 2 Chronicles 22:2. The Syriac Peshitta and the Arabic versions of Chronicles agree outright with the "twenty-two" of Kings; the Septuagint reads the round "twenty." No ancient version reproduces the Masoretic "forty-two."

Notes

  • The disagreement is Masoretic-Hebrew-versus-everything-else. Kings (Hebrew and Greek), the Septuagint of Chronicles, the Syriac, and the Arabic all give a figure in the low twenties; only one line of the Hebrew of Chronicles carries "forty-two." That is the signature of a corruption confined to the Masoretic transmission of Chronicles, not of a genuine ancient variant tradition about Ahaziah's birth.
  • Why this crux runs cleaner than Solomon's stalls. On Solomon's forty-thousand stalls the oldest Greek is divided, carrying both numbers, so the original reading stays genuinely open. Here the versions do not split — they converge on the smaller figure — so the original "twenty-two" is not in real doubt, and the case for the scribal-error reading is correspondingly firmer. See the argument page.
  • "Twenty" versus "twenty-two." That the Septuagint reads the round "twenty" while Kings and the Syriac read "twenty-two" is a minor slip within the smaller figure, not a third tradition; both point away from "forty-two." The exact original — "twenty" or "twenty-two" — is a lesser question than the settled one, that it was not "forty-two."
