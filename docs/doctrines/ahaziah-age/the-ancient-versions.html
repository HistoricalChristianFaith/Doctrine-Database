The ancient versions on Ahaziah's age (Septuagint, Syriac, and Arabic)

Dates: c. 150 BC onward (the Septuagint of Chronicles; the Syriac Peshitta and the Arabic later) · Wikipedia
Summary: The Cambridge Bible and Ellicott report that the Septuagint of Chronicles reads "twenty," and Ellicott that the Syriac and Arabic read "twenty-two," where the Masoretic Hebrew of Chronicles reads "forty-two"; the Cambridge Bible notes that Kings reads "twenty-two" in both Hebrew and Greek.

Claims & evidence

The Septuagint of Chronicles reads "twenty"

"ὦn eἴkosi ἐtῶn" — "being twenty years old."

— Septuagint (2 Paralipomenon 22:2), as reported by the Cambridge Bible for Schools and Colleges: "LXX., ὦn eἴkosi ἐtῶn agreeing nearly with 2 Kings 8:26, 'two-and-twenty years old.'" Poole, on the same verse, argues that the ancient translators followed Hebrew copies that read the smaller figure.

The Syriac and Arabic read "twenty-two"

"An error of transcription. 2 Kings 8:26, twenty and two; and so the Syriac and Arabic: the LXX. has 'twenty.'"

— Ellicott's Commentary for English Readers, on 2 Chronicles 22:2. The Syriac Peshitta and the Arabic versions of Chronicles agree with the "twenty-two" of Kings; the Septuagint reads the round "twenty." Poole, Benson and Gill, on the same verse, also report Greek copies of Chronicles that read "twenty-two."

Reception and debate

  • How the versional readings have been used. Poole and Gill cite the Greek, Syriac and Arabic readings for a copyist's error in the Hebrew of Chronicles; Poole finds it "unreasonable and uncharitable to think that all of them would have conspired to have changed the text, and put in twenty and two for forty and two, if they had so read it in their Hebrew copies" (on 2 Chronicles 22:2). Other readings of the figure are set out on the disputed reading page.
  • Comparison with Solomon's stalls. On Solomon's forty-thousand stalls the oldest Greek is divided, carrying both numbers. Here the versions reported by Ellicott and the Cambridge Bible give figures in the low twenties.
  • "Twenty" and "twenty-two." The Septuagint reads the round "twenty," while Kings and the Syriac read "twenty-two"; the Cambridge Bible describes the Greek as "agreeing nearly with 2 Kings 8:26."
