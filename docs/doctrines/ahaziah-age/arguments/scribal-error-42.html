How has the "forty-two" of 2 Chronicles 22:2 been read beside the "twenty-two" of 2 Kings 8:26?

Disputed reading · on Ahaziah's Age: 22 or 42? (2 Kings 8:26 vs. 2 Chronicles 22:2) · witnesses: the Chronicler and the book of Kings

The passage

"Forty and two years old was Ahaziah when he began to reign, and he reigned one year in Jerusalem. His mother's name also was Athaliah the daughter of Omri."

— 2 Chronicles 22:2 (KJV), source.1

"Two and twenty years old was Ahaziah when he began to reign; and he reigned one year in Jerusalem. And his mother's name was Athaliah, the daughter of Omri king of Israel."

— 2 Kings 8:26 (KJV), source.2

The question

Both books give Ahaziah of Judah a one-year reign and the mother Athaliah, and both give his father Jehoram an accession at thirty-two and a reign of eight years (2 Chronicles 21:20; 2 Kings 8:17); they differ only in Ahaziah's age, by twenty years.3 The Cambridge Bible and Ellicott report that the Septuagint of Chronicles reads "twenty"; Ellicott adds that the Syriac and Arabic read "twenty-two."4 What is disputed is what the "forty-two" of the received Hebrew of Chronicles counts, and whether it stood in the Chronicler's own text.

The figure counts the years of Athaliah's house, the dynasty of Omri

In brief: "a son of forty-two years" dates Ahaziah's accession by the reign of his mother's royal family, so Kings gives the man's age and Chronicles the age of the dynasty.

Held by: the rabbinic chronology Seder Olam Rabbah (ch. 17) and Gersonides ⚠ unverified; Matthew Poole (English Protestant, Annotations upon the Holy Bible, 1685); the Jamieson–Fausset–Brown commentary; Lightfoot ⚠ unverified.5

Poole starts from the Hebrew wording: "In the Hebrew it is, a son of forty-two years, &c., which is an ambiguous phrase; and though it doth for the most part, yet it doth not always, signify the age of the person, as is manifest from 1 Samuel 13:1." He grants that it is "generally presumed" to give Ahaziah's age, "and that is the only ground of the difficulty." He points to the same kind of reckoning earlier in the book: "such a kind of computation of the years, not of the king's person, but of his reign or kingdom, we had before, 2 Chronicles 16:1."6

The verse names Ahaziah's mother as "Athaliah the daughter of Omri," and it is that house's reigns that Poole counts: "Omri, who reigned six years… Ahab his son reigned twenty-two years… Ahaziah his son two years… Joram his son twelve years; all which, put together, make up exactly these forty-two years; for Ahaziah began his reign in Joram's twelfth year, 2 Kings 8:25."7 Jamieson–Fausset–Brown put the same count as "the forty-second year of the kingdom of his mother's family."8 Poole also allows that the phrase may give "the age of his mother Athaliah; who being so great, and infamous, and mischievous a person to the kingdom and royal family of Judah, it is not strange if her age be here described, especially seeing she herself did for a season sway this sceptre."9

Poole sums up the reading: "so we have an account of the person's age in 2 Kings 8:26, and here of the kingdom to which he belonged."10 On this reading Kings gives Ahaziah's age and Chronicles the dynasty's, neither text is corrupt, and the received Hebrew stands as written.

Two accessions: at twenty-two beside his father, at forty-two in his own right

In brief: Kings dates Ahaziah's elevation to share his father's throne, and Chronicles his reign alone after his father's death.

Held by: David Kimchi and Isaac Abarbanel (Jewish commentators) ⚠ unverified; the notes of the Geneva Bible (English Protestant), on 2 Kings 8:26 and 2 Chronicles 22:2.11

The Geneva note on 2 Kings 8:26 reads the two figures as two moments in one career: the verse "is to be understood, that he was made king when his father reigned, but after his father's death he was confirmed king when he was forty-two years old, as in 2Ch 22:2." Its note on the "one year in Jerusalem" of 2 Chronicles 22:2 dates that reign the same way: "That is, after the death of his father."12

Gill gives the account he attributes to Kimchi and Abarbanel: that Ahaziah "was twenty two when he began to reign in his father's lifetime, and forty two when he began to reign in his own right," for Jehoram "reigned eight years very happily when his son was twenty two, and taken on the throne with him, after which he reigned twenty more ingloriously, and died, when his son was forty two."13 On this account the eight years both books give Jehoram are the years of his reign before the joint rule, the two age notices date two different accessions, and both stand as written.

A copyist turned an original "twenty-two" into "forty-two"

In brief: the Chronicler wrote the "twenty-two" of his parallel in Kings, and a later copy of Chronicles misread the numeral.

Held by: Matthew Poole (Annotations, 1685), as his second answer; John Gill (Exposition of the Old and New Testament); Keil & Delitzsch (German Lutheran, Biblical Commentary on the Old Testament, 1872); Gleason Archer (Encyclopedia of Bible Difficulties, 1982).14

The case starts from the Chronicler's own figures. Jehoram became king at thirty-two and reigned eight years, so he died at forty; the Pulpit Commentary calls the "two and forty" of Chronicles "absolutely impossible, since his father was but forty when he died," and Archer writes that Ahaziah "could not have been forty-two at the time of his father's death at age forty."15 The parallel in Kings, which the Chronicler's account of the same reign follows, reads "twenty-two." Poole reports Greek copies and "those two ancient translations, the Syriac and the Arabic," with the smaller figure, including the Antioch Syriac copy from which Archbishop Ussher had a transcript made, and he finds it "unreasonable and uncharitable to think that all of them would have conspired to have changed the text, and put in twenty and two for forty and two, if they had so read it in their Hebrew copies."16

Its holders give two routes for the slip. Keil & Delitzsch, on 2 Kings 8:26, describe "a confusion of כ twenty with מ forty" in the letters used as numerals, as does the Cambridge Bible ("a misreading of the Hebrew letters which were used as numerals"); Archer places it in an older notation of strokes and hooks, in which a lost or added decade-mark could change a figure.17

Poole and Gill set the reading within a view of Scripture's authority: for Poole, "the question here is not whether this text be true, but which is the true reading of the text," and Gill holds it "more to the honour of the sacred Scriptures to acknowledge here and there a mistake in the copiers, especially in the historical books."18 On this reading the two books agree that Ahaziah came to the throne at twenty-two, and only one copied numeral in Chronicles differs.
