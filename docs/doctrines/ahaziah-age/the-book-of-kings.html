The book of Kings on Ahaziah's age

Dates: c. 560 BC (the composition of 1–2 Kings) · Wikipedia
Summary: 2 Kings 8:26 makes Ahaziah twenty-two at his accession; the same book gives his father Jehoram an accession at thirty-two and a reign of eight years. Ellicott reports the same figure in the Syriac and Arabic versions of Chronicles.

Claims & evidence

Ahaziah was twenty-two at his accession

"Two and twenty years old was Ahaziah when he began to reign; and he reigned one year in Jerusalem. And his mother's name was Athaliah, the daughter of Omri king of Israel."

— 2 Kings 8:26 (KJV), source. The verse is the parallel to 2 Chronicles 22:2, which in the received Hebrew reads "forty and two years old." Both notices end with the same one-year reign and the same mother, Athaliah; only the age differs.

Jehoram's age in Kings

"Thirty and two years old was he when he began to reign; and he reigned eight years in Jerusalem."

— 2 Kings 8:17 (KJV), source, on Ahaziah's father Jehoram. The Cambridge Bible, on 2 Kings 8:26, notes: "If Ahaziah was two and twenty at his father's death, he was born when Jehoram was eighteen. This is not uncommon in the East."

Placement and reception

  • Kings and Chronicles. Kings is conventionally dated to the exilic period (c. 560 BC); Chronicles, conventionally dated c. 400 BC, parallels its account of Ahaziah's reign. See the summary timeline.
