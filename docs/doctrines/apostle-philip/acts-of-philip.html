The Acts of Philip

Dates: c. 350–450 (an apocryphal narrative in fifteen “Acts” plus a Martyrdom, surviving in Greek) · Wikipedia
Summary: The Acts of Philip is a Greek apocryphal narrative of the fourth or fifth century and the earliest extant account of Philip’s death. Philip travels with the apostle Bartholomew and his own sister Mariamne — and a talking leopard and kid — to “Ophiorymos,” the “city of the serpent,” which most manuscripts identify with Hierapolis ⚠ unverified, where the people worship a great viper. He converts the proconsul’s wife Nicanora, is seized, and is executed by being hung head-downward from a tree by his pierced ankles, preaching all the while. Whether it preserves the memory of how Philip died is disputed.

Claims & evidence

The mission to the “city of the serpent”

In the surviving narrative Philip, Bartholomew, and Mariamne come to Ophiorymos/Hierapolis, a city given over to the worship of a viper. Philip’s preaching and a healing win over Nicanora, the wife of the proconsul — the conversion that turns the city against him and sets the martyrdom in motion. The narrative includes talking animals and dragons, and encratite (rigorously ascetic) teaching, which later readers found suspect ⚠ unverified.

The martyrdom: hung head-downward by the ankles

The proconsul orders Philip hung head-downward from a tree before the temple, his ankles pierced and roped; Bartholomew is bound beside him. From the cross Philip continues to preach, and in the fuller Greek text he calls down the earth to swallow his persecutors, until Christ rebukes him for repaying evil with evil.

And he ordered to bring clubs and strong cords; and after piercing Philip’s ankles they brought hooks, and put the cords through his ankles, and hung him head downwards on a tree that was before the door of the temple; and they fixed pegs into the temple wall, and left him.

— the Acts of Philip (ANF vol. 8, tr. Alexander Walker). A fuller critical translation is François Bovon & Christopher R. Matthews, The Acts of Philip: A New Translation (Baylor University Press, 2012).

Reception and debate

  • The death-scene. The head-downward hanging, the pierced ankles, and the preaching from the tree are first attested in this text. Papias, Polycrates, and Proclus, as Eusebius quotes them, speak of Philip’s residence, daughters, and tomb, and do not describe his death. Whether the account of the death is history is disputed; see the disputed-reading page.
  • The apocryphal Acts. The Acts of Philip is one of the apocryphal Acts of the apostles, and has been classed with the “Leucian” Acts ⚠ unverified. On the class as a whole, see the apostles hub.
  • Bartholomew and Mariamne. The text keeps the Gospel pairing of Philip with Bartholomew (the two stand side by side in three of the four apostle-lists) and adds Philip’s sister Mariamne, who does not appear in the earlier sources surveyed here.
  • Ophiorymos and Hierapolis. Most manuscripts are reported to equate the “city of the serpent” with Hierapolis, where the earlier sources place Philip’s grave, and some to append an account of the translation of Philip’s body to Hierapolis ⚠ unverified (manuscript evidence not yet sourced).
