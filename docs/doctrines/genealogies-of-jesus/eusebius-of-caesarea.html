Eusebius of Caesarea on the Two Genealogies of Jesus

Dates: c. 320 (the Church History) · Wikipedia
Summary: The transmitter. Eusebius preserves Africanus's letter by quoting it at length in Church History 1.7, presents the levirate solution as "the account of the matter which has come down to us" for a difficulty "many suppose" to be a contradiction, and draws from it the corollary that, since the Law barred marriage across tribes, Joseph's Davidic descent "virtually" shows Mary's. In his own comment on Luke he reads the guarded "as was supposed" as the Evangelist voicing the common opinion, not his own.

Claims & evidence

The genealogies are widely thought to contradict; Africanus's is the account "which has come down to us"

"Matthew and Luke in their gospels have given us the genealogy of Christ differently, and many suppose that they are at variance with one another. Since as a consequence every believer, in ignorance of the truth, has been zealous to invent some explanation which shall harmonize the two passages, permit us to subjoin the account of the matter which has come down to us, and which is given by Africanus… After refuting the opinions of others as forced and deceptive, he give the account which he had received from tradition."

— Church History 1.7.1–2, source. Eusebius frames the problem — the two lists look discrepant, and believers have improvised harmonies — and presents Africanus's levirate solution as the account that has come down. He then quotes the letter at length (the Matthan/Melchi–Estha mechanism, Jacob and Heli as uterine brothers, Joseph "his own son by nature, but by law the son of Eli").

Joseph's traced descent "virtually" shows Mary to be of the same tribe

"Thus far Africanus. And the lineage of Joseph being thus traced, Mary also is virtually shown to be of the same tribe with him, since, according to the law of Moses, inter-marriages between different tribes were not permitted. For the command is to marry one of the same family and lineage, so that the inheritance may not pass from tribe to tribe. This may suffice here."

— Church History 1.7.17, source. On Eusebius's reasoning, because a lawful marriage kept Mary within Joseph's tribe, showing his Davidic descent shows hers too, without Luke's list itself being Mary's. Jerome makes the same point; John of Damascus (c. 743) gives Mary a line of her own.

"As was supposed" reports the common opinion; the shunning of Solomon's cursed line

"For if when Matthew affirmed Joseph to be the son of Jacob, Luke had in like manner affirmed that Joseph was the son of Eli, there would be some dispute. But seeing the case is that Matthew gives his opinion, Luke repeats the common opinion of many, not his own, saying, as was supposed… For since there were among the Jews different opinions of the genealogy of Christ… some shunned this opinion [that Christ came through Solomon and the kings] because of the many crimes related of their kings, and because Jeremiah said of Jechonias that 'a man should not rise of his seed to sit on the throne of David.'"

— on Luke 3:23, as preserved in Aquinas, Catena Aurea, source. Here Eusebius adds a second, exegetical layer: Luke's "as was supposed" signals that he is reporting how the descent was commonly reckoned; and, he reports, some Jews shunned Solomon's line because of the crimes of its kings and the curse of Jeremiah 22:30 on Jeconiah, that none of his seed should prosper on David's throne.

Reception and debate

  • Transmission. The Epistle to Aristides survives chiefly through Eusebius's quotation in Church History 1.7; Jerome names "Eusebius of Caesarea" with Africanus as having "disputed more fully" on the question.
  • Tradition and testimony. Eusebius says Africanus "received [the account] from tradition"; Africanus's own words (§ V of the letter) state that his account "is not supported by testimony." Spitta is reported to distinguish the genealogical lists, which the kinsmen preserved, from the levirate explanation of them (⚠ unverified). Whether a pedigree kept by the kinsmen lies behind the lists is set out on the interpretation page on the desposynoi genealogy.
  • The Jeconiah thread. The curse of Jeremiah 22:30 on Jeconiah, which Eusebius cites, recurs in later comparisons of the two lines (⚠ unverified); see the summary's Disputed readings section.
