John of Damascus on the Two Genealogies of Jesus

Dates: c. 743 (An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith) · Wikipedia
Summary: The mature Greek synthesis. Gathering the tradition into a systematic theology, John of Damascus restates Africanus's levirate scheme almost verbatim — Matthan of Solomon's line and Melchi of Nathan's by one wife, Jacob and Heli half-brothers, Jacob raising up Joseph to the childless Heli, so Joseph is "by nature the son of Jacob… but by law the son of Heli." To this he adds what the Gospels leave unsaid: an explicit Davidic descent for Mary, through Nathan's line by way of Panther, Barpanther, and Joachim her father. He keeps the ancient rule that Scripture does not genealogize women, yet supplies Mary's ancestry from tradition.

Claims & evidence

Both Evangelists give Joseph's Davidic descent; Scripture does not trace women

"But that Joseph is descended from the tribe of David is expressly demonstrated by Matthew and Luke, the most holy evangelists. But Matthew derives Joseph from David through Solomon, while Luke does so through Nathan; while over the holy Virgin's origin both pass in silence. One ought to remember that it was not the custom of the Hebrews nor of the divine Scripture to give genealogies of women; and the law was to prevent one tribe seeking wives from another… he would not have taken her unless she had been of the same tribe. It was sufficient, therefore, to demonstrate the descent of Joseph."

— An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith 4.14, source. John states the standard settlement: both lists are Joseph's; neither gives Mary's; and Joseph's descent suffices because the within-the-tribe marriage law carries Mary's Davidic blood with his — the very logic Eusebius and Jerome had used.

The levirate mechanism, restated from Africanus

"And of the line of Solomon, the son of David, Mathan had a wife of whom he begat Jacob. Now on the death of Mathan, Melchi, of the tribe of Nathan, the son of Levi and brother of Panther, married the wife of Mathan, Jacob's mother, of whom he begat Heli. Therefore Jacob and Heli became brothers on the mother's side, Jacob being of the tribe of Solomon and Heli of the tribe of Nathan. Then Heli of the tribe of Nathan died childless, and Jacob his brother, of the tribe of Solomon, took his wife and raised up seed to his brother and begat Joseph. Joseph, therefore, is by nature the son of Jacob, of the line of Solomon, but by law he is the son of Heli of the line of Nathan."

— Orthodox Faith 4.14, source. This is Africanus's scheme carried whole into the eighth century, down to the one wife married in succession by a Solomon-line and a Nathan-line husband. John's version substitutes "Mathan" and "Melchi" and adds a "Levi" and "Panther" generation, harmonizing the received text of Luke; the load-bearing conclusion is identical.

An explicit line for Mary: Panther, Barpanther, Joachim

"Born then of the line of Nathan, the son of David, Levi begat Melchi and Panther: Panther begat Barpanther, so called. This Barpanther begat Joachim: Joachim begat the holy Mother of God… Joachim then took to wife that revered and praiseworthy woman, Anna."

— Orthodox Faith 4.14, source. Here John goes beyond his predecessors. Having said Scripture does not genealogize women, he nonetheless supplies Mary a pedigree from tradition — running through the same Nathan branch to her father Joachim (a name drawn from the Protevangelium of James). Crucially, he does not claim this is Luke's list; Luke's genealogy stays Joseph's, and Mary's Davidic descent is asserted separately.

Notes

  • Why he is the synthesis. John of Damascus holds together three threads the earlier witnesses had kept apart: the levirate reconciliation of the two lists (Africanus), the corollary that Mary shares Joseph's tribe (Eusebius, Jerome), and an explicit Marian genealogy (from the Protevangelium). The result is the settled Eastern position.
  • Not the "Luke traces Mary" reading. It is easy to mistake John for the later view that Luke's list is Mary's; he is its opposite. He gives Mary a separate line precisely because Luke's is Joseph's. This is why the argument page on the Mary-genealogy reading cannot claim the Damascene — or any early father — as a proponent.
  • The "Panther" name. "Barpanther / Panther" as Mary's ancestral name is a tradition of contested origin (some connect it, disputedly, to the ben Pantera of hostile Jewish polemic); John takes it as an honorable family name. It bears on Mary's line, not on the reconciliation of the two Gospel lists.
