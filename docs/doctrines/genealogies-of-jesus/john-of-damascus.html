John of Damascus on the Two Genealogies of Jesus

Dates: c. 743 (An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith) · Wikipedia
Summary: In a systematic exposition of the faith, John of Damascus restates Africanus's levirate scheme almost verbatim — Matthan of Solomon's line and Melchi of Nathan's by one wife, Jacob and Heli half-brothers, Jacob raising up Joseph to the childless Heli, so Joseph is "by nature the son of Jacob… but by law the son of Heli." To this he adds an explicit Davidic descent for Mary, over whose origin, he says, both Evangelists "pass in silence": through Nathan's line by way of Panther, Barpanther, and Joachim her father. He keeps the ancient rule that Scripture does not genealogize women, yet supplies Mary's ancestry from tradition.

Claims & evidence

Both Evangelists give Joseph's Davidic descent; Scripture does not trace women

"But that Joseph is descended from the tribe of David is expressly demonstrated by Matthew and Luke, the most holy evangelists. But Matthew derives Joseph from David through Solomon, while Luke does so through Nathan; while over the holy Virgin's origin both pass in silence. One ought to remember that it was not the custom of the Hebrews nor of the divine Scripture to give genealogies of women; and the law was to prevent one tribe seeking wives from another… he would not have taken her unless she had been of the same tribe. It was sufficient, therefore, to demonstrate the descent of Joseph."

— An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith 4.14, source. John states that both lists are Joseph's; neither gives Mary's; and Joseph's descent suffices because the within-the-tribe marriage law carries Mary's Davidic blood with his — the very logic Eusebius and Jerome had used.

The levirate mechanism, restated from Africanus

"And of the line of Solomon, the son of David, Mathan had a wife of whom he begat Jacob. Now on the death of Mathan, Melchi, of the tribe of Nathan, the son of Levi and brother of Panther, married the wife of Mathan, Jacob's mother, of whom he begat Heli. Therefore Jacob and Heli became brothers on the mother's side, Jacob being of the tribe of Solomon and Heli of the tribe of Nathan. Then Heli of the tribe of Nathan died childless, and Jacob his brother, of the tribe of Solomon, took his wife and raised up seed to his brother and begat Joseph. Joseph, therefore, is by nature the son of Jacob, of the line of Solomon, but by law he is the son of Heli of the line of Nathan."

— Orthodox Faith 4.14, source. This is Africanus's scheme restated in the eighth century, down to the one wife married in succession by a Solomon-line and a Nathan-line husband. John names Levi as Melchi's father and Panther as Melchi's brother; Africanus's letter names neither. The conclusion is the same.

An explicit line for Mary: Panther, Barpanther, Joachim

"Born then of the line of Nathan, the son of David, Levi begat Melchi and Panther: Panther begat Barpanther, so called. This Barpanther begat Joachim: Joachim begat the holy Mother of God… Joachim then took to wife that revered and praiseworthy woman, Anna."

— Orthodox Faith 4.14, source. Here John adds to the earlier witnesses on this timeline. Having said Scripture does not genealogize women, he gives Mary a pedigree from tradition — running through the same Nathan branch to her father Joachim (a name drawn from the Protevangelium of James). He does not present this as Luke's list; Luke's genealogy stays Joseph's, and Mary's Davidic descent is asserted separately.

Reception and debate

  • Three threads. John of Damascus combines three threads found separately in earlier witnesses: the levirate reconciliation of the two lists (Africanus), the corollary that Mary shares Joseph's tribe (Eusebius, Jerome), and an explicit Marian genealogy (with Joachim, from the Protevangelium).
  • Luke's list and Mary's line. John keeps Luke's list as Joseph's and gives Mary a separate line; he does not read Luke's genealogy as Mary's.
  • The "Panther" name. John gives "Barpanther / Panther" as a family name in Mary's ancestry. It bears on Mary's line, not on the reconciliation of the two Gospel lists.
  • Cross-links. John of Damascus also appears on the timelines for perpetual virginity, the Filioque, and the person of Christ.
