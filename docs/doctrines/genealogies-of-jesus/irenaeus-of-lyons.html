Irenaeus of Lyons on the Genealogy of Jesus

Dates: c. 180 (Against Heresies; bishop of Lyons, d. c. 202) · Wikipedia
Summary: The earliest witness on this timeline, a generation before Julius Africanus. Irenaeus does not discuss the clash between Matthew and Luke; his interest is in Luke's ascending line and where it ends. Luke runs the pedigree of Jesus back not merely to Abraham but all the way to Adam, "which was the son of God," and Irenaeus reads that Adam-ward reach as the hinge of his recapitulation theology: Christ the Second Adam "gathers up" into Himself the whole human race from Adam downward. His is also the earliest extant count of the generations in Luke's list — he reckons "seventy-two" — a figure shorter than the received text's.

Claims & evidence

Luke's genealogy runs back to Adam — and Christ "sums up" all humanity from Adam onward

"Wherefore Luke points out that the pedigree which traces the generation of our Lord back to Adam contains seventy-two generations, connecting the end with the beginning, and implying that it is He who has summed up in Himself all nations dispersed from Adam downwards, and all languages and generations of men, together with Adam himself. Hence also was Adam himself termed by Paul 'the figure of Him that was to come,' because the Word, the Maker of all things, had formed beforehand for Himself the future dispensation of the human race, connected with the Son of God."

— Against Heresies 3.22.3, ANF 1, historicalchristian.faith (also newadvent.org). On Irenaeus's reading this is why Luke, unlike Matthew, climbs past Abraham to "Adam, which was the son of God" (Luke 3:38): for him the ascending list is soteriological. Because the Word "recapitulates" — sums up, re-heads — the whole race in Himself, His pedigree must reach the whole race's head, Adam. Irenaeus ties the "seventy-two" to "all nations dispersed from Adam" and "all languages"; the Table of Nations in Genesis 10 numbers seventy peoples in the Hebrew and seventy-two in the Septuagint tradition.

Irenaeus on the Second Adam's birth from Mary

"…so did He who is the Word, recapitulating Adam in Himself, rightly receive a birth, enabling Him to gather up Adam [into Himself], from Mary, who was as yet a virgin. If, then, the first Adam had a man for his father, and was born of human seed, it were reasonable to say that the second Adam was begotten of Joseph. But if the former was taken from the dust, and God was his Maker, it was incumbent that the latter also, making a recapitulation in Himself, should be formed as man by God, to have an analogy with the former as respects His origin."

— Against Heresies 3.21.10, ANF 1, historicalchristian.faith (also newadvent.org). Irenaeus states that Joseph is not Jesus's natural father. His clause "it were reasonable to say that the second Adam was begotten of Joseph" is conditional on a first Adam born of human seed; since the first Adam had God for his maker, the Second Adam, for Irenaeus, was "formed as man by God" from the Virgin.

Reception and debate

  • The generation-count and the text of Luke 3. Irenaeus reckons Luke's list at seventy-two generations; the received Greek text of Luke 3:23–38 yields seventy-seven names from Jesus up to God (seventy-six generations to Adam, seventy-seven counting "the son of God"). Africanus's version, as Bede reports, also differs from the received text, naming Melchi where Luke has Matthat (see the Africanus page).
  • Recapitulation. Irenaeus does not raise the discrepancy between Matthew's and Luke's lists; Against Heresies is written against Gnostic teachers who separated the Creator from the Redeemer. Later witnesses on this timeline also read Luke's ascent theologically: Ambrosiaster (c. 380) against Photinus, and Augustine (c. 400) as pointing to the atoning Priest and to adoption.
  • Placement on the timeline. At c. 180 Irenaeus predates Africanus (c. 220) by a generation and is the earliest witness here to comment on Luke's genealogy as a genealogy: its terminus, its length, and its Christological point.
