Was Matthew martyred in Ethiopia?

Disputed reading · on Matthew · witness: the Golden Legend and Clement of Alexandria

The passage

And after the solemnities of the mass, the king sent a tormentor which slew Matthew with a sword behind him, which was standing by the altar holding up his hands into heaven, and so was consecrate a martyr.

— Jacobus de Voragine, the Golden Legend (c. 1260), tr. William Caxton, life of St Matthew.1

Of whom are Matthew, Philip, Thomas, Levi, and many others.

— Heracleon, quoted in Clement of Alexandria, Stromata 4.9 (c. 200), naming examples in a discussion of confession “with the voice, and before the authorities”.2

The question

The Golden Legend records that Matthew, after converting Ethiopia and consecrating the king’s daughter to virginity, was killed at the altar on the order of King Hirtacus; Heracleon, quoted by Clement of Alexandria, names Matthew among his examples in a discussion of confession “with the voice, and before the authorities.” The question is how Matthew’s life ended, and which of these traditions records it. No modern scholarly holder of the Ethiopian martyrdom was located in the sources consulted for this page.

Matthew was not put to death for the faith

This reading holds that Matthew was among the apostles who bore witness by their lives rather than by a martyr’s death.

Held by: Heracleon (Valentinian teacher, c. 200).3

Heracleon, “the most distinguished of the school of Valentinians” in Clement’s description, distinguishes two kinds of confession: one “by faith and conduct,” and one “with the voice, and before the authorities,” the confession made before a tribunal. Of the second he writes, in the English rendering cited, that “neither will this utterance be found to be spoken universally; for all the saved have confessed with the confession made by the voice, and departed,” names his examples, “Matthew, Philip, Thomas, Levi, and many others,” and adds that “confession by the lip is not universal, but partial.” On this reading the list names exceptions to the confession made by the voice and places Matthew among those who did not die under persecution, and it is the earliest extant notice of how Matthew’s life ended, written two centuries before the historians who assign him Ethiopia and about a thousand years before the Golden Legend. The early writers who name Matthew in the sources gathered on this topic’s pages (Papias, Irenaeus, Origen, Eusebius) record no martyrdom of him. Heracleon’s remark is incidental to his argument about confession, and on this reading it preserves a second-century memory that Matthew’s witness was given by his life.4

Matthew was martyred at the altar in Ethiopia

This reading holds that Matthew, after a long mission in Ethiopia, was killed at the altar on the order of King Hirtacus.

Held by: Jacobus de Voragine (Golden Legend, c. 1260); Rufinus (Ecclesiastical History 1.9) and Socrates Scholasticus (Ecclesiastical History 1.19), on Ethiopia as Matthew’s field only.5

The mission field comes first. Rufinus and Socrates Scholasticus, expanding Eusebius’s account of the regions allotted to the apostles, give Matthew Ethiopia; Socrates writes that “Matthew was allotted Ethiopia.” The Golden Legend then tells the mission and the death in full. Matthew comes to the city of Nadaber, lodges with the eunuch of Queen Candace whom Philip baptized, and overcomes the enchanters Zaroes and Arphaxat and their two dragons. He raises the son of King Egippus, and the king, his household and the people are baptized. He remains thirty-three years, builds a church, and consecrates the king’s daughter Ephigenia as mistress of more than two hundred virgins. When Hirtacus succeeds and seeks to marry her, Matthew preaches on “good and lawful matrimony” and on the vow she has made to God, and the king has him slain at the altar after mass. In Western art the sword or halberd of his death is counted among Matthew’s emblems ⚠ unverified. On this reading a mission placed in Ethiopia by the fourth- and fifth-century historians ends there in the death the Golden Legend narrates, dated by tradition to about the year 68 ⚠ unverified.6
