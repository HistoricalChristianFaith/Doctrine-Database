Clement of Alexandria on Matthew

Dates: c. 200 (the Stromata) · Wikipedia
Summary: In a discussion of what it means to “confess” Christ, Clement quotes the Valentinian teacher Heracleon, who distinguished confession by faith and conduct from confession “with the voice, and before the authorities,” and named as examples “Matthew, Philip, Thomas, Levi, and many others.” Whether the list bears on how Matthew’s life ended is disputed (see Was Matthew martyred in Ethiopia?). It lists “Matthew” and “Levi” side by side.

Claims & evidence

Heracleon’s list of those who confessed by conduct, not by the voice

Clement is expounding Christ’s saying that whoever confesses him before men will be confessed before the Father. He cites Heracleon — “the most distinguished of the school of Valentinians” — who distinguishes two kinds of confession, one made by faith and conduct and one made aloud before the magistrates:

The confession that is made with the voice, and before the authorities, is what the most reckon the only confession. Not soundly…

Of the confession “with the voice” Heracleon continues, in the English rendering cited: “But neither will this utterance be found to be spoken universally; for all the saved have confessed with the confession made by the voice, and departed.” He then names his examples:

Of whom are Matthew, Philip, Thomas, Levi, and many others.

— Heracleon, quoted in Clement of Alexandria, Stromata 4.9. The next sentence in the same rendering reads: “And confession by the lip is not universal, but partial.” A reading of the list as naming exceptions — men who did not make the confession before the authorities — is set out, with its holder, on Was Matthew martyred in Ethiopia?; a rendering of the Greek giving the sense “not all the saved” is not cited here ⚠ unverified. Clement quotes Heracleon to correct him on a further point about confession, but does not dispute the roster.

Notes

  • Heracleon’s list and Matthew’s death. The Golden Legend (c. 1260) makes Matthew a martyr in Ethiopia; Heracleon, about two centuries after the events, names Matthew among examples in a discussion of confession, and whether this bears on his death is disputed. The list comes at second hand, through a Valentinian teacher quoted by Clement. The readings are set out on Was Matthew martyred in Ethiopia?
  • Matthew and Levi named as two. The list sets “Matthew” and “Levi” apart, as though they were different men; Jerome (393), by contrast, writes “Matthew, also called Levi.”
  • The source. Heracleon was a teacher of the Valentinian school, and Clement quotes him in order to argue with him; the roster of apostles is incidental to Heracleon’s point about confession.
