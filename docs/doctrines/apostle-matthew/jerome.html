Jerome on Matthew

Dates: 393 (De Viris Illustribus, “On Illustrious Men”) · Wikipedia
Summary: Jerome opens his catalogue of Christian writers with Peter, James, and — at number three — Matthew, as the author of a gospel. He states that Matthew is the Levi of the other Gospels, and that he wrote his Gospel first in Hebrew for Jewish believers. Jerome adds that the Hebrew was preserved at Caesarea and that the Nazarenes of Beroea, who used it, had described the volume to him; in the preceding chapter he says he had translated the Gospel according to the Hebrews.

Claims & evidence

“Matthew, also called Levi” — a Hebrew Gospel for the circumcision

Jerome states the identity and the Hebrew original, and places the composition in Judea before the Greek translation:

Matthew, also called Levi, apostle and aforetimes publican, composed a gospel of Christ at first published in Judea in Hebrew for the sake of those of the circumcision who believed, but this was afterwards translated into Greek, though by what author is uncertain.

— Jerome, Lives of Illustrious Men 3. In the translation cited the chapter is headed simply “Matthew”; a heading “Matthew, surnamed Levi” in other editions is ⚠ unverified.

Jerome on the Hebrew text at Caesarea and Beroea

Jerome then reports that the Hebrew original survived — a copy kept at Caesarea, and one used by the Nazarenes at Beroea in Syria, who described the volume to him:

The Hebrew itself has been preserved until the present day in the library at Cæsarea which Pamphilus so diligently gathered. I have also had the opportunity of having the volume described to me by the Nazarenes of Berœa, a city of Syria, who use it.

— Jerome, Lives of Illustrious Men 3. In chapter 2 of the same work (on James) Jerome says that “the Gospel also which is called the Gospel according to the Hebrews” is one “which I have recently translated into Greek and Latin” (Lives of Illustrious Men 2).

Notes

  • Jerome’s two statements. Jerome states in Latin both that Matthew is Levi and that his Gospel was written in Hebrew and afterwards translated into Greek. The second is set out on Was Matthew’s Gospel first written in Hebrew?
  • The Gospel according to the Hebrews. J. E. Jacquier (“Gospel of St. Matthew,” Catholic Encyclopedia, 1911) writes that Jerome’s testimony “remains rather ambiguous, since in neither case is it positively known that the writer did not mistake the Gospel according to the Hebrews (written of course in Hebrew) for the Hebrew Gospel of St. Matthew.” The readings of the text Jerome saw are set out on the interpretation page.
  • Placement on the timeline. Jerome’s notice records, in 393, Matthew as the author of the first Gospel and the existence of a Hebrew form of it; Jacquier lists Cyril of Jerusalem, Gregory of Nazianzus, Epiphanius, John Chrysostom and Augustine among later writers who repeat that Matthew wrote in Hebrew.
