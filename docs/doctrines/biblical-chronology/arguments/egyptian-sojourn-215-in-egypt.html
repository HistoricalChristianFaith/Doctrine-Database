Do the 430 years of Exodus 12:40 cover Israel's stay in Egypt alone, or Canaan and Egypt together?

Disputed reading · on Reckoning the Age of the World · witness: Josephus and Seder Olam Rabbah

The passage

Now the sojourning of the children of Israel, who dwelt in Egypt, was four hundred and thirty years. And it came to pass at the end of the four hundred and thirty years, even the selfsame day it came to pass, that all the hosts of the LORD went out from the land of Egypt.

— Exodus 12:40–41 (KJV, following the Masoretic Hebrew), source.

And the sojourning of the children of Israel, while they sojourned in the land of Egypt and the land of Chanaan, was four hundred and thirty years.

— Exodus 12:40 in the Septuagint (Brenton), the Samaritan Pentateuch reading likewise "in the land of Canaan and in the land of Egypt" (and adding "and of their fathers").1

The question

The Masoretic Hebrew gives the 430 years as the time Israel "dwelt in Egypt"; the Septuagint and Samaritan Pentateuch give it as the sojourn in Canaan and Egypt together. Since Genesis puts 215 years between Abraham's call and Jacob's descent (25 + 60 + 130), the two texts yield a stay in Egypt of either 430 years or about 215, and every reckoning of the Exodus, and of the dates above it, inherits the choice.2 Ancient Jewish witnesses are named on the first reading; the modern holders cited on both readings write chiefly in ministry and popular journals, and the academic commentary literature on the variant was not opened for this page.

The 430 years run from Abraham's entry into Canaan, leaving about 215 (or 210) years in Egypt

On this reading the 430 years count from the patriarchs' arrival in Canaan, so Israel spent only about half that time in Egypt.

Held by: Josephus (Jewish historian, Antiquities 2.15.2, c. AD 93); Seder Olam Rabbah (rabbinic, c. AD 160) and Rashi (rabbinic commentator, 11th century), on a 210-year count; Edward D. Andrews (UASV textual commentary, 2025).3

The versions and Josephus. The Septuagint and the Samaritan Pentateuch both spell out that the 430 covers Canaan as well as Egypt. Josephus states the figure outright: Israel left Egypt "four hundred and thirty years after our forefather Abraham came into Canaan, but two hundred and fifteen years only after Jacob removed into Egypt." Andrews counts the Septuagint and Samaritan as "two independent witnesses," which with Josephus and Paul "strongly argues" for the longer wording, and he proposes that the Masoretic text lost "and in Canaan" by haplography.4

Paul counts from the promise. In Galatians 3:17 the Law comes "four hundred and thirty years after" the covenant confirmed to Abraham. Butt and Andrews read Paul as measuring the 430 from the promise, which places the patriarchs' Canaan years inside the total.5

The Levite genealogy. Kohath entered Egypt with Jacob (Genesis 46:11); his grandson Moses was 80 at the Exodus. Seder Olam adds Kohath's 133 years, Amram's 137 and Moses' 80 to reach 350, and counts 210 years remaining in Egypt. Rashi makes the same reckoning: "go and reckon all his years and all the years of Amram his son and the whole eighty years of Moses… and you will not find that they total to so many." Genesis 15:16 places the return "in the fourth generation."6

The four hundred years of affliction. Genesis 15:13 promises that Abraham's "offspring" will be strangers four hundred years. Rashi counts the 400 from Isaac's birth, since only then had Abraham offspring, with 30 years from the covenant between the pieces to Isaac; Butt, quoting Hoehner, counts it "from the weaning of Isaac to the time of the Exodus." On Rashi's and Butt's counts the 400 spans the whole sojourn of the seed in lands not their own, Hebron included, and the 430 of Exodus 12:40 runs from the covenant to the Exodus.7

The 430 years were spent in Egypt itself

On this reading the Masoretic wording is the original text, and Israel spent the full 430 years in Egypt.

Held by: William H. C. Propp (Anchor Bible Exodus 1–18, 1999) ⚠ unverified; Paul J. Ray Jr. (Bible and Spade, 2007); Hugh Henry and Daniel J. Dyke (Reasons to Believe, 2013).8

The Hebrew text and its versions. The Masoretic Hebrew reads the sojourn of those "who dwelt in Egypt," and Ray notes that the Vulgate, Peshitta and Targum follow it. He describes the Canaan-and-Egypt division in the Samaritan and in two Septuagint manuscripts as "Midrashic exegesis," observing that Codex Vaticanus "originally added an extra five years to the sojourn." A Qumran Exodus fragment, 4Q14 (4QExodc), is reported to carry the short Hebrew form, and the Samaritan's added "and of their fathers" is read as a further expansion ⚠ unverified.9

The four hundred years. Ray takes 430 as "the more exact amount for the round number of Genesis 15:13," the affliction in "a land that is not theirs," and reads Stephen in Acts 7:6–7 as allocating "a full and literal 400 years" to Egypt.10

The genealogies and the population. Of the Exodus 6 line Ray writes that "several generations appear to have been telescoped here." He sets beside it the long line of Ephraim, which he relates to "the population explosion toward the end of the 430 years," and the 8,600 Kohathite males of Numbers 3:27–28, a growth he takes to need the full 430 years.11

Paul and the promise to the seed. Henry and Dyke report Leon Wood's view that Paul was deliberately ambiguous, leaving either sojourn open. Ray reads Paul in Galatians 3:17 as "following the LXX of Exodus 12:40," and notes that the promises were made "to his seed" and last spoken to Jacob just before the descent, which falls "exactly 430 years before the Law was given, if the long chronology is allowed."12
