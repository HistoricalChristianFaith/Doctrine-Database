John Climacus and the Sinai tradition on the power of the Name

Dates: c. 630 (John Climacus, abbot of Sinai, c. 579–649; his Ladder of Divine Ascent) · Wikipedia
Summary: John Climacus, abbot of Sinai, asks that the remembrance of Jesus be present with every breath, and calls the name of Jesus the strongest weapon “in heaven or earth” for the soul’s warfare. Hesychios of Sinai, a later abbot of the same monastery, writes that the name of Jesus should “adhere to your breath.”

Claims & evidence

“Let the remembrance of Jesus be present with your every breath”

Let the remembrance of Jesus be present with your every breath. Then indeed you will appreciate the value of stillness.

— The Ladder of Divine Ascent, Step 27 (on stillness), trans. Colm Luibheid & Norman Russell (Classics of Western Spirituality), source. The older Holy Transfiguration / Lazarus Moore rendering reads: “Let the remembrance of Jesus be present with each breath, and then you will know the value of solitude” (Step 27:61), source.1 Climacus joins the remembrance of Jesus to breathing and to hesychia (stillness), the term from which the Byzantine practice takes its other name, hesychasm.

“Flog your enemies with the name of Jesus” — the Name as the soul’s weapon

As you go on your way, arm yourself with prayer. When you reach the place, stretch out your hands. Flog your enemies with the name of Jesus, for there is no stronger weapon in heaven or earth.

— The Ladder of Divine Ascent, Step 21 (on cowardice), trans. Archimandrite Lazarus Moore, source.2 Climacus applies the Name to the ascetic’s warfare against fear and the demons that provoke it, and calls the invoked Name itself the weapon: “there is no stronger weapon in heaven or earth.”

Notes

  • Hesychios of Sinai (a later abbot of the same Sinai monastery, dated by the Philokalia translators to the eighth–ninth century) writes in On Watchfulness and Holiness: the watchful heart “breathes and invokes, endlessly and without ceasing, only Jesus Christ who is the Son of God and Himself God” (Text 5), and “lash the enemy with the name of Jesus and, as a certain wise man has said, let the name of Jesus adhere to your breath, and then you will know the blessings of stillness” (Text 100). Source (The Philokalia, vol. 1, Palmer/Sherrard/Ware).3 The sentence Hesychios gives from “a certain wise man” closely matches Climacus’s Step 27 line quoted above.
  • John Climacus is seated on the power of the Name timeline at c. 630, after Diadochos of Photike (c. 486) and before Bernard of Clairvaux (c. 1140). In the fourteenth century Gregory Palamas defended the breathing method of the Athonite monks against Barlaam.
  • Like Diadochos, Climacus gives the shorter invocation (“the name of Jesus,” “the remembrance of Jesus”). The formula “Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me” appears in the hesychast Method quoted by Palamas and in The Way of a Pilgrim.
