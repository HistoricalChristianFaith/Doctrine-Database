Justin Martyr on the power of the Name

Dates: c. 160 (Justin Martyr, c. 100–165; the Dialogue with Trypho and Second Apology) · Wikipedia
Summary: The earliest extant father on this timeline to present the exorcistic power of the Name as a public, repeatable argument. Writing around 160, Justin appeals four times to a fact he says his hearers can verify with their own eyes: demons are “overcome and subdued” when Christians exorcise them “in the name of Jesus Christ, who was crucified under Pontius Pilate,” where, he says, the professional exorcists, incantations, and drugs fail. He also distinguishes this Name from the names of “kings, or righteous men, or prophets, or patriarchs,” which, he writes, do not subject the demon.

Claims & evidence

“The power of whose name even the demons do fear” — exorcism in the name of Jesus crucified

For we call Him Helper and Redeemer, the power of whose name even the demons do fear; and at this day, when they are exorcised in the name of Jesus Christ, crucified under Pontius Pilate, governor of Judaea, they are overcome. And thus it is manifest to all, that His Father has given Him so great power, by virtue of which demons are subdued to His name, and to the dispensation of His suffering.

— Dialogue with Trypho, Chapter XXX, source.1 In this passage Justin places the power in the Name (“the power of whose name even the demons do fear”), describes it as present (“at this day”), and ascribes it to what the Father has given the Son, tied to “the dispensation of His suffering” — the Name of Jesus crucified.

“When we exorcise all demons and evil spirits, have them subjected to us”

And now we, who believe on our Lord Jesus, who was crucified under Pontius Pilate, when we exorcise all demons and evil spirits, have them subjected to us.

— Dialogue with Trypho, Chapter LXXVI, source.2 In the same chapter Justin has just quoted the Lord’s own warning — “Many shall say to Me in that day, Lord, Lord… cast out demons in Thy name? And I will say to them, Depart from Me” (Matthew 7:22–23) — and the promise “I give unto you power to tread on serpents… and on all the might of the enemy” (Luke 10:19). Justin thus sets the Lord’s warning and the Lord’s promise in the same chapter as his claim that demons are subjected to those who believe.

“Overcome and subdued” — this Name and no other name of kings or patriarchs

For every demon, when exorcised in the name of this very Son of God—who is the First-born of every creature, who became man by the Virgin, who suffered, and was crucified under Pontius Pilate by your nation, who died, who rose from the dead, and ascended into heaven—is overcome and subdued. But though you exorcise any demon in the name of any of those who were amongst you—either kings, or righteous men, or prophets, or patriarchs—it will not be subject to you. But if any of you exorcise it in [the name of] the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, it will perhaps be subject to you. Now assuredly your exorcists, I have said, make use of craft when they exorcise, even as the Gentiles do, and employ fumigations and incantations.

— Dialogue with Trypho, Chapter LXXXV, source.3 Here Justin defines the Name that subdues demons by the story of the incarnate, crucified, risen Lord, and sets out a graded series: the names of Israel’s kings, righteous men, prophets, and patriarchs, he says, do not subdue the demon; the name “the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob” “perhaps” will; and the Jewish exorcists of his day, he charges, “make use of craft… even as the Gentiles do, and employ fumigations and incantations.” In Justin’s account the efficacy depends on who Jesus is, not on the technique of the operator.

“Have healed and do heal” where all the other exorcists failed

For numberless demoniacs throughout the whole world, and in your city, many of our Christian men exorcising them in the name of Jesus Christ, who was crucified under Pontius Pilate, have healed and do heal, rendering helpless and driving the possessing devils out of the men, though they could not be cured by all the other exorcists, and those who used incantations and drugs.

— Second Apology, Chapter VI, source.4 Addressed not to a Jewish interlocutor but to the Roman authorities, this is Justin’s public, empirical claim: the cures are “throughout the whole world, and in your city,” done by ordinary “Christian men,” and they succeed, he says, where “all the other exorcists, and those who used incantations and drugs” fail. Justin presents the claim as something his readers could check: a contrast between exorcism in the Name of Jesus and the professional exorcists of his day. Origen’s Against Celsus (c. 248), on the same timeline, sets the Name against incantation in a similar way.

Placement on the timeline

  • Justin is the earliest extant father on the power of the Name timeline, placed by the date of the Dialogue and Second Apology (c. 160). Origen (Against Celsus I.24–25, VII.4) appears later on the same timeline with related material.
  • Justin’s distinction in Dialogue 85 — the Name of Jesus crucified subdues the demon where, he says, the names of kings, prophets, and patriarchs do not — concerns the question this timeline follows: how the power of the Name relates to the incantations of the exorcists. In Dialogue 76 Justin also quotes Matthew 7:22–23, the warning to those who cast out demons in the Lord’s name whom he does not know.
  • Justin appears across this wiki as a witness on several timelines — the sons of God and the Nephilim, the divine council, aliens, UFOs, and the demonic, the Eucharist, and more — where the Second Apology’s account of the fallen angels and their demon offspring describes the spirits that, on this page, Justin says are subdued by the Name.
