Bernardine of Siena on the power of the Name

Dates: c. 1420 (Bernardino degli Albizzeschi, Franciscan Observant preacher, 1380–1444; his Holy Name missions and the golden IHS tablet were at their height across the 1410s–1420s, and the controversy they provoked came to a head in 1427) · Wikipedia
Summary: Bernardine made the Holy Name of Jesus a public, civic devotion — preaching the Name and raising for veneration a tablet blazoned with the monogram IHS in a sunburst of rays. The devotion was charged with exposing the people to the danger of idolatry. He was examined before Pope Martin V, with John of Capistrano named as his defender, and acquitted; in 1432 a bull of Eugene IV annulled the proceedings against him.

Claims & evidence

The Name preached — “the glory of preachers”

The name of Jesus is the glory of preachers, because the shining splendor of that name causes his word to be proclaimed and heard. And how do you think such an immense, sudden and dazzling light of faith came into the world, if not because Jesus was preached? Was it not through the brilliance and sweet savor of this name that God ‘called us into his marvelous light’? When we have been enlightened, and in that same light behold the light of heaven, rightly may the apostle Paul say to us: ‘Once you were darkness, but now you are light in the Lord; walk as children of light.’

— Bernardine of Siena, Sermon on the glorious Name of Jesus Christ (De glorioso Nomine Iesu Christi, ch. 2), source (the reading appointed for his feast, from his collected works, Opera omnia 4:505–506).1 Bernardine’s preaching turned on the Name: to make Jesus “proclaimed and heard” is, for him, to release into the world the “dazzling light of faith” that the Name itself carries.

The IHS tablet — the monogram raised for veneration

He used to hold a board in front of him while preaching, with the sacred monogram painted on it in the midst of rays and afterwards expose it for veneration.

Everywhere Bernardine persuaded the cities to take down the arms of their warring factions from the church and palace walls and to inscribe there, instead, the initials I. H. S.

— Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “St. Bernardine of Siena,” source.2 The IHS trigram (the first letters of ΙΗΣΌΥΣ, IHSOUS, “Jesus,” in Greek capitals) painted in a blaze of gilded rays is Bernardine’s emblem, later adopted as the badge of the Society of Jesus (see the Holy Name confraternity and feast). In the encyclopedia’s account he exposed the tablet for veneration, and the cities inscribed the letters in place of the arms of their warring factions.

The charge of idolatry and the papal examination

He was accused of heresy, the tablets he had used to promote devotion to the Holy Name being made the basis of a clever attack by the adherents of the Dominican, Manfred of Vercelli… The saint was charged with having introduced a profane, new devotion which exposed the people to the danger of idolatry.

The latter took place at St. Peter’s in presence of the pope, 8 June, St. John Capistran having charge of the saint’s defence… The malice and futility of the charges against Bernardine were so completely demonstrated that the pope not only justified and commended the saint’s teaching, but urged him to preach in Rome.

— Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), source.3 Bernardine was summoned to Rome to answer the charge (the Order of Friars Minor and Mormando place the summons in 1426), examined before Pope Martin V — who, in the Order’s account, at first received him coldly and forbade him to preach or show the tablets pending inquiry — and then acquitted, the pope not only clearing him but pressing him to preach in the city. (the Order of Friars Minor’s account) John of Capistrano is named as conducting the defence, and Iris Origo gives the affair a whole chapter (“The charge of heresy”) in The World of San Bernardino (1962), as does Franco Mormando’s The Preacher’s Demons (1999). The attack was renewed under Eugene IV, whose Bull of 7 January 1432 reserves the case to the pope, annuls the proceedings taken against Bernardine, and imposes perpetual silence on his accuser (edited in the Bullarium Franciscanum, Nova Series I, ed. U. Hüntemann, Quaracchi 1929, pp. 27–28, no. 40). The accounts differ on two details. The “8 June” hearing at St. Peter’s, in 1427, is the encyclopedia’s dating; the Order of Friars Minor and Mormando put the summons in 1426, and the year is given variously (1426–1427). The encyclopedia names “the adherents of the Dominican, Manfred of Vercelli” as the attackers; the 1432 bull, as reported from the Bullarium edition, names a different accuser, a Prague parish priest (⚠ unverified).(near-contemporary Life by Leonardo Benvoglienti, written 1446 at John of Capistrano’s instance, ed. Van Ortroy)

Notes

  • Bernard of Clairvaux. An earlier Latin text on the Name is Bernard of Clairvaux’s Sermon 15 on the Song of Songs (“The Name of Jesus”), where Bernard reads the Name as oil that “gives light… nourishes… and soothes” and writes “Jesus to me is honey in the mouth, music in the ear, a song in the heart” (given its own block on the timeline at c. 1140).4
  • The hymn Jesu dulcis memoria. The medieval hymn Jesu dulcis memoria (“Jesu, the very thought of thee / With sweetness fills my breast” in Edward Caswall’s 1849 rendering) was long ascribed to Bernard; the Hymnology Archive, following Dom André Wilmart, assigns it to an anonymous English Cistercian of the late twelfth century, with Bernard’s name attaching only from the late 1200s.5
  • Later institutions. After Bernardine: the Feast of the Most Holy Name of Jesus was granted to the Franciscans in 1530 and extended to the universal church in 1721, the Dominican Holy Name Society — whose object is “to beget due love and reverence for the Holy Name of God and Jesus Christ” and to suppress blasphemy — traces its charge to Gregory X’s letter to the Order of Preachers in 1274, and Bernardine’s IHS monogram was carried on as the seal of the Jesuits. That whole institutionalization is told on its own block on the timeline, seated at c. 1564 — the Holy Name confraternity and feast.6
  • Bernardine is seated on the power of the Name timeline at c. 1420, after Gregory Palamas (c. 1340). Martin V’s acquittal and Eugene IV’s bull of 1432 are the papal rulings in his case on the charge that the veneration of the tablets exposed the people to idolatry.
