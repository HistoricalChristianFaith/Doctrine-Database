Augustine of Hippo on the veneration of relics

Dates: c. 354–430 (City of God Book 22 written c. 426) · Wikipedia
Summary: In City of God Augustine catalogues the miracles he reports at the newly-arrived relics of St. Stephen in Roman Africa, and writes, in this book and earlier, that Christians build no temples and offer no sacrifice to the martyrs, but to God alone at their tombs, while honouring their reliquaries.

Claims & evidence

Augustine’s record of miracles at the relics of St. Stephen

Relics of the protomartyr Stephen reached Hippo c. 424; Augustine writes that Uzalis and Calama had received relics of Stephen earlier. He recorded a wave of healings and resurrections at the shrines that held them — a blind woman given sight, a dead man raised, a crushed child restored — and reckoned nearly seventy such miracles at Hippo alone in the “not yet two years” since the relics arrived there.1

“When the bishop Projectus was bringing the relics of the most glorious martyr Stephen to the waters of Tibilis, a great concourse of people came to meet him at the shrine. There a blind woman entreated that she might be led to the bishop who was carrying the relics. He gave her the flowers he was carrying. She took them, applied them to her eyes, and forthwith saw.”

— Augustine, City of God 22.8. He opens the chapter writing that “even now miracles are wrought in the name of Christ, whether by His sacraments or by the prayers or relics of His saints,” and closes that at Calama, where the relics had been longer, the miracles were “incomparably more” numerous.

Augustine: no temples or sacrifices to the martyrs, but to their God

Augustine writes that the honour paid the martyrs is not the worship due God: Christians raise memorials, not temples; the altar over a martyr's body is an altar to God, and the priest sacrifices to God, not to the martyr.2

“To our martyrs we build, not temples as if they were gods, but monuments as to dead men whose spirits live with God. Neither do we erect altars at these monuments that we may sacrifice to the martyrs, but to the one God of the martyrs and of ourselves… For it is to God, not to them, he sacrifices, though he sacrifices at their monument; for he is God's priest, not theirs.”

— Augustine, City of God 22.10. He says the same at 8.27: “we do not build temples, and ordain priests, rites, and sacrifices for these same martyrs; for they are not our gods, but their God is our God. Certainly we honour their reliquaries, as the memorials of holy men of God…”

Reception and debate

  • Two themes in one book. City of God 22 both reports miracles at the relics and distinguishes the honour paid the martyrs from the sacrifice offered to God; Jerome, writing against Vigilantius twenty years earlier, also argues from the miracles at the relics and from the difference between honouring and adoring.
  • Augustine's earlier view of miracles. The Augustinian Order's Augnet resource reports that in De vera religione (390) Augustine wrote that miracles like those of the Bible “were no longer permitted to continue in our time, lest the mind should always seek visible things, and the human race should be chilled by the customariness of the very things whose novelty had inspired them,” and that the miracle-reports of City of God 22.8 show “how dramatically he had changed his mind on the subject of miracles.”3 The chapter's title in the NPNF translation speaks of miracles “Which Have Not Ceased Since the World Believed.”
