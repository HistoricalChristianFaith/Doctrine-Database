The Council of Trent on the veneration of relics

Dates: Session XXV, 3–4 December 1563 · Wikipedia
Summary: The Council of Trent's decree on relics, issued in the same decree that treated the sacred images. Trent teaches that the bodies of the martyrs and saints are to be venerated, condemns those who deny it, and orders superstition and “filthy lucre” removed from the cult, with new relics to be approved by the bishop.

Claims & evidence

Trent: the bodies of the saints are to be venerated, and those who deny it condemned

Trent teaches that the relics of the saints — once temples of the Holy Ghost, destined for resurrection — are to be venerated by the faithful, and condemns the contrary teaching.1

“The holy bodies of holy martyrs, and of others now living with Christ,—which bodies were the living members of Christ, and the temple of the Holy Ghost…—are to be venerated by the faithful; through which (bodies) many benefits are bestowed by God on men; so that they who affirm that veneration and honour are not due to the relics of saints… are wholly to be condemned, as the Church has already long since condemned, and now also condemns them.”

— Council of Trent, Session XXV (1563), “On the Invocation, Veneration, and Relics of Saints, and on Sacred Images.” The same decree grounds the veneration of images in the “prototype” principle and Nicaea II (see the images half of the same decree).

Trent: superstition and gain to be removed from the cult

The same decree charges the bishops to remove every superstition and “filthy lucre” from the cult, while maintaining the veneration itself.2

“All superstition shall be removed, all filthy lucre be abolished; finally, all lasciviousness be avoided…”

— Session XXV (1563). Trent also ordained that no “new relics” be “recognised, unless the said bishop has taken cognizance and approved thereof.”

Reception and debate

  • Catholic summary. Herbert Thurston (Catholic, Catholic Encyclopedia, 1911) writes that the Catholic Church's teaching on the veneration of relics “is summed up in a decree of the Council of Trent (Sess. XXV).”3
  • The decree's title joins relics and images — “On the Invocation, Veneration, and Relics of Saints, and on Sacred Images”; the images half is traced on the veneration of images and icons. It also treats the invocation of the saints (see the invocation of the saints).
