Justin Martyr on the Place of Christian Worship

Dates: c. 165 · Wikipedia
Summary: Tried at Rome under the prefect Rusticus (c. 165) and pressed to disclose where the Christians assembled, Justin gave the earliest datable testimony to the question this page traces: he named no dedicated meeting-place at all, only the lodging where he stayed — "above one Martinus, at the Timiotinian Bath" — and professed to be "unaware of any other meeting than his." His refusal first turns theological ("the God of the Christians is not circumscribed by place"), then names a private dwelling as the place where he knew a meeting to be held.

Claims & evidence

Asked where the Christians assemble, Justin names no church — only a private lodging

Rusticus the prefect said, "Where do you assemble?" Justin said, "Where each one chooses and can: for do you fancy that we all meet in the very same place? Not so; because the God of the Christians is not circumscribed by place; but being invisible, fills heaven and earth, and everywhere is worshipped and glorified by the faithful." Rusticus the prefect said, "Tell me where you assemble, or into what place do you collect your followers?" Justin said, "I live above one Martinus, at the Timiotinian Bath; and during the whole time (and I am now living in Rome for the second time) I am unaware of any other meeting than his. And if any one wished to come to me, I communicated to him the doctrines of truth."

— The Martyrdom of the Holy Martyrs (the Acts of Justin), ch. 2, source (cf. New Advent).1 The exchange carries two distinct answers. The first is theological: the prefect assumes a single fixed cult-site, and Justin denies the premise — the Christians' God "is not circumscribed by place," so there is no one shrine where the community must gather, a theme the later apologists also pressed against the pagan temple-cult. The second is factual: pressed for a location, Justin names only the private dwelling he lodged in, "above one Martinus, at the Timiotinian Bath," and knows of no other meeting. Justin names no building set apart for worship.

Reception and debate

  • This is the earliest witness on the Place of Christian Worship timeline, a generation before the earliest physically attested Christian building. J. B. Lightfoot, whose "necessity, not principle" thesis is set out on the interpretation page, glossed the scene — "still the disciples, as of old, meet in an upper room, for fear, not now of the Jews, but of the Gentiles."2
  • Both of Justin's answers are cited on the interpretation page: the first (God "is not circumscribed by place") in the reading that the absence of temples expressed a conviction about God, the second (the lodging "above one Martinus") in Lightfoot's reading that it followed from the Christians' legal position.
