Lactantius on Astrology and Fate

Dates: c. 310 AD (Lucius Caecilius Firmianus Lactantius, c. 250 – c. 325; a Latin rhetorician of North African origin, pupil of Arnobius, summoned by Diocletian to teach rhetoric at Nicomedia, later tutor to Constantine’s son Crispus — the “Christian Cicero,” whose Divine Institutes were composed c. 303–311 during and after the Great Persecution) · Wikipedia
Summary: The Latin West’s fullest early statement of the demonological aetiology of astrology. In Book II of the Divine Institutes Lactantius sets out the Genesis 6 / Watcher story in his own two-tier form — God sends angels to guard mankind, they are “polluted… by intercourse with women” and fall, and from them spring a second, earth-bound kind of demon — and then names those spirits, flatly, “the inventors of astrology, and soothsaying, and divination.” The art is not a human discovery but a demonic imposition, framed to turn men from the true God. He also carries the liberation half of the motif: quoting Hermes, he holds that the man who knows God is “not even bound by fate.” A patristic proponent of the fallen-angels aetiology, and, through the same Watcher narrative, a witness tied to the sons of God and the Nephilim.

Claims & evidence

The fallen angels beget the earth-demons who “were the inventors of astrology” (Div. Inst. II.15–17)

Lactantius tells the Genesis 6 story as the hinge of his demonology. God, he says, “sent angels for the protection and improvement of the human race,” charging them not to defile themselves with earthly contamination; but the devil “gradually enticed them to vices, and polluted them by intercourse with women.” Barred from returning to heaven, they “fell to the earth,” and their half-human offspring formed a second order of spirits:

But they who were born from these, because they were neither angels nor men, but bearing a kind of mixed nature, were not admitted into hell, as their fathers were not into heaven. Thus there came to be two kinds of demons; one of heaven, the other of the earth. The latter are the wicked spirits, the authors of all the evils which are done, and the same devil is their prince.

Two chapters on, under the heading “That Astrology, Soothsaying, and Similar Arts are the Invention of Demons,” those same earth-demons are named as the source of the whole divinatory apparatus — astrology at its head:

These were the inventors of astrology, and soothsaying, and divination, and those productions which are called oracles, and necromancy, and the art of magic, and whatever evil practices besides these men exercise, either openly or in secret.

The design behind the art is deception: “all these things are false of themselves… Thus they delude the credulity of men by lying divination, because it is not expedient for them to lay open the truth” — the whole point being “to turn away the minds of men from the worship of the true God.”1

The man who knows God is “not even bound by fate” (Div. Inst. II.16)

Lactantius also carries the other half of the anti-astrology motif — that the Christian is lifted out of the bondage the art imposes. Enlisting the Hermetic corpus he prizes, he writes that those who have known God “are not only safe from the attacks of demons, but that they are not even bound by fate”:

“The only protection,” he says, “is piety, for over a pious man neither evil demon nor fate has any power: for God rescues the pious man from all evil; for the one and only good thing among men is piety.”

Since the demons themselves are the authors of the astral Fate, to be freed from the demons is to be freed from Fate — the same liberation Tatian frames (“we are superior to Fate… we reject its lawgivers”) and Tertullian ties to conversion.2

Notes

  • A two-tier variant of the Watcher aetiology. Where 1 Enoch 8 has the Watchers themselves teach astrology to their human wives, Lactantius routes it through a generation: the fallen angels beget the earth-demons, and it is those spirits who “were the inventors of astrology.” The origin-story is the same Genesis 6 descent, filtered through his distinctive scheme of “two kinds of demons.” The mechanism (a demonic invention imposed to enslave) matches Tatian and Tertullian even where the exact link in the chain differs.
  • Fate is demonic, not stellar. Lactantius does not locate Fate in the stars as causes at all; he elsewhere insists the heavenly bodies move “not by their own design, but by the genius of the artificer” — “there is therefore in the stars design… but it is the design of God.” The “fate” astrology trades on is a fraud floated by the demons, which is why piety, not better astronomy, dissolves it.
