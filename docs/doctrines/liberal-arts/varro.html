Varro and the canon of the liberal arts

Dates: c. 33 BC · Wikipedia
Summary: Marcus Terentius Varro (116–27 BC), a Roman antiquarian scholar, set out a list of the liberal arts in Latin. His lost Disciplinarum Libri Novem (“Nine Books of Disciplines”) treated nine subjects: the seven arts later listed by the medieval schools, plus medicine and architecture. Some four and a half centuries later Martianus Capella presented a set of seven, without Varro’s medicine and architecture; the medieval schools grouped these as the trivium of grammar, dialectic, and rhetoric and the quadrivium of arithmetic, geometry, astronomy, and music.

Claims & evidence

Varro’s Disciplinae and the nine arts

The “Libri IX Disciplinarum” of the learned M. Terentius Varro of Reate, an earlier contemporary of Cicero, treats of the seven liberal arts adding to them medicine and architectonics.

— The Catholic Encyclopedia, “The Seven Liberal Arts,” source. Varro’s own text does not survive; it is known through later testimonia and reconstruction (the nine-book division has been argued from Vitruvius; ⚠ unverified — not yet sourced). It is the earliest work on this timeline reported to set out the Greek circle of studies as a Latin list of named disciplinae. Martianus Capella’s allegory De Nuptiis Philologiae et Mercurii (c. AD 420) is reported to follow Varro’s model (⚠ unverified — not yet sourced); its set of seven omits Varro’s medicine and architecture (see Martianus Capella). The medieval schools grouped the seven as the trivium and quadrivium.

The “circle of learning” — enkyklios paideia — behind the word “encyclopedia”

Varro’s work is associated with the Greek ideal of enkyklios paideia, the “circle” or all-round course of general education — the notion that the free studies form a single connected round that the educated person should traverse whole. The later Roman rhetorician Quintilian (c. AD 35–100) names that circle explicitly (“that… general education” the Greeks call enkyklios paideia), and from this phrase, by way of the Renaissance, the modern word encyclopedia descends — a book covering the whole round of the arts.

— See The Catholic Encyclopedia, “The Seven Liberal Arts,” source, on Varro’s place in the transmission of the arts; and Quintilian, Institutio Oratoria I.10.1 for the enkyklios paideia.

Reception and context

  • Varro is a pagan Roman witness, not a Christian one. His entry records a list of the arts as a bounded set; later entries on this timeline (Cassiodorus, Isidore of Seville) list seven liberal arts.
  • Because the Disciplinae is lost, everything said of its contents rests on later report and scholarly reconstruction, including the nine-book structure.
  • Compare Cicero, Varro’s younger contemporary (Varro was, in the Catholic Encyclopedia’s words, “an earlier contemporary of Cicero”), whose entry records the terms artes liberales and humanitas; Varro’s entry records a syllabus.
