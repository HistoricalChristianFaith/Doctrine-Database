Origen of Alexandria on purgatory and prayer for the dead

Dates: c. 240 · Wikipedia
Summary: Origen (c. 184–253), Clement's successor as head of the Alexandrian school, speaks of a fire that acts on the sinner after death. Building on Paul's "wood, hay, stubble" burned at the judgment (1 Corinthians 3:12–15) and Isaiah's "the fire which ye have kindled," he teaches that each sinner kindles his own fire — its "fuel and food are our sins" — and that this fire is medicinal: "God our Physician" applies it "to remove the defects of our souls," and the outcome is "a restoration." He sets this within his teaching on the restoration of all things (apokatastasis) and at the judgment.

Claims & evidence

Origen: "every sinner kindles for himself the flame of his own fire" — Paul's "wood, hay, stubble"

We find in the prophet Isaiah, that the fire with which each one is punished is described as his own; for he says, "Walk in the light of your own fire, and in the flame which ye have kindled." By these words it seems to be indicated that every sinner kindles for himself the flame of his own fire, and is not plunged into some fire which has been already kindled by another, or was in existence before himself. Of this fire the fuel and food are our sins, which are called by the Apostle Paul wood, and hay, and stubble.

— De Principiis (On First Principles) II.10.4, source. Origen ties the "eternal fire" of judgment to Paul's words: the sinner's own accumulated sins are the combustible — "wood, and hay, and stubble" (1 Cor 3:12) — so that the fire is not an external cauldron but the soul's own evil set alight. The conscience, receiving back "into the memory all those things of which it had stamped on itself certain signs and forms at the moment of sinning," becomes "an accuser and a witness against itself."

Origen on "God our Physician" and the fire that effects "a restoration"

And when this dissolution and rending asunder of soul shall have been tested by the application of fire, a solidification undoubtedly into a firmer structure will take place, and a restoration be effected. … [I]f the virulence of the disease shall transcend even these remedies, the evil has at last to be burned out by fire; how much more is it to be understood that God our Physician, desiring to remove the defects of our souls, which they had contracted from their different sins and crimes, should employ penal measures of this sort, and should apply even, in addition, the punishment of fire to those who have lost their soundness of mind!

— De Principiis II.10.5–6, source. Here the fire is curative. As a physician resorts to bitter drugs, then the knife, and at last cautery to burn out a disease, so God applies the fire to "remove the defects of our souls" — and the outcome is "a restoration." Compare Clement of Alexandria on reformative chastisement and Gregory of Nyssa on the refiner's fire.

Origen on building on the foundation: "the fire will consume my work on the day of judgment"

This warning applies to you and me as well. If I do not build properly on the foundation already laid for me, then the fire will consume my work on the day of judgment. … If we think what is right and good, then we are building on a foundation of gold. … But if I sin after laying the foundation, then I am building on wood; if I continue, I am building on hay, and finally, if I still go on, I am building on straw.

— Commentary on 1 Corinthians, on 3:10 and 3:12 (surviving Greek catena-fragments), 1 Cor 3:10 · 1 Cor 3:12. In his verse-by-verse commentary Origen reads the passage morally and eschatologically: the "gold, silver, precious stones" are our good thoughts, holy words, and good works; the "wood, hay, straw" are the successive stages of sin built on the one foundation (Christ), and these the fire tries and consumes "on the day of judgment."

Reception and debate

  • Origen reads the fire of 1 Corinthians 3:12–15 as a fire that acts on the person after death. Later witnesses on this timeline who speak of a purifying fire include Gregory of Nyssa in the Greek East and Ambrosiaster and Augustine in the Latin West; John Chrysostom reads the same passage of the penalty of the last judgment.
  • The setting of the fire. Origen describes the fire as medicinal — it heals the soul's defects and issues in "a restoration" — and places it within his teaching on the restoration of all things (see his page on universal restoration). Edward Hanna, in the Catholic Encyclopedia article “Purgatory” (1911), counts Origen among his patristic witnesses: “In Origen the doctrine of purgatory is very clear. If a man departs this life with lighter faults, he is condemned to fire which burns away the lighter materials, and prepares the soul for the kingdom of God, where nothing defiled may enter” (newadvent.org). Teachings attributed to Origen — the pre-existence of souls and universal restoration — were condemned in the sixth-century anathemas against Origenism transmitted with the Second Council of Constantinople (553); the anathemas' relation to the council's own acts is disputed (see Was Origen himself anathematized by an ecumenical council?).
  • A textual note. De Principiis survives in full only in the Latin of Rufinus, who admits to smoothing passages he judged interpolated or over-harsh; its precise wording therefore stands at one remove from Origen's Greek. The image of a fire that tests and burns the "wood, hay, and stubble" also appears in the Greek catena-fragments on 1 Corinthians above and in the Homilies on Jeremiah: the one who "has need of the baptism that comes from fire … comes to that fire, and the fire tests him, and that fire finds wood, hay, and stubble, so as to burn them up" (Hom. 2.3, historicalchristian.faith). In the Homilies on Leviticus he writes, "we shall be purged by another fire" (Hom. 9.8, historicalchristian.faith), and in the Homilies on Luke (extant in Jerome's Latin) Christ stands "in the river of fire," so that "whoever, after the departure from this life, desires to pass over to paradise and has need of cleansing, he may baptize with that stream" (Hom. 24, historicalchristian.faith).
