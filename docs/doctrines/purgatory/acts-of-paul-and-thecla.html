The Acts of Paul and Thecla on purgatory and prayer for the dead

Dates: c. 160 · Wikipedia
Summary: The apocryphal Acts of Paul and Thecla (part of the Acts of Paul, composed in Asia Minor in the later second century) is the earliest Christian narrative of prayer for the dead on this timeline. Queen Tryphaena's deceased daughter Falconilla appears to her in a dream asking that the holy stranger Thecla pray for her, "that I may be transferred to the place of the just"; Tryphaena begs Thecla to do so, and Thecla prays aloud that Falconilla "may live for ever." The work is dated earlier than Tertullian's report of offerings for the dead.

Claims & evidence

The dead Falconilla asks, in a dream, that Thecla pray her into "the place of the just"

For her daughter Falconilla had died, and said to her in a dream: Mother, you shall have this stranger Thecla in my place, in order that she may pray concerning me, and that I may be transferred to the place of the just.

— Acts of Paul and Thecla 28 (Roberts-Donaldson / ANF translation), newadvent.org. In the dream the departed Falconilla asks to be "transferred to the place of the just," and names Thecla's prayer as the means.

Tryphaena asks Thecla to pray for her dead child

My second child Thecla, come and pray for my child, that she may live for ever; for this I saw in my sleep.

— Acts of Paul and Thecla 29, newadvent.org. Tryphaena, who has taken Thecla in as a "second child," turns the dream into a request: she asks Thecla to intercede so that the dead Falconilla "may live for ever."

Thecla prays aloud for Falconilla's eternal life

God most high, grant to this woman according to her wish, that her daughter Falconilla may live for ever.

— Acts of Paul and Thecla 29, newadvent.org. Thecla complies with a supplication to God for the eternal life of the departed Falconilla, naming her.

Reception and debate

  • On how the ancient record of prayer for the dead has been read, see Was prayer for the dead uncontested in the ancient Church?
  • Character of the work. The Acts of Paul is apocryphal. Tertullian reports (De Baptismo 17) that a presbyter in Asia composed it, "confessing that he had done it from love of Paul," and was removed from his office (historicalchristian.faith).
  • Falconilla. In the narrative Falconilla is the daughter of a pagan queen and is not said to have been baptized.
  • Placement on the timeline. It follows 2 Maccabees and precedes Tertullian. See also the purgatory timeline.
