Caesarius of Arles on purgatory and prayer for the dead

Dates: c. 520 · Wikipedia
Summary: Preaching on 1 Corinthians 3, Caesarius, bishop of Arles, teaches that the fire of which the apostle speaks purges only slight sins, not grave ones. Those who build serious sins on the foundation of Christ are not cleansed by that transitory fire but face the eternal flames.

Claims & evidence

The purgatorial fire purges slight sins, not serious ones

There are many people who understand this text incorrectly, deceiving themselves with a false assurance. They believe that if they build serious sins upon the foundation of Christ, those very offenses can be purified by transitory flames, and they themselves can later reach eternal life. This kind of understanding must be corrected. People deceive themselves when they flatter themselves in this way. For in that fire it is slight sins which are purged, not serious ones. Even worse, it is not only the greater sins but the smaller ones as well which can ruin a person.

— Sermon 179, §1 (trans. Mary Magdeleine Mueller, Fathers of the Church 47), historicalchristian.faith. Caesarius is correcting those who comfort themselves with a false reading of 1 Corinthians 3:11–15 — supposing that grave sins built on Christ can be burned away by "transitory flames" so the sinner still reaches eternal life. He teaches that the cleansing fire reaches only "slight sins … not serious ones." Capital sins bar heaven outright; the unrepentant of grave sin "cannot be purged in that transitory fire of which the apostle spoke, but the eternal flames will torture him without any remedy" (Sermon 179.2, as quoted in Catholic Answers, "What the Early Church Believed: Mortal Sin," catholic.com).

Reception and debate

  • Augustine, a century earlier, had likewise limited the post-mortem fire to believers who will inherit the kingdom, rejecting the reading of "saved, yet so as by fire" as a license for the unrepentant.
  • Other readings of 1 Corinthians 3 appear on the purgatory timeline: Ambrosiaster reads verse 15 of a purifying fire, and John Chrysostom reads the fire as the penalty of judgment.
  • Catholic Answers dates Sermon 179 to 522 ("What the Early Church Believed: Mortal Sin," catholic.com). The wording above (the Mueller / Fathers of the Church 47 translation) is the text given on the 1 Corinthians 3:15 verse page, indexed there to Sermons 179.1.
