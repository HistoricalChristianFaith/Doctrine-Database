Epiphanius of Salamis on purgatory and prayer for the dead

Dates: c. 375 · Wikipedia
Summary: Epiphanius, bishop of Salamis, lists among the heresies of his Panarion the teaching of Aerius, who held that praying for the dead is pointless, and defends the naming of and prayer for the departed as a practice "the church has received" (75.6.4), "useful" even if it "does not force back the whole of guilty charges laid to them."

Claims & evidence

Epiphanius's answer: naming and praying for the dead is "useful"

As to mentioning the names of the dead, how is there anything very useful in that? What is more timely or more excellent than that those who are still here should believe that the departed do live, and that they have not retreated into nothingness, but that they exist and are alive with the Master? … Useful too is the prayer fashioned on their behalf, even if it does not force back the whole of guilty charges laid to them. And it is useful also, because in this world we often stumble either voluntarily or involuntarily, and thus it is a reminder to do better.

— Panarion 75 (Against the Aerians), §8 (Frank Williams trans.), source. Epiphanius answers Aerius's objection — that prayer for the dead is useless — by arguing that the practice confesses the survival of the departed and is "useful" to them, "even if it does not force back the whole of guilty charges laid to them," and that it reminds the living "to do better."

Reception and debate

  • Aerius (Panarion 75) rejected prayer and offering for the dead as pointless — "if the prayer of the people here has benefited the people there, no one should practice piety or perform good works" (75.3.5). Epiphanius lists the teaching among the heresies and answers that the practice is "useful." How this exchange has been read — whether prayer for the dead was a received, world-wide custom, a custom the Fathers held without agreement on its effect, or a practice from which Aerius dissented on principle — is set out at Was prayer for the dead uncontested in the ancient Church?
  • Epiphanius's clause "even if it does not force back the whole of guilty charges laid to them" speaks of charges laid against the dead that the prayer addresses. Compare Cyprian's witness to the oblation for the dead and the other entries on the purgatory timeline.
  • The text is quoted from Frank Williams's English translation of the Panarion (Brill). Other renderings of §8 read "even if it does not cut off all of [the] sins" ⚠ unverified.
