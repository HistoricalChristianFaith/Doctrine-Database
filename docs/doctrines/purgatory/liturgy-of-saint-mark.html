The Liturgy of Saint Mark on purgatory and prayer for the dead

Dates: c. 350 · Wikipedia
Summary: The Greek Liturgy of Saint Mark, the historic anaphora of Alexandria, contains an explicit commemoration of the dead: the deacon "reads the record of the dead" (the diptychs) and the priest prays God to "give peace to the souls of our fathers and brethren who have fallen asleep in Jesus" and to "deem them worthy of the kingdom of heaven." It is a Eucharistic intercession for the departed from the Alexandrian church.

Claims & evidence

A prayer for peace to the souls of those who have fallen asleep

O Lord our God, give peace to the souls of our fathers and brethren who have fallen asleep in Jesus, remembering our forefathers of old, our fathers, patriarchs, prophets, apostles, martyrs, confessors, bishops, and the souls of all the holy and just men who have died in the Lord.

— The Divine Liturgy of the Holy Apostle and Evangelist Mark, source. Within the anaphora the priest petitions God on behalf of "the souls of our fathers and brethren who have fallen asleep in Jesus" — the dead are the objects of petition at the altar.

A petition that the dead be deemed worthy of the kingdom — read over the diptychs of the dead

The Deacon reads the record of the dead. … Give peace, O Sovereign Lord our God, to the souls of all who dwell in the tabernacles of Thy saints. Graciously bestow upon them in Thy kingdom Thy promised blessing … Give peace to their souls, and deem them worthy of the kingdom of heaven.

— The Divine Liturgy of the Holy Apostle and Evangelist Mark, source. The rubric "the Deacon reads the record of the dead" is the reading of the diptychs — the named lists of the departed for whom the church prays. The accompanying prayer asks God to "deem them worthy of the kingdom of heaven," an intercession for the final blessedness of the dead.

Reception and debate

  • As with all the anaphoras, dating is layered: it is placed here c. 350 as an Alexandrian witness. ⚠ unverified: that the developed Greek text is fourth-to-fifth century and rests on older Alexandrian strata, with the Strasbourg Papyrus preserving a third-to-fourth-century kernel of the Mark-type prayer. The Coptic Liturgy of Saint Cyril is in the same Alexandrian line.
  • The reading of the diptychs of the dead has parallels in the commemorations of the Liturgy of Addai and Mari and the Liturgy of Saint James; Epiphanius defends the naming of the dead against Aerius. See Was prayer for the dead uncontested in the ancient Church?
  • On the fire of 1 Corinthians 3, a separate strand of the history, see Origen, John Chrysostom, and Thomas Aquinas.
