The Westminster Confession on purgatory and prayer for the dead

Dates: 1646 · Wikipedia
Summary: The Westminster Confession of Faith (completed 1646 by the Westminster Assembly) rejects purgatory in a clause of chapter XXXII, "Of the State of Men after Death, and of the Resurrection of the Dead." The Confession recognises two places for departed souls: at death the righteous are received immediately into the highest heavens, "being then made perfect in holiness," and the wicked are cast into hell — and "besides these two places for souls separated from their bodies, the Scripture acknowledgeth none." In chapter XXI it states that prayer is to be made "for all sorts of men living, or that shall live hereafter; but not for the dead."

Claims & evidence

"Besides these two places … the Scripture acknowledgeth none"

Besides these two places for souls separated from their bodies, the Scripture acknowledgeth none.

— Westminster Confession of Faith, XXXII.1, creeds.net. The Confession names two destinations for departed souls and states that Scripture acknowledges no other; it thereby rejects a third, purgatorial place.

The righteous "made perfect in holiness" at death

The souls of the righteous, being then made perfect in holiness, are received into the highest heavens, where they behold the face of God in light and glory, waiting for the full redemption of their bodies; and the souls of the wicked are cast into hell, where they remain in torments and utter darkness, reserved to the judgment of the great day.

— Westminster Confession of Faith, XXXII.1, creeds.net. On the Confession's account the perfecting of the righteous takes place at death ("being then made perfect in holiness"), and they "behold the face of God in light and glory" while awaiting the resurrection of the body.

Souls "neither die nor sleep" and "immediately return to God"

The bodies of men, after death, return to dust, and see corruption; but their souls (which neither die nor sleep), having an immortal subsistence, immediately return to God who gave them.

— Westminster Confession of Faith, XXXII.1, creeds.net. The Confession denies that souls die or sleep after death and states that they "immediately return to God who gave them."

Reception and debate

  • Calvin argues against purgatory from the sufficiency of Christ's satisfaction (it "makes void the cross of Christ"); the Confession states that Scripture acknowledges no place for departed souls besides heaven and hell.
  • This page treats the Confession's rejection of purgatory and prayer for the dead. Its broader teaching on the conscious survival of the soul and the resurrection — the same chapter XXXII — is set out on the intermediate-state detail page, with which it shares the WCF 32.1 text.
  • On prayer for the dead the Confession states: "Prayer is to be made for things lawful, and for all sorts of men living, or that shall live hereafter; but not for the dead, nor for those of whom it may be known that they have sinned the sin unto death" (XXI.4, creeds.net).
