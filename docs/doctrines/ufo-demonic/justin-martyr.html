Justin Martyr on aliens, UFOs, and the demonic

Dates: c. 155 (the First Apology) · Wikipedia
Summary: The earliest seated witness for the reading's oldest premise — that unseen spirits present a false appearance of themselves to deceive human beings, are mistaken for exalted powers, and so are "called gods." Writing around 155, Justin says the "evil demons" of old were "effecting apparitions of themselves," terrified those who saw them, and were taken for deities, "each the name which each of the demons chose for himself." That is the root of the modern "costume-change" thesis (gods → fairies → "aliens") long before the flying saucer: a deceiving intelligence wearing whatever guise the age will believe.

Claims & evidence

Demons "effecting apparitions of themselves" were mistaken for gods

For the truth shall be spoken; since of old these evil demons, effecting apparitions of themselves, both defiled women and corrupted boys, and showed such fearful sights to men, that those who did not use their reason in judging of the actions that were done, were struck with terror; and being carried away by fear, and not knowing that these were demons, they called them gods, and gave to each the name which each of the demons chose for himself.

— The First Apology, Chapter V, source. This is the pivotal text for the demonic reading of the UFO phenomenon. Justin's claim is not merely that the pagan gods were false; it is that a real, deceiving intelligence staged an appearance of itself — an "apparition," a "fearful sight" — engineered to overwhelm the reason of the witness and be received as something higher than it was. The mechanism the modern writers ascribe to abduction encounters (a dread-laden, reason- disabling manifestation that presents itself as a superior being) Justin already ascribes to the "gods": the same strategy of impersonation, dressed to a different age. It is this passage, not the Genesis 6 material alone, that grounds the "gods → fairies → aliens" continuity the timeline traces.

The fallen angels "begat children who are those that are called demons"

But the angels transgressed this appointment, and were captivated by love of women, and begat children who are those that are called demons; and besides, they afterwards subdued the human race to themselves, partly by magical writings, and partly by fears and the punishments they occasioned, and partly by teaching them to offer sacrifices, and incense, and libations, of which things they stood in need after they were enslaved by lustful passions; and among men they sowed murders, wars, adulteries, intemperate deeds, and all wickedness.

— The Second Apology, Chapter V, source. Justin supplies the reading's origin story for the deceiving spirits: they are (on the Enochic reading he shares) tied to the Genesis 6 transgression, and having "subdued the human race to themselves" they turned the worship owed to God toward themselves — "the poets," he adds elsewhere, later mistook their doings for the deeds of gods. Read with the First Apology passage above, the two halves of the reading are already in place by the mid-second century: who the deceivers are (fallen powers and their offspring) and how they work (staged apparitions received as gods).
