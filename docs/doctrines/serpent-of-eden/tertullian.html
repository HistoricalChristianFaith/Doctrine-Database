Tertullian on the Serpent of Eden

Dates: c. 210 (Tertullian, c. 155–220) · Wikipedia
Summary: Roots the whole Fall in the devil's impatience and envy that man was set over creation, and is the earliest to press both Ezekiel 28 and Isaiah 14 into service as the devil's own fall.

Claims & evidence

The devil's envy, breathed on the woman through the serpent's speech

Therefore I detect the nativity of impatience in the devil himself, at that very time when he impatiently bore that the Lord God subjected the universal works which He had made to His own image, that is, to man. For if he had endured (that), he would not have grieved; nor would he have envied man if he had not grieved. Accordingly he deceived him, because he had envied him; but he had envied because he had grieved… The woman, immediately on being met by him… was, through his very speech with her, breathed on by a spirit infected with impatience…

— Of Patience V, source. The motive is again envy at man's dignity; the serpent's "very speech" is the channel of a spirit "infected with impatience."

Ezekiel 28 read of the devil

If you turn to the prophecy of Ezekiel, you will at once perceive that this angel was both by creation good and by choice corrupt. For he speaks of the devil there in the person of the prince of Tyre.

— Against Marcion II.10, source. "By creation good and by choice corrupt" is the anti-dualist point the later tradition inherits: the rebel was made good and fell by will.

Isaiah 14 read of the devil

even as the prophet makes him say: "I will set my throne above the stars; … I will go up above the clouds; I will be like the Most High." This must mean the devil, whom in another passage … we shall recognize in the appellation the god of this world.

— Against Marcion V, source. Tertullian is the earliest firm witness to reading both prophetic fall-texts of the devil — the pattern Origen will systematize.

Notes

  • With Tertullian the two great "fall of the devil" proof-texts (Ezekiel 28, Isaiah 14) are already fixed to the Eden adversary. The wiki weighs each reading adversarially on the serpent's-fall argument pages.
