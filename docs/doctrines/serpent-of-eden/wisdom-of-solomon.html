The Wisdom of Solomon on the Serpent of Eden

Dates: c. 50 BC (Alexandrian Judaism; deuterocanonical in Catholic and Orthodox canons, among the Apocrypha in Protestant usage) · Wikipedia
Summary: Attributes the entry of death into the world to the envy of the devil; the verse does not name the serpent. It is the earliest witness on this timeline.

Claims & evidence

"Through envy of the devil came death into the world"

Nevertheless through envy of the devil came death into the world: and they that do hold of his side do find it.

— Wisdom of Solomon 2:24, source. Written in Greek at Alexandria, Wisdom attributes the entry of death into the world to the envy of the diabolos. Irenaeus, Tertullian and Augustine, on their pages, also give envy as the devil's motive.

Reception and debate

  • The verse names a "devil" but not the serpent; on this timeline the serpent is first called "the Devil, and Satan" in Revelation 12:9. The Wisdom of Solomon is also a witness supplying the "deified dead" and giant-cult material on the Nephilim timeline.
