The Wisdom of Solomon on the Serpent of Eden

Dates: c. 50 BC (Alexandrian Judaism; deuterocanonical) · Wikipedia
Summary: The earliest surviving witness that a personal, envious devil stood behind the death that entered the world in Eden — though it does not yet name that adversary the serpent.

Claims & evidence

"Through envy of the devil came death into the world"

Nevertheless through envy of the devil came death into the world: and they that do hold of his side do find it.

— Wisdom of Solomon 2:24, source. Written in Greek at Alexandria, Wisdom reads the death that entered in Genesis 3 as the work of an envious spiritual adversary (Greek diabolos). It is the seed of the later serpent–devil identification, and it fixes envy as the motive the Fathers — Irenaeus, Tertullian, Augustine — will repeat.

Notes

  • The verse names a "devil" but not the serpent; the explicit serpent = devil equation waits for the New Testament (Revelation 12:9). The Wisdom of Solomon also appears elsewhere on this wiki, supplying the "deified dead" and giant-cult material on the Nephilim timeline.
