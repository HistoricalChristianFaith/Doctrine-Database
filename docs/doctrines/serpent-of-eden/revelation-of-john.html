The Revelation to John on the Serpent of Eden

Dates: c. 95 (traditionally ascribed to the apostle John) · Wikipedia
Summary: Calls the dragon cast out of heaven with his angels "that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan."

Claims & evidence

"That old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan"

And the great dragon was cast out, that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world: he was cast out into the earth, and his angels were cast out with him.

— Revelation 12:9 (cf. 20:2), source. Michael Heiser cites this verse and 20:2 for the serpent of Genesis 3 (see his page). The fall is a casting "out into the earth" together with "his angels." Revelation 20:2 repeats the identification: "that old serpent, which is the Devil, and Satan."

Jesus on the fall from heaven

And he said unto them, I beheld Satan as lightning fall from heaven.

— Luke 10:18, source. Satan's fall is pictured as lightning falling from heaven.

Reception and debate

  • The later witnesses on this timeline all connect the serpent with the devil. They differ on the relation between the two: Irenaeus, Augustine and John Chrysostom describe an animal used by a fallen angel, while Michael Heiser and Fr. Stephen De Young read the figure of Genesis 3 as a divine or spiritual being of God's court.
