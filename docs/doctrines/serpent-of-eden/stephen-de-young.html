Fr. Stephen De Young on the Serpent of Eden

Dates: c. 2018 (Fr. Stephen De Young, b. 1980) · Wikipedia
Summary: Gives the same reading an Orthodox frame — the serpent a spiritual, "clever" being; Ezekiel 28 an angelic being in God's presence; the garden-and-mountain the merged "dwelling place of the gods" imagery — so that Genesis 3's serpent and Ezekiel 28's cherub are "commensurate."

Claims & evidence

The Hebrew word denotes a deceptive or clever being, not only a snake

The Hebrew word used, however, means not only a snake or serpent, but also refers to a being which is deceptive or clever

— "Who is the Devil?", The Whole Counsel Blog (Ancient Faith, 5 Dec 2018), source. De Young reaches Heiser's conclusion from the Fathers' side: the figure of Genesis 3 is a spiritual being, not merely an animal.

Ezekiel 28's cherub is "commensurate" with the Eden serpent

Thus, the identification of the Devil as a serpent in Gen 3 and a cherub in Ezek 28 are actually commensurate.

— "Who is the Devil?", The Whole Counsel Blog (Ancient Faith, 5 Dec 2018), source. He reads the cherub "as an angelic being… coated in precious gems and dwelling in the presence of God," in a setting where "the dwelling place of the gods was conceived of as a garden or as a mountain of assembly," here "merged to describe the dwelling place of the true God of Israel."

Notes

  • De Young develops the reading at length in The Religion of the Apostles (Ancient Faith, 2021). His Orthodox framing and Heiser's Semitic-philology framing converge on the same "unseen realm" account of Eden.
