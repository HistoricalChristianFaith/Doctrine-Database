Ephrem the Syrian on the Serpent of Eden

Dates: c. 373 (Ephrem the Syrian, c. 306–373) · Wikipedia
Summary: Keeps the serpent a real animal but the deceiver a spirit working through it — the Syriac form of the "mouthpiece" reading.

Claims & evidence

"Satan spoke through it"

As for the serpent's speech, either Adam understood the serpent's own mode of communication, or Satan spoke through it, or the serpent posed the question in his mind and speech was given to it, or Satan sought from God that speech be given to the serpent for a short time.

— Commentary on Genesis II.16.1, tr. E. G. Mathews & J. P. Amar (Fathers of the Church 91, 1994), p. 103. Ephrem canvasses the options but in each the beast is a real creature and the deceiver a distinct spirit; he does not collapse the serpent into a divine being.

Notes

  • The Syriac tradition holds the beast and the "accursed one" together without merging them — nearer to Augustine's "mouthpiece" than to the modern reading of the serpent-figure itself as a member of God's council.
