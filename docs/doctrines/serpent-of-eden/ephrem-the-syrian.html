Ephrem the Syrian on the Serpent of Eden

Dates: c. 373 (Ephrem the Syrian, c. 306–373) · Wikipedia
Summary: Lists four ways the serpent might have spoken, two of which bring in Satan, as speaker through it or as petitioner for its speech.

Claims & evidence

"Satan spoke through it"

As for the serpent's speech, either Adam understood the serpent's own mode of communication, or Satan spoke through it, or the serpent posed the question in his mind and speech was given to it, or Satan sought from God that speech be given to the serpent for a short time.

— Commentary on Genesis II.16.1, tr. E. G. Mathews & J. P. Amar (Fathers of the Church 91, 1994), p. 103. Ephrem leaves the question open among four options. In each the serpent is a creature; in the second and fourth Satan speaks through it or asks God to give it speech.

Reception and debate

  • Ephrem's second option, "Satan spoke through it," matches the "mouthpiece" language of Augustine (City of God XIV.11) on this timeline.
