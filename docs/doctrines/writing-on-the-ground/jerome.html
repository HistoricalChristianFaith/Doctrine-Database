Jerome — "he wrote their sins"

Dates: c. 415 (the Dialogue against the Pelagians); Jerome c. 347–420 · Wikipedia
Summary: Jerome names the content of the writing as the sins of the accusers. In one sentence of the Dialogue against the Pelagians he names it ("the sins… of those who were making the accusation, as well as the sins of all mortal beings") and gives a proof-text (Jeremiah 17:13, "Those who depart from you shall be written in the earth"). The Interlinear Gloss, as Bonaventure reports it, later gives "their sins," and from about the ninth century some manuscripts add "the sins of every one of them" to John 8:8.

The content named (Against the Pelagians 2.17)

Jerome is arguing, against the Pelagians, that all are sinners — and reaches for the adultery pericope as an illustration. In passing he names the content:

The Scribes and Pharisees kept accusing her and kept earnestly pressing the case, for they wished to stone her to death, according to the law. "But Jesus, stooping down, began to write with his finger on the ground," the sins, to be sure, of those who were making the accusation, as well as the sins of all mortal beings according to what is written in the prophet, "Those who depart from you shall be written in the earth."

— Dialogue against the Pelagians 2.17. Two features mark the passage. First, it names a content: where Augustine reads the act as sign and names no words, Jerome says what was written — sins, the accusers' and everyone's. Second, it gives a proof-text. On Jerome's reading of Jeremiah 17:13 ("Those who depart from you shall be written in the earth") the ground-writing enrolls the departing accusers among those "written in the earth." The verse recurs in Bonaventure and Aquinas.1

Placement on the timeline

Jerome writes at almost the same time as Augustine. Augustine reads the gesture and names no content; Jerome names the content and gives it a proof-text. The later catalogues list both — Bonaventure lists "the Interlinear Gloss says that He was writing 'their sins'" alongside Augustine's and Ambrose's options, and Aquinas ranks Ambrose's Jeremiah 22 oracle below the "better opinion" that Jesus wrote his own spoken words, and cites Jeremiah 17:13 for his first reading of the ground itself. Around the ninth century a cluster of mostly Byzantine witnesses added to John 8:8 the words "the sins of every one of them." Metzger judges the addition a later expansion made "in order to satisfy pious curiosity concerning what it was that Jesus wrote upon the ground," and Jennifer Knust and Tommy Wasserman (2010) trace the "sins of the accusers" reading from Jerome through the Gloss to the scribal addition.2

Notes

  • Jerome names a content; Augustine reads the gesture. The schoolmen (Bonaventure, Aquinas) catalogue both, alongside Ambrose's Jeremiah oracle. See the summary timeline for the later scribal gloss.
  • The proof-text Jerome uses (Jeremiah 17:13) is the same verse Augustine and Aquinas invoke for the "written on earth, not in heaven" figure — a verse shared across these Latin readings of the scene.
  • The Evangelist does not give the content (John 8:6, 8); Jerome gives his reading in a single sentence of an argument that all are sinners, not in an exegesis of John 8.
