Was Thomas the twin brother of Jesus?

Disputed reading · on Thomas · witness: the Acts of Thomas

Related readings: Did Thomas evangelize India? · Was Jude the Lord’s brother the apostle Thomas, Jesus’ physical twin? (the same “Judas Thomas” name, read under the question of Jesus’ brothers)

The passage

These are the secret sayings which the living Jesus spoke and which Didymos Judas Thomas wrote down.

— incipit of the Gospel of Thomas.1

Then said Thomas, which is called Didymus, unto his fellowdisciples, Let us also go, that we may die with him.

— John 11:16.2

The question

“Thomas” (Aramaic te’oma) and “Didymus” (Greek didymos) both mean “twin,” and Syriac sources call the apostle Judas Thomas; the Gospels do not say whose twin he was. The question is whether the epithet names him the twin of Jesus, as some Syriac and Nag Hammadi texts present him, or marks him as a twin of someone the sources do not name. No modern scholarly proponent of either reading is named in the sources surveyed here.

Thomas as the twin of Jesus

In this reading “the Twin” is the twin of the Lord himself, which is why he is the apostle chosen to receive the Saviour’s secret words.

Held by: the Acts of Thomas (Syriac, c. 220–240); the Book of Thomas the Contender (Nag Hammadi II,7).3

The man is doubly labelled “the Twin”: “Thomas” renders the Aramaic te’oma, and John three times supplies the Greek equivalent Didymus. The Syriac-speaking church gave him the personal name Judas, and the form Judas Thomas, “Judas the Twin,” is old and widespread in the East: Eusebius already reports the Edessene document that calls him “Judas, who was also called Thomas.” The Acts of Thomas repeatedly presents Jesus and Thomas as look-alikes whom onlookers confuse, and speaks of Thomas as the Lord’s twin. The Book of Thomas the Contender opens with the risen Christ addressing “my twin and true companion.” Across the Thomas literature the twin of the Saviour, as his closest likeness, is uniquely fit to receive his hidden teaching, and the double epithet of the Gospel of Thomas’s incipit names the one who wrote it down. Read together, these texts present a coherent tradition in which Thomas is the physical twin of Jesus.

“The Twin” as a by-name whose referent is unnamed, or as a symbol

In this reading “Thomas” and “Didymus” tell us that the apostle was a twin, or carry a figurative sense, without making him a brother of Jesus.

Held by: ⚠ unverified — no named proponent located yet.

A by-name meaning “twin” records that a man was a twin, as “Bartholomew” records that a man was a son of Tolmai; it does not say whose. The Gospels name the brothers of Jesus as James, Joses, Judas and Simon, with unnamed sisters, and Thomas is not among them; a twin who shared Mary’s womb would belong in that family notice.4 For those who hold this reading, a literal twin of Jesus would also bear on the virginal conception and the uniqueness of the Incarnation. The twin-of-Jesus motif appears in the Syriac and Nag Hammadi Thomas texts, which connect it with their claim to secret revelation. Elsewhere, the epithet is taken as marking a natural twin of an ordinary sibling, or as a symbol of the man of two minds, faith and doubt, whose story John tells (⚠ unverified: no source for these uses is cited here).
