The Septuagint on Solomon's Stalls

Dates: c. 250–150 BC (the Old Greek of Kingdoms) · Wikipedia
Summary: The Greek Bible carries both numbers. Its reorganized 3 Kingdoms puts "forty thousand" in a supplement after 2:46 and "four thousand" at 10:26 — so the discrepancy is already inside the tradition by the second century BC, and the Greek cannot be enlisted as a clean witness for the smaller figure.

Claims & evidence

"Forty thousand" in the supplement after 3 Kingdoms 2:46

"And Solomon had forty thousand brood mares for his chariots, and twelve thousand horses."

— Septuagint, 3 Kingdoms 2:46 (supplement), Elpenor bilingual edition (Greek tessarakonta chiliades tokades hippoi). The Old Greek of Kingdoms reorders the material of Hebrew 1 Kings 4–5, gathering scattered notices about Solomon into a block of "miscellanies" after 3 Kingdoms 2:46; the horse-count lands there, and it agrees with the Masoretic "forty thousand."

"Four thousand" at 3 Kingdoms 10:26

"And Solomon had four thousand mares for his chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen."

— Septuagint, 3 Kingdoms 10:26, Elpenor bilingual edition (Greek tessares chiliades thleiai hippoi). At the point corresponding to Hebrew 1 Kings 10:26 the Greek gives the horse-count again, this time as four thousand — matching the Chronicler.

Notes

  • The Greek doubles the problem instead of solving it. Because the Old Greek of Kingdoms both relocates material and repeats it, the horse-notice surfaces twice with two different numbers. This is the crucial difference from the height of Goliath, where the Septuagint, the Qumran scroll, and Josephus line up as a united front for the shorter figure. Here the Greek is split down the middle.
  • It does establish that the inflation is old. Whatever produced the "forty thousand," it was present in a Hebrew exemplar early enough to reach the Greek translators before c. 150 BC — not a medieval Masoretic invention. So the argument for "four thousand" cannot lean on the antiquity of the witnesses; it has to rest on the internal parallel with Chronicles and the arithmetic of the chariots.
  • Modern translators still cite the Greek for the emendation. The NIV footnote at 1 Kings 4:26 — "Some Septuagint manuscripts (see also 2 Chron. 9:25); Hebrew forty" — points to the 10:26 branch of exactly this split tradition. The full weighing is on the argument page.
