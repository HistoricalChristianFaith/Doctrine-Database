The Septuagint on Solomon's Stalls

Dates: c. 250–150 BC (the Old Greek of Kingdoms) · Wikipedia
Summary: The Greek Bible carries both numbers. Its 3 Kingdoms has "forty thousand" in a supplement after 2:46 and "four thousand" at 10:26, so both figures stand in the Greek tradition by the second century BC.

Claims & evidence

"Forty thousand" in the supplement after 3 Kingdoms 2:46

"And Solomon had forty thousand brood mares for his chariots, and twelve thousand horses."

— Septuagint, 3 Kingdoms 2:46 (supplement), Elpenor bilingual edition (Greek tessarakonta chiliades tokades hippoi). The Old Greek of Kingdoms has a block of "miscellanies" after 3 Kingdoms 2:46 gathering notices about Solomon that stand in Hebrew 1 Kings 4–5; the horse-count stands there, and it agrees with the Masoretic "forty thousand."

"Four thousand" at 3 Kingdoms 10:26

"And Solomon had four thousand mares for his chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen."

— Septuagint, 3 Kingdoms 10:26, Elpenor bilingual edition (Greek tessares chiliades thleiai hippoi). At the point corresponding to Hebrew 1 Kings 10:26 the Greek gives the horse-count again, this time as four thousand — matching the Chronicler.

Reception and debate

  • Two figures in one translation. The Old Greek of Kingdoms carries the horse-notice twice, with two different numbers. On the height of Goliath, by contrast, the Septuagint, the Qumran scroll, and Josephus give the shorter figure.
  • Where each figure stands. The "forty thousand" stands in the Greek supplement after 2:46, in the Old Greek of Kingdoms (c. 250–150 BC, as dated above); the "four thousand" stands at 3 Kingdoms 10:26 and in Chronicles.
  • The NIV note. The NIV footnote at 1 Kings 4:26 — "Some Septuagint manuscripts (see also 2 Chron. 9:25); Hebrew forty" — credits "four" to Septuagint manuscripts. The readings of the difference are set out on the interpretation page.
  • The supplementary blocks. The Old Greek of Kings has two supplementary blocks — the "miscellanies" after 3 Kingdoms 2:35 and 2:46 — that repeat verses standing elsewhere in the narrative, and the 2:46 block holds one of the two horse-counts. Whether that arrangement reaches the Hebrew stage (a variant edition) or is an inner-Greek reshuffling is a separate disputed question; see the Septuagint-origin timeline's page on the 3 Kingdoms reorganization.
