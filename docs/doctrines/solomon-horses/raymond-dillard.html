Raymond Dillard on Solomon's Stalls

Dates: c. 1987 (2 Chronicles, Word Biblical Commentary) · Wikipedia
Summary: The cautionary modern voice. Dillard agrees the ratios favour Chronicles' four thousand over Kings' forty thousand, but warns that the variants are so tangled that the original number cannot be recovered with confidence.

Claims & evidence

A "thicket of textual difficulties" — the original number is uncertain

"…the number of Solomon's chariot horses is surrounded by a thicket of textual difficulties… the diversity and interrelatedness of the variants suggests a basic difficulty in the history of transmission, such that it is not possible to speak with confidence regarding the original text. Though the ratios work better in the Chronicles passage (4000/1400) than in the Kings passage (40,000/1400), we do not know the exact number."

— 2 Chronicles (Word Biblical Commentary 15; Word, 1987), on 9:25, cited via the survey at BibleHub. Dillard grants the proportionality point — four thousand fits the 1,400 chariots, forty thousand does not — while declining to certify four thousand as the demonstrable autograph reading.

Notes

  • He splits the two claims that apologists run together. "The forty thousand is too large" and "the four thousand is the original" are different theses; Dillard affirms the first and suspends judgement on the second. His caution is the honest counterweight to Archer's "undoubtedly" and Keil & Delitzsch's confident emendation — and it is why the argument page rates the inflation-claim sound while flagging the exact-original-wording question as genuinely open.
  • The variants are interrelated. The Masoretic Kings (forty thousand), the Chronicler (four thousand), and the split Greek (both) form a tangle in which a single clean stemma is hard to draw — the reason a text-critic is warier than a harmonist about naming the original.
