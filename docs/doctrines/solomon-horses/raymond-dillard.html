Raymond Dillard on Solomon's Stalls

Dates: c. 1987 (2 Chronicles, Word Biblical Commentary) · Wikipedia
Summary: A modern commentator on Chronicles. Dillard holds that the ratios favour Chronicles' four thousand over Kings' forty thousand, and that the variants are so tangled that the original number cannot be recovered with confidence.

Claims & evidence

A "thicket of textual difficulties"

"…the number of Solomon's chariot horses is surrounded by a thicket of textual difficulties… the diversity and interrelatedness of the variants suggests a basic difficulty in the history of transmission, such that it is not possible to speak with confidence regarding the original text. Though the ratios work better in the Chronicles passage (4000/1400) than in the Kings passage (40,000/1400), we do not know the exact number."

— 2 Chronicles (Word Biblical Commentary 15; Word, 1987), on 9:25, cited via the survey at BibleHub; that survey does not reproduce the quotation, and the commentary itself has not been opened (⚠ unverified). Dillard grants the proportionality point while not naming four thousand as the original reading.

Reception and debate

  • The ratios and the original number. Dillard is quoted as granting that the ratios work better in Chronicles while holding that "we do not know the exact number" (⚠ unverified). Archer ("undoubtedly") and Keil & Delitzsch read four thousand as the original figure. The readings are set out on the interpretation page.
  • The variants. The variants Dillard describes are the Masoretic Kings (forty thousand), the Chronicler (four thousand), and the split Greek (both).
