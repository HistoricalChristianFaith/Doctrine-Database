Matthew Poole on Solomon's Stalls

Dates: c. 1685 (the Annotations upon the Holy Bible, published posthumously) · Wikipedia
Summary: An early-modern annotator who gives two answers to the difference. His first reports that "forty" may be a transcriber's error for "four"; his second distinguishes the units, holding that Kings and Chronicles use different Hebrew words and count different things.

Claims & evidence

The "stables vs. stalls" distinction: 4,000 stables, 40,000 stalls

"It is not exactly the same Hebrew word which is here and there, though we translate both stalls… the one signifying properly stables, of which there were 4000, the other stalls or partitions for each horse, which were 40,000."

— Annotations upon the Holy Bible, on 1 Kings 4:26, source. Poole's second answer keeps both numbers true by making them count different objects: Chronicles' four thousand are stable-buildings, Kings' forty thousand the individual stalls or partitions within them — roughly ten horses to a stable. On 2 Chronicles 9:25 he reads the Chronicler's figure as "greater stalls, in each of which were ten stalls, in all forty thousand stalls."

The copyist-error answer

Poole's first answer reports the view that the larger figure is a transcription error: "Answ. First, Some acknowledge an error of the transcriber, writing arbahim, forty, for arbah, four, which was an easy mistake." The same option is reported by John Gill on the same verse: "some think that here is a mistake of the copier, of arbaim, forty, for arbah, four." Later commentators on the verse give one or the other of these two answers: a count of different units, or an emended text.

Reception and debate

  • Responses to the units-distinction. Keil & Delitzsch, on the same verse, take the noun as "a rack or box in a stable," one horse to a crib, and read the "forty" as a copyist's error; Jamieson–Fausset–Brown, on 2 Chronicles 9:25, report that "more recent critics reject this mode of solving the difficulty." Both readings are set out on the interpretation page.
  • Two answers side by side. Poole sets a textual answer and a harmonization next to each other. Keil & Delitzsch (1872) and Archer (1982) give the textual explanation.
