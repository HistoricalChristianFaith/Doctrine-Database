Josephus on Solomon's Stalls

Dates: c. 93 (the Antiquities of the Jews) · Wikipedia
Summary: A first-century witness to the larger figure. Retelling Solomon's reign, Josephus gives forty thousand stalls, the figure of the Kings tradition.

Claims & evidence

Forty thousand stalls for the chariot-horses

"Solomon had also so great a number of chariots, that the stalls of his horses for those chariots were forty thousand; and besides these he had twelve thousand horsemen, the one half of which waited upon the king in Jerusalem, and the rest were dispersed abroad, and dwelt in the royal villages; but the same officer who provided for the king’s expenses supplied also the fodder for the horses, and still carried it to the place where the king abode at that time."

— Antiquities of the Jews 8.41, trans. William Whiston, source. Writing in Greek from a Hebrew source at the end of the first century, Josephus follows the "forty thousand" of the Kings tradition, not the "four thousand" of Chronicles.

Reception and debate

  • An early witness to "forty thousand." Josephus's forty thousand agrees with the "forty thousand" carried in the Septuagint's 3 Kingdoms 2:46 supplement.
  • Compared with Goliath's height. On Goliath's height Josephus gives the smaller figure against the Masoretic number; here his figure agrees with the Masoretic text. The readings of the Kings–Chronicles difference are set out on the interpretation page.
  • Report without comment. Josephus reports the larger number without mentioning the Chronicler's smaller one. The discussions of the difference surveyed here begin with the early-modern commentators (Poole, Keil & Delitzsch).
