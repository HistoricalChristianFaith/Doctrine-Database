Josephus on Solomon's Stalls

Dates: c. 93 (the Antiquities of the Jews) · Wikipedia
Summary: A first-century witness to the larger figure. Retelling Solomon's reign, Josephus gives forty thousand stalls — showing that the inflated number was the received text of Kings a thousand years before the Masoretic codices, and that on this crux the oldest witnesses do not converge on the smaller figure.

Claims & evidence

Forty thousand stalls for the chariot-horses

"Solomon had also so great a number of chariots, that the stalls of his horses for those chariots were forty thousand; and besides these he had twelve thousand horsemen, the one half whereof were tall, and in beautiful aspect."

— Antiquities of the Jews 8.41, trans. William Whiston, source. Writing in Greek from a Hebrew source at the end of the first century, Josephus follows the "forty thousand" of the Kings tradition, not the "four thousand" of Chronicles.

Notes

  • The corruption, if it is one, is early. Josephus is roughly a thousand years older than the Masoretic codices, so his forty thousand shows that the inflated number was already the standard reading of Kings by the first century AD — consistent with the "forty thousand" carried in the Septuagint's 3 Kingdoms 2:46 supplement. There is no early witness in which Kings itself reads "four."
  • He cuts the opposite way from the Goliath parallel. On Goliath's height Josephus is a prized witness against the Masoretic number; here he stands with it. That asymmetry is why the case for "four thousand" rests on the internal parallel with Chronicles and the chariot-arithmetic rather than on an early, independent external line. Weighed on the argument page.
  • He preserves, he does not harmonize. Josephus simply reports the larger number without noticing the Chronicler's smaller one — the discrepancy is not yet felt as a problem to be solved. The harmonizing begins only with the later commentators (Poole, Keil & Delitzsch).
