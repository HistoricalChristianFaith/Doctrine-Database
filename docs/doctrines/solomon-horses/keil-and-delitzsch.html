Keil & Delitzsch on Solomon's Stalls

Dates: c. 1872 (the Biblical Commentary on the Books of Kings) · Wikipedia
Summary: A two-author commentary (C. F. Keil & Franz Delitzsch), not a single person. It gives the crux its influential verdict: the Hebrew "forty" of 1 Kings 4:26 is a copyist's error for "four," the figure preserved by 2 Chronicles 9:25.

Claims & evidence

"Forty" is an old copyist's error for "four"

"ארבּעים (40) is an old copyist's error for ארבּעה (4), which we find in the parallel passage 2 Chronicles 9:25."

— Biblical Commentary on the Old Testament, on 1 Kings 4:26 (5:6 in the Hebrew versification), source (also at StudyLight). Keil & Delitzsch locate the slip in the Hebrew word itself: ʾarbāʿîm ("forty") written where the shorter ʾarbāʿā ("four") stood, a near-identical unpointed form.

Notes

  • Chronicles keeps the original. On their reading the Chronicler transmits the number that stood in the older text of Kings, and the Masoretic Kings the corruption — the same camp, and the same commentators, that held Chronicles original against Samuel in the two-slayers crux (with Kennicott).
  • A word-slip, not a decimal slip. Keil & Delitzsch frame the error as a confusion of two similar Hebrew words rather than a dropped or added zero; Archer later frames the same result as a "multiplication by ten." Both mechanisms deliver an inflated Kings number over an original four thousand — the two are compared on the argument page.
  • It does not prove the original wording. That "forty" is inflated is one claim; that "four" is demonstrably the original text is a stronger one, and modern text-critics such as Dillard are more cautious about the second than Keil & Delitzsch's confident emendation.
