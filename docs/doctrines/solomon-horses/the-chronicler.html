The Chronicler on Solomon's Stalls

Dates: c. 400 BC (the composition of 1–2 Chronicles) · Wikipedia
Summary: The earliest extant attestation of the smaller figure. Retelling Solomon's reign from the older book of Kings, the Chronicler writes that the king had four thousand stalls, one tenth of the "forty thousand" that stands in the Masoretic text of 1 Kings 4:26.

Claims & evidence

Four thousand stalls (2 Chronicles 9:25)

"And Solomon had four thousand stalls for horses and chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen; whom he bestowed in the chariot cities, and with the king at Jerusalem."

— 2 Chronicles 9:25 (KJV), source. The verse is the Chronicler's parallel to 1 Kings 4:26, which in the received Hebrew reads "forty thousand stalls of horses for his chariots." Both notices end with the same twelve thousand horsemen; only the stall-count differs, and by exactly a factor of ten.

Reception and debate

  • A reading of an earlier book. The Chronicler wrote after the exile (conventionally c. 400 BC) and drew on Samuel–Kings as a written source. Keil & Delitzsch hold that his copy still read "four" and that the Kings tradition later came to read "forty"; Matthew Poole, in his second answer, reads the Chronicler's figure as a count of larger stables, each holding ten stalls. The readings are set out on the interpretation page; see also the summary timeline.
  • Other Kings–Chronicles differences. A number that differs between the Samuel–Kings tradition and Chronicles also arises in who killed Goliath and Ahaziah's age.
