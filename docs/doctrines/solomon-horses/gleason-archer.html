Gleason Archer on Solomon's Stalls

Dates: c. 1982 (the Encyclopedia of Bible Difficulties) · Wikipedia
Summary: A conservative evangelical writer on Bible difficulties. He reads the "forty thousand" of Kings as a numerical copying-error, ten times too large, and the "four thousand" of Chronicles as the credible original.

Claims & evidence

The Kings figure was "multiplied by ten" in copying

"We have a probable example of this as we compare 2 Chronicles 9:25 (which gives four thousand as the number of stalls built for Solomon's chariot horse) and 1 Kings 4:26 (which gives the figure as forty thousand). The latter citation has undoubtedly undergone multiplication by ten because of an obscurity in or misunderstanding of the Vorlage."

— Encyclopedia of Bible Difficulties (Zondervan, 1982), borrowable at the Internet Archive. Archer elsewhere names it "a good example of a more modest (and credible) figure… four thousand… whereas 1 Kings 4:26 puts the figure at forty thousand." For him the four thousand is the reliable number and the forty thousand a scribe's inflation of it.

Reception and debate

  • The difficulty placed in transmission. Archer's work defends the reliability of Scripture; on this verse he places the difficulty in the copying of the text, with the smaller figure surviving in Chronicles. He does not adopt the "stables vs. stalls" harmonization.
  • "Multiplication by ten" and word-confusion. Archer's decimal-slip mechanism and Keil & Delitzsch's ʾarbāʿîm/ʾarbāʿā word-confusion are two accounts of the same corruption; both, and the harmonization, are set out on the interpretation page.
  • Dillard on the original figure. Archer writes that the Kings figure has "undoubtedly" been multiplied by ten. Raymond Dillard (1987), given the variants across Kings, Chronicles, and the Greek, is quoted as holding that "it is not possible to speak with confidence regarding the original text" (⚠ unverified; see his page).
