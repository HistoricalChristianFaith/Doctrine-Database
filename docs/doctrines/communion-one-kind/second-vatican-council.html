The Second Vatican Council and communion under both kinds

Dates: c. 1963 (Sacrosanctum Concilium §55, promulgated 4 December 1963) · the Second Vatican Council sat 1962–1965 · Wikipedia
Summary: Four centuries after Trent defined the one-kind discipline, the Second Vatican Council's Constitution on the Sacred Liturgy, Sacrosanctum Concilium (4 December 1963), reopened the chalice to the laity of the Latin rite in defined cases — explicitly "the dogmatic principles which were laid down by the Council of Trent remaining intact." The Council presents it as a change of discipline, which Trent had said the church may set, with Trent's doctrine of concomitance kept.

Claims & evidence

Both kinds granted to the laity in defined cases (Sacrosanctum Concilium §55)

The Council commends the faithful's receiving "the Lord's body from the same sacrifice," then reopens the cup to the laity in stated cases at the bishops' discretion — carefully leaving Trent's doctrine untouched: "The dogmatic principles which were laid down by the Council of Trent remaining intact, communion under both kinds may be granted when the bishops think fit, not only to clerics and religious, but also to the laity, in cases to be determined by the Apostolic See, as, for instance, to the newly ordained in the Mass of their sacred ordination, to the newly professed in the Mass of their religious profession, and to the newly baptized in the Mass which follows their baptism" (Sacrosanctum Concilium §55). The clause "the dogmatic principles … of Trent remaining intact" presents the change as disciplinary, made under the authority Trent had described (Session 21, ch. II).

The subsequent widening of both-kinds administration

What §55 opened as a short list of occasions was progressively broadened. The General Instruction of the Roman Missal enlarged the permitted cases far beyond the ordination, profession, and baptism Masses the Council named, so that in much of the Latin Church — and especially in the English-speaking world under later conferences' norms — communion under both kinds became a widely available option at ordinary Mass (General Instruction of the Roman Missal; cf. Redemptionis Sacramentum §100–107). Communion under both kinds is thus offered to the Roman-rite laity, without any obligation to receive it; the General Instruction (§281) states that "Holy Communion has a fuller form as a sign when it is distributed under both kinds."

Notes

  • Placement on the timeline. It is the latest witness here. The Council offers the cup to the laity while keeping Trent's teaching, with its doctrine of concomitance, that Christ is received whole under either kind.
  • Relation to Trent. Sacrosanctum Concilium reopens the cup in the same sentence that keeps "the dogmatic principles which were laid down by the Council of Trent"; it does not adopt Luther's reading of "Drink ye all of it" as a divine command binding the laity, and offers both kinds as a fuller sign.
  • The Eastern churches have continued to communicate the laity under both kinds, the Byzantine rite administering them together by intinction and the communion-spoon.
  • Vatican II appears elsewhere on this wiki as a witness — notably on the ministerial priesthood (Lumen Gentium §10, 1964), where the common and ministerial priesthoods are said to differ "in essence and not only in degree."
