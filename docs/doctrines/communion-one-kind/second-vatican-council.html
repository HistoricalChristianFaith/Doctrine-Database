The Second Vatican Council and the partial restoration of the cup

Dates: c. 1963 (Sacrosanctum Concilium §55, promulgated 4 December 1963) · the Second Vatican Council sat 1962–1965 · Wikipedia
Summary: Four centuries after Trent defined the one-kind discipline, the Second Vatican Council's Constitution on the Sacred Liturgy, Sacrosanctum Concilium (4 December 1963), reopened the chalice to the laity of the Latin rite in defined cases — explicitly "the dogmatic principles which were laid down by the Council of Trent remaining intact." It is the modern re-restoration of the cup within Roman Catholicism itself: not a reversal of Trent's doctrine of concomitance, but a change of the discipline Trent had said the church was free to set, restoring in part what the medieval West had withdrawn.

Claims & evidence

Both kinds granted to the laity in defined cases (Sacrosanctum Concilium §55)

The Council commends the fuller sign of the faithful receiving from the same sacrifice, then reopens the cup to the laity in stated cases at the bishops' discretion — carefully leaving Trent's doctrine untouched: "The dogmatic principles which were laid down by the Council of Trent remaining intact, communion under both kinds may be granted when the bishops think fit, not only to clerics and religious, but also to the laity, in cases to be determined by the Apostolic See, as, for instance, to the newly ordained in the Mass of their sacred ordination, to the newly professed in the Mass of their religious profession, and to the newly baptized in the Mass which follows their baptism" (Sacrosanctum Concilium §55). The clause "the dogmatic principles … of Trent remaining intact" is the hinge: the change is disciplinary, not doctrinal — exactly the church-authority Trent had claimed (Session 21, ch. II), now exercised in the opposite direction.

The subsequent widening of both-kinds administration

What §55 opened as a short list of occasions was progressively broadened. The General Instruction of the Roman Missal enlarged the permitted cases far beyond the ordination, profession, and baptism Masses the Council named, so that in much of the Latin Church — and especially in the English-speaking world under later conferences' norms — communion under both kinds became a widely available option at ordinary Mass (General Instruction of the Roman Missal; cf. Redemptionis Sacramentum §100–107). The cup withdrawn across the medieval West, and defended at Constance and Trent, is thus once more offered to the Roman-rite laity — though as a permitted enrichment of the sign, not as anything the faithful are held bound to receive.

Notes

  • What Vatican II establishes on this timeline. It is the closing witness and the mirror of the medieval withdrawal: the same authority that had reserved the cup restores it, without conceding that the host alone ever left the laity lacking. That is the point Trent had insisted on with its doctrine of concomitance — and it is why the restoration could be made as a change of discipline rather than a correction of doctrine.
  • Continuity with Trent, not reversal of it. Vatican II does not grant Luther's premise that "Drink ye all of it" binds the laity by divine command; it reaffirms Trent's contrary teaching in the very sentence that reopens the cup. The restoration is real but bounded: both kinds as a fuller sign, freely offered, not as a debt of divine precept.
  • The East, by contrast, never withdrew the cup at all, communicating the laity under both kinds throughout — having merged the species and administered them together by intinction and the communion-spoon, so it had no withdrawal to restore.
  • Vatican II appears elsewhere on this wiki as a witness — notably on the ministerial priesthood (Lumen Gentium §10, 1964), where the common and ministerial priesthoods are said to differ "in essence and not only in degree."
