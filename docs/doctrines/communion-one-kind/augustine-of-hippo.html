Augustine of Hippo on the faithful drinking the consecrated cup

Dates: c. 410 (preaching during his episcopate at Hippo, 395/6–430) · Wikipedia
Summary: Preaching to his North-African congregation, Augustine describes the converts of Pentecost — the very crowd that had cried for Christ's blood — coming to the Lord's table and, "in faith," drinking that Blood. Like Ambrose a generation earlier, he is not defending the lay chalice but simply assuming it: the whole body of the faithful receives the cup. He is a fifth-century witness from the Latin West to lay reception under both kinds, standing alongside Leo and Gelasius at Rome.

Claims & evidence

The converted people "came to the Lord's table, and in faith drank that Blood" (Sermon 27 on the New Testament §4)

They were converted from among this people of the Jews; were converted, and baptized. They came to the Lord's table, and in faith drank that Blood, which in their fury they had shed.

— Sermon 27 on the New Testament [77 in the Benedictine numbering], §4, on Matthew 15:21–28 (the Canaanite woman), source (cf. New Advent). The striking figure — the crowd that clamoured for Christ's blood now drinking it at the altar — assumes that the converted laity drink the cup: Augustine describes what the baptized multitude does on coming "to the Lord's table."

Notes

  • The passage in context. As with Ambrose, the text is incidental: Augustine is preaching on the forgiveness of even Christ's killers, and reaches for the eucharist as an illustration that assumes his hearers know that the faithful drink the Blood. The later Latin practice (see Aquinas) differs from the practice the sermon assumes.
  • Elsewhere in Augustine. The same assumption runs through his eucharistic preaching to the newly baptized: in Sermon 272, showing the neophytes what lies on the altar, he says "the bread is the body of Christ, the cup is the blood of Christ," and has them answer "Amen" as they receive; and in his sermons on John 6 he speaks throughout of the faithful eating the flesh and drinking the blood.
  • Augustine appears on this wiki as a witness on many doctrines; the eucharistic teaching cited here is representative of his preaching across his episcopate.
