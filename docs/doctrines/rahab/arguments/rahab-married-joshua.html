Did Rahab marry Joshua and become the ancestress of priests and prophets?

Disputed reading · on Who Was Rahab of Jericho? · witness: Rahab in Jewish tradition

The passage

Eight prophets, who were also priests, descended from Rahab the prostitute… Rabbi Yehuda said: So too, Huldah the prophetess was a descendant of Rahab the prostitute… For Rahab converted and married Joshua.

— Babylonian Talmud, Megillah 14b (William Davidson translation), Sefaria.1

The question

The book of Joshua says that Rahab, spared at the fall of Jericho, “dwelleth in Israel even unto this day” (Joshua 6:25), but names no husband for her. Two traditions supply one: the Gospel of Matthew names a Rachab as the mother of Boaz by Salmon of Judah (Matthew 1:5), whom Chrysostom and Bede identify with the woman of Jericho, and the Babylonian Talmud records that she became a proselyte and married Joshua son of Nun, with priests and prophets among her descendants. This page sets out each tradition’s account of her marriage and line.

Rahab the mother of Boaz by Salmon, in the line of Judah

In this reading Rahab of Jericho is the wife of Salmon and the mother of Boaz, and so an ancestress of David and of Christ.

Held by: the Gospel of Matthew (1:5, first century); John Chrysostom (Homilies on Matthew 3, c. 390); Bede (Commentary on the Catholic Epistles, on James 2:25, c. 710).2

Matthew’s genealogy of Jesus names her in the generation before Boaz: “Salmon begat Booz of Rachab.” Salmon stands in the line of Judah that the book of Ruth traces from Perez to David (“Nahshon begot Salmon, Salmon begot Boaz”), and 1 Chronicles places him as the son of Nahshon, “prince of the sons of Judah.”3 Matthew names four women in the line, Tamar, Rahab, Ruth and the wife of Uriah, and Chrysostom reads his Rahab as the woman of Jericho: “he hath mentioned Ruth also and Rahab, the one an alien, the other an harlot, that thou mayest learn that He came to do away with all our ills.”4 Bede reads her reward in the same terms: the foreign woman who received the spies at the risk of her life was “deemed worthy to be justified from sins, to be numbered among the citizens of the people of Israel… to be inserted in the genealogies of our Lord and Saviour.”5 Both read her presence in Matthew’s genealogy, four generations above David, as a sign that Christ came for sinners and for the nations.

Rahab the proselyte, wife of Joshua and ancestress of priests and prophets

In this reading Rahab converted, married Joshua, and became the ancestress of eight prophets who were also priests and of the prophetess Huldah.

Held by: Rav Naḥman, with the baraita and Rabbi Yehuda he answers (Babylonian Talmud, Megillah 14b, traditions from c. 200, redacted c. 500).6

The Talmud reaches Rahab through Huldah. Rav Naḥman teaches that Huldah descended from Joshua, linking “Harhas” in her genealogy (2 Kings 22:14) with Joshua’s burial-place, Timnath-heres (Judges 2:9). Rav Eina the Elder cites a baraita that eight prophets who were also priests, Neriah, Baruch, Seraiah, Mahseiah, Jeremiah, Hilkiah, Hanamel and Shallum, descended from Rahab, and that Rabbi Yehuda counted Huldah among her descendants too, joining “the son of Tikvah” in Huldah’s line with the cord Rahab hung in her window, “This cord of [tikvat] scarlet thread” (Joshua 2:18). Rav Naḥman joins the two teachings: “Rahab converted and married Joshua,” so that Huldah descends from both. When the Gemara asks how Joshua could have descendants, since the genealogy of Ephraim ends with him (1 Chronicles 7:27), it answers that “he did not have sons, but he did have daughters.” In this reading the woman whom Joshua saved alive is received into Israel as a proselyte, joined to the house of the leader of the conquest, and honoured with a posterity of priests and prophets, among them Jeremiah and his father Hilkiah. The same tractate numbers her among the four women of surpassing beauty in the world, with Sarah, Abigail and Esther.7
