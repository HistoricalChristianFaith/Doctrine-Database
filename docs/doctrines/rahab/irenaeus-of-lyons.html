Irenaeus of Lyons on Rahab — the three spies as Father, Son, and Holy Spirit; the Gentile saved by the scarlet sign

Dates: c. 180 (Against Heresies IV.20.12; Irenaeus b. c. 130, bishop of Lyons, d. c. 202) · Wikipedia
Summary: Irenaeus reads the three spies whom Rahab received as a figure of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, and the Gentile harlot preserved “through faith of the scarlet sign” as the sinner who enters the kingdom ahead of the unbelieving.

Claims & evidence

Rahab the Gentile, the three spies, and the scarlet sign

Thus also did Rahab the harlot, while condemning herself, inasmuch as she was a Gentile, guilty of all sins, nevertheless receive the three spies, who were spying out all the land, and hid them at her home; [which three were] doubtless [a type of] the Father and the Son, together with the Holy Spirit. And when the entire city in which she lived fell to ruins at the sounding of the seven trumpets, Rahab the harlot was preserved, when all was over [in ultimis], together with all her house, through faith of the scarlet sign; as the Lord also declared to those who did not receive His advent,—the Pharisees, no doubt, nullify the sign of the scarlet thread, which meant the passover, and the redemption and exodus of the people from Egypt,—when He said, ‘The publicans and the harlots go into the kingdom of heaven before you.’

— Irenaeus, Against Heresies 4.20.12 (ANF vol. 1), source. Rahab appears in Irenaeus's argument that the one God and His Word were revealed by the prophets in figures throughout the Old Testament, the Gentile harlot standing as a type of those saved by faith while the unbelieving are shut out.1

Notes

  • Irenaeus explicitly ties the scarlet thread to the passover and the exodus-redemption — a distinct move from the plainer blood-of-Christ reading of the scarlet cord found in others.
  • The trinitarian reading of the “three spies” is his own and would not be followed by all later Fathers; Jerome, for instance, reads the two spies as sent to Jew and Gentile.
  • The scarlet-cord typology recurs across the timeline: where Irenaeus routes it through passover and exodus, later witnesses read the same sign directly as the blood of Christ.
