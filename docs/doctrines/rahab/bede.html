Bede on Rahab — justified by works: the foreign harlot written into the genealogy of Christ

Dates: c. 710 (the Commentary on the Seven Catholic Epistles, on James 2:25; Bede b. c. 673, d. 735) · Wikipedia
Summary: Reading James, Bede makes Rahab the model of a faith proved by works: a sinful foreign woman who, by mercy and hospitality at the risk of her life, was justified, numbered among Israel, and written into the genealogy of the Saviour.

Claims & evidence

Justified by works; numbered in Israel and in the Lord’s genealogy

…was not Rahab the harlot also justified by works when she received the messengers and sent them out another way?… a sinful woman, a foreign woman, who nevertheless by acts of mercy, by the duty of hospitality, even at the risk of her life, received the servants of God and was deemed worthy to be justified from sins, to be numbered among the citizens of the people of Israel… to be inserted in the genealogies of our Lord and Saviour…

— Bede, Commentary on the Seven Catholic Epistles, on James 2:25, trans. David Hurst (Cistercian, 1985), source. Expounding James’s appeal to Rahab, Bede draws the whole arc: her hospitality to the spies, at mortal peril, is the work by which her faith is justified, and the reward is threefold — cleansing from sin, adoption into Israel, and a place in the ancestry of Christ.1

Notes

  • Bede unites the two New Testament strands — the faith of Hebrews 11:31 and the works of James 2:25 — and ties both to her place in Matthew’s genealogy.
  • This is the reading the Latin Middle Ages inherited.
