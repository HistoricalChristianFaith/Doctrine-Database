Josephus on Rahab — the harlot recast as an innkeeper

Dates: c. AD 93 (the Antiquities of the Jews 5.1.2; Josephus b. c. AD 37, d. c. 100) · Wikipedia
Summary: Josephus, the earliest post-biblical witness, retells the Jericho episode but silently drops Rahab's harlotry, presenting her instead as the keeper of an inn.

Claims & evidence

Rahab the keeper of an inn (not a harlot)

…they were in the inn kept by Rahab, and were very solicitous that they might not be discovered.

…but they saved alive Rahab, with her family, who had fled to her inn. And when she was brought to him, Joshua owned to her that they owed her thanks for her preservation of the spies… he gave her certain lands immediately, and had her in great esteem ever afterwards.

— Josephus, Antiquities of the Jews 5.1.2, trans. William Whiston, source. Josephus twice calls Rahab's house an inn and her its keeper; the underlying Greek word is katagōgion (“lodging, inn”), and he never uses pornē/harlot. The rescue, the lands, and Joshua's lasting esteem are kept — only the scandal is removed. (The Greek can be checked at Perseus.)1

Notes

  • This is the earliest datable post-biblical treatment of Rahab, and it already softens the scandal — the beginning of the “innkeeper not harlot” reading later fixed by the Targum and Rashi (see the harlot-or-innkeeper question on the timeline).
  • The rehabilitation runs opposite to the Christian stream, which keeps the word “harlot” precisely to magnify grace.
