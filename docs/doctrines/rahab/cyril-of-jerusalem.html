Cyril of Jerusalem on Rahab — Rahab as the pattern of salvation by repentance

Dates: c. 350 (the Catechetical Lectures 2.9; Cyril b. c. 313, bishop of Jerusalem, d. 386) · Wikipedia
Summary: Instructing catechumens on repentance, Cyril holds up Rahab as proof that even a public sinner is saved through repentance and a bare confession of the God of heaven and earth.

Claims & evidence

Saved by repentance — ‘your God’, not yet ‘my God’

Pass now, pray, to the others who were saved by repentance. Perhaps even among the women someone will say, ‘I have committed fornication and adultery… Can there be salvation for me?’ Fix your eyes, woman, upon Rahab, and look for salvation for yourself too. For if she who openly and publicly practiced fornication was saved through repentance… For observe how she was saved. She said only this: ‘Since the Lord, your God, is God in heaven above and on earth below.’ ‘Your God,’ she said, for she did not dare call him her God, because of her wantonness.

— Cyril of Jerusalem, Catechetical Lecture 2.9 (NPNF2 vol. 7), source. Cyril is instructing the catechumens on the reach of repentance, offering Rahab as the encouragement to any woman who fears her sins put her beyond salvation.1

Notes

  • Cyril's is a penitential rather than a typological reading — Rahab as example, not figure of the church.
  • The delicate observation that she says “your God,” not daring “my God,” is Cyril's own pastoral touch.
