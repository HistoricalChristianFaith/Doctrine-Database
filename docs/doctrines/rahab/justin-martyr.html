Justin Martyr on Rahab — the scarlet thread as the symbol of the blood of Christ that saves the harlots of all nations

Dates: c. AD 160 (the Dialogue with Trypho 111; Justin b. c. 100, martyred c. 165) · Wikipedia
Summary: Justin, arguing with the Jew Trypho, makes the scarlet thread the express symbol of the blood of Christ, by which former harlots and the unrighteous of every nation receive remission of sins.

Claims & evidence

The scarlet thread manifests the blood of Christ, by which the nations are saved

For the sign of the scarlet thread, which the spies, sent to Jericho by Joshua, son of Nave (Nun), gave to Rahab the harlot, telling her to bind it to the window through which she let them down to escape from their enemies, also manifested the symbol of the blood of Christ, by which those who were at one time harlots and unrighteous persons out of all nations are saved, receiving remission of sins, and continuing no longer in sin.

— Justin Martyr, Dialogue with Trypho 111 (ANF vol. 1), source. Justin presses the scarlet=blood reading in the midst of his disputation with Trypho, drawing it as a proof from the Hebrew Scriptures themselves.1

Notes

  • Independent of Clement, and it widens the figure — “out of all nations” makes Rahab the type of the saved Gentile sinner.
  • Set in an anti-Jewish disputation, so the scarlet=blood reading is being pressed as a proof from the Hebrew Scriptures themselves.
