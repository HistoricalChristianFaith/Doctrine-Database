Origen on Rahab — Rahab the Gentile church, no salvation outside her house

Dates: c. 240 (the Homilies on Joshua, hom. 3, 6, 7; Origen b. c. 185, d. c. 253) · Wikipedia
Summary: Origen gives the fullest early reading: the scarlet sign is the blood of Christ, the sole means of salvation; Rahab's house is the church, outside which no one is saved; and the former harlot becomes a pure virgin joined to Christ.

Claims & evidence

The scarlet sign is the blood of Christ

She herself puts the scarlet-colored sign in her house, through which she is bound to be saved from the destruction of the city. No other sign would have been accepted, except the scarlet-colored one that carried the sign of blood. For she knew there was no salvation for anyone except in the blood of Christ.

— Origen, Homilies on Joshua 3.5 / 6.4, trans. Barbara J. Bruce, Fathers of the Church 105 (CUA Press, 2002), source. Homily 3.5; Origen reads the scarlet cord as the redeeming blood of Christ, the single sign that secures Rahab's rescue.1

Outside this house — the church — no one is saved

Therefore, if anyone wants to be saved, let him come into the house of this one who was once a prostitute. … Let no one persuade himself, let no one deceive himself. Outside this house, that is, outside the church, no one is saved.

— Origen, Homilies on Joshua 3.5 / 6.4, trans. Barbara J. Bruce, Fathers of the Church 105 (CUA Press, 2002), source. Homily 3.5; Rahab's house becomes the type of the church, the one place of refuge from the coming destruction.1

Once a prostitute, now a pure virgin joined to Christ

Once she was a prostitute, but now ‘a pure virgin, to one man’ she has been united, ‘to Christ.’

— Origen, Homilies on Joshua 3.5 / 6.4, trans. Barbara J. Bruce, Fathers of the Church 105 (CUA Press, 2002), source. Homily 6.4; the transformed harlot images the church gathered from the nations and betrothed to Christ.1

Notes

  • Origen's “extra ecclesiam nulla salus” reading of Rahab's house predates and shapes Cyprian's ecclesiological use of the same figure.
  • The Homilies on Joshua survive in Rufinus's Latin translation, so the wording is at one remove from Origen's Greek — the sense is Origen's, but the exact phrasing is Rufinus's rendering.
