Jerome on Rahab — the scarlet cord handed to the Latin West; Rahab the church of the Gentiles

Dates: c. 400 (Letter 52 and the Homilies on the Psalms; Jerome b. c. 347, d. 420) · Wikipedia
Summary: Jerome carries the scarlet-cord typology into the Latin West — the harlot's scarlet cord “a mystic reference to the shedding of blood” — and reads Rahab as the church gathered from the Gentiles, her very name marking a turn from pride to humility.

Claims & evidence

The scarlet cord, a mystic reference to the shedding of blood

So, too, with a mystic reference to the shedding of blood, it was a scarlet cord which the harlot Rahab (a type of the church) hung in her window that she might be saved at the destruction of Jericho.

— Jerome, Letter 52.3 (to Nepotian) (NPNF2 vol. 6), source. Jerome reads the scarlet cord of Joshua 2 as pointing to the blood of Christ, and Rahab as a type of the church.1

The two spies sent to Jew and Gentile; Rahab the church of the Gentiles

Two messengers he sends: one to the circumcised; the other to the Gentiles, Peter and Paul. Jericho seeks to kill them; the harlot takes them in, meaning, of course, the church gathered together from the Gentiles.

— Jerome, Homilies on the Psalms 18 (on Psalm 86), trans. Marie Liguori Ewald, Fathers of the Church 48 (CUA Press, 1964). Jerome allegorizes the two spies as the apostles to Jew and Gentile, with Rahab as the Gentile church that receives them.2

The name Rahab — from a broad road to humility

Rahab thus admits of two interpretations: the name may imply either a ‘broad space’ or, better, ‘pride.’… She who formerly walked the broad, spacious road to death… was later converted unto humility.

— Jerome, Homilies on the Psalms 18 (on Psalm 86), trans. Marie Liguori Ewald, Fathers of the Church 48 (CUA Press, 1964). Jerome draws a conversion out of the very etymology of Rahab's name.3

Notes

  • Jerome's “two spies to Jew and Gentile” differs from Irenaeus's “three spies = the Trinity” — the tradition varies at this detail.
  • Jerome's is the reading the medieval Latin West inherited.
