Cyprian on Rahab — Rahab's house as the one Church, those who go outside perish

Dates: c. 255 (Epistle 75, to Magnus; Cyprian b. c. 200, bishop of Carthage, martyred 258) · Wikipedia
Summary: Cyprian, arguing that there is no salvation outside the one Church, makes Rahab's house the type of that Church: all who gather in it are saved, and whoever goes out into the street bears his own blood.

Claims & evidence

Rahab's house is the one Church outside which none is saved

Which also we see expressed concerning Rahab, who herself also bore a type of the Church, who received the command which said, ‘Thou shalt bring thy father, and thy mother, and thy brethren … into thine house; and whosoever shall go out of the doors of thine house into the street, his blood shall be upon him.’ … they who will live, and escape from the destruction of the world, must be gathered together into one house alone, that is, into the Church…

— Cyprian, Epistle 75.4 (to Magnus) (ANF vol. 5), source. Cyprian presses the Rahab-house figure in his argument that salvation is found only within the one Church.1

Notes

  • Cyprian applies the Rahab-house figure to his baptismal controversy — an ecclesiological use kin to Origen's reading of the same house.
  • The reading fixes the “one house = one Church” typology for the Latin West.
