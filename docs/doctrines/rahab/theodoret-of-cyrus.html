Theodoret of Cyrus on Rahab — faith over lineage: the foreign harlot ranked with the patriarchs

Dates: c. 450 (On Divine Providence and the Interpretation of Hebrews; Theodoret b. c. 393, bishop of Cyrus, d. c. 458/466) · Wikipedia
Summary: Theodoret makes Rahab the standing rebuke to Jewish confidence in descent: a foreign prostitute whose faith covered her sins and set her beside Moses, Abraham, and Noah, her scarlet cord prefiguring the church saved by the Lord’s blood.

Claims & evidence

The harlot testifies to the universal call

Rahab, the harlot, testifies that this is so. Though she belonged to a different race and was a harlot, she relied solely on their reputation, accepted their religion, abandoned her own beliefs and entrusted herself to strangers. ‘We have heard,’ she said, ‘what things the Lord your God has done to the Egyptians, and fear of you fell on us.’

— Theodoret of Cyrus, On Divine Providence 10.49, trans. Thomas Halton, Ancient Christian Writers 49 (Paulist Press, 1988). Preaching on providence, Theodoret adduces Rahab as living proof that the call goes out beyond Israel: a woman of another race and a discreditable trade who, on report alone, forsook her own gods and threw in her lot with the people of the Lord.1

Faith covered the wounds of sin; the scarlet cord and the church

Lawlessness of life did not prevent salvation; faith covered the wounds of sin… the divine Spirit in associating a foreign woman and prostitute with Moses, Abraham, Noah, Enoch, and the other saints so as to establish the power of faith and repress the Jews’ conceit… as she was given the scarlet cord as a sign of salvation, so the church through the Lord’s blood enjoyed the eternal goods.

— Theodoret of Cyrus, Interpretation of the Epistle to the Hebrews, on Hebrews 11:31, trans. Robert Charles Hill (Holy Cross, 2001). Commenting on Rahab’s place in the roll of faith, Theodoret presses the scandal of the company she keeps: a foreign prostitute listed among the patriarchs precisely to exalt faith and humble those who trusted in birth, with her scarlet cord read as the sign of a salvation the church now holds through the blood of Christ.2

Notes

  • The “repress the Jews’ conceit” line makes Rahab a proof that faith, not descent, saves — the Antiochene counterpart to the Alexandrian church-typology.
  • Theodoret still keeps the scarlet-cord = blood-of-Christ figure alongside the faith reading.
