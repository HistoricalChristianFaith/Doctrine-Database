The Second Council of Nicaea on venerating and invoking the saints

Dates: c. 787 — the Seventh Ecumenical Council, seven sessions at Nicaea (September–October 787), summoned by the Empress Irene and Patriarch Tarasius to reverse the iconoclasm of the Council of Hieria (754); the doctrinal Definition (horos) was decreed at the seventh session · Wikipedia
Summary: The council's central business was the veneration of images. Its Definition distinguishes the honorific reverence (τιμητικὴ προσκύνησις) paid to images, relics, the Cross and the saints from the adoration (λατρεία) that "pertains alone to the divine nature," and the council received into its record the confessions of returning bishops who "ask for the intercessions" of the Mother of God and of all the saints. Its acts also preserve the Epitome of the iconoclast Council of Hieria (754), whose anathemas likewise defend seeking the intercessions of the Theotokos and "the invocation of Saints."

Claims & evidence

The Definition: veneration (proskynesis) of the saints and their images, distinct from the adoration (latreia) due to God alone

… the venerable and holy images, as well in painting and mosaic as of other fit materials, should be set forth in the holy churches of God … and to these should be given due salutation and honourable reverence (ἀςπασμὸν καὶ τιμητικὴν προσκύνησιν), not indeed that true worship of faith (λατρείαν) which pertains alone to the divine nature … For the honour which is paid to the image passes on to that which the image represents, and he who reveres the image reveres in it the subject represented.

— The Definition of the Second Council of Nice (the horos, seventh session), trans. H. R. Percival, The Seven Ecumenical Councils (NPNF2 vol. 14), source. The council here distinguishes proskynesis (relative, honorific reverence) for images, relics, the Cross, and the saints from latreia (adoration) reserved to God. The horos itself speaks directly of images and, in the same breath, of the veneration of relics; the explicit petitioning of the saints appears in the confessions the council received (below).1

"I ask for the intercessions of our spotless Lady the Holy Mother of God … and of all the Saints"

Likewise also the venerable images of the incarnation of our Lord Jesus Christ … and of our spotless Lady, the holy Mother of God; and of the angels like unto God; and of the holy Apostles, Prophets, Martyrs, and of all the Saints — the sacred images of all these, I salute and venerate … I ask for the intercessions (πρεσβείας) of our spotless Lady the Holy Mother of God, and those of the holy and heavenly powers, and those of all the Saints.

— the written confession of Basil, bishop of Ancyra, read on his return from iconoclasm and received by the council in its first session (24 September 787), Percival, NPNF2 vol. 14, source. In this confession the bishop directly asks the Mother of God and the saints for their intercessions, and the council received it as the profession of a returning bishop. Basil adds that he receives the saints' relics "with all honour" and "venerate[s] these with honour" (τιμητικῶς προσκυνῶ), "hoping to have a share in their holiness."2

The invocation of saints in the iconoclast Epitome of 754, preserved in the acts

Read into the acts of 787 to be refuted point by point was the Epitome of the earlier Iconoclast Council of Hieria (754). Its anathemas, condemning the making of images of the saints, also defend seeking the intercessions of the Theotokos and the invocation of the saints:

(15) If anyone shall not confess the holy ever-virgin Mary, truly and properly the Mother of God, to be higher than every creature … and does not with sincere faith seek her intercessions as of one having confidence in her access to our God, … let him be anathema. … (17) If anyone denies the profit of the invocation of Saints … let him be anathema.

— The Epitome of the Definition of the Iconoclastic Conciliabulum held in Constantinople, A.D. 754, Percival, NPNF2 vol. 14, source. These numbered anathemas belong to the iconoclast synod of 754, not to the horos of 787 (the same list, at (16), condemns representing the saints "in lifeless pictures with material colours" — the iconoclast position 787 overturned); they are preserved because the acts of 787 quote the 754 document in full. Both the image-venerating council of 787 and the iconoclast council of 754 thus affirm, in their own texts, the seeking of the intercessions of the saints and the Theotokos.3

Placement on the timeline

The council meets in 787, nearly two centuries after Gregory the Great (d. 604) and four after the fourth-century homilies of the Cappadocians, Ambrose, and Chrysostom. Its record is a conciliar statement from the East in the late eighth century: the confession of a returning bishop asking the saints and the Theotokos for their intercessions, and the proskynesis/latreia distinction between honour to the saint and worship to God alone. The distinction figures again on this timeline at Trent (1563), and the practice is rejected by Calvin (1559). The council's texts bear on Is the invocation of saints a fourth-century development or an older practice? and on Is invoking a saint the same act as believing the saints intercede?, where Basil of Ancyra's received confession is a second-person petition.

Reception and debate

  • The anathema "If anyone denies the profit of the invocation of Saints, let him be anathema" is item (17) of the Epitome of the iconoclast council of 754, printed in the acts of 787 as the text to be answered; it is not part of the 787 horos.
  • Augustine earlier reserves worship to God while naming the martyrs (they "are named … but they are not invoked by the sacrificing priest"), and the Council of Trent answers the charge of idolatry against invoking the saints in 1563.
