Josephus on Og of Bashan — the giant king and his iron bed

Dates: c. AD 93 (the Antiquities of the Jews 4.5.3; Josephus b. c. AD 37, d. c. 100) · Wikipedia
Summary: Josephus, the earliest post-biblical witness to Og, turns Deuteronomy’s terse notice into a portrait of a marvel of stature, and reads his size directly off the iron bed still shown at Rabbath.

Claims & evidence

Og a giant beyond his peers — and his iron bed the proof

Now Og had very few equals, either in the largeness of his body, or handsomeness of his appearance. He was also a man of great activity in the use of his hands, so that his actions were not unequal to the vast largeness and handsome appearance of his body. And men could easily guess at his strength and magnitude when they took his bed at Rabbath, the royal city of the Ammonites; its structure was of iron, its breadth four cubits, and its length a cubit more than double thereto.

— Josephus, Antiquities of the Jews 4.5.3, trans. William Whiston, source. Josephus takes the “bedstead of iron” of Deuteronomy 3:11 as literal furniture and its dimensions as the measure of the man: the breadth “four cubits” and the length “a cubit more than double thereto” (= nine cubits) reproduce the biblical figures exactly, and he locates the relic at Rabbath (Rabbath-Ammon), just as Deuteronomy does. Note the actual wording is “handsomeness of his appearance,” not “comely”; and Josephus makes no mention of the Rephaim or of any Flood-survival — the giant here is simply a very large and able man.1

Notes

  • This is the earliest datable treatment of Og after the Bible, and it fixes the reading that the whole giant tradition — Jewish and Christian — would follow: the iron bed measures a literal giant. Whether the object measures the man (rather than a coffin, throne-couch, or cult-object) is the question weighed on the iron-bed argument page (assessment: weak, as material evidence) and the bed-of-Marduk page (assessment: sound, as a dimensional parallel).
  • Josephus keeps the giant strictly human and heroic — large, handsome, dexterous — a contrast to the rabbinic Og, who becomes an antediluvian Watcher-descendant of cosmic size (see Og in Jewish tradition).
