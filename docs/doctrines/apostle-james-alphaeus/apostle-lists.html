The apostle-lists: the martyrdom notices

Dates: uncertain (the apostle-list literature, c. 3rd–8th centuries) · Pseudo-Hippolytus, On the Twelve Apostles
Summary: The catalogues of the apostles, of uncertain date, are the earliest extant sources on this timeline to assign James son of Alphaeus a death. They give two: one strand has him stoned at Jerusalem and buried by the temple, as Hegesippus reports of James the Just; another has him crucified in Egypt.

Claims & evidence

Pseudo-Hippolytus: stoned at Jerusalem, buried beside the temple

The catalogue that goes under the name of Hippolytus gives the apostle a Jerusalem death; Hegesippus reports the same place, manner and burial for James the brother of the Lord — stoned in the city, buried by the temple.

And James the son of Alphaeus, when preaching in Jerusalem, was stoned to death by the Jews, and was buried there beside the temple.

— Pseudo-Hippolytus, On the Twelve Apostles, 9 (ANF vol. 5). Hegesippus (Eusebius, Ecclesiastical History 2.23) reports that James the Just was cast from the temple, stoned, clubbed and “buried on the spot, by the temple.”

A second strand: crucified in Egypt

Other branches of the same literature treat James son of Alphaeus as distinct from James the Just and send him not to Jerusalem but to Egypt, reporting that he was crucified at Ostrakine (Ostracine) in Lower Egypt while preaching there. The two notices differ on place and manner of death.

Reception and debate

  • Date and character of the lists. The apostle-lists are of uncertain authorship and date. On the whole class and its use as a source, see the apostles hub.
  • The Jerusalem death and the two Jameses. Pseudo-Hippolytus assigns the son of Alphaeus the place and manner of death Hegesippus reports for James the Just. Jerome (c. 383) identified the two Jameses on other grounds (see the summary timeline). Whether the shared manner of death supports identifying them is set out on the perpetual-virginity timeline: “Does the shared manner of death support identifying the two Jameses?”
  • The Golden Legend and the Jerusalem death. The medieval legend follows the Jerusalem strand, giving “St James the Less” the temple martyrdom and the fuller’s club of James the Just (see the Golden Legend page).
