The Golden Legend: the life of St James the Less

Dates: c. 1260 (Jacobus de Voragine, Legenda Aurea) · the Golden Legend, life of St James the Less
Summary: The thirteenth-century legendary follows Jerome’s identification of the two Jameses. Its life of “St James the Less” names one man as the son of Alphaeus, the brother of the Lord, James the Just, and the first bishop of Jerusalem, and narrates his death as Hegesippus tells the death of James the Just.

Claims & evidence

The saint’s titles

The Legend explains each of the saint’s titles in turn:

He was also called James the son of Alpheus… He was called also the brother of our Lord, because he resembled much well our Lord in body, in visage, face and of manner… He was called James the Just for his right great holiness… he sang in Jerusalem the first mass that ever was sung therein, and he was first bishop of Jerusalem.

— Jacobus de Voragine, the Golden Legend, tr. William Caxton, life of St James the Less. Here the apostle of the Gospel lists bears the titles, the holiness, and the see that Hegesippus and Jerome report of James the Just.

The martyrdom

The martyrdom the Legend narrates follows Hegesippus’s account of the death of James the brother of the Lord: cast down from a height, stoned by the crowd, and finished with a blow from a fuller’s club.

… there was a man in that company took a fuller’s staff and smote him on the head, that his brain fell all abroad, and thus by martyrdom he finished his life.

— the Golden Legend, life of St James the Less. The fuller’s club is the emblem by which “James the Less” is known in Western art, and he is the patron of fullers.

Reception and debate

  • Date and character. The Golden Legend is a thirteenth-century compilation of saints’ lives; it is placed on the timeline as a witness to how the apostle was pictured in the Latin West in its day. On the whole class of late hagiography, see the apostles hub.
  • Earlier sources for the details. The holiness and the death by the fuller’s club are reported of James the brother of the Lord by Hegesippus (Eusebius, Ecclesiastical History 2.23); Jerome reports him “ordained by the apostles bishop of Jerusalem” (Lives of Illustrious Men 2).
  • The identification in debate. Whether James son of Alphaeus and James the brother of the Lord are one man is set out on the summary timeline and across the perpetual-virginity timeline.
