Dante on the living heavens

Dates: c. 1320 (Paradiso); Dante Alighieri 1265–1321 · Wikipedia
Summary: The Paradiso is the poetic apex of the angelic-mover settlement that Aquinas had fixed in prose. Dante’s heavens are not ensouled bodies but bodies turned by Intelligences — the angelic orders, each order assigned to a sphere, imprinting its power and motion on the star as a craftsman’s hammer works his design. The nine orders of angels correspond to the nine revolving heavens. It is the medieval consensus at its most vivid: the stars live by the mind that moves them, not by a soul of their own.

The “blessed motors” that move the spheres (Paradiso II)

Beatrice explains the differing powers of the stars: they descend not from the bodies themselves but from the angelic movers whose virtue the spheres transmit, as a hammer transmits the smith’s art:

The power and motion of the holy spheres,
As from the artisan the hammer's craft,
Forth from the blessed motors must proceed.

The heaven, which lights so manifold make fair,
From the Intelligence profound, which turns it,
The image takes, and makes of it a seal.

— Paradiso II, trans. H. W. Longfellow (1867), Wikisource. The star is a seal stamped by the turning Intelligence; the life and motion are the mover’s, not the body’s.1

The celestial Princes who move the third heaven (Paradiso VIII)

In the sphere of Venus the blessed souls name the angelic order that turns their heaven — the Principalities (“Princes”) — quoting the opening of Dante’s own earlier canzone on the movers of the third sky:

We turn around with the celestial Princes,
One gyre and one gyration and one thirst,
To whom thou in the world of old didst say,
“Ye who, intelligent, the third heaven are moving;”

— Paradiso VIII, trans. Longfellow, Wikisource. Each sphere has its proper angelic mover, named and ranked.2

Nine orders, nine heavens (Paradiso XXVIII)

At the height of the ascent Dante sees the nine angelic orders as nine circling fires, and learns that each corresponds to one of the nine revolving heavens, the greater virtue turning the wider sphere:

The circles corporal are wide and narrow
According to the more or less of virtue
Which is distributed through all their parts.

— Paradiso XXVIII, trans. Longfellow, Wikisource. The scheme is complete: Seraphim, Cherubim, Thrones, Dominions, Virtues, Powers, Principalities, Archangels, Angels — each order the intelligence of a heaven.3

Notes

  • Dante versifies the doctrine Aquinas had settled: the luminaries are not living beings in themselves but bodies moved by separate intelligences (angels). He is the timeline’s image of the angelic-mover consensus at its cultural peak — the reading that would dissolve, three centuries later, when the “Intelligence that turns” the sphere gave way to a physical force in Kepler.
  • The idea reaches back through the whole tradition: the angels “appointed… to occupy themselves about… the heavens” already in Athenagoras, hardened by the scholastics into a mover for each sphere.
