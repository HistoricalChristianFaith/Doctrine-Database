Cyril of Alexandria on the procession of the Holy Spirit (the Filioque)

Dates: c. 429 · Wikipedia
Summary: Cyril, a Greek father, uses language both sides would claim — the Spirit is “proper to” and “of the Son” and poured out “through the Son,” yet always “from the Father” as source. The ambiguity is the Filioque argument in miniature.

Claims & evidence

The Spirit proceeds from the Father, but comes through the Son and is His own

“For albeit the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father, yet He comes through the Son, and is His Own; for all things are through the Son from the Father.”

— Commentary on the Gospel of John 9, on John 14:20, trans. T. Randell (Library of Fathers), source. Cyril keeps the verb “proceeds” tied to the Father, but the Spirit “comes through the Son” and is “His Own” — the very juxtaposition both traditions would quarry.

The Spirit of Truth is the Son’s own, not foreign or alien

“How is it, pray, that the Father gives the Spirit of Truth, that is, of the Son, not as foreign or alien, but as His own Spirit… the Spirit is the Son’s?”

— Commentary on the Gospel of John 9, on John 14:16–17, trans. T. Randell (Library of Fathers), source. That the Spirit is “of the Son” and “the Son’s own” is the possessive language later Latins read as an ownership grounded in procession — while the East reads it as consubstantial belonging, not origin.

Shed forth from the Father, but through the Son conveyed to creation

“…inasmuch as the Son by Nature proceeds from the Father and is in Him (being the true Offspring of His Essence), the Spirit—Which is the Father’s by Nature—is brought down to men; shed forth indeed from the Father, but through the Son Himself conveyed to the creature; not merely ministerially or in the manner of a servant, but… proceeding from the Substance Itself of God the Father; and shed forth on those worthy to receive Him through the Word, Which is Consubstantial with and proceeded from Him…”

— Commentary on the Gospel of John 11, on John 17:11, trans. T. Randell (Library of Fathers), source. The Spirit is “shed forth… from the Father, but through the Son… conveyed to the creature” — source in the Father, mediation through the Son. Whether this “through” is economic (the sending in time) or eternal is precisely the point on which East and West divide.

Notes

  • These “of the Son / through the Son” texts are exactly what later Latins quarried for double procession, while Cyril keeps the Father as the one from whom the Spirit “proceeds” and the Son as the one “through” whom He is given — so the East reads the same texts as its own dia tou Huiou. See the Filioque timeline and the Eastern reading fixed by John of Damascus.
  • Cyril also appears on this wiki as the central witness on the person of Christ — see his Christology detail page.
  • Do not overstate Cyril as a Filioque proponent: he is genuinely ambiguous, which is precisely why both traditions cite him as their own.
