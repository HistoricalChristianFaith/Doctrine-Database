Gregory of Nyssa on the procession of the Holy Spirit (the Filioque)

Dates: c. 385 · Wikipedia
Summary: The Cappadocian who comes nearest the Latin instinct yet stops short of it. In On “Not Three Gods” (to Ablabius, c. 385) Gregory distinguishes the Persons by cause and caused: the Father the Cause, the Son directly from Him, and the Spirit “of the Cause” but mediately — by way of the Son. The Spirit is caused through the Son yet remains, by nature, related to the Father. The East reads this as excluding the Filioque; the West read the mediating Son as an early witness to double procession.

Claims & evidence

Cause and caused: the Spirit “of the Cause” through the mediating Son

“…while we confess the invariable character of the nature, we do not deny the difference in respect of cause, and that which is caused, by which alone we apprehend that one Person is distinguished from another;—by our belief, that is, that one is the Cause, and another is of the Cause; and again in that which is of the Cause we recognize another distinction. For one is directly from the first Cause, and another by that which is directly from the first Cause; so that the attribute of being Only-begotten abides without doubt in the Son, and the interposition of the Son, while it guards His attribute of being Only-begotten, does not shut out the Spirit from His relation by way of nature to the Father.”

— On “Not Three Gods” (to Ablabius), trans. Moore & Wilson (NPNF Series 2, vol. 5), source. The Son is directly from the first Cause; the Spirit is “of the Cause” but mediately, “by that which is directly from the first Cause.” The Son’s “interposition” is the Cappadocian formula that comes nearest the Latin instinct — yet Gregory is careful that it does not sever the Spirit’s relation, by nature, to the Father.

Every divine operation from the Father, through the Son, perfected in the Spirit

“…every operation which extends from God to the Creation… has its origin from the Father, and proceeds through the Son, and is perfected in the Holy Spirit.”

— On “Not Three Gods” (to Ablabius), trans. Moore & Wilson (NPNF Series 2, vol. 5), source. The one operation runs “from the Father… through the Son… perfected in the Holy Spirit” — the same ordered from–through–in pattern Basil uses, here applied to the Godhead’s single outward working.

Notes

  • This “the Son between” / “of the Cause by way of the Son” is the Cappadocian formula that comes nearest the Latin instinct yet stops short: the Spirit is caused mediately by way of the Son but of the Father. The West read the mediating Son as an early witness to double procession; the East reads it as order and manner, the Father remaining sole cause.
  • His fellow Cappadocians set the same grammar: Basil of Caesarea and Gregory of Nazianzus. The Latin systematization into a full double procession arrives with Augustine. See the whole arc on the Filioque timeline.
