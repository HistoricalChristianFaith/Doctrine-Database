Gregory of Nazianzus on the procession of the Holy Spirit (the Filioque)

Dates: c. 380 · Wikipedia
Summary: The Cappadocian who gives the East its technical word. Preaching at Constantinople c. 380, Gregory the Theologian distinguishes the Persons by their modes of origin — the Father Unoriginate, the Son by Generation, the Spirit by Procession (a word he says he must coin for clearness) — and grounds the Spirit’s coming forth on “from the Father.” The Father alone is source; the Spirit proceeds from Him, in an ordered Godhead through the Son. The East reads this as excluding the Filioque; the West read the same ordering as an early witness to double procession.

Claims & evidence

The Spirit comes forth from the Father by Procession, not Generation

“The Father is Father, and is Unoriginate, for He is of no one; the Son is Son, and is not unoriginate, for He is of the Father… The Holy Ghost is truly Spirit, coming forth from the Father indeed, but not after the manner of the Son, for it is not by Generation but by Procession (since I must coin a word for the sake of clearness); for neither did the Father cease to be Unbegotten because of His begetting something, nor the Son to be begotten because He is of the Unbegotten (how could that be?), nor is the Spirit changed into Father or Son because He proceeds, or because He is God…”

— Oration 39.12, trans. Browne & Swallow (NPNF Series 2, vol. 7), source. The Spirit “comes forth from the Father” — the Father alone named as origin — and the mode is “Procession,” distinct from the Son’s “Generation.” This is the Eastern vocabulary that later reading takes to shut out a second source in the Son.

The Spirit has all the Son has, save the Generation

“All that the Father hath the Son hath also, except the being Unbegotten; and all that the Son hath the Spirit hath also, except the Generation. And these two matters do not divide the Substance, as I understand it, but rather are divisions within the Substance.”

— Oration 41.9, trans. Browne & Swallow (NPNF Series 2, vol. 7), source. The Persons are distinguished only by their relations of origin (Unbegotten · Generation · Procession), which divide within the one Substance without dividing it — the Cappadocian ordering in which the Spirit is third yet fully God.

Notes

  • Gregory’s fullest procession discussion is his Fifth Theological Oration (Oration 31), where he rests the Spirit’s origin on John 15:26, “who proceeds from the Father” — the proof-text weighed on the “proceeds from the Father” argument page.
  • He appears also on the Trinity timeline. His fellow Cappadocians frame the same grammar: Basil (Father–Son–Spirit order) and Gregory of Nyssa (the Son as mediating “interposition”); the Latin systematization is Augustine. See the Filioque timeline.
