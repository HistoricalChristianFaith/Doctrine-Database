The Third Council of Toledo (589) on the procession of the Holy Spirit (the Filioque)

Dates: 589 · Wikipedia
Summary: At Toledo (589), the council at which the Visigothic kingdom under King Reccared renounced Arianism, the creed was professed with the Spirit proceeding from the Father and the Son — what Philip Schaff called “the first clear trace of the Filioque in the Nicene Creed.” A. E. Burn, Knut Schäferdiek and Shawn S. Smith regard the creed-words in the acts of 589 as a likely later interpolation and place the first secure creedal appearance at the Eighth Council of Toledo (653).

Claims & evidence

Convened by King Reccared I to renounce Arianism (May 589)

The council met at Toledo in May 589 under King Reccared I, its sessions presided over by Leander of Seville. Some sixty-odd bishops attended, among them eight formerly Arian bishops who abjured Arianism; the assembly brought the Visigoths out of Arianism and into the Catholic faith (council overview).

The recited creed professes the Spirit “from the Father and the Son”

Credo in Spiritum Sanctum qui ex Patre Filioque procedit.

— received Latin text of the creed as professed at the Third Council of Toledo, source. In the received form the Nicene-Constantinopolitan article on the Spirit carries the added word Filioque (“and the Son”), in a creed the assembly confessed aloud.

“The first clear trace of the Filioque in the Nicene Creed”

“The first clear trace of the Filioque in the Nicene Creed we find at the third Council of Toledo in Spain, A.D. 589, to seal the triumph of orthodoxy over Arianism.”

— Philip Schaff, The Creeds of Christendom, vol. 1, source.

The purpose ascribed to the clause: the Son’s equality with the Father

Schaff writes that the Latin Church taught the double procession “in the interest of the co-equality of the Son with the Father” (Schaff, source). On this account, to confess that the Spirit proceeds from the Son as well asserted the Son’s equality with the Father against the Arian position.

Whether the words stood in the acts of 589

Whether the Filioque stood in the acts of 589, or was inserted into the creed-text later, is disputed. Philip Schaff finds “the first clear trace of the Filioque in the Nicene Creed” at the Third Council of Toledo (above), and Avery Dulles (Catholic, 1995) writes that the clause was “probably” inserted into the Nicene Creed “when it began to be sung in Spain, late in the sixth century, about the time of the Third Council of Toledo (A.D. 589)” (“The Filioque: What Is at Stake?” Concordia Theological Quarterly 59 (1995), p. 31, source). A. E. Burn, Knut Schäferdiek and Shawn S. Smith regard the creed-words of 589 as a likely later interpolation, placing the secure first creedal appearance at the Eighth Council of Toledo (653); Smith argues the case in “The Insertion of the Filioque into the Nicene Creed and a Letter of Isidore of Seville,” Journal of Early Christian Studies 22.2 (2014), 261–286 (source). The Fourth Council of Toledo (633) carries a double-procession profession — “sed procedentem ex Patre et Filio profitemur” (“but we profess him proceeding from the Father and the Son”). Henry R. Percival held that “the Spanish Church was unaware that it had added to or changed the Constantinopolitan creed” (source).
