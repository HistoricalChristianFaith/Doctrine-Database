John of Damascus on the procession of the Holy Spirit (the Filioque)

Dates: c. 743 · Wikipedia
Summary: The last of the Greek fathers fixes the Eastern doctrine in its permanent form and builds a deliberate wall against the Filioque. The Spirit is “from the Father,” is called “of the Son,” and is manifested and imparted “through the Son” — but is never “from the Son” as from a source, “for the Father alone is cause.” His Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith became the standard synthesis of patristic Trinitarian teaching for the East.

Claims & evidence

The Spirit proceeds from the Father and rests in the Word

“But we must contemplate it as an essential power, existing in its own proper and peculiar subsistence, proceeding from the Father anti resting in the Word, and shewing forth the Word, neither capable of disjunction from God in Whom it exists, and the Word Whose companion it is, nor poured forth to vanish into nothingness…”

— An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith 1.7, trans. S. D. F. Salmond (NPNF Series 2, vol. 9), source. (The “anti” [sic] is an error in the printed text for “and.”) The Spirit proceeds from the Father and rests in the Word — the Son is where the Spirit reposes and is made manifest, not a second fount from which the Spirit springs.

The Spirit proceeds from the Father’s essence, ungenerated

“And only the Holy Spirit proceedeth from the Father’s essence, not having been generated but simply proceeding. For this is the doctrine of Holy Scripture. But the nature of the generation and the procession is quite beyond comprehension.”

— An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith 1.8, trans. S. D. F. Salmond (NPNF Series 2, vol. 9), source. Procession from the Father is grounded in Scripture; its inner nature is a mystery. Note the single, undivided source: the Spirit is from the Father’s essence.

“Spirit of the Son” — yes; “from the Son” — no

“And we speak likewise of the Holy Spirit as from the Father, and call Him the Spirit of the Father. And we do not speak of the Spirit as from the Son: but yet we call Him the Spirit of the Son. For if any one hath not the Spirit of Christ, he is none of His, saith the divine apostle. And we confess that He is manifested and imparted to us through the Son. For He breathed upon His Disciples, says he, and said, Receive ye the Holy Spirit.”

— An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith 1.8, trans. S. D. F. Salmond (NPNF Series 2, vol. 9), source. This is the exact hinge of the whole dispute. The title “Spirit of the Son” is affirmed (Romans 8:9); the Spirit is “manifested and imparted… through the Son”; but the phrase “from the Son” is expressly refused. The closing appeal is to John 20:22 (Christ breathing on the disciples) — a text the West would later cite for the Son’s role in the Spirit’s procession, read here only as the Spirit’s temporal mission, not His eternal origin.

“Through Him from the Father, for the Father alone is cause”

“And we speak also of the Spirit of the Son, not as through proceeding from Him, but as proceeding through Him from the Father. For the Father alone is cause.”

— An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith 1.12, trans. S. D. F. Salmond (NPNF Series 2, vol. 9), source. The formula that fixed the Eastern position: dia tou Huiou (“through the Son”) names the manner of the Spirit’s manifestation, but the single cause (aitia) of both the Son and the Spirit is the Father alone. The Latin double procession is thus excluded at the root: not two causes, but one.

Notes

  • This is the sharpest East/West hinge in the whole timeline: “of the Son” and “through the Son” are affirmed, but “from the Son” as source is denied outright. See the doctrine summary. The systematized Latin double procession is at Augustine; the earlier Greek ambiguity that both sides read their own way is in Cyril of Alexandria; and John’s refutation is carried forward and sharpened into a polemic by Photios of Constantinople.
  • His last quotation invokes John 20:22 (Christ breathing on the disciples) — a proof-text the West adduces for the Son’s role in the procession; John reads it as the Spirit’s temporal sending, not His eternal origin. See the argument page on “from the Father” (John 15:26).
