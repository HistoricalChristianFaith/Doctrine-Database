Basil of Caesarea on the procession of the Holy Spirit (the Filioque)

Dates: c. 375 · Wikipedia
Summary: The first of the three Cappadocians, and the one who fixes the Eastern grammar of the Godhead: an ordered Trinity in which the Father is the sole original cause, the Son the one “through” whom, and the Spirit third and perfecting. In On the Holy Spirit (c. 375) Basil has the Spirit conjoined to the Father “through the one Son,” yet proceeding “out of God” as Breath of His mouth — from the Father alone, but through the Son. The East reads this as excluding the Filioque; the West read the “through the Son” as an early witness to double procession.

Claims & evidence

The Father the original cause, the Son the creative cause, the Spirit the perfecting cause

“And in the creation bethink thee first, I pray thee, of the original cause of all things that are made, the Father; of the creative cause, the Son; of the perfecting cause, the Spirit… For the first principle of existing things is One, creating through the Son and perfecting through the Spirit.”

— On the Holy Spirit 16.38, trans. Blomfield Jackson (NPNF Series 2, vol. 8), source. The one first principle (the Father) works “through the Son and… through the Spirit” — an ordered Godhead in which the Son is the middle term and the Spirit the perfecting third.

Knowledge ascends Spirit–Son–Father; goodness descends Father–Son–Spirit

“Thus the way of the knowledge of God lies from One Spirit through the One Son to the One Father, and conversely the natural Goodness and the inherent Holiness and the royal Dignity extend from the Father through the Only-begotten to the Spirit.”

— On the Holy Spirit 18.47, trans. Blomfield Jackson (NPNF Series 2, vol. 8), source. The order is symmetrical and directional: the divine goodness runs from the Father through the Only-begotten to the Spirit. The West heard in this “through the Son” a channel of the Spirit’s very being; the East reads it as the order of manifestation and operation, with the Father remaining sole source.

The Spirit conjoined to the Father “through the one Son”

“One, moreover, is the Holy Spirit, and we speak of Him singly, conjoined as He is to the one Father through the one Son, and through Himself completing the adorable and blessed Trinity.”

— On the Holy Spirit 18.45, trans. Blomfield Jackson (NPNF Series 2, vol. 8), source. The Spirit is joined to the Father through the one Son — the same dia tou Huiou instinct, here naming the Spirit as the one who completes and perfects the Trinity.

The Spirit proceeds “out of God” — not by generation, but as Breath of His mouth

“…in the sense of proceeding out of God, not by generation, like the Son, but as Breath of His mouth.”

— On the Holy Spirit 18.46, trans. Blomfield Jackson (NPNF Series 2, vol. 8), source. Basil marks the mode of the Spirit’s origin: He comes “out of God” — from the Father — but not by the Son’s manner of generation. The distinct mode (procession, not generation) is the Cappadocian ground on which the East later denies a second source in the Son.

Notes

  • Basil sets the pattern his brother and his friend refine: Gregory of Nyssa sharpens the “cause / caused” grammar and the Son as the mediating “interposition,” and Gregory of Nazianzus names the Spirit’s mode “Procession” over against the Son’s “Generation.”
  • The “from the Father through the Son” language reaches back to Tertullian and forward into the dispute the Latin systematization by Augustine would force; see the whole arc on the Filioque timeline.
