Basil of Caesarea on the procession of the Holy Spirit (the Filioque)

Dates: c. 375 · Wikipedia
Summary: In On the Holy Spirit (c. 375) Basil describes God’s action toward creation as from the Father, through the Son, perfected in the Spirit: the Father “the original cause of all things that are made,” the Son the creative cause and the Spirit the perfecting cause. The Spirit is conjoined to the Father “through the one Son,” and proceeds “out of God” not by generation but “as Breath of His mouth.”

Claims & evidence

Basil on the original, creative and perfecting causes “of all things that are made”

“And in the creation bethink thee first, I pray thee, of the original cause of all things that are made, the Father; of the creative cause, the Son; of the perfecting cause, the Spirit… For the first principle of existing things is One, creating through the Son and perfecting through the Spirit.”

— On the Holy Spirit 16.38, trans. Blomfield Jackson (NPNF Series 2, vol. 8), source. Basil describes God’s action toward creation as from the Father, through the Son, perfected in the Spirit: the one first principle creates “through the Son” and perfects “through the Spirit.”

Knowledge ascends Spirit–Son–Father; goodness descends Father–Son–Spirit

“Thus the way of the knowledge of God lies from One Spirit through the One Son to the One Father, and conversely the natural Goodness and the inherent Holiness and the royal Dignity extend from the Father through the Only-begotten to the Spirit.”

— On the Holy Spirit 18.47, trans. Blomfield Jackson (NPNF Series 2, vol. 8), source. Basil sets out an order running both ways: knowledge ascends from the Spirit through the Son to the Father, and the divine goodness runs from the Father through the Only-begotten to the Spirit.

The Spirit conjoined to the Father “through the one Son”

“One, moreover, is the Holy Spirit, and we speak of Him singly, conjoined as He is to the one Father through the one Son, and through Himself completing the adorable and blessed Trinity.”

— On the Holy Spirit 18.45, trans. Blomfield Jackson (NPNF Series 2, vol. 8), source. Basil writes that the Spirit is joined to the Father through the one Son (dia tou Huiou), and names the Spirit as the one who completes the Trinity.

The Spirit proceeds “out of God” — not by generation, but as Breath of His mouth

“…in the sense of proceeding out of God, not by generation, like the Son, but as Breath of His mouth.”

— On the Holy Spirit 18.46, trans. Blomfield Jackson (NPNF Series 2, vol. 8), source. Basil marks the mode of the Spirit’s origin: He comes “out of God,” not by the Son’s manner of generation.
