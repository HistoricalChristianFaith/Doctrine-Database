Photios I of Constantinople on the procession of the Holy Spirit (the Filioque)

Dates: c. 867–886 · Wikipedia
Summary: Photios, patriarch of Constantinople, argues in the Mystagogy of the Holy Spirit that the Spirit proceeds from the Father (John 15:26), that the Filioque introduces two causes into the Godhead against the Father’s “Monarchy,” and that the addition to the creed is illegitimate. The Mystagogy is usually dated c. 885/886; his encyclical of 867 had already condemned the Filioque — the “c. 867–886” span covers both.

Claims & evidence

Photios on “two causes” and the Monarchy of the Father

“if two causes are discerned in the divine, sovereign, and transcendent Trinity, and if the Spirit thus flows from two hypostases, then where is the much-hymned, divine majesty of the Monarchy?”

— On the Mystagogy of the Holy Spirit §11, Myriobiblos text. Photios’s objection: to make the Spirit proceed from the Father and the Son is, he argues, to posit two causes (aitiai) in the Godhead, against the Father’s “Monarchy,” the Father being the origin of both Son and Spirit. (A scholarly translation is Joseph P. Farrell, The Mystagogy of the Holy Spirit, Holy Cross Orthodox Press, 1987.)

Photios on the Second Ecumenical Council’s confession

“the second of the seven Holy and Ecumenical Synods directly dogmatised that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father.”

— On the Mystagogy of the Holy Spirit §5, Myriobiblos text. Photios grounds the Eastern position in the creed of the First Council of Constantinople (381), which confessed the Spirit “who proceedeth from the Father” — with no “and the Son.” On his reckoning the Latin insertion is also an unauthorized addition to a conciliar creed; see the interpretation page Did the councils’ ban on “a different faith” forbid adding the Filioque to the creed?
