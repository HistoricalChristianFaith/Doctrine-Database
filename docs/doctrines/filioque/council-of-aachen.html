Charlemagne, the Council of Aachen (809), and Pope Leo III on the procession of the Holy Spirit (the Filioque)

Dates: 809 · Wikipedia
Summary: After Greek monks in Jerusalem objected to the Frankish chanting of the creed with the Filioque (c. 808), Charlemagne’s Council of Aachen (809) sent envoys to Rome about the addition. Pope Leo III affirmed the doctrine but declined to add the words to the creed, and set up the creed without them on two silver shields in St. Peter’s. The creed with the Filioque was first sung at a papal Mass in 1014.

Claims & evidence

c. 808: Greek monks in Jerusalem charge the Franks with heresy for chanting the Filioque

Around 808 Greek monks in Jerusalem accused Frankish monks at the monastery on the Mount of Olives of heresy for chanting the creed with the Filioque, the dispute reaching both the pope and the Frankish court (source).

809: the Council of Aix-la-Chapelle (Aachen) affirms the doctrine and sends envoys to Rome

“In 809 a council was held at Aix-la-Chapelle by Charlemagne, and from it three divines were sent to confer with the Pope, Leo III, upon the subject. The Pope opposed the insertion of the Filioque on the express ground that the General Councils had forbidden any addition to be made to their formulary.”

— Henry R. Percival, “Historical Excursus on the introduction into the Creed of the words ‘and the Son,’” in The Seven Ecumenical Councils (NPNF2 vol. 14), source. On Percival’s account Leo objected not to the doctrine but to altering the conciliar creed.

The two silver shields in St. Peter’s: the creed hung without the addition

“he presented two silver shields to the Confessio in St. Peter’s at Rome, on one of which was engraved the creed in Latin and on the other in Greek, without the addition.”

— Percival, The Seven Ecumenical Councils (NPNF2 vol. 14), source.

Reported: the shields are said to have carried a personal inscription by Leo

⚠ unverified: reference works widely report that the shields bore the line “Haec Leo posui amore et cautela orthodoxae fidei” (“These I, Leo, set up for love and safeguard of the orthodox faith”). This wording is repeated in secondary literature but has not been checked against a primary source here.

1014: the creed with the Filioque is first sung at a papal Mass

“It was not till 1014 that for the first time the interpolated creed was used at mass with the sanction of the Pope. In that year Benedict VIII. acceded to the urgent request of Henry II. of Germany and so the papal authority was forced to yield.”

— Percival, The Seven Ecumenical Councils (NPNF2 vol. 14), source. Percival places the change at the request of Emperor Henry II.

Reception and related pages

  • The North American Orthodox–Catholic Theological Consultation (2003) dates the meeting between Leo III and Charlemagne’s delegation to 810, and reports that Leo “affirmed the orthodoxy of the term Filioque, and approved its use in catechesis and personal professions of faith,” but disapproved its inclusion in the text of the creed of 381 (source). Anthony Maas (1909) writes that Leo “approved of the doctrine conveyed by the Filioque, but gave the advice to omit the expression in the Creed” (source); the Pontifical Council for Promoting Christian Unity (1995), that he acted “in his anxiety to preserve unity with the Orient in the confession of faith” (source). How Leo’s refusal bears on the canonical question is set out on the interpretation page Did the councils’ ban on “a different faith” forbid adding the Filioque to the creed?
  • The words appear in a recited creed in Visigothic Spain in the received acts of the Third Council of Toledo (589); A. E. Burn, Knut Schäferdiek and Shawn S. Smith place the first secure creedal appearance at the Eighth Council of Toledo (653). See the Third Council of Toledo (589).
