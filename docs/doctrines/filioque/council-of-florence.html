The Council of Florence (1439) on the procession of the Holy Spirit (the Filioque)

Dates: 1439 · Wikipedia
Summary: The last great reunion attempt tried to dissolve the quarrel by declaring the Greek “through the Son” and the Latin “from the Son” equivalent — double procession “as from one principle and a single spiration.” The union collapsed when one Greek bishop, Mark of Ephesus, refused to sign.

Claims & evidence

Double procession “as from one principle and a single spiration”

“the holy Spirit is eternally from the Father and the Son … proceeds from both eternally as from one principle and a single spiration.”

— Laetentur Caeli, the Bull of Union with the Greeks (6 July 1439), Pope Eugenius IV, trans. Norman Tanner, source (cf. Papal Encyclicals text). The phrase “one principle” was the Latin answer to the Eastern charge that the Filioque posited two causes in the Godhead.

“Through the Son” and “from the Son” declared equivalent

“when holy doctors and fathers say that the holy Spirit proceeds from the Father through the Son, this bears the sense that thereby also the Son should be signified, according to the Greeks indeed as cause, and according to the Latins as principle of the subsistence of the holy Spirit.”

— Laetentur Caeli (6 July 1439), trans. Norman Tanner, source. The bull ruled the two idioms — the Greek dia tou Huiou and the Latin Filioque — as expressing one and the same faith in different words.

Mark of Ephesus refused to sign, and the union collapsed

Mark of Ephesus (Mark Eugenikos) was the one bishop present who refused to sign the decree of union; his refusal led the East to repudiate it (source). It is widely reported that Emperor John VIII Palaiologos remarked the union had accomplished nothing since Mark had not signed — a line to be taken as reported rather than primary-verified. The union was never received in the East, and the schism endured.

Notes

  • “One principle, not two” was the Latin reply to the “two causes” charge pressed by Photios I of Constantinople; the doctrine it defends is the double procession systematized by Augustine.
  • This same council later (1442) issued a separate bull, Cantate Domino, on the biblical canon — a different document treated elsewhere on this wiki. This page concerns only the 1439 Greek-union session and the Filioque.
