Tertullian on the procession of the Holy Spirit (the Filioque)

Dates: c. 213 · Wikipedia
Summary: The earliest Latin procession-language, and it already routes the Spirit “from the Father through the Son.” Writing against the modalist Praxeas c. 213, Tertullian places the Spirit “third” in an ordered Godhead that flows “from the Father through” connected steps — not yet the developed Filioque, but the seed the Latin tradition would grow.

Claims & evidence

The Spirit proceeds “from the Father through the Son”

“The same remark (I wish also to be formally) made by me with respect to the third degree in the Godhead, because I believe the Spirit to proceed from no other source than from the Father through the Son.”

— Against Praxeas 4, trans. Dr. Holmes (ANF vol. 3), source. This a Patre per Filium (“from the Father through the Son”) is the phrase both later traditions would claim: the West as an early anticipation of double procession, the East as its own “through the Son” (dia tou Huiou) with the Father as sole source.

The Spirit is “third” in a Godhead that flows down from the Father

“Now the Spirit indeed is third from God and the Son; just as the fruit of the tree is third from the root, or as the stream out of the river is third from the fountain, or as the apex of the ray is third from the sun. Nothing, however, is alien from that original source whence it derives its own properties. In like manner the Trinity, flowing down from the Father through intertwined and connected steps, does not at all disturb the Monarchy, whilst it at the same time guards the state of the Economy.”

— Against Praxeas 8, trans. Dr. Holmes (ANF vol. 3), source. The analogies (root–tree–fruit, fountain–river–stream, sun–ray–apex) put the Son on the path by which the Spirit derives — the derivation runs through the middle term. Tertullian’s point is anti-modalist (the Persons are distinct, not divided), but the ordered “flowing down… through” is the Latin instinct Augustine would later systematize.

Notes

  • This is a seed, not the doctrine: Tertullian never says the Spirit proceeds “from the Son” as from a source, and his “through the Son” is compatible with the later Eastern reading. Do not overstate it as an early Filioque proper — it is the raw material both sides would develop.
  • Tertullian coined the Latin vocabulary of the Trinity itself; see his fuller treatment on the Trinity timeline. The systematized Latin double procession arrives with Augustine; the Eastern “through the Son / from the Father alone” reading is fixed by John of Damascus.
