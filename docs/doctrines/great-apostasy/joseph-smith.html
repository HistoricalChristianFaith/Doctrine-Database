Joseph Smith on the Great Apostasy

Dates: 1805–1844 · Wikipedia
Summary: The founding prophet of the Latter-day Saint movement teaches a total apostasy as the premise of the Restoration: in his First Vision account he is told that all the existing churches are wrong, their creeds an abomination and their professors corrupt; the loss he describes is not of doctrine only but of divine authority — a broken priesthood requiring literal restoration by heavenly ministration.

Claims & evidence

“They were all wrong” — the First Vision account on the churches

I was answered that I must join none of them, for they were all wrong; and the Personage who addressed me said that all their creeds were an abomination in his sight; that those professors were all corrupt; that: ‘they draw near to me with their lips, but their hearts are far from me, they teach for doctrines the commandments of men, having a form of godliness, but they deny the power thereof.’

— Joseph Smith—History 1:19 (Pearl of Great Price), churchofjesuschrist.org. The question that prompts it (v. 18) is “which of all the sects was right… which I should join”; the answer is that none is. The account presents the total-apostasy premise as revelation. 1

Universal straying, and the “only true” church raised to repair it

For they have strayed from mine ordinances, and have broken mine everlasting covenant;

— Doctrine and Covenants 1:15, churchofjesuschrist.org. The same revelation names the newly organized church “the only true and living church upon the face of the whole earth” (1:30).2

The loss is of authority, not doctrine only

They told me that all religious denominations were believing in incorrect doctrines and that none of them was acknowledged of God as His Church and kingdom

— the Wentworth Letter (1842), as reprinted in Ensign, July 2002. The distinctively Latter-day Saint form of the claim is that valid priesthood authority disappeared with the apostles and had to be restored by angelic ordination, so that on this teaching reform from within an existing church could not suffice.3

Reception and debate

  • As a historical claim about the patristic and medieval church — that all valid authority, ordinances, and doctrine were lost, so that no legitimate church survived — the premise is disputed by Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant confessional readings of the same centuries, which hold that the church continued; the readings are set out at “Did the church wholly apostatize after the apostolic age?”
  • The restoration narrative. Latter-day Saint teaching places the restoration of the priesthood in 1829, the Aaronic by John the Baptist and the Melchizedek by the resurrected Peter, James, and John; the accounts are Smith’s and Oliver Cowdery’s ⚠ unverified. The Joseph Smith Papers editors record that Smith’s 1832 history speaks of “the reception of the holy Priesthood by the ministring of—Aangels,” naming no messenger, and call the revelation as expanded for the 1835 Doctrine and Covenants (now section 27) “the first clear documentary reference to angelic visitations by Peter, James, and John.”4
  • The systematic marshalling of this premise into a historical case is James E. Talmage’s The Great Apostasy (1909); the pre-Mormon total-apostasy position on this timeline is Roger Williams’s.
