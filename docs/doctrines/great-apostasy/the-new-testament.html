The New Testament on the Great Apostasy

Dates: c. 50–95 (the apostolic writings)
Summary: The apostolic letters foretell a serious falling-away from the faith — the seed-texts every restorationist cites — while the same writings promise that the church itself will never be destroyed. Both sets of texts are gathered here; whether the first predicts a total apostasy is weighed on the argument page.

Claims & evidence

Paul foretells “a falling away” and a “man of sin”

Let no man deceive you by any means: for that day shall not come, except there come a falling away first, and that man of sin be revealed, the son of perdition;

— 2 Thessalonians 2:3, KJV. The Greek hē apostasia (“the apostasy / falling away”) is the word that names the whole question. Paul makes it the sign preceding “the day of the Lord” — a defection and a coming “man of sin,” not, on its face, the disappearance of the church.1

Wolves from without and within (Paul to the Ephesian elders)

For I know this, that after my departing shall grievous wolves enter in among you, not sparing the flock. Also of your own selves shall men arise, speaking perverse things, to draw away disciples after them.

— Acts 20:29–30, KJV. A warning of corruption arising even from the ordained leadership — “of your own selves” — that restorationist readers take as the opening of the great decline.2

A departure in “the latter times”

Now the Spirit speaketh expressly, that in the latter times some shall depart from the faith, giving heed to seducing spirits, and doctrines of devils;

— 1 Timothy 4:1, KJV; cf. 2 Timothy 4:3–4 (“they will not endure sound doctrine… shall be turned unto fables”) and, from the prophets, Amos 8:11 (“a famine… of hearing the words of the LORD”), read by restorationists as a type of the apostasy. Note the quantifier: “some shall depart,” not all.3

Yet the church will not be destroyed (Christ’s promises)

And I say also unto thee, That thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my church; and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it.

— Matthew 16:18, KJV; with Matthew 28:20 (“lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world”). These are the counter-texts: the same corpus that predicts defection promises the church’s permanence. The tension between the two sets is the whole crux — a falling away of many is compatible with a surviving remnant church, which is the reading that resists the total-apostasy thesis. 4

Notes

  • These texts are the shared scriptural floor for every restorationist movement — Roger Williams’s Seekers, the Stone–Campbell Restoration, the Latter-day Saints, later the Jehovah’s Witnesses. Their force for a total apostasy is weighed at “Do the New Testament prophecies foretell a total falling-away?”
  • The permanence promise of Matthew 16:18 is itself contested in the other direction: it does not, on its own, guarantee an unbroken visible succession either — see, on the church-order timeline, “Does ‘the gates of hell shall not prevail’ promise an unbroken visible succession?” (weak). The verse tells against the church’s disappearance without settling the shape of its continuance.
