The New Testament on the Great Apostasy

Dates: c. 50–95 (the apostolic writings)
Summary: The apostolic letters warn of a falling-away from the faith, texts that restorationist writers cite as foretelling the apostasy; the same writings contain Christ’s promises about his church, which the Catechism of the Catholic Church and the Westminster Confession cite for its continuance. Both sets of texts are gathered here; whether the first foretells a total apostasy is disputed on the interpretation page.

Claims & evidence

Paul foretells “a falling away” and a “man of sin”

Let no man deceive you by any means: for that day shall not come, except there come a falling away first, and that man of sin be revealed, the son of perdition;

— 2 Thessalonians 2:3, KJV. The Greek hē apostasia (“the apostasy / falling away”) is the word that names the whole question. Paul makes it, with the revealing of a “man of sin,” a sign preceding “that day.”1 Kent P. Jackson (Latter-day Saint) reads apostasia as “rebellion” and the verse as describing “a rebellion against God and His position in the Church,” with the “man of sin” equated with Satan;2 Luther and the Westminster Confession identify the “man of sin” with the papacy, within a church they hold continued.3

Wolves from without and within (Paul to the Ephesian elders)

For I know this, that after my departing shall grievous wolves enter in among you, not sparing the flock. Also of your own selves shall men arise, speaking perverse things, to draw away disciples after them.

— Acts 20:29–30, KJV. A warning of corruption arising from within the leadership addressed — “of your own selves” — which Talmage reads as foretelling the apostasy.4

A departure in “the latter times”

Now the Spirit speaketh expressly, that in the latter times some shall depart from the faith, giving heed to seducing spirits, and doctrines of devils;

— 1 Timothy 4:1, KJV; cf. 2 Timothy 4:3–4 (“they will not endure sound doctrine… shall be turned unto fables”) and, from the prophets, Amos 8:11 (“a famine… of hearing the words of the LORD”), which the Latter-day Saint teaching summary on apostasy lists among its scripture references.5

Christ’s promises concerning his church (Matthew 16:18; 28:20)

And I say also unto thee, That thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my church; and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it.

— Matthew 16:18, KJV; with Matthew 28:20 (“lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world”).6 These texts are cited on the other side: the Catechism of the Catholic Church cites Matthew 16:18 for the church being “indestructible,”7 and the Westminster Confession cites Matthew 16:18 and 28:19–20 for its teaching that “there shall be always a Church on earth.”3

Reception and debate

  • These texts are cited by restorationist writers, among them the Latter-day Saints, whose teaching summary on apostasy lists Amos 8:11–12, Acts 20:28–30 and 2 Timothy 4:3–4 among its references;5 Roger Williams’s Seekers, the Stone–Campbell Restoration and the Jehovah’s Witnesses are also named as citing them ⚠ unverified. How they bear on a total apostasy is recorded at “Did the church wholly apostatize after the apostolic age?”
  • Matthew 16:18 is also cited in the separate question of an unbroken visible succession, on the church-government and Baptist successionism timelines.
