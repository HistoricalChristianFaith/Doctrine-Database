Ambrose of Milan on the sacraments

Dates: c. 390 · Wikipedia
Summary: Ambrose's mystagogical catecheses expound "the sacraments" to the newly baptized as a small, concrete cluster of initiation rites — the font, the anointing/"spiritual seal", and the eucharist — each made effective by the word of Christ. He is a rich witness to sacramental theology, but he counts nothing: the "sacraments" are simply the holy rites at hand, with no total and no closed list.

Claims & evidence

Baptism: the "Sacrament of Baptism," effective by the cross of Christ

Ambrose calls the font a sacrament and insists its power lies not in the water but in the word:

in baptism, the water, the blood, and the Spirit, are one, for if you take away one of these, the Sacrament of Baptism does not exist. For what is water without the cross of Christ? A common element, without any sacramental effect.

— Ambrose, On the Mysteries (De Mysteriis), ch. 4, trans. H. de Romestin (Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, 2nd ser., vol. 10), source.1

The "spiritual seal" — the post-baptismal anointing

After the font comes the anointing (later called confirmation), which Ambrose expounds as the sealing of the Spirit:

God the Father sealed you, Christ the Lord strengthened you, and gave the earnest of the Spirit in your heart…

— Ambrose, On the Mysteries, ch. 7, source.2 The parallel On the Sacraments calls this the "spiritual seal" that follows the font.

The eucharist: made what it is by the word of Christ

The third rite of initiation is the altar, whose consecration Ambrose grounds, again, in the word:

For that sacrament which you receive is made what it is by the word of Christ… In that sacrament is Christ, because it is the Body of Christ, it is therefore not bodily food but spiritual.

— Ambrose, On the Mysteries, ch. 9, source.3

Notes

  • Theology without arithmetic. Ambrose has a developed doctrine of the sacraments — visible sign, word joined to element, the effect worked by Christ — but he treats them as the concrete rites of Christian initiation (font, seal, altar), never as a numbered set. The very idea of asking "how many sacraments are there?" is absent; that question belongs to the schoolmen eight centuries later.
  • Ambrose across the wiki. He is also a witness on the Real Presence, on the perpetual virginity of Mary, and on remarriage, among others.
