Tertullian on the sacraments

Dates: c. 203 · Wikipedia
Summary: The Latin word later fixed to a numbered list of rites begins, in the first Latin theologian, as an ordinary word for the soldier's oath of enlistment — a pledge of allegiance — carried over to the Christian's bond to Christ in baptism. There is no fixed category of "the sacraments" and no count; the term is still doing everyday military and religious work.

Claims & evidence

"Sacrament" is the soldier's oath — the divine one against the human one

Weighing whether a believer may serve in the army, Tertullian sets the Christian's sacramentum (his oath to Christ) against the soldier's sacramentum (the military oath of loyalty to Caesar) — two oaths that cannot both be sworn:

There is no agreement between the divine and the human sacrament, the standard of Christ and the standard of the devil, the camp of light and the camp of darkness. One soul cannot be due to two masters—God and Caesar.

— Tertullian, On Idolatry, ch. XIX ("Concerning Military Service"), trans. S. Thelwall (Ante-Nicene Fathers, vol. III), source.1 The whole force of the sentence turns on sacramentum meaning an oath: the "human sacrament" is the enlisting soldier's pledge, the "divine sacrament" the Christian's answering pledge to God. The word names a bond of allegiance, not a rite in a list.

Baptism as the oath of enlistment in God's warfare

The same military metaphor governs his exhortation to the imprisoned confessors: the Christian life is a campaign entered by an oath, and baptism is that swearing-in:

we were called to the warfare of the living God in our very response to the sacramental words. Well, no soldier comes out to the campaign laden with luxuries…

— Tertullian, To the Martyrs, ch. 3, trans. S. Thelwall (Ante-Nicene Fathers, vol. III), source.2 The Latin sacramentum rendered "the warfare… in our very response to the sacramental words" is the oath of enlistment: baptism admits the believer to God's army as the military oath admitted the recruit to Rome's.

Notes

  • Why this stands at the head of the timeline. Nothing in Tertullian counts the sacraments or treats "sacrament" as a bounded class of grace-conferring rites. The word is a live metaphor — oath, pledge, mystery — used of baptism, of the faith, of the soldier's loyalty. The fixed enumeration at seven lies more than a thousand years off; the concept it would number has not yet hardened.
  • Tertullian across the wiki. He is also a witness on penance (the "second plank after shipwreck" and, as a Montanist, the denial of church forgiveness for grave sin), among many other doctrines.
