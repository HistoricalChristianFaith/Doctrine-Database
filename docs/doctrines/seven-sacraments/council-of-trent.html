The Council of Trent on the seven sacraments

Dates: 1547 (Session 7, under Paul III) · Wikipedia
Summary: Answering Luther and the Reformers, the seventh session of the Council of Trent (3 March 1547) defined that the sacraments of the new law were "all instituted by Jesus Christ, our Lord," and anathematized anyone who says that "they are more, or less, than seven" or that any one of them "is not truly and properly a sacrament." The list names the same seven, in the same order, as Peter Lombard and the Council of Florence.

Claims & evidence

Seven, all instituted by Christ

If any one saith, that the sacraments of the New Law were not all instituted by Jesus Christ, our Lord; or, that they are more, or less, than seven, to wit, Baptism, Confirmation, the Eucharist, Penance, Extreme Unction, Order, and Matrimony; or even that any one of these seven is not truly and properly a sacrament; let him be anathema.

— Council of Trent, Session 7 (1547), Canons on the Sacraments in General, Canon 1, trans. J. Waterworth, source.1

Notes

  • Form of the canon. Where Florence had stated the seven, Trent's canon is cast as an anathema. It fixes both the definition (a true and proper sacrament, instituted by Christ) and the number (seven). The later Session 14 (1551) went on to define penance in detail.
  • Scope of the definition. D. J. Kennedy (Catholic, 1912) writes that "The Council of Trent did not define explicitly and formally that all the sacraments were instituted immediately by Christ," noting that before the council "Peter Lombard … Hugh of St. Victor … Alexander of Hales … held that some sacraments were instituted by the Apostles, using power that had been given to them by Jesus Christ," and that "now nearly all theologians unite in saying: it is theologically certain, but not defined (de fide) that Christ immediately instituted all the sacraments of the New Law."2 Luther (1520) had held that a sacrament requires a sign instituted by Christ with a promise of forgiveness, and so counted two (see Luther detail). What the late list of seven shows is set out on the disputed reading.
  • Trent across the wiki. The Council of Trent is also a witness on penance (Session 14), the Real Presence, justification, and the Old Testament canon.
