The Council of Florence on the seven sacraments

Dates: 1439 (Session 8, Exsultate Deo) · Wikipedia
Summary: In the decree of union with the Armenians, Exsultate Deo (22 November 1439), the Council of Florence enumerated the seven sacraments as a body — with their matter, form, and minister — as sacraments that not only prefigure but contain and bestow grace. The decree names the same seven, in the same order, as Peter Lombard's list.

Claims & evidence

Seven sacraments of the new law, enumerated

The decree names the same seven, in the same order, as Lombard's list, and distinguishes the sacraments of the new law from those of the old by their causing grace:

There are seven sacraments of the new Law, namely baptism, confirmation, eucharist, penance, extreme unction, orders and matrimony, which differ greatly from the sacraments of the old Law. The latter were not causes of grace, but only prefigured the grace to be given through the passion of Christ; whereas the former, ours, both contain grace and bestow it on those who worthily receive them.

— Council of Florence, Session 8, Bull of union with the Armenians Exsultate Deo (22 Nov. 1439), trans. Norman P. Tanner, Decrees of the Ecumenical Councils, source.1

The rationale for the count

The decree explains the ordering of the seven — five for the individual, two for the church:

The first five of these are directed to the spiritual perfection of each person in himself, the last two to the regulation and increase of the whole church.

— Council of Florence, Exsultate Deo (Tanner), source.2 The decree introduces the scheme in these words: "for the easier instruction of the Armenians… we reduce the truth about the sacraments of the church to the following brief scheme."

Notes

  • Form of the decree. Florence states the seven in a teaching scheme and attaches no anathema; Trent's canon of 1547 is cast as an anathema. Pierre Pourrat (Catholic, 1910) writes that at Florence the number "was considered as one of those upon which no discord had ever arisen between the two Churches"; Philip Schaff (Protestant, 1867) writes that the number was "publicly sanctioned by the council of Florence in 1439 with the concurrence of the Greek church."3 John Meyendorff (Orthodox, 1974) dates the first appearance of the seven in an Eastern text to the profession of faith required of Michael Palaeologus in 1267, "prepared, of course, by Latin theologians."4 Philip L. Reynolds (2016) writes that the bull "incorporated a summary of the doctrine of the sacraments adapted from an exposition of the articles of faith and the sacraments by Thomas Aquinas," and that the summary "followed the plan established by Peter Lombard."5
  • Florence across the wiki. The same council is a witness on the Old Testament canon (its 1442 bull Cantate Domino, listing the full deuterocanon) and on the Filioque (the 1439 union with the Greeks). This page concerns its 1439 decree for the Armenians.
