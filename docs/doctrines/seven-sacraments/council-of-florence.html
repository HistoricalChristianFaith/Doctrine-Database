The Council of Florence on the seven sacraments

Dates: 1439 (Session 8, Exsultate Deo) · Wikipedia
Summary: In the decree of union with the Armenians, Exsultate Deo (22 November 1439), an ecumenical council for the first time enumerated the seven sacraments as a body — with their matter, form, and minister — and grounded them in the definition Peter Lombard had made standard: sacraments that not only prefigure but contain and bestow grace. Lombard's schoolroom list here becomes conciliar teaching.

Claims & evidence

Seven sacraments of the new law, enumerated

The decree adopts Lombard's exact list and, echoing Hugh and Lombard, distinguishes the sacraments of the new law from those of the old precisely by their causing grace:

There are seven sacraments of the new Law, namely baptism, confirmation, eucharist, penance, extreme unction, orders and matrimony, which differ greatly from the sacraments of the old Law. The latter were not causes of grace, but only prefigured the grace to be given through the passion of Christ; whereas the former, ours, both contain grace and bestow it on those who worthily receive them.

— Council of Florence, Session 8, Bull of union with the Armenians Exsultate Deo (22 Nov. 1439), trans. Norman P. Tanner, Decrees of the Ecumenical Councils, source.1

The rationale for the count

The decree even explains the ordering of the seven — five for the individual, two for the church — presenting the number as an intelligible whole:

The first five of these are directed to the spiritual perfection of each person in himself, the last two to the regulation and increase of the whole church.

— Council of Florence, Exsultate Deo (Tanner), source.2 The council frames the whole as a teaching aid: "for the easier instruction of the Armenians… we reduce the truth about the sacraments of the church to the following brief scheme."

Notes

  • Standard, not yet defended. Florence states the seven with confidence but does not yet anathematize the alternatives — there was, in 1439, no organized Protestant denial to answer. That defensive, dogmatic form waits for Trent a century later. Florence's role is to lift Lombard's enumeration into an ecumenical decree.
  • Florence across the wiki. The same council is a witness on the Old Testament canon (its 1442 bull Cantate Domino, listing the full deuterocanon) and on the Filioque (the 1439 union with the Greeks). This page concerns its 1439 decree for the Armenians.
