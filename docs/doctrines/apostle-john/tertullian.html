Tertullian on John, son of Zebedee

Dates: c. 155–220 (writing c. 200) · Wikipedia
Summary: Tertullian gives the earliest extant witness to the Roman “boiling oil” story: arguing that the apostolic churches still keep the apostles' teaching, he points to Rome, where (he says) John was plunged unhurt into boiling oil before being sent to his island exile.

Claims & evidence

Plunged unhurt into boiling oil at Rome, then exiled

Tertullian is listing the great churches that can show an unbroken apostolic pedigree; of Rome he writes:

How happy is its church, on which apostles poured forth all their doctrine along with their blood! where Peter endures a passion like his Lord's! where Paul wins his crown in a death like John's; where the Apostle John was first plunged, unhurt, into boiling oil, and thence remitted to his island-exile!

— Prescription against Heretics 36.

This is the earliest extant mention of the boiling-oil ordeal, later localized at the Latin Gate in Rome (the feast “St John before the Latin Gate”, which Leopold Fonck reports is “first mentioned in the Sacramentary of Adrian I (772-95)”). Tertullian tells it as an ordeal survived, and pairs it with the Patmos exile that Revelation reports (Revelation 1:9).

Reception and debate

  • No earlier extant source reports the ordeal, and it is absent from Irenaeus, Clement and Eusebius, who otherwise chronicle John's life. Leopold Fonck (“St. John the Evangelist,” Catholic Encyclopedia, 1910) places it before the Patmos exile, “according to Tertullian's testimony.” See the summary timeline.
  • The medieval Golden Legend expands it into the cauldron before the Latin Gate; see the summary timeline.
  • Tertullian and the New Prophecy. Tertullian spent his later years in the Montanist “New Prophecy”; he was never tried or condemned by any synod.
