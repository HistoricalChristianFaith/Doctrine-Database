David Kimhi (Radak) on Who Killed Goliath?

Dates: c. 1200 · Wikipedia
Summary: The Provençal grammarian and commentator "Radak" reports both answers to the crux — the harmonization from 1 Chronicles ("Elhanan killed the kinsman of Goliath") and the Targum's identification of Elhanan with David — and then says that the identification puzzles him. Why Scripture would call David "Elhanan," he writes, "I do not know the reason."

Claims & evidence

Both readings reported, with a comment on the name "Elhanan"

…ולדעת יונתן אלחנן זהו דוד וכן תרגם הפסוק וקטל דוד בר ישי מחי פרוכת בית מקדשא דמבית לחם ית גלית… אבל מה שקרא דוד אלחנן לא ידעתי לו טעם:

— Radak on II Samuel 21:19, Hebrew via Sefaria (no published English translation is available there; the Hebrew is quoted verbatim). Rendered: "…and according to Jonathan [the Targum], this Elhanan is David, and so he translated the verse: 'and David son of Jesse, weaver of the curtains of the sanctuary, of Bethlehem, killed Goliath'… But as to why [Scripture] called David 'Elhanan,' I do not know the reason for it." Radak also relays the alternative that harmonizes via 1 Chronicles — that the man slain was Goliath's kinsman — setting the two solutions side by side.

Reception and debate

  • Two answers side by side. Radak reports both the Chronicles-harmonization and the Targum-identification without choosing between them, and adds his comment on the name, "lo yadaʿti lo taʿam" ("I do not know the reason"). Rashi gives the identification alone; Kennicott (1753) and McCarter (1984) hold instead that one of the two texts is corrupt.
  • Disputed reading. The identification Radak reports is set out, with the reading that Elhanan is a distinct warrior, on the page Is "Elhanan" in 2 Samuel 21:19 another name for David?
