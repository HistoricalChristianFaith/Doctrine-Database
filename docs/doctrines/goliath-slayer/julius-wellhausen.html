Julius Wellhausen on Who Killed Goliath?

Dates: 1871 (Der Text der Bücher Samuelis) · Wikipedia
Summary: The German critic's monograph on the text of Samuel locates a mechanical fault in 2 Samuel 21:19 — the intruded word "weavers" (oregim) — and warns against simply importing the Chronicler's reading. Where Kennicott treated Chronicles as the original, Wellhausen points to the Chronicler's "tendency to avoid archaisms."

Claims & evidence

Wellhausen on the spelling "Jaare" and the word "oregim"

"19. Die Schreibung [jaʿrê] statt [jāʿîr] Chr. wurde wohl durch das folgende [ōregîm] veranlasst, dessen zufälliges Eindringen aus der darunter stehenden Zeile leicht zu erkennen ist."

— Der Text der Bücher Samuelis (Göttingen, 1871), p. 210, source. Rendered: "The spelling Jaare instead of Jair (Chronicles) was probably occasioned by the following oregim ('weavers'), whose accidental intrusion from the line below is easy to recognize." The corrupt patronymic and the intruded "weavers," on his account, are copyist's accidents in the text of Samuel.

On adopting the Chronicler's text for Samuel

"Bei der Tendenz der letzteren, die Archaismen zu vermeiden, wird man sich hüten müssen, einfach ihren Text auch für 2 Sam. zu adoptieren."

— Der Text der Bücher Samuelis, p. 210, source. Rendered: given the Chronicler's tendency to avoid archaisms, "one must beware of simply adopting his text also for 2 Samuel." On this view the Chronicler is a reviser rather than a purer witness; Driver (1890) lists Wellhausen among those who take Chronicles' reading as a correction of Samuel.

Notes

  • Direction of the change. Driver (1890), H. P. Smith (1899) and McCarter (1984) hold that Chronicles is a harmonizing correction of Samuel; Driver argues that a scribe with 1 Samuel 17 before him would not have turned a text like Chronicles into one that has Elhanan killing Goliath.
  • The intruded "oregim." Kennicott, Keil & Delitzsch, Driver and Smith likewise take the first "oregim" as an intrusion from the "weaver's beam" at the verse's end. The direction of the remaining differences is set out on the disputed-reading page.
  • Text note. The quotations are Wellhausen's own German; the English is an editorial rendering, not his words.
