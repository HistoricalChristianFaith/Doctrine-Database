Benjamin Kennicott on Who Killed Goliath?

Dates: 1753 (The State of the Printed Hebrew Text… Dissertation the First) · Wikipedia
Summary: The Oxford Hebraist and collator of Hebrew manuscripts uses 2 Samuel 21:19 to argue that the received Hebrew text contains transcribers' errors. The verse, he argues, is a copyist's corruption of the reading preserved in 1 Chronicles 20:5 — "Elhanan slew Lahmi the brother of Goliath" — the two texts differing far less in Hebrew than in English.

Claims & evidence

The contradiction with 1 Samuel 17 as evidence of a transcriber's mistake

"Now every one knows, that Goliath the Gittite was slain by David, and therefore there must have been a Mistake of some Transcriber here; since Goliath could not be slain also by Elhanan the son of Jaare Oregim."

— The State of the Printed Hebrew Text of the Old Testament Considered: Dissertation the First (Oxford, 1753), pp. 78–79, source.

1 Chronicles 20:5 as the original of the Samuel text

"We read then, 1 Chron. 20, 5 — And Elhanan, the son of Jaor, slew Lahmi the Brother of Goliath of Gath &c. Here all is plain and consistent, and these words have evidently been corrupted into the words now found in Samuel."

— Dissertation the First, p. 79, source. Kennicott then reconstructs the mechanism letter by letter: Jair corrupted to Jaare, the object-marker eth confused with beth, a he inserted at the head of "Lahmi" to make the local name "Bethlehemite" (Beth-hallahmi), and "Lahmi" thereby swallowed up — while the first "Oregim" ("weavers") "most probably was inserted from the Line immediately under" the text being copied. On Kennicott's account, the differences that look enormous in English are, in the Hebrew consonants, a short chain of scribal slips.

Reception and debate

  • Direction of the change. Kennicott holds that 1 Chronicles 20:5 preserves the true text and 2 Samuel 21:19 is the damaged copy, as Keil & Delitzsch also hold (c. 1875). Wellhausen, Driver, H. P. Smith and McCarter hold the reverse direction — Samuel's "Elhanan slew Goliath" is primary, and the Chronicler harmonized it — while also taking the Masoretic text of Samuel here to be corrupt. The readings are set out on the disputed-reading page.
  • The wider debate. Kennicott's argument that the printed Hebrew text here contains a scribal error bears on debates over the integrity of the Masoretic Text, traced on the Septuagint-vs.-Masoretic page.
  • Reception. His argument is summarized in McClintock & Strong's Cyclopedia (s.v. "Elhanan"): "the differences between them being much smaller in the original than in English (see Kennicott, Dissertation, page 78)."
