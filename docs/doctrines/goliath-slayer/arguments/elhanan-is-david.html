Is "Elhanan" in 2 Samuel 21:19 another name for David?

Disputed reading · on Who Killed Goliath? (2 Samuel 21:19) · witness: Targum Jonathan

Related readings: How did 2 Samuel 21:19 and 1 Chronicles 20:5 come to differ?

The passage

"And there was again war with the Philistines at Gob, and Elhanan the son of Jaare-oregim, the Bethlehemite, struck down Goliath the Gittite, the shaft of whose spear was like a weaver's beam."

— 2 Samuel 21:19 (English Standard Version), source.1

"And there was again war with the Philistines at Gob. And David the son of Jesse, weaver of the curtains of the sanctuary, killed Goliath the Gittite, the wood of whose spear was like a weaver's heddle rod."

— Targum Jonathan on II Samuel 21:19, trans. E. van Staalduine-Sulman, via Sefaria.2

The question

Is the "Elhanan the son of Jaare-oregim, the Bethlehemite" of 2 Samuel 21:19 a warrior distinct from David, or David himself under another name, with the patronymic read as a set of epithets? The answer decides whether the verse names a second slayer of Goliath or restates the combat of 1 Samuel 17.

Elhanan is a warrior distinct from David

On this reading Elhanan is one of David's men, and "son of Jaare-oregim" (or "son of Jair") is his real patronymic.

Held by: Josephus (Antiquities, c. 93), whose retelling makes him David's kinsman; John Gill (Exposition); the Cambridge Bible for Schools and Colleges; P. Kyle McCarter (Anchor Bible, 1984).3

Josephus, retelling the giant-wars of 2 Samuel 21, writes that "Nephan his kinsman fought in a single combat with the stoutest of all the Philistines, and slew him": the hero is a relative of the king, not the king.4 Gill grounds the distinction in the record of names: "nor had David any such name as Elhanan," and an Elhanan "was one of David's worthies," called "the son of Dodo" in the list of the Thirty. The Cambridge Bible likewise points to "another Elhanan of Beth-lehem" in 2 Samuel 23, and Ellicott judges it "altogether probable" that the two are the same man.5 The setting points the same way. 2 Samuel 21:18–22 places the killing "at Gob," among combats that fell "by the hand of David, and by the hand of his servants" in his later wars, and Gill notes that David's Goliath "was slain not at Gob, but in the valley of Elah"; Halpern reports that David Kimhi observed that the two killings happen in different places.6 The patronymic, on this reading, is a name damaged in copying rather than a set of titles: Wellhausen and Driver take the "oregim" ("weavers") as a word that slipped in from the "weaver's beam" at the end of the verse, and Chronicles gives the father's name as "Jair."7 McCarter accordingly translates "Elhanan, a Jearite from Bethlehem," a named warrior of David's circle.8

Elhanan is David, and the patronymic describes him

On this reading the verse speaks of David under a second name, so that it restates 1 Samuel 17 and names one slayer of one Goliath.

Held by: Targum Jonathan (final redaction c. 400); the Quaestiones Hebraicae in libros Regum et Paralipomenon (pseudo-Jerome, traditionally c. 400), in its note on 2 Samuel 21:19 (its note on Chronicles reads otherwise); Rashi (c. 1090).9

The Targum reads the whole clause as a description of David: "David the son of Jesse, weaver of the curtains of the sanctuary, killed Goliath the Gittite." It changes no consonant of the Hebrew; the obscure patronymic, which contains the word oregim ("weavers"), becomes an epithet for David's service to the sanctuary.2 The Latin Quaestiones argue from the meaning of each name. "Elhanan" is rendered Adeodatus ("God-given"): "Adeodatus is David himself. He is called Adeodatus because he was chosen by God for the kingdom." "Son of the wood" refers to the woodland where he pastured the sheep, and "Bethlehemite" to his town. The work then appeals to the chapter's own summary: "That Adeodatus is David himself the following words declare, where it says: These four were born to the giant in Gath, and fell by the hand of David and of his servants."10 Rashi reasons from the earlier narrative: "We know that it was Dovid who killed Golias in Shmuel I, 17:51, consequently this Elchonon must be Dovid." He glosses the patronymic through the same image: "His family weaved the partition curtain of the Beis Hamikdosh that is [also] called a forest," because the sanctuary "was made with planks of acacia wood."11 On this reading Scripture names one hero, and the verse in 2 Samuel 21 recalls his victory among the other giant combats of his reign.
