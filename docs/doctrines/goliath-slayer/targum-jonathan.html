Targum Jonathan on Who Killed Goliath?

Dates: c. 400 (Targum Jonathan to the Former Prophets; core traditions earlier, final redaction c. 4th–5th c.) · Wikipedia
Summary: The Aramaic rendering of the Prophets identifies Elhanan with David: Elhanan is David. Where the Hebrew of 2 Samuel 21:19 reads "Elhanan son of Jaare-oregim the Bethlehemite," the Targum reads the strange patronymic as a set of titles for David — "David son of Jesse, weaver of the curtains of the sanctuary" — so that the verse states, in as many words, that David killed Goliath.

Claims & evidence

Elhanan the Bethlehemite = "David son of Jesse, weaver of the curtains of the sanctuary"

"And there was again war with the Philistines at Gob. And David the son of Jesse, weaver of the curtains of the sanctuary, killed Goliath the Gittite, the wood of whose spear was like a weaver's heddle rod."

— Targum Jonathan on II Samuel 21:19, trans. E. van Staalduine-Sulman (The Targum of Samuel), via Sefaria. The Targum keeps the Hebrew consonants and reads them as description. The obscure name "Jaare-oregim" — literally containing the word oregim, "weavers" — is read as a description: David, of Bethlehem, who "weaves" the curtains of the Temple. On this reading Elhanan is a byname of David, and the verse no longer competes with 1 Samuel 17; it restates it.

Notes

  • An exegetical reading. Where translations such as the King James and NIV insert "the brother of" into 2 Samuel 21:19, the Targum leaves the consonants and reads the patronymic as an epithet. It is the earliest extant attestation of the identification of Elhanan with David, which Rashi (c. 1090) repeats and Radak (c. 1200) reports.
  • Wording note. The published Sefaria English reads "weaver of the curtains of the sanctuary"; older renderings give "weaver of the veils" and add "who was of Bethlehem," reflecting the Aramaic mi-Bet Lechem. The identification with David is constant across the renderings.
  • Josephus and the Targum. Josephus left Elhanan's giant anonymous and called Elhanan David's kinsman; the Targum makes Elhanan David. Both keep a single Goliath slain by David. McCarter writes that "the contradiction in the tradition was eliminated in the targumic and midrashic literature by identifying David with Elhanan."
  • Disputed reading. The identification the Targum gives is set out, with the reading that Elhanan is a distinct warrior, on the page Is "Elhanan" in 2 Samuel 21:19 another name for David?
