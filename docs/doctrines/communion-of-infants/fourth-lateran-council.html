The Fourth Lateran Council on the age of first communion

Dates: 1215 · Wikipedia
Summary: Lateran IV bound every believer "of either sex" to confess yearly and receive at least at Easter once come to the "age of discretion."

Claims & evidence

Reception is bound to the "age of discretion"

All the faithful of either sex, after they have reached the age of discretion, should faithfully confess all their sins at least once a year to their own priest … and let them reverently receive the sacrament of the eucharist at least at Easter, unless they think, for a good reason and on the advice of their own priest, that they should abstain from receiving it for a time.

— Fourth Lateran Council (1215), canon 21, Omnis utriusque sexus, source. The canon does not mention infants; it makes the annual obligation begin at "the age of discretion." The decree Quam singulari (1910) describes the canon as having "solemnly confirmed" a "new practice, already accepted by certain local councils," under which "children were not permitted to approach the Holy Table until they had come to the use of reason and had some knowledge of this august Sacrament" (source).

Notes

  • An earlier Carolingian text bears on the same question: the Council of Tours (813), whose canon 19 told priests not to distribute the Body "indiscriminately to boys or any bystanders" on worthiness grounds (1 Cor 11), and which the Catholic Encyclopedia (1908) describes as prohibiting young children's communion except in danger of death.
  • Charles Crawford and J. D. C. Fisher connect the end of infant communion in the West over the twelfth and thirteenth centuries with the withdrawal of the chalice from the laity; see John the Deacon and the Roman ordines.
  • Quam singulari (1910) reports local customs that had placed first communion "at ten years or twelve, and in places fourteen years or even more," and set the age of discretion at "about the seventh year"; see Pius X.
