The Council of Tours (813) on giving communion to children

Dates: 813 · Wikipedia
Summary: One of the Carolingian reform councils convened under Charlemagne in 813. The Catholic Encyclopedia (1908) describes it as prohibiting young children's communion except in danger of death; its canon 19 tells priests not to distribute the Eucharist indiscriminately to boys or bystanders who may be in grave sin, on the worthiness ground of 1 Corinthians 11.

Claims & evidence

Canon XIX — priests are not to give the Body "indiscriminately" to boys or bystanders

Presbyteri omnino admonendi sunt, ut, cum sacra missarum sollemnia peregerint atque communicaverint, pueris aut aliis quibuslibet personis adstantibus corpus Domini indiscrete non tribuant. Qui si forte maioribus peccatis fuerint irretiti, magis sibi damnationem quam remedium sumere noscuntur iuxta sententiam apostoli dicentis: Quicumque manducaverit panem vel biberit calicem Domini indigne, reus erit corporis et sanguinis Domini. Probet autem se ipsum homo et sic de pane illo edat et de calice bibat.

— Council of Tours (813), canon 19, source (Latin text after A. Werminghoff, ed., Concilia aevi Karolini I, MGH Concilia 2/1, no. 38). In English: "Priests are earnestly to be admonished that, when they have completed the sacred solemnities of the Mass and have communicated, they should not give the Body of the Lord indiscriminately to boys or to any other persons standing by. For if such should chance to be entangled in graver sins, they are known to take damnation upon themselves rather than a remedy, according to that saying of the Apostle: 'Whoever eats the bread or drinks the cup of the Lord unworthily shall be guilty of the body and blood of the Lord. But let a man examine himself, and so let him eat of that bread and drink of that cup'" (cf. 1 Corinthians 11:27–28).

Notes

  • The Catholic Encyclopedia's description. The council enters most modern discussion through Patrick Morrisroe's article "Communion of Children" in the Catholic Encyclopedia (1908), which states: "In the reign of Charlemagne an edict was published by a Council of Tours (813) prohibiting the reception by young children of Communion unless they were in danger of death, and Odo, Bishop of Paris, renewed this prohibition in 1175." Morrisroe cites Zaccaria, Bibliotheca ritualis II, 161, for his description (source); Werminghoff's edition of canon 19, quoted above, has no danger-of-death clause.
  • Placement on the timeline. The canon is a dated Western text, from the interval between Augustine (c. 412) and the Fourth Lateran Council (1215), on who may be given communion.
  • A separate frequency canon. The same council's canon 50 sets a minimum communion frequency for all laity — "that, if not more often, lay people should communicate at least three times a year, unless perhaps someone is impeded by graver crimes" — a rule about the whole laity, and it too rests on the worthiness proviso (nisi … maioribus … criminibus impediatur).
