Cyprian of Carthage on the communion of infants

Dates: c. 251 (bishop of Carthage c. 249–258) · Wikipedia
Summary: Cyprian's On the Lapsed contains the earliest extant account of an infant receiving the Eucharist: a baby girl takes her turn at the cup, offered by the deacon, "as the rest received it."

Claims & evidence

An infant receives the cup in On the Lapsed 25

When, however, the solemnities were finished, and the deacon began to offer the cup to those present, and when, as the rest received it, its turn approached, the little child, by the instinct of the divine majesty, turned away its face, compressed its mouth with resisting lips, and refused the cup. Still the deacon persisted, and, although against her efforts, forced on her some of the sacrament of the cup. Then there followed a sobbing and vomiting.

— On the Lapsed 25, source (cf. New Advent). The story is told to show the sacrament's power over one defiled by the pagan sacrifices — the infant, taken by her nurse to eat the idol-food during the persecution, cannot retain the Eucharist. In the narrative the deacon offers the cup to those present, and the infant's "turn" comes "as the rest received it"; she receives the consecrated wine, not the bread.
