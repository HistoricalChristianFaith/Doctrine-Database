The Apostolic Constitutions on the communion of infants

Dates: c. 375–380 (Syrian compilation) · Wikipedia
Summary: This late-fourth-century Syrian church order lists "the children" among the ranks brought to communion in order.

Claims & evidence

The children are named among the communicants, in their order

And after that, let the bishop partake, then the presbyters, and deacons, and sub-deacons, and the readers, and the singers, and the ascetics; and then of the women, the deaconesses, and the virgins, and the widows; then the children; and then all the people in order, with reverence and godly fear, without tumult. And let the bishop give the oblation, saying, The body of Christ; and let him that receiveth say, Amen.

— Constitutions of the Holy Apostles VIII.13, source (cf. New Advent). The rite gives the Body from the bishop and the Cup from the deacon; "the children" have their fixed place in the procession of communicants, listed in the same manner as the other ranks.

Notes

  • The rubric places "the children" between the women's ranks and "all the people"; it does not state the age at which children were communicated. Tommy Lee (Reformed), in "The History of Paedocommunion from the Early Church Until 1500" (reformed.org), cites its "then the children" and the deacon's "Let the mothers receive their children" as "primary evidence in favor of the early practice of paedocommunion." On the wider dispute over the practice's origin, see Was the communion of infants a practice received from the apostles, or a custom that arose after them?
  • The same compilation is a witness also on the communion under one kind timeline, where VIII.13 supplies the earliest surviving text naming the deacon as the cup's minister.
