The Apostolic Constitutions on the communion of infants

Dates: c. 375–380 (Syrian compilation) · Wikipedia
Summary: The fullest early order of service lists "the children" among the ranks brought to communion in order — direct rubrical evidence that the fourth-century Eastern church communicated children as part of the assembly.

Claims & evidence

The children are named among the communicants, in their order

And after that, let the bishop partake, then the presbyters, and deacons, and sub-deacons, and the readers, and the singers, and the ascetics; and then of the women, the deaconesses, and the virgins, and the widows; then the children; and then all the people in order, with reverence and godly fear, without tumult. And let the bishop give the oblation, saying, The body of Christ; and let him that receiveth say, Amen.

— Constitutions of the Holy Apostles VIII.13, source (cf. New Advent). The rite gives the Body from the bishop and the Cup from the deacon; "the children" have their fixed place in the procession of communicants, listed as unremarkably as any other rank. This is not an argument for infant communion but a rubric that assumes it — the practice built into the very order of the fourth-century Syrian liturgy.

Notes

  • "The children" here are those old enough to walk in procession; the youngest infants would be communicated in arms, under the wine. The rubric establishes the settled fact that the young were communicated as part of the assembly — the practice the East would retain and the Latin West would let lapse.
  • The same compilation appears on this wiki on the communion under one kind timeline, where VIII.13 supplies the earliest surviving text naming the deacon as the cup's minister.
