Pope Pius X (Quam singulari) on the age of first communion

Dates: 8 August 1910 (decree Quam singulari, under Pius X) · Wikipedia
Summary: Quam singulari set the "age of discretion" for first communion at "about the seventh year," where local customs had placed it at ten, twelve or fourteen, and opened by recalling the ancient communion of nursing infants.

Claims & evidence

The decree's account of the ancient communion of nursing infants

The Catholic Church, bearing this in mind, took care even from the beginning to bring the little ones to Christ through Eucharistic Communion, which was administered even to nursing infants. This, as was prescribed in almost all ancient Ritual books, was done at Baptism until the thirteenth century, and this custom prevailed in some places even later. It is still found in the Greek and Oriental Churches. But to remove the danger that infants might eject the Consecrated Host, the custom obtained from the beginning of administering the Eucharist to them under the species of wine only. … This practice later died out in the Latin Church, and children were not permitted to approach the Holy Table until they had come to the use of reason and had some knowledge of this august Sacrament.

— Quam singulari (1910), source. The decree places the end of baptismal communion in the West in the thirteenth century.

First communion belongs at the age of reason, about the seventh year

The age of discretion, both for Confession and for Holy Communion, is the time when a child begins to reason, that is about the seventh year, more or less. From that time on begins the obligation of fulfilling the precept of both Confession and Communion.

— Quam singulari (1910), decree 1, source. A second decree adds that "a full and perfect knowledge of Christian doctrine is not necessary" for first communion — only that the child "distinguish the Bread of the Eucharist from ordinary, material bread" and approach with such devotion as his age allows. The decree attributes the "extraordinary preparations for First Communion" it condemns to "the errors of the Jansenists," and Pius X wished it to "be read in the vernacular every year at the Easter time."

Notes

  • The Catholic Encyclopedia (J. McNicholas, 1911), citing a letter of Pius X to the Archbishop of Cologne (31 December 1910), describes Quam singulari as "not a decree inaugurating a new discipline, but one restoring the ancient and universal law of the Church, wherever it has not been observed" (source). The decree does not provide for the communion of infants.
  • The decree cites Trent's teaching that "children who have not attained the use of reason are not by any necessity bound to Sacramental Communion of the Eucharist."
