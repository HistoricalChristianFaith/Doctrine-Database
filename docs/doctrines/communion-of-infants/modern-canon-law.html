The modern codes: Latin and Eastern canon law on infant communion

Dates: 1983 (Latin Code of Canon Law, c. 913) · 1990 (Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches, cc. 697, 710) · CIC / CCEO
Summary: the two current codes of the Catholic communion legislate differently on infant communion — the Latin code fixing first communion at the age of reason, the Eastern code providing for the participation of infants in the Eucharist after baptism and chrismation according to the liturgical books of each Church sui iuris.

Claims & evidence

The Latin Church: first communion waits on the age of reason (CIC c. 913)

§1. The administration of the Most Holy Eucharist to children requires that they have sufficient knowledge and careful preparation so that they understand the mystery of Christ according to their capacity and are able to receive the body of Christ with faith and devotion.

§2. The Most Holy Eucharist, however, can be administered to children in danger of death if they can distinguish the body of Christ from ordinary food and receive communion reverently.

— 1983 Code of Canon Law, canon 913, source. Ordinary first communion presupposes "sufficient knowledge and careful preparation"; the exception of §2, for children in danger of death, applies to a child able to "distinguish the body of Christ from ordinary food." Earlier Latin texts on the age of first communion are those of Lateran IV, Trent and Pius X.

The Eastern Catholic Churches: CCEO cc. 697, 710 on infant communion

[c. 697] Sacramental initiation in the mystery of salvation is perfected in the reception of the Divine Eucharist, and thus the Divine Eucharist is administered after baptism and chrismation with holy myron as soon as possible according to the norms of the particular law of the each Church sui iuris.

[c. 710] With respect to the participation of infants in the Divine Eucharist after baptism and chrismation with holy myron, the prescriptions of the liturgical books of each Church sui iuris are to be observed with the suitable due precautions.

— 1990 Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches, canons 697 and 710, 697 / 710. Canon 697 makes the Eucharist the perfecting of initiation, administered after baptism and chrismation "as soon as possible," and canon 710 provides for "the participation of infants in the Divine Eucharist," deferring to the liturgical books of each Church; in the Byzantine rite those books communicate baptized infants by the spoon.

Notes

  • The two codes legislate differently, within one communion, on whether a baptized infant is communicated. The Council of Trent (Session 21, 1562) had defined that little children are under no necessity of communion, adding: "not therefore, however, is antiquity to be condemned, if, in some places, it, at one time, observed that custom."
  • On practice in the Eastern Catholic Churches, Mark M. Morozowich (Catholic, 2008) writes that "the effects of Latinization continue in Eastern Catholic parishes today," that "chief among the varied contemporary difficulties remains the question of whether or not to reintroduce the practice of infant reception of Holy Communion," and that "Eastern and Western Christians shared this tradition for well over a millennium" (Logos 49 (2008): 73).
  • Canon 710 asks that infants be communicated "with the suitable due precautions" (opportunis cautelis adhibitis).
