Does Irenaeus’s advocata (Against Heresies V.19.1) present Mary as Eve’s intercessor?

Disputed reading · on Mary as the New Eve · witness: Irenaeus

Related readings: Does the second-century Eve–Mary parallel assign Mary an active part of her own in the reversal of the fall?

The passage

And if the former did disobey God, yet the latter was persuaded to be obedient to God, in order that the Virgin Mary might become the patroness (advocata) of the virgin Eve. And thus, as the human race fell into bondage to death by means of a virgin, so is it rescued by a virgin; virginal disobedience having been balanced in the opposite scale by virginal obedience.

— Irenaeus, Against Heresies V.19.1, source; Latin uti virginis Evae virgo Maria fieret advocata.1

The question

Irenaeus wrote in Greek, but Book V of Against Heresies survives whole only in an early Latin version, so advocata is the Latin translator’s word for a Greek term no longer extant. The same thought in the Demonstration of the Apostolic Preaching §33, extant in Armenian, is rendered “that a virgin should be a virgin’s intercessor” by J. Armitage Robinson (1920) and “that a virgin, become the advocate of a virgin, should undo and destroy virginal disobedience by virginal obedience” by Joseph P. Smith (1952).2 Michael O’Carroll (Catholic, 1982) reports that “it has been assumed that the Greek word used was Paracletos,” and that “in view of the differences of interpretation proposed by expert patrologists, it is difficult to decide what exactly St. Irenaeus meant by the word.”3 The question is whether the word presents Mary as one who pleads for Eve, and so as an early witness to Marian intercession, or names the part her obedience at the Annunciation plays as Eve’s counterpart and helper. The later Latin title advocata nostra of the Salve Regina is not itself in dispute here.

The word presents Mary as Eve’s advocate and intercessor

On this reading Irenaeus gives Mary the office of an advocate who takes up Eve’s cause, an early form of the belief that Mary intercedes.

Held by: John Henry Newman (Catholic, Letter to Pusey, 1866); Anthony Maas (Catholic, Catholic Encyclopedia, 1912); Leena Mari Peltomaa (“Mary as Intercessor,” 2015); Robert Fastiggi (Catholic, Ecce Mater Tua, 2023).4

Newman takes the lost Greek word to be the Johannine one: “It is supposed by critics, Protestant as well as Catholic, that the Greek word for Advocate in the original was Paraclete,” so that Irenaeus “bestows on her the special Name and Office proper to the Holy Ghost.” Mary is “Eve’s Advocate, or friend in need,” and Newman argues from that role to her present one: “If, as St. Irenæus says, she acted the part of an Advocate, a friend in need, even in her mortal life,” she is to be looked to still “with gratitude and expectation.”5 Peltomaa glosses fieret advocata with paraklētos, following the Sources Chrétiennes edition, and holds that Mary “was considered to be an advocate/intercessor/mediator as long as the idea of the second Eve existed – probably long before Irenaeus.”6 Robinson’s English of the Demonstration reads “intercessor,” with a note to the Latin advocata of Book V.2

Holders of this reading also read the word against its setting. M. Jourjon, as reported by Eamon Carroll, reads the phrase against the martyrs of Lyons and Vienne, who interceded for those who had lapsed: “Irenaeus’ phrase advocata Evae, in the context of letters of intercession by martyrs for apostates at Lyons and Vienne, implies a rudimentary Marian intercession.”7 Maas cites Irenaeus as witness for the Church of Lyons: “he calls Mary our most eminent advocate.” Fastiggi sets the passage at the head of a line of invocation: Irenaeus “referred to Mary as ‘the advocate of the Virgin Eve’… and she has been invoked as Advocate by numerous Church Fathers, saints, and popes,” down to the Salve Regina’s advocata nostra.8 On this reading the title Mary receives in Irenaeus is the title later used in Latin prayer to her, and the second Eve who took up Eve’s cause is the advocate to whom believers turn.

The word names Mary’s obedience as Eve’s counterpart and helper

On this reading advocata describes what Mary’s obedience at the Annunciation did for Eve, as her counterweight in the balance of the fall and its reversal; holders differ over whether the title also bears on Mary’s present intercession.

Held by: Philip Schaff (Protestant, History of the Christian Church III); the editor’s note in the Ante-Nicene Fathers; John Paul II (Catholic, general audience, 1997; on Irenaeus’s sense only); Theo Andreou (on the Eastern Orthodox tradition, 2026; on Irenaeus’s sense only).9

The sentence’s own image is a pair of scales: “virginal disobedience having been balanced in the opposite scale by virginal obedience.” The Ante-Nicene Fathers note at this line reads: “This word patroness is ambiguous. The Latin may stand for Gr. ἀντίληψις, —a person called in to help, or to take hold of the other end of a burden. The argument implies that Mary was thus the counterpart or balance of Eve.”10 Schaff writes that Irenaeus “calls her also the ‘advocate of the virgin Eve,’ which, at a later day, is understood in the sense of intercessor,” and takes advocata to be “probably a translation of συνήγορος or παράκλητος.”11 Smith’s rendering of the Demonstration ties the advocacy to its purpose clause: the virgin becomes the advocate of a virgin to “undo and destroy virginal disobedience by virginal obedience.”2

John Paul II locates Irenaeus’s sense at the Annunciation: “with her ‘yes’ she defended our first mother and freed her from the consequences of her disobedience, becoming the cause of salvation for her and the whole human race.”12 Andreou reads the passage within the recapitulation of Eve, which “centres on the theme of freedom”: “Through their respective uses of freedom, Eve played an active role in the condemnation of the cosmos, and Mary played an active role in its salvation.”13 J. A. Ross MacKenzie, granting that the Greek may have been paraklētos, writes that “Irenaeus may not be saying more than that Mary marks a new phase in history: Mary stands, as it were, in the new creation where Eve stood in the old.”14 On this reading Mary is Eve’s advocate in the act by which she undid Eve’s act: her free “yes” is the weight laid in the opposite scale, and in that obedience she takes up the other end of the burden Eve let fall.
