The Second Vatican Council on Mary as the New Eve

Dates: 1964 (the Dogmatic Constitution on the Church, Lumen Gentium, promulgated 21 November 1964; its eighth chapter gathers the Marian teaching of the council) · Wikipedia
Summary: In the Dogmatic Constitution on the Church of the Second Vatican Council, Lumen Gentium 56 quotes the fathers by name — Irenaeus above all — to say that “the knot of Eve’s disobedience was untied by Mary’s obedience,” and that the fathers “still more often… say: ‘death through Eve, life through Mary.’” The same section describes Mary as “freely cooperating… through faith and obedience.”

Claims & evidence

Lumen Gentium 56 on Eve and Mary

Rightly therefore the holy Fathers see her as used by God not merely in a passive way, but as freely cooperating in the work of human salvation through faith and obedience. For, as St. Irenaeus says, she “being obedient, became the cause of salvation for herself and for the whole human race.” Hence not a few of the early Fathers gladly assert in their preaching, “The knot of Eve’s disobedience was untied by Mary’s obedience; what the virgin Eve bound through her unbelief, the Virgin Mary loosened by her faith.” Comparing Mary with Eve, they call her “the Mother of the living,” and still more often they say: “death through Eve, life through Mary.”

— Lumen Gentium, §56, source. The council’s footnotes cite witnesses also on this timeline: Irenaeus (Against Heresies III.22.4) for both the “cause of salvation” and the “knot” sentences, Epiphanius (Panarion 78.18) and John of Damascus among the “death through Eve, life through Mary” fathers, alongside Jerome, Augustine, Cyril of Jerusalem, and Chrysostom. The section presents the reading as the teaching of the fathers.

Reception and debate

  • The title “Co-redemptrix.” Lumen Gentium frames Mary’s role as free cooperation “through faith and obedience.” It does not use the title “Co-redemptrix.” John Paul II used the title on “at least seven occasions” and stopped using it after 1996, and some later Catholic statements have declined it: Pope Francis said in a homily that she “never presented herself as co-redemptrix” (12 December 2019), and a doctrinal note of the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith, Mater Populi Fidelis (4 November 2025, approved by Pope Leo XIV), ruled that “it is always inappropriate to use the title ‘Co-redemptrix’ to define Mary’s cooperation,” since it “risks obscuring Christ’s unique salvific mediation.”1
  • The earliest witnesses on this timeline are Justin, Irenaeus, and Tertullian; the council cites Irenaeus and later fathers by name.
