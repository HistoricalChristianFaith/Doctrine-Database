Irenaeus on Mary as the New Eve

Dates: c. 180 (Against Heresies was written c. 175–185, in Irenaeus’ years as bishop of Lyons) · Wikipedia
Summary: Irenaeus sets the Eve–Mary antithesis within his doctrine of recapitulation, in which Christ re-heads and re-traces the whole human story to undo it at each point. Mary’s obedience is set against Eve’s disobedience: “the knot of Eve’s disobedience was loosed by the obedience of Mary,” and Mary becomes the advocata (patroness) of Eve.

Claims & evidence

The knot of Eve’s disobedience is loosed by the obedience of Mary

In accordance with this design, Mary the Virgin is found obedient, saying, “Behold the handmaid of the Lord; be it unto me according to thy word.” But Eve was disobedient; for she did not obey when as yet she was a virgin. … so also did Mary, having a man betrothed [to her], and being nevertheless a virgin, by yielding obedience, become the cause of salvation, both to herself and the whole human race. … And thus also it was that the knot of Eve’s disobedience was loosed by the obedience of Mary. For what the virgin Eve had bound fast through unbelief, this did the virgin Mary set free through faith.

— Against Heresies, III.22.4, source. Irenaeus matches the two women term for term: each is a virgin, each betrothed, each acts by belief or unbelief — so that the reversal proceeds “by inversion of the process” that first bound humanity to death.

The Virgin Mary becomes the advocate (advocata) of the virgin Eve

And if the former did disobey God, yet the latter was persuaded to be obedient to God, in order that the Virgin Mary might become the patroness (advocata) of the virgin Eve. And thus, as the human race fell into bondage to death by means of a virgin, so is it rescued by a virgin; virginal disobedience having been balanced in the opposite scale by virginal obedience.

— Against Heresies, V.19.1, source. The image is of a scale: the same means — a virgin’s free response — by which the race fell is the means by which it is rescued. The Greek of Book V does not survive; the book is extant in an early Latin version, so advocata is the Latin translator’s word for a Greek term no longer extant. John Henry Newman (1866) and Leena Mari Peltomaa (2015, following the Sources Chrétiennes edition) take it to render paraklētos (“advocate,” also “comforter”), with their sources on the interpretation page, and the Ante-Nicene Fathers editor’s note conjectures antilēpsis, “a person called in to help, or to take hold of the other end of a burden,” adding: “The argument implies that Mary was thus the counterpart or balance of Eve.” Whether the word attests Mary as intercessor is disputed; see the interpretation page.

Reception and debate

  • Compared with Justin’s single antithesis, Irenaeus’s text adds the recapitulation framework, in which the reversal follows a divine design (“In accordance with this design”), and the image of the “knot” loosed.
  • The parallel stands within Irenaeus’s larger Adam/Christ recapitulation, and it calls Mary “the cause of salvation” and the advocata of Eve. Whether it also assigns Mary an active, personal part of her own in the reversal of the fall is disputed; see the interpretation page.
  • Mary’s perpetual virginity is traced on its own timeline, the perpetual virginity of Mary.
