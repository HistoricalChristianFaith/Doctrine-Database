Oecumenius on the anointing of the sick

Dates: c. 990 · Wikipedia
Summary: The Greek catenist whose Commentary on the Catholic Epistles glosses James 5:14 by the Gospel practice of the apostles, who "anointed the sick with oil and healed them" (Mark 6:13). Theophylact of Ohrid repeats the gloss almost verbatim a century later.

Claims & evidence

The apostles already anointed the sick with oil (Mark 6:13)

Commenting on James 5:14, Oecumenius quotes the verse and glosses the phrase "anointing him with oil" by pointing to the apostles' own practice under Christ:

Is anyone among you sick? Let him call for the elders of the church, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord, and the prayer of faith will save the one who is sick, and the Lord will raise him up; and if he has committed sins, he will be forgiven.
"anointing him with oil." This is what the apostles did while the Lord was still among men, anointing the sick with oil and healing them. (Mark 6:13)

— Oecumenius, Commentary on James, on James 5:14, source.1

Notes

  • Relation to Theophylact. Theophylact's later gloss, "The apostles were already doing this even when the Lord still lived among men: they anointed the sick with oil (Mark 6:13)," is nearly word-for-word the same reading.
  • Reception and debate. How the Greek commentators and the Byzantine rite bear on the Latin reservation of the anointing to the dying is read differently by Protestant, Catholic and Orthodox writers; see How is the medieval reservation of the anointing of James 5:14 to the dying to be read?
  • Oecumenius glosses James 5:16, "confess your trespasses to one another, and pray for one another that you may be healed," on the efficacy of a righteous person's prayer.
