Oecumenius on the anointing of the sick

Dates: c. 990 · Wikipedia
Summary: The Greek catenist whose Commentary on the Catholic Epistles glosses James 5:14 by tying the elders' anointing straight back to the Gospel practice of the apostles, who "anointed the sick with oil and healed them" (Mark 6:13) — the healing-oriented, Gospel-rooted reading that Theophylact of Ohrid repeats almost verbatim a century later, pushing this Eastern reading back into the tenth century.

Claims & evidence

The apostles already anointed the sick with oil (Mark 6:13)

Commenting on James 5:14, Oecumenius quotes the verse and glosses the phrase "anointing him with oil" by pointing to the apostles' own practice under Christ:

Is anyone among you sick? Let him call for the elders of the church, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord, and the prayer of faith will save the one who is sick, and the Lord will raise him up; and if he has committed sins, he will be forgiven.
"anointing him with oil." This is what the apostles did while the Lord was still among men, anointing the sick with oil and healing them. (Mark 6:13)

— Oecumenius, Commentary on James, on James 5:14, source.1

Notes

  • The earlier Greek witness behind Theophylact. Theophylact's later gloss — "The apostles were already doing this even when the Lord still lived among men: they anointed the sick with oil (Mark 6:13)" — is nearly word-for-word the same reading; the Byzantine commentators drew heavily on the earlier catenae, and Oecumenius is the earlier Greek link in that chain. Slotting him a century before Theophylact carries the Eastern healing reading, and its Mark 6:13 anchor, back into the tenth century.
  • The Eastern trajectory. Like Theophylact and, before him, Chrysostom, Oecumenius reads James 5:14 as a healing rite continuous with the apostles' mission, with no hint of a reservation to the dying — the unbroken emphasis of the Greek euchelaion. This bears on the narrowing thesis: the deathbed contraction was a specifically Latin development the East never adopted.
  • On the surrounding verses Oecumenius keeps the same practical, non-narrowing frame — James 5:16's "confess your trespasses to one another, and pray for one another that you may be healed" is glossed on the efficacy of a righteous person's prayer, not on a deathbed rite.
