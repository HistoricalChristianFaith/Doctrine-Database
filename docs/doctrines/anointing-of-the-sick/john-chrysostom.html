John Chrysostom on the anointing of the sick

Dates: c. 390 · Wikipedia
Summary: In his early treatise On the Priesthood, Chrysostom cites James 5:14–15 as proof that priests, not only at baptism but afterward, have authority to forgive sins — the anointing of the sick read as an exercise of the priestly power over sin, and paired with the recovery of the body.

Claims & evidence

The elders' anointing shows priests forgive sins after baptism

Arguing that the priest's dignity exceeds a natural parent's, Chrysostom turns to James: the anointing of the sick by the elders is a standing case of priests forgiving sins beyond the font, and saving "a sick soul, or one which was on the point of perishing":

For not only at the time of regeneration, but afterwards also, they have authority to forgive sins. "Is any sick among you?" it is said, "let him call for the elders of the Church and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord. And the prayer of faith shall save the sick, and the Lord will raise him up: and if he have committed sins they shall be forgiven him."

— John Chrysostom, On the Priesthood, Book III.6, trans. W. R. W. Stephens (Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, 1st ser., vol. 9), source.1 The same passage reads the "raising up" and the "saving of the sick" alongside the forgiveness — body and soul together — and grounds the whole in the priest's power "to deal, not with bodily leprosy, but spiritual uncleanness… actually and absolutely to take it away."

Notes

  • Which power? Chrysostom does not treat the anointing as a rite reserved for the dying; he cites it, with baptism and the general power over sin, as one proof of the priesthood's authority — the same James text that Trent would later make the promulgation of a distinct sacrament. His interest is the forgiveness the elders' prayer carries, held together with the bodily recovery, not a deathbed narrowing. Cf. the effective-absolution strand on the power of the keys.
  • Chrysostom appears on this wiki also on penance and elsewhere for the priestly power to bind and loose.
