John Chrysostom on the anointing of the sick

Dates: c. 390 · Wikipedia
Summary: In his early treatise On the Priesthood, Chrysostom cites James 5:14–15 as evidence that priests, not only at baptism but afterward, have authority to forgive sins, quoting with it the promise of the sick person's recovery.

Claims & evidence

James 5:14–15 and the priests' authority to forgive sins after baptism

Arguing that the priest's dignity exceeds a natural parent's, Chrysostom turns to James 5:14–15 for priests' power over sins committed after baptism, and for their saving "a sick soul, or one which was on the point of perishing":

For not only at the time of regeneration, but afterwards also, they have authority to forgive sins. "Is any sick among you?" it is said, "let him call for the elders of the Church and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord. And the prayer of faith shall save the sick, and the Lord will raise him up: and if he have committed sins they shall be forgiven him."

— John Chrysostom, On the Priesthood, Book III.6, trans. W. R. W. Stephens (Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, 1st ser., vol. 9), source.1 The passage quotes the "raising up" and the "saving of the sick" with the forgiveness, and grounds the priests' authority in a power "to deal, not with bodily leprosy, but spiritual uncleanness… actually and absolutely to take it away."

Notes

  • Reception and debate. Chrysostom cites the passage with baptism as proof of the priesthood's authority over sin; he does not discuss who may receive the anointing. Whether he refers the forgiveness of James 5:15 to the anointing or to penance is read differently by Anglican, Catholic and Orthodox writers; see Do the early witnesses attach the forgiveness of James 5:15 to the anointing or to penance?
  • Chrysostom also appears on the penance timeline and elsewhere for the priestly power to bind and loose.
