Serapion of Thmuis on the anointing of the sick

Dates: c. 350 · Wikipedia
Summary: Serapion's Euchologion preserves the fullest early prayer for the blessing of oil for the sick: the bishop asks God to send "healing power" upon the oil so that those anointed with it or partaking of it may be freed from every sickness and evil spirit, and receive "remission of sins… health and soundness of soul, body, spirit." Body and soul, healing and forgiveness, in one prayer.

Claims & evidence

Healing power sent upon the oil for body and soul

The prayer over the oil (and bread and water) of the sick asks for a sweeping therapeutic and exorcistic effect, crowned by the remission of sins and life itself:

We invoke thee who hast all authority and power, the Saviour of all men… and pray thee to send healing power of the only-begotten from heaven upon this oil, that it may become to those who are being anointed (with it), or are partaking of these thy creatures, for a throwing off of every sickness and every infirmity, for a charm against every demon… for good grace and remission of sins, for a medicine of life and salvation, for health and soundness of soul, body, spirit, for perfect strengthening.

— Serapion of Thmuis, Euchologion, Prayer 17 ("Prayer in regard to oil of the sick"), trans. John Wordsworth, source.1

Notes

  • The Eastern healing rite. Serapion's prayer, like the Apostolic Tradition, blesses oil that may be tasted as well as applied, and aims squarely at "health and soundness of soul, body, spirit." It is fourth-century Egypt's counterpart to what Caesarius would preach in Gaul — and it keeps the anointing a rite of recovery, feeding the Eastern euchelaion tradition that never narrowed to the dying (cf. Theophylact). Bears on the narrowing thesis.
  • The prayer is numbered 17 in Wordsworth's edition and sometimes 29 in other reckonings.
