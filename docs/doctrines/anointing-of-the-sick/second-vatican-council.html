The Second Vatican Council on the anointing of the sick

Dates: 1963 · Wikipedia
Summary: In Sacrosanctum Concilium, the Council reversed the medieval narrowing: "extreme unction" is "more fittingly" called "anointing of the sick," and is "not a sacrament for those only who are at the point of death" — the fitting time being whenever a member of the faithful "begins to be in danger of death from sickness or old age." Paul VI's 1972 revision then gave the rite its new form.

Claims & evidence

Not for the dying only — a rite for the sick again

"Extreme unction," which may also and more fittingly be called "anointing of the sick," is not a sacrament for those only who are at the point of death. Hence, as soon as any one of the faithful begins to be in danger of death from sickness or old age, the fitting time for him to receive this sacrament has certainly already arrived.

— Second Vatican Council, Sacrosanctum Concilium §73 (4 Dec 1963), source.1

The revised rite and form (1972)

Implementing the Council, Paul VI promulgated a revised rite and a new sacramental form, restoring the accent on the Holy Spirit's help and the raising up of the sick:

The sacrament of the anointing of the sick is administered to those who are dangerously ill, by anointing them on the forehead and hands with olive oil, or, if opportune, with another vegetable oil, properly blessed…

— Paul VI, Apostolic Constitution Sacram unctionem infirmorum (30 Nov 1972), source.2

Notes

  • The narrowing reversed. The Council's own language — "more fittingly… anointing of the sick," "not… for those only who are at the point of death" — is a self-conscious return from the deathbed rite of Florence and Trent toward the healing horizon of the earliest witnesses. It is Rome's own concession of the historical arc set out in the narrowing thesis — healing → dying → healing.
  • Continuity claimed. The reform is presented as recovering, not repudiating, the tradition: the sacrament remains the one James named, but its scope is broadened back toward the sick as such. Even the change of name enshrines the point this doctrine's timeline traces.
