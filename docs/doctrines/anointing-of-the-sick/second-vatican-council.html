The Second Vatican Council on the anointing of the sick

Dates: 1963 · Wikipedia
Summary: In Sacrosanctum Concilium, the Council declared that "extreme unction" is "more fittingly" called "anointing of the sick," and is "not a sacrament for those only who are at the point of death" — the fitting time being whenever a member of the faithful "begins to be in danger of death from sickness or old age." Paul VI's 1972 revision then gave the rite its new form.

Claims & evidence

"Not a sacrament for those only who are at the point of death"

"Extreme unction," which may also and more fittingly be called "anointing of the sick," is not a sacrament for those only who are at the point of death. Hence, as soon as any one of the faithful begins to be in danger of death from sickness or old age, the fitting time for him to receive this sacrament has certainly already arrived.

— Second Vatican Council, Sacrosanctum Concilium §73 (4 Dec 1963), source.1

The revised rite and form (1972)

Implementing the Council, Paul VI promulgated a revised rite and a new sacramental form:

The sacrament of the anointing of the sick is administered to those who are dangerously ill, by anointing them on the forehead and hands with olive oil, or, if opportune, with another vegetable oil, properly blessed…

— Paul VI, Apostolic Constitution Sacram unctionem infirmorum (30 Nov 1972), source.2

Notes

  • Reception and debate. Whether the Council's change returned to the rite of the earliest witnesses or adjusted the time of reception within one continuous sacrament is read differently by Protestant, Catholic and Orthodox writers; see How is the medieval reservation of the anointing of James 5:14 to the dying to be read?
  • Paul VI's account. Sacram unctionem infirmorum presents the revision within one tradition: "From ancient times testimonies of the Anointing of the Sick are found in the Church's Tradition, particularly her liturgical Tradition, both in the East and in the West," citing Innocent I's letter, and the teaching of Florence, Trent and the Council.3
