The Council of Florence on extreme unction

Dates: 1439 · Wikipedia
Summary: The Decree for the Armenians defines extreme unction among the seven sacraments: its matter is olive oil blessed by a bishop, its minister is the priest, and it "should be given only to the sick of whose death there is fear."

Claims & evidence

Given only to the sick whose death is feared

Listing extreme unction as the fifth of seven sacraments, the decree fixes its matter, minister, effect, and recipients:

The fifth sacrament is extreme unction, whose matter is the olive oil blessed by the bishop. This sacrament should be given only to the sick of whose death there is fear… The minister of this sacrament is the priest. Now the effect is the healing of the mind and, moreover, in so far as it is expedient, of the body itself also.

— Council of Florence, Decree for the Armenians (Exsultate Deo), 1439 (Denzinger 700), trans. Roy J. Deferrari, The Sources of Catholic Dogma, source.1 The decree also prescribes anointing the several senses "on the eyes because of sight, on the ears because of hearing…" with the form "Through this holy anointing and his most kind mercy may the Lord forgive you whatever [you have sinned]."

Notes

  • Reception and debate. How Florence's "only… the sick of whose death there is fear" is to be read in relation to the earlier practice is disputed; see How is the medieval reservation of the anointing of James 5:14 to the dying to be read? How its "The minister of this sacrament is the priest" relates to the lay use of the oil permitted by Innocent I and Bede is also disputed; see Was the lay anointing permitted by Innocent I (416) the same rite as the presbyters' anointing of James 5:14?
