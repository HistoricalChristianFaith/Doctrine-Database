Bede on the anointing of the sick

Dates: c. 710 · Wikipedia
Summary: In his Commentary on the Catholic Epistles, Bede describes James 5:14–15 as the practice of his day: the sick are anointed with consecrated oil by the presbyters and healed with prayer; and, following Pope Innocent I, he records that ordinary Christians too may anoint with the oil, provided a bishop has blessed it.

Claims & evidence

The church's custom: presbyters anoint the sick with consecrated oil

Bede links the apostles' practice to the custom of the church in his own day and, citing Innocent, records lay use of the oil, which only bishops may consecrate:

And let them pray over him, anointing him, etc. We read that the apostles did this in the Gospel, and now the custom of the Church holds that the sick are anointed with consecrated oil by the presbyters, and are healed with accompanying prayer. Not only the presbyters, but as Pope Innocent writes, also all Christians are permitted to use the same oil to anoint in their own or their relatives' necessity, though this oil may only be consecrated by bishops.

— Bede, Commentary on the Catholic Epistles, on James 5:14, trans. David Hurst (Cistercian Studies 82), source.1

Sickness, sin, and forgiveness after confession

On v. 15 ("if he be in sins, they shall be forgiven him"), Bede links bodily illness to sin — citing Paul on the Corinthians who communicated unworthily — and makes forgiveness turn on confession to the elders and real amendment:

If therefore the sick are in sins, and they have confessed these to the elders of the Church, and have endeavored with a perfect heart to abandon and correct them, they will be forgiven. For sins cannot be forgiven without the confession of amendment.

— Bede, Commentary on the Catholic Epistles, on James 5:15, source.2

Notes

  • Healing and lay use of the oil. Bede describes the sick anointed and "healed with accompanying prayer," and repeats Innocent's permission for lay use of the oil "in their own or their relatives' necessity." How his witness bears on the later reservation of the rite to the dying is disputed; see How is the medieval reservation of the anointing of James 5:14 to the dying to be read? Whether the lay use he reports was the rite of James itself is also disputed; see Was the lay anointing permitted by Innocent I (416) the same rite as the presbyters' anointing of James 5:14?
  • Transmitting Innocent I. Bede's "as Pope Innocent writes" is a direct debt to Innocent I's letter to Decentius (416), which he read as authorizing lay use of the episcopally-blessed oil.
  • Forgiveness and confession. Bede makes the forgiveness of v. 15 turn on confession to the elders and amendment ("sins cannot be forgiven without the confession of amendment"). Whether he ascribes that forgiveness to penance or to the anointing is read differently by Anglican, Catholic and Orthodox writers; see Do the early witnesses attach the forgiveness of James 5:15 to the anointing or to penance?
