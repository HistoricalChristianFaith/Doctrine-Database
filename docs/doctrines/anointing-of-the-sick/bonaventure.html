Bonaventure on the anointing of the sick

Dates: c. 1257 · Wikipedia
Summary: In the Breviloquium, Bonaventure defines the rite as extreme unction, "the Sacrament of those departing from this life," preparing them for "perfect health," which also remits venial sins and may restore bodily health "if it be expedient."

Claims & evidence

The sacrament of those departing this life

Bonaventure orders the ends of the sacrament:

Extreme unction is the Sacrament of those departing from this life, preparing and disposing them for perfect health; it avails also for the removal of venial sins and for the recovery of present health, if it be expedient for the sick person.

— Bonaventure, Breviloquium, Part VI (on the sacraments), source.1

Its effects, and why it can still heal the body

He explains the double working — devotion and the remission of the "dross of sins" — and why, for those whom it is still expedient to live, it "frequently also alleviates from disease," grounding it in James 5:15:

This Sacrament has efficacy both for arousing devotion and for remitting venial sins and for more easily removing the dross of sins.… hence it is that this Sacrament, both by invigorating the soul in good and by unburdening it from evil, frequently also alleviates from disease. And this is what blessed James says, that the prayer of faith shall save the sick person, and if he be in sins, they shall be forgiven him.

— Bonaventure, Breviloquium, Part VI, source.2

Notes

  • Reception and debate. P. Toner (Catholic, 1909) names "the teaching of the Scotist School and of other theologians that, as the principal effect of the sacrament was the final remission of venial sins, it should not be given except to those who could not recover," citing Bonaventure's Breviloquium, among the causes of the twelfth- and thirteenth-century practice of reserving the sacrament to the dying.3 How that change is to be read is disputed; see How is the medieval reservation of the anointing of James 5:14 to the dying to be read?
  • Institution and Mark 6:13. Elsewhere in Part VI Bonaventure grounds the sacrament's dominical origin in the Gospel healing mission: "Christ only initiated and intimated these two Sacraments… Extreme unction he initiated by sending the disciples to heal, who anointed the sick with oil" (Mark 6:13).
  • Peter Lombard (d. 1160) listed extreme unction among the seven sacraments.4
