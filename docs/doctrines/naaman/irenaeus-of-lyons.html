Irenaeus of Lyons on Naaman — the sevenfold Jordan-washing as a figure of baptism

Dates: c. 180 (the Fragments from the Lost Writings, frag. 34; Irenaeus b. c. 130, bishop of Lyons, d. c. 202) · Wikipedia
Summary: Irenaeus is the earliest surviving Christian writer to read Naaman’s cleansing explicitly as baptism — “purified upon his being baptized” — a type of the sinner regenerated as a new-born babe by water and the Spirit.

Claims & evidence

Naaman “purified upon his being baptized” — a figure of the sinner reborn (Fragment 34)

In a surviving fragment of his lost writings, Irenaeus takes the prophet’s command that Naaman dip seven times in the Jordan and reads it as no mere cure for the flesh. The washing was, he says, an “indication to us” — a figure of Christian baptism, in which the sinner is cleansed of old transgressions and born again by water and the Spirit:

“And dipped himself,” says [the Scripture], “seven times in Jordan.” It was not for nothing that Naaman of old, when suffering from leprosy, was purified upon his being baptized, but [it served] as an indication to us. For as we are lepers in sin, we are made clean, by means of the sacred water and the invocation of the Lord, from our old transgressions; being spiritually regenerated as new-born babes, even as the Lord has declared: “Except a man be born again through water and the Spirit, he shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven.”

— Irenaeus, Fragments from the Lost Writings of Irenaeus 34.1 The reading turns on a chain of equivalences: the leprosy Naaman carried in his body stands for the sin every soul carries; the Jordan he was sent to stands for the baptismal font; and the sevenfold dipping ends not merely in clean skin but in a man “spiritually regenerated as new-born babes.” Irenaeus seals the figure with the Lord’s own words on the new birth — “Except a man be born again through water and the Spirit” (John 3:5) — so that the Old Testament cleansing and the Gospel promise of regeneration are made to interpret one another.

Notes

  • The earliest datable statement of the reading. This fragment is the first surviving Christian text to make Naaman’s Jordan-washing a type of baptism outright. It is the seed of the fuller typology that the later tradition draws out at length — the treatments of Ambrose, Caesarius of Arles, and Bede that appear later on this timeline all elaborate what Irenaeus states here in a single stroke.
  • Four elements already fused. In these few lines Irenaeus already holds together the four elements the later tradition keeps: the leper as the sinner (“we are lepers in sin”); the sacred water joined to the invocation of the Lord; regeneration as new birth (“new-born babes”); and the anchoring of the whole in John 3:5. The subsequent witnesses vary the emphasis, but they add no new load-bearing part to the figure Irenaeus sets down here.
