Ephrem the Syrian on Balaam — the star Balaam spoke of, risen at Bethlehem

Dates: c. 373 · Wikipedia
Summary: Ephrem treats Balaam in his hymns rather than in commentary. In the Hymns on the Nativity he sings Balaam’s star oracle (Numbers 24:17) as fulfilled on the day of Christ’s birth, and sets the “stranger” Balaam beside the “strangers” who saw the star; in a hymn on the Faith he takes the speaking ass as a rebuke to the “foolish” seer.

Claims & evidence

“The prophecy that Balaam spake had its interpreting to-day” (Hymns on the Nativity 1)

Ephrem’s first hymn on the Nativity opens with a roll of prophecies said to be fulfilled on the feast — Isaiah, David, Micah — and Balaam’s star oracle stands among them:

This is the day that gladdened them, the Prophets, Kings, and Priests, for in it were their words fulfilled, and thus were the whole of them indeed performed! … The word that Micah once spake, to-day was come indeed to pass! For there came from Ephrata a Shepherd, and His staff swayed over souls. Lo! from Jacob shone the Star, and from Israel rose the Head. The prophecy that Balaam spake had its interpreting to-day!

— Ephrem, Hymns on the Nativity 1.1

“A stranger he who spread abroad concerning it” (Hymns on the Nativity 17.20)

In a later hymn of the same cycle Ephrem returns to the star seen by the nations, and pairs the foreign seer who foretold it with the foreigners who saw it:

The Star that is written in Scripture, the nations beheld from afar,-that the People might be shamed which is near; O People instructed and puffed up! which by the nations hast been in turn instructed, how and where they saw,-that vision whereof Balaam spake; a stranger he who spread abroad concerning it,-strangers they who saw it.

— Ephrem, Hymns on the Nativity 17, stanza 20.2

The speaking ass as rebuke (The Pearl, Hymn IV)

In the seven hymns on the Faith gathered as The Pearl, Ephrem sets the ass of Numbers 22:28 against the seer it carried:

Because Balaam was foolish, a foolish beast in the ass spoke with him, because he despised God Who spoke with him. Thee too let the pearl reprove in the ass’s stead.

— Ephrem, The Pearl: Seven Hymns on the Faith, Hymn IV.2.3

Reception and debate

  • On this timeline. Ephrem’s hymns are the Syriac witness to the Balaam material here; for Greek and Latin readings of the star oracle see Origen and Ambrose, and for the oracle in the Byzantine Nativity services see the Byzantine Nativity services.
