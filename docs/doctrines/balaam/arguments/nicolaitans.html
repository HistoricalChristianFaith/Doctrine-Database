Are the Nicolaitans of Revelation 2:15 the “doctrine of Balaam”?

Disputed reading · on Balaam son of Beor · witness: Revelation 2:14–15

The passage

thou hast there them that hold the doctrine of Balaam, who taught Balac to cast a stumblingblock before the children of Israel, to eat things sacrificed unto idols, and to commit fornication

So hast thou also them that hold the doctrine of the Nicolaitans, which thing I hate

— Revelation 2:14 and 2:15 (KJV), the letter to Pergamum.1

The question

The two rebukes stand in parallel and carry the same charge, and the second opens οῡτως ἔχεις καὶ σύ…ο̇μοιως (“so you also have… likewise”). The question is whether the “doctrine of Balaam” and the “doctrine of the Nicolaitans” name two groups, one movement described twice, or one group under a Hebrew and a Greek name; and whether the Nicolaitans take their name from Nicolaus of Antioch, the deacon of Acts 6:5. The proposed name-play turns on Greek Nikolaos (“victory” + “people”) and Hebrew Bil‘am, which the Talmud derives as belo ‘am and billa‘ ‘am; Greek nikaō means “conquer,” Hebrew bala‘ “swallow, devour, destroy.”2

A sect from Nicolaus of Antioch, compared with Balaam

In this reading the Nicolaitans are followers of Nicolaus the deacon, a present-day party set beside Balaam, the Old Testament type of the same sin.

Held by: Irenaeus (Against Heresies 1.26.3, c. 180); Clement of Alexandria (Stromateis 3.4); Hippolytus (Refutation of All Heresies 7.24); Victorinus of Pettau (Commentary on the Apocalypse).3

Irenaeus reports the Nicolaitans as “the followers of that Nicolas who was one of the seven first ordained to the diaconate by the apostles,” who “lead lives of unrestrained indulgence.” Clement tells the story of Nicolaus’ wife and the maxim that one “must abuse the flesh,” defending Nicolaus as chaste while faulting the followers; Hippolytus has Nicolaus himself depart “from correct doctrine”; Eusebius quotes Clement. These writers keep Balaam and Nicolaus distinct: Balaam is the scriptural type of the sin, as 2:14 makes him, and Nicolaus the founder of the present party. The commentators on 2:15, Victorinus, Oecumenius, Andreas of Caesarea, Bede and Alcuin, read the two “doctrines” as parallel but separate; Oecumenius distinguishes those “who come from Nicolaus,” Bede derives the sect from “Nicholas the deacon,” and Alcuin notes that “the speech is turning from one species to another.” The phrase καὶ σύ, “you also,” adds an item, and Albert Barnes reads the verse as one that “expressly distinguishes” the two. On this reading a named contemporary sect stands where the scriptural precedent stood in verse 14, and the letter compares the one with the other.

“Nicolaitans” as a Greek rendering of “Balaamites”

In this reading John coined “Nicolaitans” as a symbolic Greek equivalent of Balaam’s name, so that the two are one group under two names.

Held by: Johannes Cocceius (17th c.); Campegius Vitringa (Anakrisis Apocalypsios); E. W. Hengstenberg (The Revelation of St. John); W. M. Mackay (Evangelical Quarterly, 1973).4

The two clauses are a matched pair with a verbally identical charge, eating things offered to idols and committing fornication: a single congregation rebuked for a single laxity, described twice. Both names yield “…the people.” Nikolaos means “conqueror of the people” on its face, and the Hebrew play on Bil‘am is anciently attested: the Babylonian Talmud derives the name as “without a people” and “one who wore down the people” (Sanhedrin 105a), the rabbinic name-midrash that Jerome also knew. A reader with both languages could hear “Balaam” and “Nikolaos” saying the same thing about a corrupter of God’s people. Michaelis paired Nikolaos, “conqueror of the people,” with Balaam, “destroyer of the people”; Hengstenberg made the identification widely known; and Mackay pressed it to the conclusion that no literal sect existed, the “swallowers of the people” and the “conquerors of the people” being one symbolic pair. Revelation elsewhere sets a Hebrew and a Greek name side by side, Abaddon and Apollyon (9:11). On this reading the book’s own habit of symbolic naming gives the Pergamum faction its Greek name.5

One libertine movement, likened to Balaam

In this reading the two clauses describe one real party in the Asian churches, whose teaching the letter identifies with the doctrine of Balaam.

Held by: David E. Aune (WBC, 1997); G. K. Beale (NIGTC, 1999); Grant R. Osborne (BECNT, 2002).6

Aune takes the paired adverbs of 2:15 to coordinate it with 2:14 “by way of…explanation,” so that verse 15 restates verse 14 and the teaching of the Nicolaitans is the teaching of Balaam just described. Beale renders the pair “in the same manner…in the same way” and takes the two as one libertine movement; Osborne treats the Nicolaitans as a real proto-Gnostic libertine group whose teaching Revelation deliberately parallels with Balaam’s. Balaam supplies the Old Testament template for the error, as the letter to Thyatira supplies a “Jezebel” (2:20). Charles observes that Irenaeus “adds nothing which might not have been inferred from the Apocalypse,” so that the founder-story may derive from Revelation and the name-echo of Acts 6:5. On this reading the Nicolaitans are a real faction in the churches of Asia, and “the doctrine of Balaam” is the letter’s scriptural name for what they taught.
