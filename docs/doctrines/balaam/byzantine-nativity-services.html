The Byzantine Nativity services on Balaam — the star oracle read and sung at the feast

Dates: c. 743 (the year Cosmas of Maiuma, to whom the first canon of the feast is ascribed in the Menaion, became bishop of Maiuma; the service as a whole is a compilation of hymns by several writers, and the date of the Vespers reading is not fixed here) · Wikipedia
Summary: In the Byzantine-rite services for the Nativity of Christ (25 December), as given in the Menaion, Balaam’s oracle is read at Vespers as one of the Old Testament readings of the feast, and the first canon of Matins addresses Christ as the “Star out of Jacob” who filled with joy “the followers of the words of Balaam.”

Claims & evidence

“A Reading from Numbers” at Vespers of the Nativity

The Menaion for 25 December places a reading from Numbers 24 between the readings from Genesis and Micah:

A Reading from Numbers

The Spirit of God came upon Balaam, and he took up his parable, and said: “Balaam, the son of Beor saith: How goodly are thy habitations, O Jacob, and thy tents, O Israel! … Blessed are they that bless Thee, and accursed are they that curse Thee! A Star shall rise out of Jacob; a Man shall spring forth from Israel, and shall crush the princes of Moab, and shall despoil the sons of Seth. And Edom shall be an inheritance, and Esau, His enemy, shall be an inheritance for Israel, and Israel wrought valiantly.”

— Menaion, 25 December, at Vespers.1

“The followers of the words of Balaam” (the first canon of Matins, Ode IV)

The first canon of Matins, “the composition of Cosmas of Maïuma” in the Menaion, sings the magi as heirs of Balaam’s words:

Shining forth, a Star out of Jacob, O Master, Thou hast filled with joy the wise watchers of the stars, the followers of the words of Balaam, the soothsayer of old; and Thou hast manifestly received the firstfruits of the nations who have entered in to worship Thee. Glory to Thy power, O Lord!

— Menaion, 25 December, Matins, Canon I, Ode IV.2

Reception and debate

  • On this timeline. The magi as heirs of Balaam’s prophecy appear earlier in Origen, Ambrose and Jerome; the star oracle as fulfilled at the Nativity appears in the hymns of Ephrem.
