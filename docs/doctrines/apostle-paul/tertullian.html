Tertullian on Paul

Dates: c. 200 (Tertullian of Carthage, The Prescription Against Heretics) · Prescription Against Heretics · back to the Paul timeline
Summary: Tertullian is among the earliest extant witnesses to the manner of Paul’s death. Pointing readers to the churches the apostles founded, he singles out Rome — “happy” for the blood of Peter and Paul — and describes Paul’s end by a comparison, a death “like John’s,” which, with John identified as the Baptist (⚠ unverified: no named holder located yet), is death by beheading, the citizen’s execution, beside Peter’s cross; in the Scorpiace he writes “that Paul is beheaded.”

Claims & evidence

Rome, “where Paul wins his crown in a death like John’s”

Arguing that the apostolic churches are the touchstone of true doctrine, Tertullian tells his reader to visit them — Corinth, Philippi, Thessalonica, Ephesus — and above all Rome:

Since, moreover, you are close upon Italy, you have Rome, from which there comes even into our own hands the very authority (of apostles themselves). How happy is its church, on which apostles poured forth all their doctrine along with their blood! where Peter endures a passion like his Lord’s! where Paul wins his crown in a death like John’s! where the Apostle John was first plunged, unhurt, into boiling oil, and thence remitted to his island-exile.

— Tertullian, The Prescription Against Heretics 36. The three comparisons: Peter’s “passion like his Lord’s” is crucifixion; the Apostle John’s ordeal is the boiling oil and exile; and Paul’s “death like John’s,” with John identified as John the Baptist, who was beheaded, is execution by the sword (⚠ unverified: no named holder of the identification located yet).

“That Paul is beheaded” — the sword and the citizen

In the Scorpiace Tertullian names the beheading and ties it to Paul’s Roman citizenship. Appealing to the public record of the Caesars against the opponents he is answering, he writes:

…that Paul is beheaded has been written in their own blood… We read the lives of the Caesars: At Rome Nero was the first who stained with blood the rising faith. Then is Peter girt by another, when he is made fast to the cross. Then does Paul obtain a birth suited to Roman citizenship, when in Rome he springs to life again ennobled by martyrdom.

— Tertullian, Scorpiace 15. Here the three points are named together: Paul is beheaded; Peter is fixed to the cross; and Paul’s beheading is “a birth suited to Roman citizenship” — the sword being the citizen’s execution. Tertullian names the emperor elsewhere as well: “Nero was the first who assailed with the imperial sword the Christian sect, making progress then especially at Rome” (Apology 5). Tertullian’s works thus name Peter crucified and Paul beheaded, both at Rome, under Nero.

Reception and debate

  • “A death like John’s.” Tertullian does not name which John. The same sentence has just described the Apostle John surviving boiling oil and going into exile; the identification with John the Baptist (beheaded) is set out above. Clement of Rome had said only “martyrdom under the prefects”; the Martyrdom that closes the Acts of Paul also has Paul beheaded.
  • Beheading and citizenship. Tertullian himself links the sword to Paul’s Roman citizenship (“a birth suited to Roman citizenship”); Acts records Paul as “free born” (Acts 22:28). See Was Paul beheaded at Rome under Nero?
  • Carthage. Tertullian writes from North Africa and refers to Peter’s and Paul’s Roman deaths as known to his readers.
  • The New Prophecy. Tertullian spent his later years in the Montanist “New Prophecy”; he was never tried or condemned by any synod (⚠ unverified), and the later judgements on his standing are set out on the timeline “Was Tertullian a heretic?”
