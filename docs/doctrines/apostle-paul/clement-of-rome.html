Clement of Rome on Paul

Dates: c. 96 (Clement, bishop of Rome, First Epistle to the Corinthians) · 1 Clement · back to the Paul timeline
Summary: The earliest extant witness after the New Testament to Paul’s death: a Roman bishop, writing from the city where it happened, perhaps thirty years after the event, to remind the Corinthians of the martyrs of “our own generation.” His sentences on Paul speak of his endurance, his reach to “the extreme limit of the west,” and his martyrdom.

Claims & evidence

Peter and Paul, the “pillars” of the present generation

Clement is urging the Corinthians away from the jealousy that had split their church, and he reaches for recent examples of what envy costs. He turns to the two apostles the Roman church buried within living memory:

Let us take the noble examples furnished in our own generation. Through envy and jealousy, the greatest and most righteous pillars [of the Church] have been persecuted and put to death. Let us set before our eyes the illustrious apostles. Peter, through unrighteous envy, endured not one or two, but numerous labours; and when he had at length suffered martyrdom, departed to the place of glory due to him.

— Clement of Rome, First Epistle to the Corinthians 5. That Peter and Paul died as martyrs “in our own generation” is stated here by a Roman leader a single generation removed.

Paul’s endurance, the “limit of the west,” and his death

Then the sentence on Paul:

Owing to envy, Paul also obtained the reward of patient endurance, after being seven times thrown into captivity, compelled to flee, and stoned. After preaching both in the east and west, he gained the illustrious reputation due to his faith, having taught righteousness to the whole world, and come to the extreme limit of the west, and suffered martyrdom under the prefects. Thus was he removed from the world, and went into the holy place, having proved himself a striking example of patience.

— First Epistle to the Corinthians 5. The catalogue of sufferings (“seven times… in captivity… stoned”) tracks Paul’s own list in 2 Corinthians 11. Two phrases figure in the later discussion: “the extreme limit of the west” (τερμα της δυσεως), read as Spain by those who take a writer at Rome to set the western edge of the world at the Pillars of Hercules (see Did Paul reach Spain?); and “suffered martyrdom under the prefects,” a death at the hands of the imperial officials.

Reception and debate

  • Date and place. Clement writes c. 96, from Rome, where Paul died, and earlier than the apocryphal Acts. His notice is the earliest extant statement outside the New Testament that Paul was martyred; see Was Paul beheaded at Rome under Nero?
  • “The extreme limit of the west.” This line is the earliest text cited for the Spanish-mission tradition. Whether it refers to Spain or expresses the reach of Paul’s preaching is discussed on Did Paul reach Spain?
  • “Under the prefects.” Clement does not name Nero or specify beheading; he says only that Paul died at the hands of the authorities. The manner (the sword) and the emperor (Nero) appear in the later witnesses — the Acts of Paul, Tertullian, Eusebius, Jerome — not here.
