Origen (in Eusebius) on Andrew

Dates: c. 230 (quoted by Eusebius, c. 325) · Wikipedia
Summary: The earliest extant datable notice of an Andrew mission field. In a passage Eusebius ascribes to Origen's Commentary on Genesis, Andrew is assigned to Scythia — identified by the editor of the English translation as the region north of the Black Sea — as part of the “division of the world” among the apostles. It says nothing of Achaia or of a martyrdom.

Claims & evidence

Andrew's field: Scythia

Such was the condition of the Jews. Meanwhile the holy apostles and disciples of our Saviour were dispersed throughout the world. Parthia, according to tradition, was allotted to Thomas as his field of labor, Scythia to Andrew, and Asia to John, who, after he had lived some time there, died at Ephesus.

— Eusebius, Ecclesiastical History 3.1, a passage he ascribes to Origen: “These facts are related by Origen in the third volume of his Commentary on Genesis.” Joseph MacRory writes that Eusebius here relies “apparently, upon Origen.” The editor of the English translation identifies this “Scythia” as “a European Scythia, lying north of the Black Sea,” and notes that “his traditional activity in Scythia has made him the patron saint of Russia” (notes 3–4). The same passage sends Thomas to Parthia, John to Asia, and Peter to Rome.

Placement on the timeline

  • A single word. The passage Eusebius ascribes to Origen is the earliest extant notice by a named writer to give Andrew a mission field, and it gives one word — Scythia (MacRory, Catholic Encyclopedia, 1907, on the attribution: “apparently, upon Origen”). The notice does not mention Achaia, Patras, or a crucifixion; those are told in the apocryphal Acts of Andrew, and Jerome places Andrew's mission in Achaia.
  • The scheme. The “division of the world” that Eusebius ascribes to Origen distributes the apostles across the map (Thomas–Parthia, Andrew–Scythia, John–Asia, Peter–Rome). On the early Fathers' notices of the apostles, see the apostles hub.
