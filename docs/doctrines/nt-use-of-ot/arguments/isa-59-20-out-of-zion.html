Where does Paul’s “out of Zion” at Romans 11:26 (Isaiah 59:20) come from?

Disputed reading · on Which text did the New Testament quote? · witnesses: Origen · Jerome · Thomas Aquinas

Related readings: Habakkuk 2:4 at Romans 1:17 and Galatians 3:11

The passage

“And so all Israel shall be saved: as it is written, There shall come out of Sion the Deliverer, and shall turn away ungodliness from Jacob: For this is my covenant unto them, when I shall take away their sins.” (Romans 11:26–27)

— Paul, Romans 11:26–27, quoting Isaiah 59:20–21 with a closing clause from Isaiah 27:9; the citation opens ἥξει ἐκ Σιων ὁ ῥυόμενος, “there shall come out of Sion the Deliverer.”1

The question

The Masoretic Hebrew of Isaiah 59:20 reads “and a Redeemer shall come to Zion” (lə-Ṣiyyôn), and continues “unto them that turn from transgression in Jacob.” The critical editions of the Septuagint print ἥξει ἕνεκεν Σιων, “the Deliverer shall come for the sake of Sion,” continuing “and shall turn away ungodliness from Jacob.” Paul has “out of Sion,” and closes with “when I shall take away their sins,” which stands in the Septuagint of Isaiah 27:9. The dispute is whether “out of Sion” is Paul’s own change or the reading of the Greek Isaiah he used. The larger question of who “all Israel” is lies outside this page.1

Paul’s own wording, conformed to the Psalter’s “out of Zion”

On this reading Paul himself changed “for the sake of Sion” to “out of Sion,” in the words of a recurring psalm formula of deliverance.

Held by: Origen (Greek, Commentary on Romans, in Rufinus’s Latin; d. 253); Dietrich-Alex Koch (1986); Christopher D. Stanley (1992); J. Ross Wagner (Heralds of the Good News, 2002).2

Origen, who collated the Greek against the Hebrew, reports the Isaiah before him: “in the prophet Isaiah, from whom Paul takes this testimony, in place of what the Apostle said, ‘he shall come out of Sion,’ it is written, ‘he shall come for the sake of Sion’; and that what he says here, ‘when I shall have taken away their sins,’ is not written there at all, nor indeed among the Hebrews: yet it has been assumed on the Apostle’s authority.” The critical editions of Ziegler and Rahlfs print ἕνεκεν as the Old Greek, and no Hebrew witness of Isaiah supports ἐκ Σιων.3

The modern holders find the source of Paul’s preposition in Scripture itself. Three psalms join deliverance and Zion under exactly his ἐκ Σιων: “Oh that the salvation of Israel were come out of Zion!” (Psalm 14:7 = 53:6, LXX 13:7 / 52:7) and “The Lord shall send the rod of thy strength out of Zion” (Psalm 110:2, LXX 109:2). On this reading Paul conforms the Isaiah oracle to the Bible’s recurring picture of salvation going forth from Zion. The same hand that joins Isaiah 59:20–21a to Isaiah 27:9b shapes the preposition, and the change serves the argument: the Deliverer’s provenance, from Zion, fits Paul’s insistence in Romans 11 that Israel’s standing endures.2

The Septuagint’s reading, “from Zion”

On this reading Paul quotes Isaiah as the Greek Bible gave it, and “from Zion” is the Septuagint’s wording.

Held by: Thomas Aquinas (Catholic, Commentary on Romans, d. 1274).4

Jerome, commenting on Isaiah 59:20, prints his rendering of the Hebrew, “And the Redeemer shall come to Zion,” beside the Septuagint’s, and gives the Greek as reading “from Zion”: “the Seventy translated it: ‘A Deliverer will come from Zion, and will turn away wickedness from Jacob.’” He glosses the Hebrew Goel as the redeemer “who is near to Zion and born of the lineage of Israel, for this is what Goel (i.e., ἀγχιστεύς) means.”4

Aquinas, expounding Romans 11:26, sets Paul beside his own Latin text and names the Apostle’s source: “where our text says: a redeemer will come from Zion and this will be my covenant with them that return to Jacob says the Lord (Isa 59:20). But the Apostle uses the Septuagint.” He then reads the phrase of Christ’s origin: “the coming of a Savior… out of Zion, i.e., from the Jewish people who are signified by Zion.” On this reading Paul’s whole line follows the Greek, which has the Deliverer “turn away ungodliness from Jacob” where the Hebrew speaks of those in Jacob who turn from transgression, and the Greek text Jerome reports already read “from Zion.”5
