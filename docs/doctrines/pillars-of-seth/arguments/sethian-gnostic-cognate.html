How are the Sethian-Gnostic Seth texts related to the pillars legend?

Disputed reading · on The Pillars of Seth · witness: the Sethian texts of the Nag Hammadi codices

Related readings: What is the pillar "in the land of Siriad" that Josephus says still stood in his day? · Is the pillars-of-Seth legend a recasting of an Enoch tradition? · Where does the fire-and-water double destruction in the pillars story come from? · How are the pillars of Seth related to the Watchers' stone-carved astrology in Jubilees 8? · How is the Surid pyramid legend related to the pillars-of-Seth tradition?

The passage

This is the book which the great Seth wrote, and placed in high mountains on which the sun has not risen… in the mountain that is called 'Charaxio'…

— The Holy Book of the Great Invisible Spirit / Coptic Gospel of the Egyptians (NHC III,2 and IV,2, 2nd c.), trans. Alexander Böhlig and Frederik Wisse, earlychristianwritings.com. The book is to come forth "at the end of the times and the eras," and the great Seth "passed through the three parousias… the flood, and the conflagration, and the judgment of the archons and the powers and the authorities, to save her (the race) who went astray."1

The question

A body of 2nd–3rd-century texts makes Seth the heavenly savior and progenitor of an elect "unshakable race" (γενεὰ ἀσάλευτος), and several of them carry inscribed revelation, a hidden book and survival through flood and fire. What is disputed is how these texts relate to the pillars legend Josephus reports: as a transformation of the two pillars of Seth themselves, or as a Gnostic revelation literature drawing on the common Jewish Seth traditions with a different purpose. The answer bears on how widely, and in what communities, the Seth-and-saving-knowledge complex was carried.

The two pillars of Seth transformed into Gnostic steles

On this reading the Three Steles of Seth reworks the two pillars of the Josephan tradition, and the wider Sethian corpus carries the same Seth-and-survival complex.

Held by: John D. Turner (introduction to The Three Steles of Seth, 2007; Sethian Gnosticism and the Platonic Tradition, 2001).2

Turner makes the connection in his introduction to the Three Steles: "the traditional two steles, or tablets of stone and brick, on which Seth was said to have preserved from destruction by flood or fire the vast sum of astrological lore revealed to him (cf. Josephus Jewish Antiquities 1.67.1–71.5) have now become three steles recording doxological hymns." The Three Steles (NHC VII,5) is a set of three hymns to the Father, the Mother Barbelo and the pre-existent One, "written" by Seth, preserved for the elect "living and unshakable generation," and "supposedly discovered by Dositheos." The skeleton is the pillars legend's: Seth inscribes knowledge, it is preserved for a chosen race, it is disclosed later; the number of steles has stepped from two to three and the lore has become hymnody. Pearson sets out the Jewish exaltation of Seth as righteous ancestor of an elect line, which the Gnostics radicalized into Seth the divine progenitor of "another race." The other texts carry the parts of the complex. In the Apocalypse of Adam (NHC V,5) the seed of Seth passes through the Flood — "rain-showers of God the almighty will be poured forth, so that he might destroy all flesh from the earth," Noah being addressed as "Deucalion" — and then through fire, when "fire and sulphur and asphalt will be cast upon those men," and Abrasax, Sablo and Gamaliel "descend and bring those men out of the fire and the wrath." The Gospel of the Egyptians sets Seth's hidden book beside the race's passage through "the flood, and the conflagration." On this reading the Sethian corpus is a branch of the motif alive in Greco-Egyptian Gnosticism within roughly a century of Josephus, and in the Three Steles the pillars image itself is reworked.3

A revelation literature drawing on the common Jewish Seth traditions

On this reading the Sethian texts take up the Jewish lore of Seth and his tablets, shared by many writers, and turn it into a revelation of saving knowledge from above, without dependence on Josephus's form of the legend.

Held by: ⚠ unverified — no named proponent located yet.

The two-tablets-against-fire-and-flood device circulated in Jewish lore apart from Josephus. The Life of Adam and Eve tells it of Seth, who "made the tables" of stone and clay against judgment by water and by fire; the Byzantine Palaea Historica tells it of Enoch, writing "on marble (tablets) and on bricks." On this reading the Three Steles drew on that common stream. The inscribed-stele image appears in the Three Steles alone. The Apocalypse of Adam saves the elect by rescue: the light-bringers "bring those men out of the fire and the wrath, and take them above the aeons"; and the Gospel of the Egyptians keeps the hidden book and the passage through flood and conflagration as distinct motifs. The purpose of the knowledge is also different. Pearson and Turner describe the Gnostic Seth as a revealer of gnosis from above, where Josephus's Sethites discover astronomy by their own virtue, and in these texts celestial lore more often belongs to the archons than to the elect. On this reading the Sethian corpus is a Gnostic reception of the Seth traditions: it keeps the frame of a written revelation carried through the world's destructions to a chosen race, and fills it with a saving knowledge given from heaven.4
