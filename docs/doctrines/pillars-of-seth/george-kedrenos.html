George Kedrenos (Cedrenus) on the Pillars of Seth

Dates: fl. c. 1050 (Byzantine chronicler; the Synopsis Historion reaches to 1057) · Wikipedia
Summary: George Kedrenos (Latin Cedrenus) gives the pillars legend its fullest surviving Greek form — and gives it to Seth himself. In the primeval-history opening of his world-chronicle he credits Seth with naming the seven planets, comprehending the courses of the heavens, and setting up two pillars, one of stone and one of brick, on which he wrote his discoveries. He writes about a century before Peter Comestor's Latin Historia Scholastica (c. 1170) assigns the same pillars to the Cainite musician Jubal.

Claims & evidence

Seth named the planets, comprehended the heavens, and set up the two pillars

Seth gave names to the seven planets, and comprehended the lore of the movements of the heavens. He also prepared two pillars, one of stone and one of brick, and wrote these things upon them… He also devised the Hebrew letters.

— George Kedrenos, Compendium Historiarum (Synopsis Historion) (c. 1050), as translated by M. R. James, The Lost Apocrypha of the Old Testament (1920), "Seth," from Migne Patrologia Graeca 121, col. 8 (wikisource.org; Greek/Latin in Bekker's Bonn edition, archive.org).1 Where Josephus credits "the posterity of Seth" collectively with the astronomy and the two pillars, and where Isidore tells it generically of "some men," Kedrenos names Seth himself as the discoverer, planet-namer, pillar-builder, and inventor of the Hebrew alphabet. James introduces it as "a passage both in Syncellus and Cedrenus which deserves to be quoted," so the earlier George Syncellus carries it too.

The Greek and Latin forms of the legend

Kedrenos's form differs from that of the Latin chronicles. In the mid-eleventh-to-twelfth-century window, the Western "school Bible" of Peter Comestor (c. 1170) gives the pillars to the Cainite musician Jubal, a form Ranulf Higden's Polychronicon (c. 1350) also carries. Kedrenos, writing about a century earlier on the Greek side, keeps the celestial science, the two pillars and the invention of writing with Seth himself, where Josephus credits Seth's posterity.2

Reception and debate

  • Enochic coloring. Kedrenos's Seth also "was caught away by an angel and instructed in what concerned the future transgression of his sons… and concerning the Flood and the coming of the Saviour" — the ascent-and-revelation role that Jewish lore more usually gives to Enoch, the seventh from Adam. M. R. James writes that "Dr. Charles may be right in regarding this statement (about the revelations made to Seth) as an attempt to transfer" to Seth "the wisdom and the position which properly belonged to Enoch." See Is the pillars-of-Seth legend a recasting of an Enoch tradition?

  • Another doctrine, same chronicler. Kedrenos surfaces elsewhere on this wiki for a different late legend — the Byzantine claim that the Simeon of Luke 2 was one of the Septuagint's seventy translators, kept alive three centuries to see the Christ he had doubted (see the Septuagint timeline's George Kedrenos page and its Simeon-the-Just argument).
