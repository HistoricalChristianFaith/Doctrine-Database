Do any authenticated remains attest gigantism at Nephilim scale?

Presupposition: inherited from The Flood and the Nephilim in the Material Record — that Noah’s Flood happened, destroying all humanity outside the ark, and that the Nephilim were real. This page works inside that presupposition: its assessment weighs a model of the event or a trace of it, not whether it happened.

Type: supporting argument (off-timeline) · Assessment: unsupported — as a claim that authenticated remains at Nephilim scale exist; of the resolutions proposed for that absence, the evidence currently favours the one on which the post-Flood giants were tall but within the human range, and few and local. Granting that the Nephilim were real, this page asks the retrospective question: has the ground already yielded their bones? It has not. Every remain ever produced at the scale the tradition describes is a manufactured object, a manufactured photograph, or a manufactured news story, and the lineage runs from a carved block of Iowa gypsum in 1869 to a satire site in 2014 with a confession or a disclaimer at nearly every link. The skeletal record does contain authenticated gigantism, and this page says so at its strongest — a 202 cm Roman from Fidenae, a 215.5 cm individual from Poland, and Robert Wadlow at 2.72 m — but every one is a documented endocrine pathology in a single individual, none reaches the register of the conquest narratives, and there is no case anywhere of extreme stature as a heritable population trait. That absence is a genuine puzzle inside the presupposition, stated plainly here; the resolutions proposed for it are weighed in full on the predictive counterpart and summarised below.

The claim

The claim assessed here is that the physical record already contains what the Nephilim, granted as real, would have left behind: authenticated human skeletal remains of extraordinary size — the "giant skeletons" of the nineteenth-century newspaper record, the mound-builder finds, the photographed excavations, and the specimens said to have been collected and then suppressed. In its strongest form it does not ask for the whole biblical roster to be vindicated at once. It asks for one thing: a single set of bones, held in a collection, measured by a competent osteologist, published, and consistent with the stature the texts describe.

Who advances it

The claim in its modern form is carried by popular giant-literature and by the online circulation of a fixed set of images and stories rather than by any research programme; its nineteenth-century ancestor was carried by the American newspaper press and by the lost-race theory of the Ohio and Mississippi earthworks. The strongest version of it — and the one worth engaging — is not "the newspapers said so" but the suppression form, argued at book length by Jim Vieira and Hugh Newman: that the Smithsonian "identified at least 17 skeletons that stood at over seven feet in their annual reports," and that a cover-up "may have been instigated in the late 1800s."1 That version deserves a straight answer rather than a sneer, because if it were true the negative evidence assembled below would be worthless.

The evidence for

Authenticated gigantism in the archaeological record is real, and the argument is entitled to it. The single most important concession here is one the debunking literature usually skips. In 1991 an unusually long tomb was excavated in the Imperial-age necropolis at Fidenae, seven kilometres north of Rome; the skeleton in it, T.30, was described by Simona Minozzi and colleagues in the Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism in 2012 as a male of sixteen to twenty years with an estimated stature of about 202 cm, with disproportionately long limbs and incomplete epiphyseal union — he was still growing. It is the first clear case of pituitary gigantism diagnosed in a complete ancient skeleton, and against a third-century Roman male mean of roughly 167 cm he was a giant by any ordinary use of the word. Two partial skeletons had already been read as probable cases, one from Poland at 215.5 cm and one from Egypt at 192.4 cm.2 So the record is not empty. People of genuinely extraordinary height lived in antiquity, were buried, and can be recovered and measured.

And the modern ceiling is documented rather than assumed. Robert Wadlow (1918–1940) reached 2.72 m — the tallest human being for whom irrefutable evidence exists, by hypertrophy of the pituitary gland and continuous growth-hormone excess. His height is not a legend or an estimate; it is a measured fact with photographs, medical records, and a manufacturer's shoe last.3 An argument that human beings can reach far outside the ordinary range does not need to be argued for. It is granted.

An ancient report of displayed bones. The claim also has an ancient witness of its own. Josephus, narrating the capture of Hebron, says that "there were till then left the race of giants, who had bodies so large, and countenances so entirely different from other men, that they were surprising to the sight," and adds: "The bones of these men are still shown to this very day."4 That is a first-century report of giant remains on public view in the land the conquest narratives name, and it belongs in the argument's column.

What the record shows

The concession does not reach the claim, and the gap is not a small one. Everything above concerns individuals with a documented endocrine disorder, in the two-metre range, appearing sporadically in populations whose ordinary stature is unremarkable. The tradition describes something else: a population — tribes, named as tribes, before whom observers were "as grasshoppers," with a measured object at nine cubits, roughly four metres. Fidenae's T.30 is 202 cm, and it took a century of European and Mediterranean excavation to produce him. Nothing in the authenticated record is at four metres, and nothing in it is a lineage. Extreme height in the record is what it is in living medicine — a rare and costly disease of one person, affecting roughly three people in a million, with the cardiovascular and skeletal consequences that killed Wadlow at twenty-two of an infection from a leg-brace sore and probably killed T.30 in his teens.

Josephus's bones are not extant, and cannot now be checked. He does not describe them, measure them or say where they were kept, and nothing of them survives. The displays of "giants' bones" that the Graeco-Roman world did record were, as Adrienne Mayor has shown, very often the fossil bones of extinct large mammals, collected, measured and exhibited in temples as the remains of heroes and giants; whether or not that is what was shown in Josephus's day, the report attests that the tradition had an exhibit, not what the exhibit was.4

Every candidate at the required scale is a manufactured object, and most of them are confessed. This is the decisive finding, and it is documented link by link on the hoax-lineage witness. The archetype was built out of this very question: George Hull, having lost an argument with a Methodist revivalist over whether Genesis 6:4 meant literal giants, had a block of Fort Dodge gypsum carved into a ten-foot man, aged it with acid and knitting-needle "pores," buried it behind his cousin's barn at Cardiff, New York in November 1868, and had well-diggers strike it on 16 October 1869. Othniel Marsh of Yale called it "a most decided humbug" — soluble gypsum does not preserve fresh tool marks — and Hull confessed on 10 December 1869. In 1877 he did it again in Colorado with the Solid Muldoon, moulded from mortar and ground bones and fitted with a small tail so it could serve as the missing link as well, on a salted site with a tame newspaper. That is a business model, not an error.5

The nineteenth-century newspaper corpus is a corpus of a different claim. The "giant skeleton unearthed" clippings that still circulate belong to the mound-builder myth — the claim that the Ohio and Mississippi earthworks were raised by a vanished superior race rather than by the ancestors of living Native peoples — and that framework was demolished from the inside by Cyrus Thomas, excavating for the Bureau of Ethnology, in his report of 1894. This matters for the suppression version of the argument: the Smithsonian's actual documented role in the giant story was to dismantle the lost-race scheme the giants were recruited to serve, which is the opposite of the role the claim assigns it.6

The two modern instalments are traceable to their manufacture. The photograph of a half-excavated giant in a trench is a 2002 entry in an image-manipulation contest on the design site Worth1000, made by replacing the bones in a published photograph of a Cornell mastodon dig; it escaped in 2004 with an Arabian-desert caption and was printed as news in Bangladesh and India. And the story that the Smithsonian destroyed thousands of giant skeletons was published on 3 December 2014 by World News Daily Report, a site whose own disclaimer states that its articles are fiction, complete with an invented complainant organisation, an invented lawsuit, and an invented Supreme Court ruling; the photographs travelling with it are recycled — Édouard Beaupré, who had genuine pituitary gigantism and died in 1904, and a 47½-inch "femur" whose museum's curator has acknowledged sculpting it.7

The suppression version is answered on its own terms, and then fails a structural test. Taken straight, it starts from something real that should be conceded: the Bureau's own reports do record a handful of long burials, such as a Kanawha County skeleton "measuring 7 feet 6 inches in length." But those are field lengths of decayed skeletons as they lay, below Wadlow's measured height even at face value — tall individuals, not giants of the conquest narratives — and they appear in the very survey programme that concluded the mounds were Native-built.6 Its best-known modern form makes a checkable claim and the check comes back negative: the named institution, the named case, and the named complainant do not exist. But the deeper problem is what the claim does when that is pointed out. A thesis that converts every absence of evidence into further evidence of the concealment has arranged never to be tested, and a claim that cannot fail cannot be confirmed either. That is a fault in the argument's construction, not a preference of ours for institutions: the same suppression story, told about an artifact that did exist, would have left the ordinary traces — accession numbers, field notes, correspondence, photographs, the memory of the excavators — and none of those is on offer.

The puzzle, and the resolutions proposed

Granting that the Nephilim were real, the absence of any authenticated remain at their scale is a genuine tension, and it is not softened here: there is no specimen. Those who hold the Nephilim to be real have proposed several resolutions, and they are weighed in full, with their sources, on the predictive counterpart to this page. In brief:

  • The stature was real but modest, or "giant" names a warrior category. On the best-attested text Goliath stands about two metres, and the conquest-era vocabulary reads naturally as a category of formidable warrior-peoples (rated sound). Men of that height would leave tall human skeletons, not a separate population of giants, so their absence as giants from the record is what one would expect. This fares best.
  • The antediluvian Nephilim perished in the Flood, and the later clans were few and local — at Hebron, in Bashan, in Moab and Ammon, in three Philistine towns — in regions that are scarcely excavated and preserve bone poorly. This does real work alongside the first resolution, at the cost of exchanging a widespread people for a local one.
  • Preservation, recovery and identification bias explains why the Levantine sample is thin, but does not remove tall bodies specifically; unusually long skeletons are what excavators notice first.
  • The Nephilim's significance is chiefly spiritual — their spirits as the unclean spirits, on the Enochic reading. True to an ancient strand of the tradition, but the same texts give the giants bodies, so it relocates the question's importance rather than explaining the absence.
  • The remains were found and suppressed. Weakest of the five, for the reasons set out above.

The retrospective finding of this page is therefore a narrow one: nothing recovered, measured and published reaches the scale of the conquest narratives. What that absence means for the Nephilim themselves depends on which of these resolutions is right, and on the present evidence it points toward giants who were tall men rather than outsized beings.

A note on method

Nothing here depends on ruling the supernatural out, and the finding is not "giants are impossible." This wiki does not discount a claim merely because it requires a miracle. But a miracle is a claim about what happened, not a claim about what is in a museum drawer, and the question this page asks is the narrow one: has anything been recovered, measured, and published. The honest answer is that the record contains authenticated pathological gigantism at two metres and nothing whatever at the scale the conquest narratives describe, and that the objects offered at that scale are, one after another, confessed fabrications. Possibility is not proof, and that should be said plainly here.

Assessment

Unsupported — as a claim about authenticated material remains at Nephilim scale. There is no specimen. Every object ever produced at the required scale traces to its manufacture, usually with a confession (Hull, 1869) or a published disclaimer (World News Daily Report, 2014) attached; the nineteenth-century newspaper corpus behind the rest belongs to a lost-race theory that the Bureau of Ethnology's own excavations dismantled in 1894; and the one ancient report of displayed bones, Josephus's, describes an exhibit that no longer exists and was never examined. The suppression variant is not merely unevidenced but built so that no evidence could count against it.

What should be carried away in the argument's favour is real and is usually withheld from it. Authenticated gigantism is in the archaeological record — Fidenae's T.30 at about 202 cm, still growing when he died, and a Polish individual at 215.5 cm — and Robert Wadlow at 2.72 m is a measured modern fact. Human stature does reach far outside the ordinary range. What it has never been shown to do is reach four metres, or to run in a family, or to characterise a people. Granting that the Nephilim were real, that is the puzzle, and it is a statement about the state of the ground, offered as such. Of the resolutions proposed, the evidence currently favours the reading on which the post-Flood giants were tall but within the human range and were a few clans in poorly sampled places — a reading on which the empty ground is what one would expect, weighed in full on the predictive counterpart.
