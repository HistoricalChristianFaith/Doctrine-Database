Clement of Alexandria on the death of Moses

Dates: c. 150–215 · Wikipedia
Summary: In his brief comments on the Epistle of Jude, Clement writes of Jude 9 that the notice of Michael disputing over Moses’ body “confirms the assumption of Moses.” Charles prints the remark among the patristic witnesses to the lost Assumption of Moses.

Claims & evidence

Jude 9 “confirms the assumption of Moses”

“When Michael, the archangel, disputing with the devil, debated about the body of Moses.” Here he confirms the assumption of Moses. He is here called Michael, who through an angel near to us debated with the devil.

— Clement of Alexandria, Adumbrationes (Comments on the Epistle of Jude), preserved in the Latin translation of Cassiodorus, source. The remark is on Jude 9.1 R. H. Charles prints it, in its Latin form (“Hic confirmat Assumptionem Moysi”), among the patristic witnesses to the lost Assumption of Moses.2

Reception and debate

  • Clement also comments on Jude’s other apocryphal citation, the Enoch prophecy of Jude 14–15 (“in these words he verifies the prophecy”). Whether Jude’s use of such sources bears on the canon is set out at “What source lies behind Jude 9?”
  • Origen names the book (The Ascension of Moses) and the later commentators retell the dispute in full — gathered at “The Fathers retell the dispute.”
