Clement of Alexandria on the death of Moses

Dates: c. 150–215 · Wikipedia
Summary: In his brief comments on the Epistle of Jude, Clement identifies the source of Jude 9 by name: the notice of Michael disputing over Moses’ body “confirms the assumption of Moses.” He is the earliest Father to tie the verse explicitly to that book.

Claims & evidence

Jude 9 “confirms the assumption of Moses”

“When Michael, the archangel, disputing with the devil, debated about the body of Moses.” Here he confirms the assumption of Moses. He is here called Michael, who through an angel near to us debated with the devil.

— Clement of Alexandria, Adumbrationes (Comments on the Epistle of Jude), preserved in the Latin translation of Cassiodorus, source. Clement reads Jude 9 as evidence for the tradition of a book on Moses’ taking-up — the Assumption of Moses — which he plainly knows and credits.1

Notes

  • Clement also comments on Jude’s other apocryphal citation, the Enoch prophecy of Jude 14–15 (“in these words he verifies the prophecy”) — so he is one of the few Fathers to treat both of Jude’s non-canonical sources; the canon question they raise is weighed at “What did Jude 9 quote?”
  • Where Clement simply credits “the assumption of Moses,” Origen names the book (The Ascension of Moses) and the later commentators retell the dispute in full — gathered at “The Fathers retell the dispute.”
