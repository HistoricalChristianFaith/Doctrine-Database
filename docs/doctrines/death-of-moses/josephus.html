Josephus on the death of Moses

Dates: c. AD 93–94 (the Antiquities) · Wikipedia
Summary: Josephus narrates that a cloud came over Moses and he “disappeared”, and in the same sentence states that Moses wrote that he died, doing so “out of fear, lest they should venture to say that… he went to God.”

Claims & evidence

A cloud comes over Moses and he “disappears” — but he wrote that he died

…and as he was going to embrace Eleazar and Joshua, and was still discoursing with them, a cloud stood over him on the sudden, and he disappeared in a certain valley, although he wrote in the holy books that he died, which was done out of fear, lest they should venture to say that, because of his extraordinary virtue, he went to God.

— Flavius Josephus, Antiquities of the Jews IV.8.48 (4.326), trans. William Whiston, source. The sentence joins the sudden cloud and the vanishing to Moses’ own written record: he “wrote… that he died.”

The motive Josephus gives for the death-notice

Josephus gives a motive for Moses’ record of his own death: “out of fear, lest they should venture to say that, because of his extraordinary virtue, he went to God.” The Loeb edition’s note compares the scene with the Greco-Roman translation and apotheosis accounts of figures who vanished in a cloud (Romulus, Aeneas). On Josephus’ telling, the written record is that Moses died.1

Reception and debate

  • Josephus’ account combines the cloud and disappearance with the death-notice of Deuteronomy 34:5; whether Moses died or was taken up is answered differently by the sources on the timeline.
  • The Babylonian Talmud (Sotah 13b) records, beside the view that Moses died, that “some say: Moses did not actually die,” reading “there” in Deuteronomy 34:5 by “there” in Exodus 34:28 to mean “that he was standing and serving before God.”2 The book linked to Jude 9 bears the title Assumption of Moses.
