The Book of Jubilees: Enoch, the first scribe who testified against the Watchers

Dates: composed in the mid-2nd century BC (c. 160–150 BC), in Hebrew; preserved complete only in Ethiopic (Ge’ez), with Hebrew fragments recovered among the Dead Sea Scrolls at Qumran · Wikipedia
Summary: Jubilees — a retelling of Genesis and early Exodus as revealed to Moses on Sinai, dividing history into “jubilees” of forty-nine years — gives the most orderly early portrait of Enoch after 1 Enoch, and draws on the Enochic booklets. In a single dense paragraph (Jubilees 4:16–25) it sets out several strands: Enoch was the first man on earth to learn writing, knowledge, and wisdom; he wrote down the signs of heaven and the calendar; he spent six jubilees with the angels; he testified against the Watchers who sinned with the daughters of men; and he was taken from among mankind into the Garden of Eden, where he still records the world’s judgment and burns the incense of the sanctuary. This is Enoch the scribe-author, the calendar-revealer, and the man translated to a paradise where he still ministers — a reading of the “God took him” of Genesis 5:24.

The book

Jubilees presents itself as the revelation an angel of the presence dictated to Moses on Mount Sinai, recounting sacred history from creation to Sinai on a rigid 364-day solar calendar and a scheme of forty-nine-year “jubilees.” Written in Hebrew in the mid-2nd century BC, it was prized at Qumran — some fifteen fragmentary copies were found there, more than of most biblical books — and survives entire only in the Ge’ez Bible of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, which counts it canonical.1 Its handling of the Watchers, the Nephilim, and the flood is drawn from and often abbreviates the older Enochic material traced on the Nephilim and astrology timelines. This page keeps to the portrait Jubilees draws of the man Enoch, which it gathers into one continuous notice.

Claims & evidence

“The first among men… who learnt writing and knowledge and wisdom”

And he was the first among men that are born on earth who learnt writing and knowledge and wisdom and who wrote down the signs of heaven according to the order of their months in a book, that men might know the seasons of the years according to the order of their (separate) months.

— Jubilees 4:17, in the R. H. Charles translation (online). Where 1 Enoch makes Enoch the “scribe of righteousness,” Jubilees makes him the first human being ever to learn writing at all — in its account, the man from whom the arts of the pen descend. A comparable claim is attached to Seth or to Enoch in the antediluvian pillars-of-knowledge legend; whether that legend is a displaced Enoch tradition is set out at “Is the pillars-of-Seth legend a displaced Enoch tradition?” on the pillars of Seth timeline.2

“The first to write a testimony”: the calendar-revealer

And he was the first to write a testimony, and he testified to the sons of men among the generations of the earth, and recounted the weeks of the jubilees, and made known to them the days of the years, and set in order the months and recounted the Sabbaths of the years as we made (them) known to him.

— Jubilees 4:18 (Charles), online. In Jubilees, Enoch is the origin of the calendar the book itself keeps: the 364-day solar reckoning, the weeks of years, the jubilee-cycles, and the sabbaths of the years. This matches the Astronomical Book of 1 Enoch (chs. 72–82), where Uriel shows Enoch the courses of the luminaries, and places Enoch on one side of a Second Temple calendar dispute — the solar reckoning shared by Jubilees and the Qumran community against the lunar. His vision reaches to the end: “what was and what will be he saw in a vision of his sleep… until the day of judgment” (4:19).3

Six jubilees with the angels of God

And he was moreover with the angels of God these six jubilees of years, and they showed him everything which is on earth and in the heavens, the rule of the sun, and he wrote down everything.

— Jubilees 4:21 (Charles), online. Jubilees reads Enoch’s “walking with God” three hundred years (Genesis 5:22) as a sojourn with the angels — six jubilees (294 years, rounded from the biblical three hundred) of angelic tutelage in which he is shown “everything which is on earth and in the heavens” and writes it down. Later books on this timeline also set Enoch among the angels: 2 Enoch in a guided ascent through the seven heavens, 3 Enoch by identifying him with the angel Metatron.4

He testified against the Watchers who sinned with the daughters of men

And he testified to the Watchers, who had sinned with the daughters of men; for these had begun to unite themselves, so as to be defiled, with the daughters of men, and Enoch testified against (them) all.

— Jubilees 4:22 (Charles), online. The Watchers’ descent and the birth of the Nephilim are set out on the Nephilim timeline. What belongs to the figure of Enoch is the office Jubilees assigns him within that story: he is the witness for the prosecution — the man who “testified against” the Watchers — the intercessor-and-judge role given him in 1 Enoch 12–16. (Jubilees 4:15 has the Watchers first descend for a good purpose, “to instruct the children of men,” before they fall — a detail not in 1 Enoch — so that in Jubilees the God-taught scribe stands against angels who first came down to teach.)5

Taken into the Garden of Eden, where he still records judgment and burns the incense of the sanctuary

And he was taken from amongst the children of men, and we conducted him into the Garden of Eden in majesty and honour, and behold there he writeth down the condemnation and judgment of the world, and all the wickedness of the children of men.

And he burnt the incense of the sanctuary, (even) sweet spices acceptable before the Lord on the Mount.

— Jubilees 4:23, 25 (Charles), online. Here Jubilees gives its reading of Genesis 5:24. The bare “God took him” becomes a translation “in majesty and honour” into the Garden of Eden — which Jubilees elsewhere calls one of the four holy places on earth and “the holy of holies and the dwelling of the Lord” (4:26) — where Enoch is not merely at rest but still at work: he goes on recording the world’s condemnation and, priest-like, burns the incense of the sanctuary before the Lord. Enoch does not simply escape death; he is removed alive to a paradise-temple where he still ministers.6

Notes

  • Placement on the timeline. Jubilees (mid-2nd c. BC) is later than the oldest booklets of 1 Enoch (3rd c. BC) and depends on them, so it follows 1 Enoch on this timeline and precedes 2 and 3 Enoch and the Fathers. It distils the Enochic material into one orderly biographical notice of the man.
  • The Watchers material. The fall of the angels, the Nephilim, and the forbidden arts are traced on the Nephilim and astrology timelines. This page keeps to the figure of Enoch in Jubilees: first scribe, calendar-revealer, witness against the Watchers, and translated priest of Eden.
  • The Eden-as-sanctuary reading. Jubilees’ picture of Enoch burning incense in Eden treats the garden as a sanctuary — “the holy of holies” (4:26) — so that the translated patriarch is a still-serving priest rather than a retired saint. Later Jewish and Christian writers develop the Eden-as-temple reading of Genesis 2–3 at length (⚠ unverified).
