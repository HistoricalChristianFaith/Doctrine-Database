Justin Martyr on the Trinity

Dates: c. 165 (lifespan c. 100–165) · Wikipedia
Summary: The earliest witness on this timeline. Justin calls the Son “the first-begotten Word of God… even God” and uses the Logos to state how the Son is distinct from the Father: begotten before all creatures, “numerically distinct,” but not by division of the Father’s essence.

Claims & evidence

The Son as the pre-existent Word, and as God

We have been taught that Christ is the first-born of God, and we have declared above that He is the Word of whom every race of men were partakers

— First Apology 46, source. In the same work he writes that Christ, “being the first-begotten Word of God, is even God” (First Apology 63).

Begotten before all creatures — and “numerically distinct” without dividing the Father

God begat before all creatures a Beginning, [who was] a certain rational power [proceeding] from Himself, who is called by the Holy Spirit, now the Glory of the Lord, now the Son, again Wisdom, again an Angel, then God, and then Lord and Logos

— Dialogue with Trypho 61, source. Justin distinguishes the Word from the Father while denying that the Father is divided. The Word is begotten “by an act of will… not by abscission, so as to lessen the word [which remains] in us… and just as we see also happening in the case of a fire, which is not lessened when it has kindled [another]” (Dialogue 61). And in Dialogue 128 the Word is “not numbered [as different] in name only like the light of the sun but is indeed something numerically distinct… begotten from the Father, by His power and will, but not by abscission, as if the essence of the Father were divided.”

Notes

  • Justin’s language of the Son as “another God” (numerically distinct) and as an “Angel” is older than the fourth-century terms homoousios and ousia/hypostasis. Where that language came from is disputed; see from what sources did the apologists draw the Logos as “another God”?
  • Justin reads the Old Testament theophanies as appearances of the Word (the one who appeared to Abraham and Moses is the pre-incarnate Word), and presses this reading against Trypho throughout the Dialogue.
