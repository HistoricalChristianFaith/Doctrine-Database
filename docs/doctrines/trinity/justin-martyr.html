Justin Martyr on the Trinity

Dates: c. 165 (lifespan c. 100–165) · Wikipedia
Summary: The earliest witness on this timeline. Justin worships the Son as divine — “the first-begotten Word of God… even God” — and reaches for the Logos to explain how the Son is distinct from the Father without being a second, separate deity. The divinity is confessed; the technical machinery is not yet built.

Claims & evidence

The Son is the pre-existent Word, and is God

We have been taught that Christ is the first-born of God, and we have declared above that He is the Word of whom every race of men were partakers

— First Apology 46, source. In the same work he states the divinity outright: Christ, “being the first-begotten Word of God, is even God” (First Apology 63).

Begotten before all creatures — and “numerically distinct” without dividing the Father

God begat before all creatures a Beginning, [who was] a certain rational power [proceeding] from Himself, who is called by the Holy Spirit, now the Glory of the Lord, now the Son, again Wisdom, again an Angel, then God, and then Lord and Logos

— Dialogue with Trypho 61, source. Justin guards against both modalism and division. The Word is begotten “by an act of will… not by abscission, so as to lessen the word [which remains] in us… and just as we see also happening in the case of a fire, which is not lessened when it has kindled [another]” (Dialogue 61). And in Dialogue 128 the Word is “not numbered [as different] in name only like the light of the sun but is indeed something numerically distinct… begotten from the Father, by His power and will, but not by abscission, as if the essence of the Father were divided.”

Notes

  • Justin’s language of the Son as “another God” (numerically distinct) and as an “Angel” is pre-Nicene and unrefined: it affirms real divinity and real distinction but has no vocabulary yet for holding the two in the one essence — the work the fourth century would do with homoousios and ousia/hypostasis. Read charitably (as Athanasius and the pro-Nicenes read the early apologists), the fire-from-fire and word-from-mind analogies already refuse the two things Arius would later assert: that the Son had a beginning and that his generation divided or diminished the Father.
  • The same Logos-theology drives Justin’s reading of the Old Testament theophanies (the one who appeared to Abraham and Moses is the pre-incarnate Word), which he presses against Trypho throughout the Dialogue.
