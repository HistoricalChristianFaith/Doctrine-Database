Tertullian on the Trinity

Dates: c. 213 (Against Praxeas; lifespan c. 155–220) · Wikipedia
Summary: Writing in Latin against the modalist Praxeas, Tertullian speaks of the Trinity of one substance and three Persons, distinct but not divided.

Claims & evidence

One substance, three Persons — the “Trinity”

the mystery of the dispensation is still guarded, which distributes the Unity into a Trinity, placing in their order the three Persons-the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost: three, however, not in condition, but in degree; not in substance, but in form; not in power, but in aspect; yet of one substance, and of one condition, and of one power, inasmuch as He is one God, from whom these degrees and forms and aspects are reckoned, under the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost.

— Against Praxeas 2, trans. Peter Holmes, source. The Latin behind “distributes the Unity into a Trinity” is unitatem in trinitatem disponit, and behind “of one substance,” unius autem substantiae (Adversus Praxean 2, Latin text).

Distinct but not divided — the root, the river, the ray

For God sent forth the Word, as the Paraclete also declares, just as the root puts forth the tree, and the fountain the river, and the sun the ray. ... But still the tree is not severed from the root, nor the river from the fountain, nor the ray from the sun; nor, indeed, is the Word separated from God.

— Against Praxeas 8, source. He states the principle in the next chapter: the three are one substance “on the ground of Personality, not of Substance-in the way of distinction, not of division… one only substance in three coherent and inseparable (Persons)” (Against Praxeas 9).

Notes

  • Tertullian is writing against modalism (Praxeas’s claim that Father, Son, and Spirit are one Person in three modes, so that the Father himself suffered — “patripassianism”), and his three-Persons/one-substance formula is set out in answer to it.
  • His “three… in degree… in form… in aspect” language and his order of derivation (root → tree) sit beside his statement that the substance is one and undivided. Whether this ordering of the Son is one of origin or of nature is disputed; see was Tertullian’s subordination of the Son one of order and origin, or of nature?
  • Tertullian’s later association with the Montanists, and how it has been read, is traced on the Was Tertullian a heretic? timeline.
  • In Against Praxeas 26 he also argues from the rite: Christians are immersed “not once only, but three times” at the three names. That argument from worship to doctrine is traced on the lex orandi page.
