Tertullian on the Trinity

Dates: c. 213 (Against Praxeas; lifespan c. 155–220) · Wikipedia
Summary: Writing in Latin against the modalist Praxeas, Tertullian coins the vocabulary the West would keep: the Trinity of one substance and three Persons, distinct but not divided — the terms handed to the councils more than a century before Nicaea.

Claims & evidence

One substance, three Persons — the “Trinity”

the mystery of the dispensation is still guarded, which distributes the Unity into a Trinity, placing in their order the three Persons-the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost: three, however, not in condition, but in degree; not in substance, but in form; not in power, but in aspect; yet of one substance, and of one condition, and of one power, inasmuch as He is one God, from whom these degrees and forms and aspects are reckoned, under the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost.

— Against Praxeas 2, trans. Peter Holmes, source. This is the earliest surviving Christian use of “Trinity” as a technical term for the Godhead (Latin trinitas; the phrase behind the translation is una substantia, tres personae).

Distinct but not divided — the root, the river, the ray

For God sent forth the Word, as the Paraclete also declares, just as the root puts forth the tree, and the fountain the river, and the sun the ray. ... But still the tree is not severed from the root, nor the river from the fountain, nor the ray from the sun; nor, indeed, is the Word separated from God.

— Against Praxeas 8, source. He states the principle exactly in the next chapter: the three are one substance “on the ground of Personality, not of Substance-in the way of distinction, not of division… one only substance in three coherent and inseparable (Persons)” (Against Praxeas 9).

Notes

  • Tertullian is fighting modalism (Praxeas’s claim that Father, Son, and Spirit are one Person in three modes, so that the Father himself suffered — “patripassianism”). His three-Persons/one-substance formula is tailored to that fight; it does not yet address the opposite error — that the distinct Son is a lesser or created being — which Arius would raise a century later, and which Nicaea’s homoousios answers.
  • His “three… in degree… in form… in aspect” language retains a mild economic subordination (an order of derivation, root → tree), read by later theologians as a limitation of his pre-Nicene vocabulary rather than Arian subordination of essence — he is emphatic that the substance is one and undivided.
  • Tertullian later broke with the catholic church for the rigorist Montanists; that arc is traced on the wiki’s Tertullian’s orthodoxy page. It does not touch the standing of his Trinitarian formula, which the church kept.
