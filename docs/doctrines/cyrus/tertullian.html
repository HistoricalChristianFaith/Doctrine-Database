Tertullian on Cyrus — “the Lord to his anointed” read straight to Christ

Dates: c. AD 200 (An Answer to the Jews; Tertullian c. 155–220) · Wikipedia
Summary: The earliest surviving Christian handling of Isaiah 45:1 does not treat it as a word to Cyrus at all: reading the Greek “to my anointed, [the] Lord,” Tertullian applies the whole oracle directly to Christ, whose right hand the Father holds and before whom the gates of the nations are opened — the christological over-reading that Jerome would later correct on philological grounds.

Claims & evidence

Isaiah 45:1 is a prophecy of Christ, not of Cyrus

“Thus says the Lord God to my Christ [the] Lord, whose right hand I have held, that the nations may hear him, ‘the powers of kings will I burst asunder; I will open before him the gates, and the cities shall not be closed to him.’” This very thing we see now fulfilled. For whose right hand does God the Father hold but Christ’s, his Son? All nations have heard of him, that is, people of all nations have believed… He is the one before whom the gates of all cities have been opened and to whom none are closed, before whom iron bars have been crumbled and brazen gates opened.

— Tertullian, An Answer to the Jews 7, historicalchristian.faith. Tertullian is reading the Septuagint’s tōi christōi mou kuriōi — “to my anointed, [the] Lord” — and takes the doubled “Lord” to name the divine Son, so that the opened gates and shattered bars become the spread of the gospel through the nations, unbarring “the hearts of individuals… by the faith of Christ.”1

Notes

  • This is the head of a stream the later tradition would have to discipline. Because the Hebrew names the addressee outright — “to Cyrus” (lə-Kōresh) — Jerome objects that “not only many Latin but also Greek people greatly err, thinking that it is written: Thus says the Lord to my Christ, the Lord,” when the text reads “to Cyrus… the king of the Persians.” The word is genuinely about the pagan liberator; the christological sense the Fathers found in it is a type, not the literal referent — weighed on the Cyrus-as-type argument page.
  • Tertullian’s successors Cyril of Alexandria and Theodoret keep the messianic resonance while conceding the plain sense — Cyrus is called “anointed” because God chose and commissioned him, and in that he “anticipat[es] Christ as king.”
