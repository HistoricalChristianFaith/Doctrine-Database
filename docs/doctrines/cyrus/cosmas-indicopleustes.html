Cosmas Indicopleustes on Cyrus — the king who read his own name and obeyed

Dates: c. AD 550 (the Christian Topography, Book 8; Cosmas fl. mid-6th century) · Wikipedia
Summary: The Alexandrian merchant-turned-monk gives the fullest Christian retelling of the predictive reading: Cyrus opened the book of Isaiah, found the prophecy naming himself written before his birth, and — struck with wonder — released the captives and funded the rebuilding of Jerusalem and its temple.

Claims & evidence

Cyrus finds his own name in Isaiah and obeys

For taking into his hands the book of Isaiah he found as he read the section which contained the prophecy concerning himself… Cyrus, struck with wonder at the foreknowledge and the prediction, and finding his own name expressly announced by the Prophet beforehand, ordered the people to go up to Jerusalem, after having released them from captivity, and given them funds wherewith to build their city and their temple. And not only did he do this, but those also who came after him did the same until the City and the Temple were finished.

— Cosmas Indicopleustes, The Christian Topography, Book 8, historicalchristian.faith. Cosmas quotes Isaiah 45:1–4 and 13 in full and reads it as the very page Cyrus turned to, drawing the same inference from Ezra 1: “See… how he acknowledges that he had read the book of Isaiah, and believed in what was written, and fulfilled what was written in scripture with all diligence and without sparing the expense.”1

Notes

  • Cosmas is the sixth-century heir of Josephus, who first told the story that Cyrus was moved by reading his own name in Isaiah — the ancient form of the predictive-prophecy reading now weighed on the Deutero-Isaiah argument page. Where Jerome stressed the philology (it is Cyrus, not “the Lord”), Cosmas stresses the wonder of the foreknowledge.
