Theodoret of Cyrus on Cyrus — anointed for a required function

Dates: c. AD 435 (the Commentary on Isaiah; Theodoret c. 393–458/466) · Wikipedia
Summary: Theodoret reads the title “anointed” of Isaiah 45:1 as a mark of divine commissioning: Scripture calls “anointed” not only those literally given unction but any whom God singles out for a needed task — and Cyrus is so titled to announce that God chose him to defeat Babylon, end the captivity, and rebuild the temple.

Claims & evidence

“Anointed” = singled out by God for a function

The divine Scripture designates under the name “anointed” not only those who are brought forward for unction but also those whom the God of the universe has singled out with a view toward [fulfilling] a required function. Thus, referring to the patriarchs who lived before the law, he has said, “Touch not my anointed ones.” Here, in any case, he has given to Cyrus the title “anointed,” in order to announce that it is [God] who has chosen him as king in such a way as to defeat the empire of the Babylonians, to make an end to the captivity of the Jews and to [re]construct the temple of God.

— Theodoret of Cyrus, Commentary on Isaiah, on 45:1, shown at historicalchristian.faith, on Isaiah 45:1. Theodoret grounds the title in a canonical usage — the patriarchs called “my anointed ones” (Psalm 105:15) though they were never given oil — so that Cyrus’s anointing names his election to a threefold commission: to break Babylon, free the captives, and raise the house of God.1

Notes

  • Theodoret converges with Cyril of Alexandria: “anointed” marks a God-given office, not personal holiness — the reading that lets the tradition keep the christological resonance without denying that the word is spoken to the Persian king. See the Cyrus-as-type argument.
  • On the proof-text that Cyrus is the conqueror “from the east” and the “ravenous bird” of Isaiah 41 and 46, Theodoret notes only the swiftness of the return — “by the term ‘bird’ he has clearly shown the speed of Israel’s return from exile” (on Isaiah 46:11).
