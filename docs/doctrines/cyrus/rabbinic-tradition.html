Cyrus in Jewish tradition — the upright king who soured

Dates: Babylonian Talmud; traditions from c. AD 200, redacted by c. 500 · Wikipedia
Summary: The rabbis hold Cyrus in a careful ambivalence: he was “a virtuous king” whose very name (Koresh) they read from the word kasher, “upright” — and yet one who later “became corrupt,” so that his regnal years are reckoned like an Israelite king’s only for the stretch before he soured. The title “anointed” they take in a purely functional sense: a commission, not a redeemer.

Claims & evidence

“A virtuous king” — the name read from kasher

All three names are referring to the same person: He is Cyrus; he is Darius; and he is also Artaxerxes. He was called Cyrus [Koresh] because he was a virtuous [kasher] king.

— Babylonian Talmud, Rosh Hashanah 3b (William Davidson translation), Sefaria. The Gemara identifies Cyrus, Darius, and Artaxerxes as one king and derives his name from his uprightness — a warm estimate matching Isaiah’s “my shepherd… his anointed.”1

…but one who “became corrupt”

Here, where his years are counted from Nisan like the kings of Israel, it speaks of him before he became corrupt, whereas there, where his years are counted from Tishrei, it speaks of him after he became corrupt.

— Babylonian Talmud, Rosh Hashanah 3b (Davidson), Sefaria. The very honour of being counted like an Israelite king is limited to his good years; the next folio hunts for the proofs that he soured — among them that he ordered the temple built with “a row of new timber” (Ezra 6:4) so that “if the Jews rebel against me, I will burn their Temple with fire.”2

Notes

  • The Jewish reading of “anointed” (Isaiah 45:1) defuses the shock of a Gentile mashiach by taking the word in its functional sense — Cyrus is set apart to the office of deliverer, not a Davidic or redemptive messiah (Rashi and Ibn Ezra; surveyed by Lisbeth Fried, “Cyrus the Messiah?,” HTR 95 [2002]). This is the same distinction the Fathers reached from the other side — see Cyril, Theodoret, and the Cyrus-as-type argument.
  • The rabbinic identification of Cyrus with Darius (and Artaxerxes) runs parallel to the modern “Darius the Mede = Cyrus” proposal built on Daniel 6:28 — though the rabbis are not consistent across tractates (Esther Rabbah 1:3 instead equates Ahasuerus with Artaxerxes).
