The Council of Chalcedon on the person of Christ

Dates: 451 (the Fourth Ecumenical Council) · Wikipedia
Summary: The Fourth Ecumenical Council in the reckoning of the churches that received it, convened by the emperor Marcian. It received the synodical letters of Cyril and the Tome of Leo, and its Definition confesses one and the same Christ in two natures, without confusion, change, division or separation. The churches now called Oriental Orthodox did not receive it.

Claims & evidence

One and the same Christ, in two natures, unconfused and undivided

This one and the same Jesus Christ, the only-begotten Son [of God] must be confessed to be in two natures, unconfusedly, immutably, indivisibly, inseparably [united], and that without the distinction of natures being taken away by such union, but rather the peculiar property of each nature being preserved and being united in one Person and subsistence, not separated or divided into two persons, but one and the same Son and only-begotten, God the Word, our Lord Jesus Christ.

— The Definition of Chalcedon, trans. NPNF² vol. 14 (The Seven Ecumenical Councils, ed. H. R. Percival), source. The Definition names whom it opposes: “those who would rend the mystery of the dispensation into a Duad of Sons,” those “who imagine a mixture or confusion of the two natures of Christ,” and those “who foolishly talk of two natures of our Lord before the union, conceiving that after the union there was only one.” “One Person and subsistence” renders prosopon and hypostasis. Philip Schaff notes that this part of the Definition runs “almost in the words of Leo’s classical epistle” (History of the Christian Church, vol. III, §141). “In two natures” is the phrase that Severus of Antioch and the churches that followed him rejected.

Born of the Virgin, the Mother of God according to his manhood

…born [into the world] of the Virgin Mary, the Mother of God according to his manhood.

— The Definition of Chalcedon, source. The Council repeated the title Theotokos, confessed at Ephesus (431), with the qualifier “according to his manhood.” Cyril had written that Mary is called mother of God “not as though the nature of the Word or his godhead received the origin of their being from the holy virgin” (Second Letter to Nestorius, quoted on the Cyril page). The title’s own timeline is on the Theotokos page.

Reception and debate

  • Opponents of the Council asked whether a complete human nature is not a second person. The reply that the manhood subsists in the hypostasis of the Word (enhypostasia) is associated with Leontius of Byzantium (c. 540); see the timeline. The Second Council of Constantinople (553), in its fifth anathema, condemned anyone who will not “recognize rather that the Word of God is united with the flesh hypostatically, and that therefore there is but one hypostasis or one only Person, and that the holy Council of Chalcedon has professed in this sense the one Person of our Lord Jesus Christ” (Capitula of the Council, 5, NPNF² vol. 14).
  • The churches that did not receive the Council, the Coptic, Syriac and Armenian among them, today the Oriental Orthodox churches, kept Cyril’s “one incarnate nature of God the Word” as their “traditional Cyrillian terminology” (Second Agreed Statement, 1990, §7). Pius XII’s account of them, for the Catholic Church in 1951, is given with his reading in the disputed reading on Chalcedon and Cyril. In 1990 the Joint Commission of the Eastern and Oriental Orthodox dialogue stated that both families “have always loyally maintained the same authentic Orthodox Christological faith… though they have used Christological terms in different ways,” and recommended that the anathemas between them be lifted (Second Agreed Statement, §§9–10).
  • Whether the Definition departed from Cyril’s Christology is set out as a disputed reading: Did the Council of Chalcedon (451) depart from Cyril of Alexandria’s Christology?
