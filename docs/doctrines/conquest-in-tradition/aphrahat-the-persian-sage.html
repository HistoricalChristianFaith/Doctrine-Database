Aphrahat the Persian Sage on the Conquest of Canaan

Dates: c. 344 (the Demonstrations: nos. 1–10 written in 337, nos. 11–22 in 344, no. 23 in 345; Aphrahat fl. 337–345, in the Persian Empire under Shapur II) · Wikipedia
Summary: Aphrahat, the earliest writer of the Syriac Church whose works survive, wrote his Demonstrations inside the Persian Empire, out of reach of the Greek and Latin debates. Several of them argue with Jews over circumcision, the Sabbath and the food laws, and Joshua is one of his witnesses. The conquest is history to him, and he never treats it as a problem. Joshua divided the Jordan, threw down Jericho's walls and "destroyed thirty-one kings" "by faith." He has his own reading of the hardest phrase in Joshua 5:2, "circumcise the children of Israel a second time." The wilderness generation had already been "circumcised in their heart," so the knives at Gilgal marked them "a second time." Circumcision itself, he argues, was given to separate Israel from "the unclean people" of Canaan, which is why it was not needed in the wilderness. On this reading baptism, "the true Jordan," is the Christian second circumcision. He sets Joshua beside Jesus in long point-by-point lists: Jericho falling on the seventh day, Achan and Judas, the sun stopped at Gibeon and the sun darkened at noon. Two smaller points look outward to the Gentiles. The sun stood still for the Gibeonites' sake, because they "took refuge in the God of Israel," and Joshua "gave the right hand" to a people "from the unclean Peoples."

Claims & evidence

"A second time": the circumcision at Gilgal

6. My beloved, this should be assured to you that circumcision is a sign so that they (Jews) should be distinguished from the unclean people. See how, when He brought them forth from Egypt and they travelled in the wilderness for forty years, they were not circumcised because they were one people and were not mixed with other people. There He did not mark them out because they were pasturing by themselves. The reason for this marking out the seed of Abraham in this: it was not because all people were not his own, that He separated the seed of Abraham for himself as a flock; rather, He abandoned all the nations who were performing evil deeds of paganism because of their deeds. …

But because they were separated from the nations and they were pasturing alone in the wilderness, they were not marked. And when they crossed Jordan, the Lord commanded Joshua the son of Nun, saying to him, "Circumcise again the children of Israel a second time" (Jos. 5:2). And why did he say to Joshua that he should circumcise them a second time, if not because they were (already) circumcised in their heart, as He had said through the prophet, "Circumcise the foreskin of your heart, and no more stiffen your neck?" (Deut. 10:16). Joshua again circumcised them and marked them in their flesh a second time.

7. … The children who were born in the wilderness and believed, even though they were not circumcised, they lived and entered to inherit the land. He circumcised them when they had entered into the land of the Canaanites and it was counted for them two circumcisions (Jos. 5:7-9).

— Demonstration 11 (On Circumcision) 6–7, trans. Kuriakose Valavanolickal.1 Joshua 5 says that the Israelites born in the wilderness had not been circumcised, and that at Gilgal, after the Jordan crossing, God told Joshua to circumcise them "again, a second time." The phrase was a known puzzle, since a man can only be circumcised once. Justin had read the stone knives as a sign of Christ's words, which circumcise the heart. Jerome, fifty years after Aphrahat, would say that the phrase "cannot possibly stand" if taken literally. Aphrahat takes the rite at Gilgal literally and solves the puzzle another way: the first circumcision was of the heart, and the second was in the flesh. The translator glosses the "two circumcisions" of §7 as "their faith and the circumcision proper." The young generation believed and lived, while the circumcised generation that left Egypt died in the wilderness for unbelief. That is the point of the whole Demonstration, which is written against Jews who boast of the rite.

The same passage explains why the knives were needed only at Gilgal. In the wilderness Israel lived apart; in Canaan it lived among the nations, so it had to be marked. This gives Aphrahat's only reason for the separation of Israel, and it is a short one. God "abandoned all the nations who were performing evil deeds of paganism because of their deeds." That is as near as he comes to a judgement on the Canaanites. He does not discuss the ban.

Baptism as "the true Jordan"

Those who are circumcised in their heart and those who will be circumcised a second time in the true Jordan, the baptism for the forgiveness of the sins, will live.

12. Joshua, the son of Nun circumcised the people a second time with a sharp flint when he and his people crossed the Jordan (Jos. 5:2). Jesus, our Saviour circumcised the peoples who believed in him a second time with the circumcision of the heart; they were baptised and circumcised with the sword, "that is his word which is sharper than the two-edged sword" (Heb. 4:12).

Joshua, the son of Nun caused the people to pass to the land of promise; Jesus our Saviour promised the land of Life to all who have passed through the true Jordan, believed and have circumcised the foreskin of his heart.

Joshua, the son of Nun, set up the stones as a witness in Israel (Jos. 4:20). Jesus our Saviour called Simon the true rock and raised him up as a faithful witness among the people (Mt. 16:18).

Joshua, the son of Nun, performed the Passover in the valley of Jericho, in a cursed land, and the people ate from the bread of the earth (Jos. 5:10-11). Jesus our Saviour made the Passover with his disciples in Jerusalem, the city which he cursed, "No stone be left in it upon (another) stone" (Mt. 24:2), and there he gave the mystery in the bread of life.

Joshua, the son of Nun, condemned the greedy Achar who stole and hid (Jos. 7:24-25). Jesus our Saviour condemned the greedy Judas who stole and hid the money from the money-box which he was holding (Jn. 12:6).

Joshua, the son of Nun, destroyed the unclean peoples. Jesus our Saviour overthrew the Satan and his army.

Joshua, the son of Nun, brought the sun to standstill in the sky (Jos. 10:12-13). Jesus our Saviour caused the sun to set in the midday, when they crucified Him (Mt. 27:45).

Joshua, the son of Nun, was the saviour of the People. Jesus was called the Saviour of the Peoples.

— Demonstration 11.11–12, trans. Valavanolickal.2 The Demonstration ends with this comparison. Each line takes an event in Joshua and pairs it with one in the Gospels: the Gilgal circumcision with baptism, the twelve stones with Peter, the Passover at Jericho with the Last Supper, Achan (the Syriac form is "Achar") with Judas. The contrast "the People" and "the Peoples" is Aphrahat's usual way of saying Israel and the Gentiles. The line on the ban is a single sentence: "Joshua, the son of Nun, destroyed the unclean peoples." Its counterpart is Christ overthrowing "the Satan and his army," which is the reading Origen gave the wars, but Aphrahat lays it over the history and does not put it in the history's place. Every line of the list depends on the event in Joshua having happened.

The wars "by faith"

Thus also Joshua the son of Nun divided Jordan by his faith, and the children of Israel crossed over as in the days of Moses. But know, my beloved, that this passage of the Jordan was three times laid open by its being divided. First through Joshua the Son of Nun, and secondly through Elijah, and then through Elisha. … Furthermore when the people crossed over in the days of Joshua the son of Nun (it was there), for thus it is written:— The people passed over, over against Jericho. Also Joshua the son of Nun by faith cast down the walls of Jericho, and they fell without difficulty. Again by faith he destroyed thirty-one kings and made the children of Israel to inherit the land. Furthermore by his faith he spread out his hands towards heaven and stayed the sun in Gibeon and the moon in the valley of Ajalon. And they were stayed and stood still from their courses.

— Demonstration 1 (Of Faith) 16, trans. John Gwynn (NPNF² 13).3 The first Demonstration is a roll-call of the righteous who conquered "by faith," on the model of Hebrews 11. It treats Joshua's campaign as a list of events in Israel's history. Aphrahat even notes the geography: the Jordan was divided three times, by Joshua, Elijah and Elisha, all at the same crossing "over against Jericho." The destruction of the thirty-one kings is listed among the deeds of faith, next to the fall of Jericho and the stopping of the sun. Aphrahat gives no sign that he finds anything here that needs defending.

Joshua and Jesus: the second list

Also Joshua the son of Nun was persecuted as Jesus our Redeemer was persecuted. Joshua the son of Nun was persecuted by the unclean nations; and Jesus our Redeemer was persecuted by the foolish people. Joshua the son of Nun took away the inheritance from his persecutors and gave it to his people; and Jesus our Redeemer took away the inheritance from His persecutors and gave it to strange nations. Joshua the son of Nun caused the sun to stand still in the heavens, and took vengeance on the nations his persecutors; and Jesus our Redeemer caused the sun to set in the midst of the day, that the persecuting people which crucified Him might be ashamed. Joshua the son of Nun divided the inheritance unto his people; and Jesus our Redeemer has promised to give to the nations the land of life. Joshua the son of Nun caused Rahab the harlot to live; and Jesus our Redeemer gathered together and gave life to the Church, though polluted by the harlotry (of idolatry). Joshua the son of Nun on the seventh day overthrew and cast down the walls of Jericho; and Jesus our Redeemer, on His seventh day, on the Sabbath of the rest of God, this world shall be dissolved and fall. Joshua the son of Nun stoned Achor, because he stole of the accursed thing; and Jesus our Redeemer separated Judas from the disciples, His friends, because he stole of the money of the poor. Joshua the son of Nun, when he was dying, laid down a testimony among his people; and Jesus our Redeemer, when He was taken up, laid down a testimony among His apostles.

— Demonstration 21 (Of Persecution) 11, trans. Gwynn.4 Demonstration 21 was written in 344, in the first years of Shapur II's persecution of the Persian Christians. It runs through the righteous of the Old Testament as types of the persecuted Christ, and Joshua's place in the series is telling: here the Canaanites are Joshua's "persecutors." Aphrahat turns the conquest into a story of a persecuted man who "took vengeance on the nations his persecutors" and took "the inheritance from his persecutors." He does not say what the Canaanites had done to Joshua. The frame is that of the whole Demonstration, which is meant to encourage a Church under persecution. The same chapter lists Joshua among "the persecuted" who "made war" (21.22). The Jericho line pairs the seventh day of the siege with the world's last Sabbath.

The Sabbath at Jericho, the sun for Gibeon, and the Gibeonites

For Joshua, the son of Nun did not give them rest on the Sabbath, when he made the war with Jericho (Jos. 6:15), as the glorious Apostle has said, "If Joshua, the son of Nun, had given them rest, He (God) would not have again spoken about the day of Sabbath. Therefore the Sabbath of God still remains" (Heb. 4:8-9).

— Demonstration 13 (On the Sabbath) 12.

Sun, moon and hail stones were obedient to the command of Joshua, son of Nun, when he had said, "Sun, stand still over Gibeon: and moon in the valley of Ajalon" (Jos. 10:12). And why did the sun stand still over Gibeon, if not because the Gibeonites took refuge in the God of Israel and the Amorite kings went to war against them till Joshua destroyed them, when the sun stood still and the moon remained (where it was).

— Demonstration 14 (On Exhortation) 36.

Joshua gave the right hand (to) the Gibeonites who are from the unclean Peoples; they entered and sought shelter in the inheritance of the Lord. They were gatherers of wood and drawers of water for the congregation and for the altar of the Lord (Jos. 9:26-27). When Saul sought to kill them, the heaven was closed without rain until the sons of Saul were killed (2 Sam. 21:1-9).

— Demonstration 16 (On the Peoples which have taken the Place of the People) 6, trans. Valavanolickal.5 These three passages use the conquest in Aphrahat's arguments with Jews. The march round Jericho on the seventh day shows that the Sabbath was not always kept, and Hebrews 4:8 shows that Joshua's "rest" was not the final one. The other two look toward the Gentiles. In Demonstration 14 Aphrahat asks why the sun stood still "over Gibeon" in particular. His answer is that God was defending the Gibeonites, who "took refuge in the God of Israel." In Demonstration 16, a treatise on the Gentiles who have taken Israel's place, the Gibeonites are an example from Israel's own history: "from the unclean Peoples," they were received into "the inheritance of the Lord," and God punished Saul's house for attacking them. So the Canaanites whom Joshua spared become a sign of the Gentile Church, much as Rahab does.

Notes

  • A Syriac voice outside the Roman Empire. Aphrahat wrote in Syriac in the Persian Empire. He knew none of the Greek allegorists by name, and his exegesis owes most to his debates with Jews. His use of Joshua is typological throughout, but every type rests on an event: Gilgal, Jericho, Achan, Gibeon, the sun. The nearest Western parallel is the "Joshua and Jesus" typology of Justin and Tertullian, which also grew out of argument with Jews.
  • The second circumcision. Aphrahat reads Joshua 5:2 literally, with the "first" circumcision taken as one of the heart. Justin read the stone knives as Christ's words, and Jerome said the literal reading "cannot possibly stand." Compare Justin Martyr and Jerome.
  • The moral question. Aphrahat never raises the problem of the ban. His one remark on the nations' guilt is that God "abandoned all the nations who were performing evil deeds of paganism because of their deeds" (Dem. 11.6). In Demonstration 21 the Canaanites appear as Joshua's "persecutors." The patristic answers are weighed at Do the patristic defenses answer the moral problem of the ḥerem?
  • Translations. Demonstrations 1 and 21 are quoted in John Gwynn's translation (NPNF² 13), which covers only eight of the twenty-three. Demonstrations 11, 13, 14 and 16 are quoted from Kuriakose Valavanolickal's complete translation from the Syriac (2005). Words in parentheses are the translator's.
  • Aphrahat appears on this wiki also on the intermediate state and Old Testament canon timelines. Rahab, whom he pairs with the Church, has a timeline of her own.
