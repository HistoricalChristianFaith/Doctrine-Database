Justin Martyr on the Conquest of Canaan

Dates: c. 155–160 (the Dialogue with Trypho; Justin b. c. 100, martyred c. 165) · Wikipedia
Summary: Justin is the first Christian writer to make Joshua himself a figure of Christ, and he does it at length. His whole case rests on a fact of the record: Moses renamed Hoshea son of Nun "Jesus" (in Greek, Iēsous), and it was this Jesus, not Moses, who led the people into the land and divided it by lot. Justin argues with a Jew, Trypho, from texts both men accept, so the events have to be real for the argument to work. He speaks of the sun standing still "for thirty-six hours" as something Trypho's fathers "witnessed." He counts the second circumcision with stone knives, the Captain of the Lord's host before Jericho, and Rahab's scarlet thread among the deeds Joshua did. He says Israel "invaded the territory of the sons of Canaan by the will of God." Each figure is drawn from a deed, and Justin marks where the type falls short: Joshua gave "a temporary inheritance," and the memorial of Amalek "remained after the son of Nave." Marcion was teaching in Rome in Justin's lifetime, but Justin never raises the moral problem of the ban.

Claims & evidence

The name changed to "Jesus," and the man who led the people into the land

What I mean is this. Jesus (Joshua), as I have now frequently remarked, who was called Oshea, when he was sent to spy out the land of Canaan, was named by Moses Jesus (Joshua). Why he did this you neither ask, nor are at a loss about it, nor make strict inquiries. Therefore Christ has escaped your notice; and though you read, you understand not; and even now, though you hear that Jesus is our Christ, you consider not that the name was bestowed on Him not purposelessly nor by chance. … But since not only was his name altered, but he was also appointed successor to Moses, being the only one of his contemporaries who came out from Egypt, he led the surviving people into the Holy Land; and as he, not Moses, led the people into the Holy Land, and as he distributed it by lot to those who entered along with him, so also Jesus the Christ will turn again the dispersion of the people, and will distribute the good land to each one, though not in the same manner. For the former gave them a temporary inheritance, seeing he was neither Christ who is God, nor the Son of God; but the latter, after the holy resurrection, shall give us the eternal possession.

— Dialogue with Trypho 113, in the chapter headed "Joshua Was a Figure of Christ." The renaming of Numbers 13:16 is the hinge of the whole reading. Justin asks why the rabbis debate the letter added to Abraham's name and never ask why Hoshea became Jesus. The comparison has two sides. Joshua really led the people in and really divided the land, and Christ will do the same "though not in the same manner." The first gift was temporary because the giver was only a man. The second is eternal. In chapter 75 he reads the angel of Exodus 23:20–21, of whom God says "My name is in Him," the same way: "He who led your fathers into the land is called by this name Jesus, and first called Auses (Oshea)." Trypho grants the force of the point in chapter 89: "the name of Jesus, by which the the son of Nave (Nun) was called, has inclined me very strongly to adopt this view." Both sides argue over details of the record. In chapter 115 Justin wonders aloud why Trypho let him say, unchallenged, that Joshua was "the only one of contemporaries who came out of Egypt" to enter the land, a statement open to challenge since Numbers 14:30 lets Caleb in too. The details matter because both men take the book as history.1

"You witnessed the sun stand still": the miracles as part of Israel's history

Yet after this you made a calf, and were very zealous in committing fornication with the daughters of strangers, and in serving idols. And again, when the land was given up to you with so great a display of power, that you witnessed the sun stand still in the heavens by the order of that man whose name was Jesus (Joshua), and not go down for thirty-six hours, as well as all the other miracles which were wrought for you as time served.

— Dialogue with Trypho 132. The sentence ends a long list that begins in chapter 131: the parting of the sea, the pillar of light, the manna, the sweetened water of Marah, the quails, the water from the rock, the shoes and garments that did not wear out. Justin uses the list to convict Israel of ingratitude, and the charge only stands if the gifts were real. The land was "given up to you with so great a display of power," and the sun stood still at "the order of that man whose name was Jesus." The figure of thirty-six hours goes beyond Joshua 10:13, which says "about a whole day." The same figure is one of three estimates (24, 36, and 48 hours) recorded in the Babylonian Talmud. Chapter 113 says the same thing more briefly: "The former, after he had been named Jesus (Joshua), and after he had received strength from. His Spirit, caused the sun to stand still." The miracle is credited to the man as the bearer of the name.2

The second circumcision "with knives of stone"

The former is said to have circumcised the people a second time with knives of stone (which was a sign of this circumcision with which Jesus Christ Himself has circumcised us from the idols made of stone and of other materials), and to have collected together those who were circumcised from the uncircumcision, i.e., from the error of the world, in every place by the knives of stone, to wit, the words of our Lord Jesus. For I have shown that Christ was proclaimed by the prophets in parables a Stone and a Rock.

— Dialogue with Trypho 113, on Joshua 5:2–3. Justin states the event first ("is said to have circumcised the people a second time") and then reads the detail of the stone knives as "a sign" of the circumcision of the heart, done by the words of Christ, who is the Stone. In chapter 114 he adds: "Blessed therefore are we who have been circumcised the second time with knives of stone." Chapter 24 had already said that "Jesus Christ circumcises all who will … with knives of stone." The figure is built on a rite the Israelites performed at Gilgal before Jericho. Justin claims that it pointed past itself. He does not claim that it never happened.3

The Captain of the Lord's host before Jericho is the Son

Listen, therefore, to the following from the book of Joshua, that what I say may become manifest to you; it is this: `And it came to pass, when Joshua was near Jericho, he lifted up his eyes, and sees a man standing over against him. And Joshua approached to Him, and said, Art thou for us, or for our adversaries? And He said to him, I am Captain of the Lord's host: now have I come. And Joshua fell on his face on the ground, and said to Him, Lord, what commandest Thou Thy servant? And the Lord's Captain says to Joshua, Loose the shoes off thy feet; for the place whereon thou standest is holy ground. And Jericho was shut up and fortified, and no one went out of it. And the Lord said to Joshua, Behold, I give into thine hand Jericho, and its king, [and] its mighty men.'

— Dialogue with Trypho 62, on Joshua 5:13–6:2. Justin quotes the scene as one of his proofs that God "conversed with some one who was numerically distinct from Himself." The figure who meets Joshua outside Jericho is the Word, the same one who "appeared to and conversed with Moses, and Abraham, and all the other patriarchs" (113). On this reading the divine figure who stands before Joshua at Jericho, just before the city is given into his hand, is the pre-incarnate Son. Justin does not take the Captain as a symbol. He takes him as a real appearance of a divine person at a real city before a real siege.4

Joshua's battle and Rahab's thread as signs of the cross and the blood

And again, by what Moses and Joshua did, the same thing was symbolically announced and told beforehand. For the one of them, stretching out his hands, remained till evening on the hill, his hands being supported; and this reveals a type of no other thing than of the cross: and the other, whose name was altered to Jesus (Joshua), led the fight, and Israel conquered. … For the sign of the scarlet thread, which the spies, sent to Jericho by Joshua, son of Nave (Nun), gave to Rahab the harlot, telling her to bind it to the window through which she let them down to escape from their enemies, also manifested the symbol of the blood of Christ, by which those who were at one time harlots and unrighteous persons out of all nations are saved, receiving remission of sins, and continuing no longer in sin.

— Dialogue with Trypho 111. The battle with Amalek (Exodus 17) is the first war Joshua fights. Justin reads it, here and in chapters 90–91, as two types joined in one event: Moses' outstretched arms are the cross, and Joshua's name is the name of Jesus. "He who prevailed prevailed by the cross" (90). Rahab's cord is treated the same way. It was a real signal that the spies sent by Joshua gave her, and it "also manifested" the blood of Christ. Justin uses "also" where Clement had used "not only … but." The figure is added to the event and does not replace it.5

Where the history falls short: "the memorial of Amalek remained after the son of Nave"

… as well as in the type of the extending of the hands of Moses, and of Oshea being named Jesus (Joshua); when you fought against Amalek: concerning which God enjoined that the incident be recorded, and the name of Jesus laid up in your understandings; saying that this is He who would blot out the memorial of Amalek from under heaven. Now it is clear that the memorial of Amalek remained after the son of Nave (Nun): but He makes it manifest through Jesus, who was crucified, of whom also those symbols were fore-announcements of all that would happen to Him, the demons would be destroyed, and would dread His name.

— Dialogue with Trypho 131. The argument depends on the history. God promised that "Jesus" would blot out Amalek. The Jesus who was son of Nave did not, because Amalek "remained." So the promise must look ahead to another Jesus. Chapter 49 makes the same move with the "concealed hand" of Exodus 17:16: "you will not deny that Amalek fell," but the concealed power was "in Christ the crucified." Jean Daniélou describes the method: Justin shows that the event "has not been effectively fulfilled in Jewish history, and yet has not a purely eschatological significance." This is the same use of the record that Origen later makes of the claim that Joshua "took all the land." The history is accepted, and its limits are used to point to Christ.6

"By the will of God": the conquest as the fulfilment of Noah's curse

Now, in what he said, he foretold that the descendants of Shem would keep in retention the property and dwellings of Canaan: and again that the descendants of Japheth would take possession of the property of which Shem's descendants had dispossessed Canaan's descendants; and spoil the descendants of Shem, even as they plundered the sons of Canaan. And listen to the way in which it has so come to pass. For you, who have derived your lineage from Shem, invaded the territory of the sons of Canaan by the will of God; and you possessed it. And it is manifest that the sons of Japheth, having invaded you in turn by the judgment of God, have taken your land from you, and have possessed it.

— Dialogue with Trypho 139, on Genesis 9:25–27. This is as close as Justin comes to the justice of the conquest, and he does not treat it as a problem. Israel "invaded" Canaan "by the will of God," fulfilling Noah's curse on Canaan. Rome has now taken the land from Israel "by the judgment of God." The two conquests are set side by side as two real steps in one prophecy. Justin's horizon is also earthly. He goes on to say that Christ "has promised … that there shall be a future possession for all the saints in this same land." In First Apology 26 he names "Marcion, a man of Pontus, who is even at this day alive, and teaching his disciples to believe in some other god greater than the Creator." He never answers a Marcionite charge about the Canaanite wars, though, and he never discusses the ban.7

Notes

  • History as the ground of the type. Justin's Joshua typology is the fullest before Origen, and every part of it is drawn from an event: the renaming, the entry, the allotment, the stone knives, the theophany at Jericho, the battle with Amalek, the scarlet cord, the sun. He argues with a Jew from texts both accept, so the events are common ground and the dispute is over what they signify. Daniélou judges that "the successors of St Justin up till Origen have nothing very considerable to add to this rich typology of Joshua."8
  • The ban. Justin never takes up the moral question that his contemporary Marcion pressed. For the objection and the classic answers, see the moral problem of the ḥerem. The first answers to Marcion on this timeline come from Irenaeus and Tertullian.
  • Rahab. The scarlet-thread passage is treated on its own on Justin Martyr on Rahab; see also the scarlet-cord typology and the Rahab timeline.
  • Noah's curse. Dialogue 139 is set out more fully on Justin Martyr on the curse of Ham and the curse of Ham timeline.
  • The material record. What the ground shows of the campaigns Justin takes for granted is on the conquest in the material record; for Jericho, see the Jericho dating argument.
  • Where this stands on the timeline. Justin writes about sixty years after Clement of Rome, who took up only Rahab, and a generation before Irenaeus, who carries the Joshua/Jesus contrast into his summary of salvation history.
