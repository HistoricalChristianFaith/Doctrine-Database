Ahaziah's Age: 22 or 42? (2 Kings 8:26 vs. 2 Chronicles 22:2)

Two books of the Old Testament give the age of Ahaziah of Judah at his accession, and they differ by twenty years. The Masoretic Hebrew of 2 Kings 8:26 makes him "two and twenty years old was Ahaziah when he began to reign."1 The parallel notice at 2 Chronicles 22:2 — the Chronicler retelling the same reign — makes him "forty and two years old… when he began to reign."2 Both notices agree that he reigned one year in Jerusalem and that his mother was Athaliah; only the age differs, by exactly twenty. Other numbers that differ between Samuel–Kings and Chronicles are traced on Solomon's forty-thousand-or-four-thousand stalls and the two slayers of Goliath.

The Chronicler gives Ahaziah's father Jehoram an accession at thirty-two and a reign of eight years (2 Chronicles 21:20 = 2 Kings 8:17).3 On these figures the Pulpit Commentary and Gleason Archer reckon that Jehoram died at forty, so that a son of forty-two would be two years older than his father; the Pulpit Commentary calls the Chronicles figure "absolutely impossible, since his father was but forty when he died."4

The Cambridge Bible and Ellicott report that the Septuagint of Chronicles reads "twenty" (eíkosi), the Cambridge Bible adding that it is "agreeing nearly with" the "two-and-twenty" of Kings; Ellicott adds that the Syriac and Arabic versions read "twenty-two."5 Commentators have explained the "forty-two" in three ways. The rabbinic chronology Seder Olam Rabbah, as cited by John Gill, and Matthew Poole (1685) take it to count the reign-years of the house of Omri, Athaliah's family; David Kimchi and Isaac Abarbanel, as cited by Gill, and the notes of the Geneva Bible take it to date a second accession, after Jehoram's death; and Poole in a second answer, Gill, Keil & Delitzsch (1872) and Gleason Archer (1982) take it to be a copyist's corruption of an original "twenty-two."6 Of the last group, Keil & Delitzsch locate the slip in the alphabetic numeral-letters, where "twenty" (כ) could be misread as "forty" (מ), and Archer in the older stroke-and-hook notation of Ezra's day.7

This page gathers the witnesses earliest first — the book of Kings that gives "twenty-two," the Chronicler whose Masoretic text reads "forty-two," the ancient versions, and the commentators from Poole to Archer — and sets out the readings of the number under Disputed readings.

Timeline

The book of Kings (c. 560 BC)

The book of Kings gives "twenty-two." 2 Kings 8:26 opens Ahaziah's brief reign: "Two and twenty years old was Ahaziah when he began to reign; and he reigned one year in Jerusalem."8 The same book gives Ahaziah's father an accession at thirty-two and a reign of eight years (2 Kings 8:17), and its account of the reign is the one the Chronicler parallels.

The Chronicler (c. 400 BC)

Retelling the reigns told in Kings, the Chronicler reaches Ahaziah at 2 Chronicles 22:2, and the received Hebrew here reads "forty and two years old."9 Two verses earlier, at 2 Chronicles 21:20, the same book gives Jehoram's accession at thirty-two and his reign as eight years. Whether the Chronicler wrote "forty-two," and what the figure counts, is disputed; see the disputed reading.

The ancient versions — Septuagint, Syriac, and Arabic (c. 150 BC onward)

In the Septuagint's 2 Paralipomenon 22:2, as the Cambridge Bible reports, Ahaziah is "twenty years old" (ὦn eἴkosi ἐtῶn), "agreeing nearly with 2 Kings 8:26"; Ellicott reports that the Syriac and the Arabic read "twenty-two."10 Poole and Gill report Greek copies of Chronicles that read "twenty-two," and the same figure in the ancient Syriac copy kept at Antioch from which Archbishop Ussher had a transcript made; both cite these readings for a copyist's error in the Hebrew of Chronicles.11

Matthew Poole (1685)

Matthew Poole, in his Annotations upon the Holy Bible, gives two answers. His first keeps "forty-two" as written by referring it not to Ahaziah's age but to the age of the dynasty from which his mother came: "Omri, who reigned six years… Ahab his son reigned twenty-two years… Ahaziah his son two years… Joram his son twelve years; all which, put together, make up exactly these forty-two years."12 He also allows that the phrase may give the age of Athaliah. His second answer reports that "Some acknowledge an error in the transcribers of the present Hebrew copies, in which language the numeral letters for twenty-two and forty-two are so like, that they might easily be mistaken," and sets out the Greek, Syriac and Arabic readings in its support.13 Both answers are set out under Disputed readings.

Keil & Delitzsch (1872)

Keil & Delitzsch, on 2 Chronicles 22:2, call the figure an orthographical error: "The number 42 is an orthographical error for 22… 2 Kings 8:26."14 They locate the slip in the Hebrew numeral-letters — "twenty" (כ) written or read as "forty" (מ) — and John Gill, on the same verse, reaches the same conclusion: "it seems best to acknowledge a mistake of the copier, which might easily be made through a similarity of the numeral letters, forty two, for twenty two."

Gleason Archer (1982)

Gleason Archer, in his Encyclopedia of Bible Difficulties, treats the pair as a numerical copying-error: the correct age is the twenty-two of Kings, and the "forty-two" of Chronicles arose in the older system of number-notation, where a smudged or flaked decade-mark could shift a figure by ten or by a factor of ten.15 Archer frames the slip not as a wound to Scripture but as the ordinary hazard of hand-copied numerals, recoverable because the parallel in Kings, and the ancient versions, preserve the smaller figure.
