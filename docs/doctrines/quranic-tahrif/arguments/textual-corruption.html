Was the text of the earlier scriptures corrupted (tahrif al-lafz)?

Type: supporting argument (off-timeline) · Assessment: weak — as the charge that the wording of the Gospel (and the Torah) was deliberately altered to bury the true revelation and manufacture Christian doctrine: the textual variation is real, but it is ordinary transmission history, not coordinated doctrinal forgery, and pre-Islamic manuscripts already carry the contested doctrines · advanced by Ibn Hazm of Cordoba (revived by Rashid Rida).

Related arguments: the nature of the corruption — see also Was only the meaning corrupted (tahrif al-ma‘na)? Across the aisle: the mirror-image charge that the Jews corrupted the Hebrew text is weighed on the rabbinic-corruption timeline; the transmission record it turns on is the terrain of the New Testament canon and the Old Testament canon.

The claim

Concerning the manifest contradictions and clear falsifications in the book the Jews call the Torah, and the rest of their books, as well as in the four gospels, confirming thereby their alteration and corruption, such that it is other than that which Allah, The Sublime, revealed to them.

— Ibn Hazm, al-Fisal fi al-Milal wa’l-Ahwa’ wa’l-Nihal, vol. 1, p. 116, tr. Abdul Rashied Omar, link. The strong form of tahrif al-lafz asserts that the actual words of the earlier scriptures were changed — not merely misread — so that the Bible in Jewish and Christian hands is a falsified text, and the Trinity, the divinity of Christ and the crucifixion are among the corruptions read into or written onto it.1

Who advances it

The most systematic classical case is Ibn Hazm of Cordoba’s (d. 1064), whose al-Fisal devotes some 140 pages to a catalogue of contradictions argued to prove deliberate textual falsification, and who names Ezra the scribe as the Torah’s forger. He was not quite the first (al-Maqdisi, d. after 966, precedes him), but he is the pole of the hard-textual reading, “a departure from the prevailing opinion before his time.” In the modern period the charge was revived by Muhammad Rashid Rida in the Tafsir al-Manar circle, who pointedly rejected the meaning-only concession and defended a real corruption of the biblical text.2

The evidence for

The steelman does not rest on Ibn Hazm’s invective but on a real datum: the transmitted text is not uniform. His strongest structural argument is a contrast of custody — the Qur’an was mass-memorized and mass-transmitted (tawatur), whereas the Torah was the possession of a narrow priestly line across centuries in which, he claims, Israel repeatedly lapsed, leaving the text vulnerable to undetectable tampering; and he plays the divergent Hebrew, Samaritan and Greek (Septuagint) Torahs against one another — three mutually contradictory recensions cannot all be the untampered original. On the Torah this observation lands: the Hebrew Bible genuinely circulated in more than one textual edition in the Second Temple period, and the Dead Sea Scrolls have since confirmed that pluriformity — the Septuagint often renders a different Hebrew Vorlage, not a corruption of the Masoretic one (Emanuel Tov). Applied to the Gospels, Ibn Hazm tabulates discrepancies among the four parallel narratives — the call of the first apostles most famously — and, by the Qur’anic test that a truly divine book contains no discrepancy (Q 4:82), infers that a contradicted text cannot be the revelation as given.3

Adversarial assessment

What holds. That the transmitted text is not letter-perfect and uniform is true, and on the Torah the point is real: the Hebrew Bible did come in divergent text-types (Masoretic, Samaritan, the Septuagint’s Vorlage), a pluriformity the Scrolls have vindicated. Ibn Hazm’s premise that a single narrow line of custody is more alterable than a mass-transmitted one is not absurd. So the flat claim “the Bible is a single, pristine, uniform text” is not one the evidence supports.

What is overstated or imprecise. Four things break the inference from variation to deliberate doctrinal forgery. (1) Variation is not fraud. Textual pluriformity, harmonizable narrative difference, independent composition and ordinary scribal drift explain the data Ibn Hazm catalogues without a coordinated rewrite; his literalist “any discrepancy proves falsification” test would condemn the parallel transmission of almost any ancient corpus. (2) The chronology is fatal to the doctrinal charge. The specifically textual form — that the Gospel was rewritten to bury the true Injil and insert the Trinity, the divinity and the crucifixion — is a historical claim about transmission, and the physical record runs against it: the Rylands fragment 𝔿52 (first half of the second century) and the fourth-century codices Vaticanus and Sinaiticus predate Islam by two to five centuries and already carry the contested material, so a post-Qur’anic rewrite is chronologically impossible, and a coordinated pre-Islamic one across the scattered churches would have left a manuscript trace it does not.4 (3) Ibn Hazm’s contradictions are partly artifacts of his source. He worked from a Christian-produced Arabic Bible resting on a Latin Vorlage, so some of his “discrepancies” reflect the peculiarities of that version rather than the Hebrew or Greek (Pulcini). (4) The charge is a minority, medieval hardening. Of nine early Muslim writers on the Hebrew Bible surveyed by Adang, only al-Maqdisi and Ibn Hazm held that the text itself had been distorted; the formative-period exegetes assumed an intact Torah and Gospel (Nickel), so the wholesale textual charge is not the original Islamic reading but a later development.5 On the evidence the manuscript record shows ordinary scribal drift within a reconstructable text — even Bart Ehrman, the tradition’s best-known skeptic, grants that of the hundreds of thousands of variants “most of them are completely insignificant,” while holding (and it is kept here) that a meaningful minority bear on doctrine — not the coordinated, doctrinally-driven rewriting the charge requires.6

Assessment

Weak — as the claim that the wording of the Gospel was deliberately altered to bury the true message and produce Christian doctrine. The evidence of textual variation is genuine, but it is ordinary transmission history, not coordinated doctrinal forgery; the pre-Islamic manuscripts already carry the contested doctrines, so the charge fails on both chronology and mechanism. The honest residue is on the Torah, not the Gospel: the Hebrew Bible’s real textual pluriformity (Masoretic / Samaritan / Septuagint, now confirmed by the Scrolls) means “the transmitted text is not uniform” is true — but that is a limit on any claim of a single pristine text, not the doctrinally-motivated rewrite tahrif al-lafz needs, and it is the same datum the wiki weighs, and finds weak, as the mirror-image charge that the Jews corrupted the Hebrew text. The proponent’s overreach — inferring deliberate falsification from bare contradiction, and from a defective Arabic version — is flagged on his page.
