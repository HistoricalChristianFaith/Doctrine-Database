What does the variant number 616 at Revelation 13:18 represent?

Disputed reading · on The Mark of the Beast and the Number 666 · witness: Irenaeus, the earliest extant witness to the variant

The passage

"Here is wisdom. Let him that hath understanding count the number of the beast: for it is the number of a man; and his number is Six hundred threescore and six."

"Such, then, being the state of the case, and this number being found in all the most approved and ancient copies [of the Apocalypse], and those men who saw John face to face bearing their testimony [to it]… I do not know how it is that some have erred following the ordinary mode of speech, and have vitiated the middle number in the name, deducting the amount of fifty from it, so that instead of six decads they will have it that there is but one. [I am inclined to think that this occurred through the fault of the copyists, as is wont to happen, since numbers also are expressed by letters; so that the Greek letter which expresses the number sixty was easily expanded into the letter Iota of the Greeks.]"

— Revelation 13:18 (KJV), and Irenaeus, Against Heresies 5.30.1 (trans. ANF).1

The question

Most witnesses to Revelation 13:18 give the beast's number as 666 (P47, Sinaiticus, Alexandrinus and the mass of later manuscripts); 616 is read by Codex Ephraemi Rescriptus (C, fifth century), by copies Irenaeus knew around 180, by Tyconius, and, according to Tischendorf, by two minuscules no longer extant. Metzger notes that in Greek numerals the difference is a single letter (ξ, sixty, against ι, ten). Codex Vaticanus does not contain Revelation. Since 2000 the number has also been read as 616 in P115 (P.Oxy. 4499, late third or early fourth century), where the INTF transcription treats the reading as conjectured from the space available.2 What is disputed is how 616 arose and what it signifies: a slip, a deliberate respelling of a name, a different name, or the original number. The answer bears on how the modern identification of 666 with Nero is argued; whether that identification also dates the book is a separate question, traced at Does 666 = "Nero Caesar" (Rev 13:18) date Revelation to Nero's reign?3

An accidental copyist's error for 666

On this reading 666 is the number John wrote, and 616 is a mechanical slip in copying a numeral.

Held by: Irenaeus (bishop of Lyons, Against Heresies 5.30.1, c. 180).4

Irenaeus rests the reading 666 on three grounds: it stands "in all the most approved and ancient copies"; "those men who saw John face to face" bore testimony to it; and the number, reckoned by Greek letter-values, has its tens equal to its hundreds and its hundreds to its units, the repeated six signifying "the recapitulations of that apostasy, taken in its full extent." The variant, on his account, arose "through the fault of the copyists, as is wont to happen, since numbers also are expressed by letters," the letter for sixty being "easily expanded" into iota. He distinguishes those who then used 616 "in their simplicity" from those who "ventured to seek out a name which should contain the erroneous and spurious number," and he reports that already in his day some interpreters built name-solutions on 616. On this reading the variant carries no meaning of its own: it is a scribal accident, and the number to be counted is 666.5

The name Nero Caesar respelled in its Latin form

On this reading 666 is original and ciphers Neron Qesar in Hebrew letters; 616 is the same name spelled the Latin way, Nero, without the final nun.

Held by: F. W. Farrar (English cleric, The Early Days of Christianity, 1882); Richard Bauckham (The Climax of Prophecy, 1993); Bruce M. Metzger (A Textual Commentary on the Greek New Testament, 2nd ed., 1994), who offers it as a possibility.6

"…the Greek form Neron Caesar written in Hebrew characters (נרון קסר) is equivalent to 666, whereas the Latin form Nero Caesar (נרו קסר) is equivalent to 616."

— Metzger, Textual Commentary, on Revelation 13:18.7

Farrar proposes that a copyist who knew that "to Romans, and Gentiles generally, the name was always Nero Cæsar, not Neron" altered the number "because he imagined that… it made the solution more suitable and easy"; Metzger writes that "perhaps the change was intentional." On this account the variant records an early reader who already took the number as Nero's name. Bauckham treats the double equivalence as the linchpin of the identification: one name, in the two languages of the empire, yields both numbers the manuscripts preserve. Metzger's committee prints 666, rated {A}, as the number John wrote.8

The Greek word for "of the beast" in Hebrew letters

On this reading 616, like 666, spells the word "beast" itself, transliterated from Greek into Hebrew, and the numeral as written in P115 may also have echoed the abbreviations for "Christ" and "Jesus."

Held by: Michael Oberweis (ZNW 77, 1986) ⚠ unverified; P. J. Williams (Tyndale Bulletin 58.1, 2007).9

Williams starts from G. K. Beale's observation that the Greek thērion ("beast"), transliterated into Hebrew, totals 666. The verse reads "let him who has understanding calculate the number of the beast," with the word in the genitive, thēriou; transliterated into Hebrew as תריו, that form totals 616. "The interpretation that the number of the Beast is simply the Greek word for 'beast' written in Hebrew characters therefore works with both numbers, 666 and 616, as does the interpretation Nero Caesar," he writes. Williams then proposes that the visual form of the numeral in P115, χις with a supralinear stroke, resembles the nomina sacra χς ("Christ") and ις ("Jesus"), which often stand together, since the strokes over numerals and over sacred names look alike and the final digit and final sigma are written the same way. A scribe may therefore have shaped the number to mimic designations of Jesus. Williams presents this as "an alternative or perhaps additional explanation," holding that explanations of the number "need not exclude each other."10

The name Gaius Caesar (Caligula)

On this reading 616 ciphers a different emperor, Gaius Caesar, by its own gematria.

Held by: J. Neville Birdsall ("Irenaeus and the Number of the Beast: Revelation 13,18," 2002).11

Birdsall reads the number 616, by a gematria different from the one that yields Nero, as the name of Gaius Caesar, the emperor Caligula. Irenaeus already reports readers in the second century who counted 616 and sought "a name which should contain" it, so that the variant circulated as a number to be solved in its own right. On this reading 616 is not a respelling of the 666-name but the trace of a separate early decipherment, fixed on a different Caesar.12

The original number, with 666 a later form

On this reading John wrote 616, and 666 is the later form that came to dominate the tradition.

Held by: ⚠ unverified — no named proponent located yet.13

The case rests on the age and quality of the witnesses. 616 is attested in the second century, before any surviving manuscript, in the copies Irenaeus knew; it is read by Tyconius in the Latin West and by Codex Ephraemi. P115, which reads 616, is the oldest manuscript of this part of Revelation; Parker, writing on it in New Testament Studies, describes it as "the oldest member of the A C text-type by over a century" and finds its agreements with other witnesses a sign of its high quality. Codex C, the other 616 witness, belongs to that same text-type. On this reading the pairing of P115 and C carries the variant back to the strongest textual line for Revelation, and the change from 616 to 666 would be the later development: a single-letter change in Greek numerals, ι to ξ, producing the symmetrical number that Irenaeus valued for its repeated six.14
