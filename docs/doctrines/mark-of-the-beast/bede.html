Bede on the Mark of the Beast and the Number 666

Dates: c. 730 · Wikipedia
Summary: In his Commentary on Revelation, Bede gathers the Greek and Latin candidate names for the beast — Titan, Antemos, Arnoume — into one place, then turns to a symbolic reading. Six, the number "by which the world was made," signifies "the perfection of the work"; 666 recalls Solomon's yearly six hundred sixty-six talents of gold, a tribute owed the true king that the "deceiving tyrant" will presume to demand.

Claims & evidence

The Greek and Latin candidate names: Titan (giant), Antemos ("contrary to honor"), Arnoume ("I deny")

"Here is wisdom. Let him who has understanding, etc. This number is said to be found among the Greeks in the name Titan, that is, giant… Τ equals 300, Ε equals 5, Ι equals 10, Τ equals 300, Α equals 1, Ν equals 50. It is thought that the Antichrist will assume this name as if he surpasses all in power… Primasius also mentions another name that completes the same number: Α equals 1, Ν equals 50, Τ equals 300, Ε equals 5, Μ equals 40, Ο equals 70, Σ equals 200, which signifies 'contrary to honor'. Also, the word Α equals 1, Ρ equals 100, Ν equals 50, Ο equals 70, Υ equals 400, Μ equals 40, Ε equals 5, meaning 'I deny'."

— Commentary on Revelation (Expositio Apocalypseos), on Revelation 13:18, source. Bede credits the two Latin names — ANTEMOS ("contrary to honor") and ARNOUME ("I deny") — to Primasius; the Greek TITAN goes back to Irenaeus.

The symbolic reading: six as the perfection of the work, and Solomon's 666 talents

"…who does not know that the number six, by which the world was made, signifies the perfection of the work?… Moreover, the weight of gold that came to Solomon each year was six hundred sixty-six talents. Therefore, what is rightly owed and paid as tribute to the true king, that same tribute the deceiving tyrant will presume to demand."

— Commentary on Revelation (Expositio Apocalypseos), on Revelation 13:18, source. Rather than resting on any single cipher, Bede reads the number symbolically: six, the number of the world's making, signifies "the perfection of the work," and the six hundred sixty-six talents brought yearly to Solomon (1 Kings 10:14) become the tribute owed the true king that the "deceiving tyrant" will presume to demand.

Notes

  • Bede's sources and the symbolic reading. Bede draws on the Latin commentators — Primasius above all — and sets the symbolic, non-cipher reading of 666 alongside the name-ciphers: six as "the perfection of the work," and 666 as the true king's tribute claimed by the tyrant. Among modern commentators G. K. Beale (1999) also reads the number symbolically, as a symbol of incompleteness and failure rather than a name to decode. See the summary timeline.
  • Bede does not give "DICLUX." The Latin cipher DICLUX (whose numeral letters total 666) does not appear in Bede's comment; it is found in Victorinus of Pettau and in Beatus of Liébana. Bede's Latin names here are Antemos and Arnoume (from Primasius), not DICLUX.
  • Bede names no emperor. Like all the fathers who handle 13:18, Bede offers no imperial identification — not Nero, not any other — but works with generic candidate names and the symbolic sense. On the modern Nero-cipher solution, see the summary timeline.
