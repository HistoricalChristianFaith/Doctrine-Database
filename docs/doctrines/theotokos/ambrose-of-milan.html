Ambrose of Milan on Theotokos

Dates: c. 380 (the treatise On the Faith [De Fide], addressed to the Emperor Gratian, written c. 378–380) · bishop of Milan c. 374–397 · Wikipedia
Summary: Ambrose, bishop of Milan, writing in Latin against the Arians, expounds Paul’s “God sent His Son, made of a woman” (Galatians 4:4): the one born of Mary is the Father’s own Son, whose generation is eternal, and the “making” is to be understood “not of the Godhead, but of the putting on of a body.” He does not use the Greek word Theotokos.

Claims & evidence

On the Faith I.14.94: “made of a woman”

“But when the fulness of time was come, God sent His Son, made of a woman, made under the Law.” “His Son,” observe, not as one of many, not as His in common with another, but His own, and in saying “His Son,” the Apostle showed that it is of the Son’s nature that His generation is eternal. Him the Apostle has affirmed to have been afterwards “made” of a woman, in order that the making might be understood not of the Godhead, but of the putting on of a body—“made of a woman,” then, by taking on of flesh; “made under the Law” through observance of the Law.

— On the Faith (De Fide) I.14.94, source.1 Ambrose reads “His Son” as the Father’s own Son, whose “generation is eternal,” and the one “made of a woman” as that same Son; the being “made” he refers “not of the Godhead, but of the putting on of a body.”

Notes

  • Ambrose distinguishes what is said of the “Godhead” from what is said of the “body” in expounding Galatians 4:4. On what the title Theotokos asserts, see the disputed reading Was “Theotokos” a confession about Christ, a title of honour for Mary, or both?
  • Ambrose does not use the Greek word Theotokos here; he writes in Latin, of the one born of Mary as the Father’s own Son.
  • Ambrose also appears as a witness on several other timelines (the New Eve, the perpetual virginity of Mary, the intermediate state, and others); here the interest is only in this passage.
