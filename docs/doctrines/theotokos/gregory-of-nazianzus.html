Gregory of Nazianzus on Theotokos

Dates: c. 382 (Letter 101) · c. 329–390 · Wikipedia
Summary: In a letter against Apollinarius, Gregory writes that whoever does not believe Mary is the Mother of God “is severed from the Godhead,” joining the title to his statement that the Son was “at once divinely and humanly formed in her.”

Claims & evidence

Letter 101: “severed from the Godhead”

If anyone does not believe that Holy Mary is the Mother of God, he is severed from the Godhead. If anyone should assert that He passed through the Virgin as through a channel, and was not at once divinely and humanly formed in her (divinely, because without the intervention of a man; humanly, because in accordance with the laws of gestation), he is in like manner godless.

— Letter 101, to Cledonius against Apollinarius, source.1 The anathema-style test is aimed at Apollinarian and docetic Christologies: Gregory yokes “Mother of God” to his insistence that the Son was “at once divinely and humanly formed in her,” not merely channelled through her. The title stands here alongside a set of Christological statements in the same letter.

Notes

  • Gregory also appears as a witness on the Old Testament canon timeline (his canon-poem lists a Hebrew-sized Old Testament) — a separate question from his Christology here.
  • Letter 101 was later read at the Council of Ephesus and at Chalcedon as a patristic authority on the two natures of Christ (⚠ unverified).
