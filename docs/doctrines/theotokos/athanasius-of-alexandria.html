Athanasius of Alexandria on Theotokos

Dates: c. 340 (the Third Discourse Against the Arians) · bishop of Alexandria 328–373 · Wikipedia
Summary: Alexander’s deacon and successor uses the title in his discourses against the Arians. Athanasius writes “Mary Bearer of God” (Θεοτόκος, rendered “Bearer of God” in this translation) in setting out how the eternal Word took flesh of the Virgin.

Claims & evidence

The “double account of the Saviour”: eternal God, then flesh of Mary Bearer of God

Now the scope and character of Holy Scripture, as we have often said, is this,—it contains a double account of the Saviour; that He was ever God, and is the Son, being the Father’s Word and Radiance and Wisdom; and that afterwards for us He took flesh of a Virgin, Mary Bearer of God, and was made man.

— Four Discourses Against the Arians III.29, source.1 The title stands inside a rule for reading Scripture: Scripture speaks of the Saviour two ways — as the ever-existing God, and as the one who “took flesh of a Virgin” and “was made man.” The phrase “Mary Bearer of God” belongs to the second half of that account. (The rendering “Bearer of God” here translates the Greek Theotokos.)

A second use in the Third Discourse

whereas the flesh is born of Mary Bearer of God, He Himself is said to have been born, who furnishes to others an origin of being.

— Four Discourses Against the Arians III.33, source. Athanasius uses “Mary Bearer of God” more than once in the Third Discourse, here of the flesh born of her, while the one born is he “who furnishes to others an origin of being.”

Notes

  • Athanasius stands in direct continuity with Alexander of Alexandria, whose deacon he was at Nicæa and whom he succeeded in the see; both use the title in writing against Arius.
  • Athanasius also appears as a witness on several other timelines (the canon, the Old Testament canon, and others); here the interest is only in his use of the title Theotokos.
