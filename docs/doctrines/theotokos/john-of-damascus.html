John of Damascus on Theotokos

Dates: c. 730 (An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith III.12) · Wikipedia
Summary: John of Damascus, a monk and theologian writing in Greek c. 730, calls “Mother of God” the name that “embraces the whole mystery of the dispensation,” and in the same chapter, written “against the Nestorians,” declines the title “Mother of Christ.”

Claims & evidence

Exposition III.12: “the whole mystery of the dispensation”

Hence it is with justice and truth that we call the holy Mary the Mother of God. For this name embraces the whole mystery of the dispensation. For if she who bore Him is the Mother of God, assuredly He Who was born of her is God and likewise also man.

— An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith III.12, source.1 John states the title as an inference from mother to child, and adds that “the name in truth signifies the one subsistence and the two natures.”

His refusal of “Mother of Christ”

But we never say that the holy Virgin is the Mother of Christ… because it was in order to do away with the title Mother of God, and to bring dishonour on the Mother of God… that the impure and abominable Judaizing Nestorius… invented this name for an insult.

— An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith III.12, source. The chapter is headed “That the holy Virgin is the Mother of God: an argument directed against the Nestorians.” John declines Nestorius’s preferred Christotokos, giving as his reason that the name was introduced “to do away with the title Mother of God.” The epithets for Nestorius are John’s own.

Notes

  • John of Damascus also appears on several other Marian timelines — the perpetual virginity, the Assumption, and Mary as the New Eve ; here the interest is in his use of the title Theotokos.
