Leo the Great on Theotokos (Mary as Mother of God)

Dates: c. 449 · Wikipedia
Summary: Pope Leo I (Leo the Great), bishop of Rome, writing between Ephesus (431) and Chalcedon (451), calls Mary “the Mother of God” in his Christmas preaching. His Tome to Flavian (Letter 28, 449) — received in Chalcedon’s Definition and acclaimed by the bishops at its second session (“Peter has spoken thus through Leo”) — speaks of the “unity of person which is to be understood in both natures,” so that “the Son of God took flesh from the Virgin who bore Him.”

Claims & evidence

Mary “the Mother of God” in a Nativity sermon (c. 440)

A royal Virgin of the stem of David is chosen, to be impregnated with the sacred seed and to conceive the Divinely-human offspring in mind first and then in body. And lest in ignorance of the heavenly counsel she should tremble at so strange a result, she learns from converse with the angel that what is to be wrought in her is of the Holy Ghost. Nor does she believe it loss of honour that she is soon to be the Mother of God.

— Sermon 21, On the Feast of the Nativity, 1, §I, historicalchristian.faith. Delivered at Rome (c. 440). Leo uses the title in a sermon on the conception of “the Divinely-human offspring” “of the Holy Ghost.”

The Tome (449) on the unity of person

Therefore in consequence of this unity of person which is to be understood in both natures, we read of the Son of Man also descending from heaven, when the Son of God took flesh from the Virgin who bore Him. And again the Son of God is said to have been crucified and buried, although it was not actually in His Divinity whereby the Only-begotten is co-eternal and con-substantial with the Father, but in His weak human nature that He suffered these things.

— Letter 28 to Flavian, commonly called “The Tome” (449), §5, historicalchristian.faith. Leo states that “in consequence of this unity of person” what belongs to the manhood is said of the Son of God: “the Son of God took flesh from the Virgin who bore Him.” In the same letter he writes that “each form does what is proper to it with the co-operation of the other.”

The Tome (449) on the birth from the Virgin

the only begotten of the eternal Father was born eternal of the Holy Spirit and the Virgin Mary. And this nativity which took place in time took nothing from, and added nothing to that divine and eternal birth… He was conceived of the Holy Spirit within the womb of His Virgin Mother, who brought Him forth without the loss of her virginity, even as she conceived Him without its loss.

— Letter 28 to Flavian (The Tome) (449), §§2, 4, historicalchristian.faith. Leo writes that the birth in time “took nothing from, and added nothing to” the eternal birth, and that the one “born eternal” of the Father was born “of the Holy Spirit and the Virgin Mary.”

Notes

  • Leo is the Western witness on this timeline between Ephesus and Chalcedon. The Council of Chalcedon’s Definition receives his letter to Flavian, and at the council’s second session the bishops cried out, “Peter has spoken thus through Leo” (Session 2, Extracts); the Definition confesses Mary “the Mother of God according to his manhood.”
  • On whether the title is chiefly a statement about Christ, a title of honour for Mary, or both, see the disputed reading Was “Theotokos” a confession about Christ, a title of honour for Mary, or both?
  • Leo also appears as a witness on other timelines — e.g. the intermediate state (the descent) and the ministerial priesthood.
