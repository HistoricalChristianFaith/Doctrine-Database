Cyril of Alexandria on Theotokos

Dates: c. 430 (the Third Letter to Nestorius) · bishop of Alexandria 412–444 · Wikipedia
Summary: Cyril, bishop of Alexandria, defended the title against Nestorius. He grounds “Mother of God” in the Incarnation — Mary bore “God made one with flesh” — and makes confession of it the first of his Twelve Anathematisms, which were read into the acts at Ephesus (431).

Claims & evidence

The Third Letter to Nestorius: why she is called Mother of God

And since the holy Virgin brought forth corporally God made one with flesh according to nature, for this reason we also call her Mother of God, not as if the nature of the Word had the beginning of its existence from the flesh.

— Third Letter to Nestorius (with the Twelve Anathematisms), source.1 Cyril states that the title does not make Mary the source of the Word’s divine nature (“not as if the nature of the Word had the beginning of its existence from the flesh”); he grounds it in her having borne “God made one with flesh.”

The First Anathematism

If anyone will not confess that the Emmanuel is very God, and that therefore the Holy Virgin is the Mother of God (Theotokos), inasmuch as in the flesh she bore the Word of God made flesh [as it is written, “The Word was made flesh”]: let him be anathema.

— First of the Twelve Anathematisms, appended to the same letter, source. The anathema binds the title to John 1:14: because “the Word was made flesh,” the one born of Mary is very God, and so she is truly Theotokos. The letter carrying the anathematisms was read into the acts of the Council of Ephesus in 431.

Notes

  • Cyril also appears as a witness on the perpetual virginity timeline; there the interest is in Mary’s ever-virginity, here in her being the God-bearer — two distinct Marian claims.
  • Whether Nestorius was condemned for his own teaching, and whether the title is chiefly Christological, Marian, or both, are disputed readings: see Was Nestorius condemned at Ephesus for a Christology he did not hold? and Was “Theotokos” a confession about Christ, a title of honour for Mary, or both?
