What Scripture does John 7:38 (“out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water”) refer to?

Disputed reading · on the Old Testament canon · witnesses: Jerome, Cyril of Jerusalem, John Chrysostom

Related readings: Ephesians 5:14 · 1 Corinthians 2:9 · Luke 11:49

The passage

"He that believeth on me, as the scripture hath said, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water."

— John 7:38 (KJV), at the Feast of Tabernacles.1

The question

The clause “as the scripture hath said” (kathôs eipen hē graphē) is a citation formula, and the words that follow match no single Old Testament verse. The verse is listed among New Testament citations with no exact Old Testament source, a group sometimes adduced for a wider apostolic “Scripture”; no named proponent of reading this verse as a quotation from outside the Old Testament was located.1 What is disputed is where the words come from: an Old Testament passage or group of passages, or no quotation at all.

An Old Testament “living water” Scripture

The formula sends the hearer back to the Old Testament: to Proverbs, or to the prophetic promises of living water.

Held by: Cyril of Jerusalem (Catechetical Lecture 16, c. 350); Ambrose (On the Holy Spirit 3); Jerome (Preface to the Pentateuch, c. 403).2

Cyril takes the formula as a real back-reference: “as the Scripture has said — here he sends you back to the Old Testament,” and reads the rivers as the Spirit. Ambrose reads the words through the river “proceeding out of the throne of God” of Revelation 22:1 and the fountain of Eden, the water being the Holy Spirit. Jerome lists “Rivers of living waters shall flow from his belly” among the things the Evangelists and Apostles quote “from the Old Testament which are not found in [the Septuagint] books,” to be sought in the Hebrew. The Catena Aurea pins that testimony to Proverbs: “this testimony is taken from the Proverbs, where it is said, Let thy fountains be dispersed abroad, and rivers of waters in the streets” (Proverbs 5:16), though Jerome’s list names no verse, and Proverbs 18:4 is also proposed.2 The setting supports a prophetic source: at Tabernacles, Zechariah 14:8 promises that “living waters shall go out from Jerusalem,” and Isaiah 58:11, Ezekiel 47:1–12, Psalm 78:15–16 and Isaiah 12:3 speak of the same water.3 On this reading “the scripture” is the Old Testament’s promise of living water, cited by its sense and fulfilled in the gift of the Spirit.

Not a quotation: “as the Scripture saith” goes with believing

The formula belongs to the preceding words, “he that believeth on me, as the Scripture saith”; the rivers of living water are Jesus’ own promise.

Held by: John Chrysostom (Homilies on John 51), as excerpted in the Catena Aurea.3

Chrysostom asks the source question directly and answers it by punctuation:

"But where does the Scripture say this? No where. What then? We should read, He that believeth in Me, as saith the Scripture, putting the stop here; and then, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water: the meaning being, that that was a right kind of belief, which was formed on the evidence of Scripture, not of miracles."

On this reading the formula governs belief: a belief formed “on the evidence of Scripture, not of miracles” is the right kind. The words that follow, “out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water,” are then not a citation of any text but Jesus’ own promise, which the Evangelist immediately interprets: “this spake he of the Spirit, which they that believe on him should receive” (John 7:39). The question of a missing source does not arise, because nothing is quoted. On Chrysostom’s reading the verse joins a call to Scripture-based faith with a promise in Jesus’ own words.
