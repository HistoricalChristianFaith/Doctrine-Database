Did the “two books of Esdras” of Hippo and Carthage include the Greek 1 Esdras, which Trent’s list does not name?

Disputed reading · on the Old Testament canon · witnesses: the African councils, Augustine, the Council of Trent

Related readings: Do the canon lists of the councils contradict one another? · Was Trent the first universal, binding definition?

The passage

Given that Pope Innocent I and Pope Gelasius both affirmed the canon list of Hippo and Carthage, yet Trent and every pope since then has affirmed a different list that excludes the extra material in Esdras, which popes were right and which popes were wrong on this issue?

— James White, cross-examining Gary Michuta (2004).1

The question

The Esdras books travel under different numbers: the Septuagint’s Esdras A (the Greek book with the contest of the three guardsmen) is the English Apocrypha’s 1 Esdras and the Vulgate’s 3 Esdras; Esdras B is Ezra–Nehemiah as one book.2,3 Carthage names “two books of Esdras”; Trent names “the first book of Esdras, and the second which is entitled Nehemias,” and the Clementine Vulgate (1592) printed 3 and 4 Esdras in an appendix.4 What is disputed is whether the African pair included Esdras A, so that the two lists differ. The reading that the identification is open and the lists can be harmonized is stated here from Catholic apologists; academic literature holding it was not located.

The African councils’ two books were Esdras A and Ezra–Nehemiah

When ancient lists name “two books of Esdras” they mean the Greek Esdras A and Ezra–Nehemiah; the African list therefore differs from Trent’s by one book.

Held by: Pierre-Maurice Bogaert (Revue Bénédictine, 2000); Edmon L. Gallagher and John D. Meade (2017);5 James White (Reformed Baptist apologist, 2004), crediting William Webster; Mike Winger.1,2

Bogaert, followed by Gallagher and Meade, concludes that when the ancient lists, Greek or Latin, name “two books of Esdras” they mean Esdras A and Esdras B; Jerome himself treats Ezra–Nehemiah as one book.5 Augustine, the theologian behind the African lists, cites the material unique to Esdras A as possible prophecy of Christ: “unless, perhaps, Esdras is to be understood as prophesying of Christ in that passage where, on a question having arisen among certain young men as to what is the strongest thing… that same third youth demonstrated that the truth is victorious over all”; Gallagher and Meade find the Latin Fathers’ citations of “Ezra” predominantly to Esdras A.6 Winger: the African councils “had a different list than the Catholic church had at the Council of Trent.”2 White asks which popes, those who affirmed the African list or those who affirmed Trent’s, were right.1 On this reading the African councils received, among their Esdras books, a book Trent’s enumeration passes over, and the two catalogues are not the same list.

The identification is contested and the lists can be harmonized

The African “two books of Esdras” does not settle which books were meant; neither council ruled on the precise Esdras unit, and Trent did not reject 3 Esdras.

Held by: Gary Michuta (Catholic apologist, debate with White, 2004); Dave Armstrong (Catholic apologist, 2020).7,8

Armstrong treats the “two books of Esdras” identification as contested and the lists as harmonizable.7 Michuta argues that the African councils, like Trent, “passed over in silence; they didn’t pass judgment” on the precise Esdras enumeration, so that the difference is one of under-specification rather than of opposed verdicts.8 Trent had the wider Latin appendix (3 and 4 Esdras, the Prayer of Manasseh) before it; only a small minority of bishops voted to reject those books explicitly, and the majority left the question open.8 The Clementine Vulgate kept them in an appendix “lest they utterly perish.”4 Trent’s “first book of Esdras, and the second which is entitled Nehemias” follows Jerome’s numbering of Ezra and Nehemiah, the numbering of the Vulgate the council received.4,2 On this reading the earlier and the later councils both received Ezra and Nehemiah and neither formally ruled on Esdras A, and the lists name the same core under different numbering conventions.
