Clement of Rome on the Old Testament Canon

Dates: c. 96 AD (floruit; bishop of Rome, d. c. 99) · Wikipedia
Summary: In 1 Clement — among the earliest post-apostolic writings, c. 95–96 AD — Clement weaves words of the deuterocanonical Wisdom of Solomon into his argument alongside the Gospels and Psalms, and holds up "the blessed Judith," beside Esther, as an example of faith. He gives no canon-list and uses no "it is written" formula for these books. The letter is some three-quarters of a century earlier than Melito's list (c. 170), the earliest extant Christian Old-Testament list on this timeline.

Claims & evidence

He quotes the Wisdom of Solomon beside the Gospel and the Psalms

By the word of His might He established all things, and by His word He can overthrow them. "Who shall say unto Him, What hast thou done? or, Who shall resist the power of His strength?" When and as He pleases He will do all things, and none of the things determined by Him shall pass away. All things are open before Him, and nothing can be hidden from His counsel. "The heavens declare the glory of God, and the firmament showeth His handy-work…"

— First Epistle to the Corinthians 27, source (Roberts–Donaldson translation; also at the Ante-Nicene Fathers). The framed sentence fuses two verses of the deuterocanonical Wisdom of Solomon — Wisdom 12:12 ("who shall say, What hast thou done?") and 11:21 (none may resist the power of God's arm) — and Clement sets it in the same breath as Matthew 24:35 ("none of the things determined by Him shall pass away") and Psalm 19:1 ("The heavens declare the glory of God"), with no mark separating the Wisdom material from the canonical Gospel and Psalm.1 He introduces none of the three texts with a citation formula such as "it is written."

He adduces "the blessed Judith" as an example of faith, paired with Esther

Many women also, being strengthened by the grace of God, have performed numerous manly exploits. The blessed Judith, when her city was besieged, asked of the elders permission to go forth into the camp of the strangers; and, exposing herself to danger, she went out for the love which she bare to her country and people then besieged; and the Lord delivered Holofernes into the hands of a woman. Esther also, being perfect in faith, exposed herself to no less danger…

— First Epistle to the Corinthians 55, source (Roberts–Donaldson translation; also at the Ante-Nicene Fathers). Judith is invoked as an example of faith from the story — set beside Esther, a protocanonical book — and no words of the book are quoted. The story of Judith is here told to the Corinthians as known history in a letter from the Roman church of the 90s.2

Reception and debate

  • No canon-list. 1 Clement contains no enumeration of the Old Testament books. Its use of Wisdom and Judith is among the evidence adduced in "How did the early church receive the deuterocanonical books: as Scripture, below the canon, or outside it?" and "Do the Apostolic Fathers use the deuterocanonical books as Scripture?"; the readings of it are set out there.
  • Form of the citations. Neither passage carries an "it is written" formula, and the Judith reference is an example from the story rather than a quoted text. Clement draws on other deuterocanonical and disputed material elsewhere in the letter in the same allusive manner.
  • Placement on the timeline. At c. 96 AD Clement is the earliest Christian witness on the Old Testament canon page, a lifetime before Melito of Sardis (c. 170), whose list is the earliest extant Christian count of the Old Testament books (see his page).
