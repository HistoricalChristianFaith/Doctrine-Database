The Apostolic Canons (canon 85) on the Old Testament Canon

Dates: c. 380 AD (canon 85, the closing canon of the eighty-five appended to Book VIII of the Apostolic Constitutions; the Constitutions are a Syrian/Antiochene compilation of c. 375–380, the canon-list itself "probably added about the year 380") · Wikipedia
Witness: The last of the Apostolic Canons enumerates the books to be held "venerable and holy." Its Old-Testament list names Judith (in some manuscripts) and three books of Maccabees, and closes by urging the newly catechized to learn "the Wisdom of the learned Sirach"; it does not name Tobit or the Wisdom of Solomon. Its New-Testament count omits the Apocalypse and adds 1–2 Clement and the Constitutions.

Claims & evidence

"Esteemed venerable and holy" — the Old-Testament list of canon 85

The eighty-five Apostolic Canons stand at the end of Book VIII of the Apostolic Constitutions, the fourth-century Syrian church order; the last of them lists the books the whole church, "both clergy and laity," is to hold as Scripture. Its Old-Testament catalogue lists Judith and the Maccabees among the other books:1

Let the following books be esteemed venerable and holy by all of you, both clergy and laity. Of the Old Testament: the five books of Moses, Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, and Deuteronomy; one of Joshua the son of Nun; one of the Judges; one of Ruth; four of the Kings; two of Paralipomena (the books of Chronicles); two of Ezra; one of Esther; [one of Judith;] three of the Maccabees; one of Job; the one hundred and fifty Psalms; three books of Solomon: Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Song of Songs; the sixteen of the Prophets. And see that those newly come to discipleship become acquainted with the Wisdom of the learned Sirach.

Judith, three Maccabees, and Sirach

Judith is named (in the manuscripts that carry the clause), the Maccabees are counted at three (the African lists count two), and the canon closes by commending the Wisdom of Sirach (Ecclesiasticus) to the newly catechized. 3 Maccabees is not in the 1546 list of the Council of Trent; it is in the wider Old Testament of the Eastern Orthodox churches.1

What the list does not name

The list does not name Tobit or the Wisdom of Solomon — the three "books of Solomon" it counts are the protocanonical Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, and Song of Songs, with no fourth and fifth Wisdom book of the kind the African councils folded into their "five books of Solomon." Sirach is not enumerated in the count of "venerable and holy" books but commended afterward as reading for beginners ("see that those newly come to discipleship become acquainted with" it). Athanasius (367) had named Sirach among the books outside the canon appointed "to be read by those who newly join us." The list thus differs from the Tridentine list in naming 3 Maccabees and in not naming Tobit and Wisdom, and it sets Sirach apart from the enumeration.1

"[One of Judith]" — a textual variant

The clause naming Judith is bracketed because it is not secure across the manuscripts: some copies of canon 85 carry "one of Judith," others omit it. Where it drops out, the list's disputed books are the three Maccabees and Sirach. Book VIII of the same Constitutions names Judith twice, ranking "Judith" with Deborah and Huldah among the women who prophesied and naming her with Anna as the model widow.2

Reception and debate

  • A document, not a person. Like the African councils, this witness is a canon-list, not an individual Father — the closing canon of a pseudonymous Syrian compilation that ascribes itself to the apostles through Clement of Rome. It is read here for its list, not for an author's biography; Westcott described the list as valuable "as evidence for the opinions of a part of the Syrian churches towards the end of the fourth century."
  • Beside the African lists. The African councils of the same years also list a longer Old Testament: Tobit, Judith, two Maccabees, and Wisdom and Sirach within the "five books of Solomon." The Apostolic Canons name three Maccabees and do not name Tobit or Wisdom.
  • The New-Testament list. The same canon 85 lists the New Testament — and there it omits the Apocalypse (Revelation) while adding two epistles of Clement and the Apostolic Constitutions themselves to the count. That list belongs to the companion New Testament canon page, not to this Old-Testament page, and is noted here for completeness.
  • Dating and provenance. The Apostolic Constitutions are placed c. 375–380 in Syria, probably Antioch ⚠ unverified; the eighty-five canons draw in part on the councils of Antioch (341) and Laodicea (363), and the book-list of canon 85 is reckoned by the editors "probably added about the year 380" — the date used to slot this witness on the timeline.
