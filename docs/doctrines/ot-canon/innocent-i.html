Innocent I on the Old Testament Canon

Dates: c. 405 AD (Consulenti tibi, Ep. 6 to Exuperius of Toulouse, dated 20 February 405; Innocent was bishop of Rome 401–417) · Wikipedia
Witness: Answering a query from Exuperius, bishop of Toulouse (405), Innocent I appends a canon-list of the longer Septuagint Old Testament: Tobit, Judith, and the two books of Maccabees among "the historical books," and Wisdom and Ecclesiasticus (Sirach) in "the five books of Solomon," with no separate rank. The letter came a decade after the African councils; Jerome and Rufinus, in the same years, ranked these books outside the canon.

Claims & evidence

The canon-list appended for Exuperius: the deuterocanon among the books

Exuperius of Toulouse had written to Rome asking, among other disciplinary questions, which books "really are received in the canon." Innocent answers with a brief catalogue. Its Old-Testament portion reads:

Five books of Moses, that is, Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, and Deuteronomy, and Joshua the son of Nun, and Judges, and the four books of Kings together with Ruth, sixteen books of the Prophets, five books of Solomon, and the Psalms. Also of the historical books, one book of Job, one of Tobit, one of Esther, one of Judith, two of Maccabees, two of Ezra, two of Chronicles.

— Innocent I, Ep. 6 to Exuperius, ch. 7, trans. B. F. Westcott.1 Tobit, Judith, and the two books of Maccabees are listed by name among "the historical books," and the "five books of Solomon" are Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Song of Songs, and the two deuterocanonical Wisdom books — the Wisdom of Solomon and Ecclesiasticus (Sirach).2 Innocent lists these books among the canon, without the "read but not canonical" qualification that Athanasius, Jerome, and Rufinus attached to the same titles. The African councils of Hippo (393) and Carthage (397) had listed the same books a few years earlier.

The same letter's New-Testament list, and the rejected pseudo-apostolic books

The catalogue continues into a New Testament identical to the present twenty-seven books, and then names and condemns the pseudo-apostolic gospels and acts circulating "under the name of Matthias … or of Peter and John … or of Andrew … or of Thomas." That side of the letter, bearing on the New-Testament boundary, is treated on the companion New Testament canon page. The books Innocent condemns there are pseudo-apostolic New-Testament writings; the Old-Testament deuterocanon he here includes.

Reception and debate

  • The disputed books listed with no second tier. Where earlier and contemporary Fathers ranked Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, and the Maccabees a step below the canon — "read" but "not in the canon" (Athanasius), "Ecclesiastical" not "Canonical" (Rufinus), read "for the strengthening of the people, but not for confirming… dogmas" (Jerome) — Innocent lists them among the received books.
  • Earlier Western lists. The African councils of Hippo (393) and Carthage (397) had listed the same books a few years before Innocent's letter. Whether a Roman synod of 382 issued such a list is discussed on the Council of Rome page.
  • Baruch is not separately named. Innocent's "sixteen books of the Prophets" may, like the Eastern lists, reckon Baruch and the Epistle of Jeremiah inside the book of Jeremiah, but he does not name Baruch on its own; the explicit deuterocanonical inclusions here are Tobit, Judith, the two Maccabees, Wisdom, and Sirach.
  • Translation provenance. The English and the underlying Latin are from B. F. Westcott's A General Survey of the History of the Canon of the New Testament (5th ed., 1881, pp. 570f.), which prints Innocent's letter in full; the canon-list is ch. 7 of the epistle.
