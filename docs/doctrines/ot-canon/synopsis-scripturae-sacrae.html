The Synopsis Scripturae Sacrae on the Old Testament Canon

Dates: c. 550 AD (the Synopsis Scripturae Sacrae, "Synopsis of Sacred Scripture," a compendium transmitted among the works of Athanasius and traditionally ascribed to him; the editor of the English translation cited below reports that "most scholars now think that it was composed by an anonymous Greek churchman sometime in the sixth century"; an earlier core has also been proposed ⚠ unverified) · Wikipedia
Witness: The Synopsis, transmitted under Athanasius's name, enumerates the canonical Old Testament as the Hebrew-sized list of twenty-two books and then, as Athanasius had done, sets a second rank of books "not considered canonical, but… only read to catechumens": the Wisdom of Solomon, the Wisdom of Sirach, Esther, Judith, and Tobit. A later section of the work (§74) adds the Maccabees and other Greek writings to the "disputed" class.

The lists in the Synopsis

The canonical books: "the canonical books of the Old Testament are twenty-two"

The compendium opens by listing the Old Testament book by book, each with the words it "begins" with, and closes the count at the Hebrew twenty-two:1

And the books of the Old Testament are these: Genesis, which begins, "In the beginning God made the heaven and the earth"; Exodus, which begins "These are the names of the sons of Israel that came into Egypt"; Leviticus, which begins "And the Lord called Moses again and spoke to him out of the tabernacle of witness"; Numbers, which begins "And the Lord spoke to Moses in the wilderness of Sina, in the tabernacle of witness"; Deuteronomy, which begins "These are the words which Moses spoke to all Israel"… Joshua son of Naue… Judges… Ruth… First and Second Kings… Third and Fourth Kings… Paralipomenon First and Second… First and Second Esdras… The Psalms of David, which includes 151 Psalms… The Parables of Solomon… Ecclesiastes… Song of Songs… Job… The Twelve Prophets… Esaias… Jeremias… Ezechiel… Daniel…

and then fixes the reckoning:1

Thus the canonical books of the Old Testament are twenty-two, corresponding with the number of letters in the Hebrew, for they have this many elementary signs.

Tobit, Judith, Sirach, the Wisdom of Solomon and the Maccabees are not in this list. The count by the letters of the Hebrew alphabet is also given by Origen, Cyril, Athanasius, and Epiphanius (see their pages). The Psalter is numbered at "151 Psalms," counting Psalm 151, which the Greek Bible carries and the Hebrew Psalter does not.

The second rank: "not considered canonical, but… only read to catechumens"

Immediately after the canon-list the Synopsis names a second group of books, "not considered canonical, but which are only read to catechumens":2

But aside from these there are moreover some other books with the Old Testament, which are not considered canonical, but which are only read to catechumens, which are these: The Wisdom of Solomon, which begins, "Love righteousness, ye rulers of the earth"; The Wisdom of Jesus son of Sirach, which begins "All wisdom cometh from the Lord, and is with him forever"; Esther… Judith… Tobit…

The five books named — Wisdom, Sirach, Esther, Judith, Tobit — are the five that Athanasius had listed as "not… included in the Canon, but appointed by the Fathers to be read." As in Athanasius, Esther, which is in the Hebrew canon, stands in this second group with the four Greek books, so the twenty-two do not correspond book for book to the thirty-nine of the Hebrew Bible. The books of this group are placed neither in the canon nor among rejected writings, but in a group to be read.

The Maccabees and other books: "these are disputed books of the Old Testament"

A later section of the work (§74) returns to the same category and adds further Greek writings to the "disputed" (antilegomena) class:3

Among the disputed books of the Old Testament, of which we spoke previously, such as the Wisdom of Solomon, the Wisdom of Jesus son of Sirach, and Esther and Judith and Tobit, also these are to be counted: the four books of the Maccabees, Ptolemaic books, the Psalms and Odes of Solomon, Susanna. These are disputed books of the Old Testament.

The Maccabees, absent from the canon-list, are here named among the "disputed books of the Old Testament," together with the five books of the second group, Susanna, and other writings. A comparable two-rank arrangement appears in Origen and Athanasius (see their pages).

Reception and debate

  • Authorship and date. The Synopsis travelled under Athanasius's name and sits in his collected works; the editor of the English translation cited here reports that "most scholars now think that it was composed by an anonymous Greek churchman sometime in the sixth century," and an earlier core has also been proposed ⚠ unverified.1 The timeline places it at c. 550, following that dating, after Athanasius's Festal Letter of 367.
  • Two ranks. Like Athanasius and Epiphanius, the Synopsis lists Tobit, Judith, Sirach, and Wisdom outside its canon while naming them as books "read to catechumens" and "disputed." In the West, the councils of Hippo and Carthage and Innocent I had listed the same books among the Scriptures (see their entries).
  • Baruch. Where Origen, Cyril, Athanasius, and Epiphanius all reckon their single Jeremiah "with Baruch… and the epistle," the Synopsis's Jeremiah entry ("Jeremias, which begins 'The word of God which came to Jeremias the son of Chelcias, of the priests'") names neither Baruch nor the epistle.1
  • The Psalter. The "151 Psalms" of the canon-list counts Psalm 151, the additional psalm of the Greek Psalter, absent from the Hebrew Psalter: the list gives a count of twenty-two books, matched to the Hebrew alphabet, over a Psalter numbered as in the Greek Bible.
