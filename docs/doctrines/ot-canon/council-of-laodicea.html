The Council of Laodicea on the Old Testament Canon

Dates: c. 363–364 (a regional synod of some two dozen bishops of Phrygia Pacatiana in Asia Minor) · Wikipedia · New Testament canon page
Witness: A regional synod in Phrygia rules that "only the canonical" books "of the New and Old Testament" may be read in church (canon 59), and in canon 60, whose genuineness is disputed, appends a book-list whose Old Testament runs the twenty-two-book Hebrew reckoning: Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, and the Maccabees are absent, and Baruch and the Epistle of Jeremiah are carried inside "Jeremiah." A later note appended canon 60 to the Trullan and Nicene-II canon collections, and the Laodicean canons were among those the Council in Trullo confirmed.

Canon 59 — only "the canonical" books in church

The fifty-ninth canon gives a rule for what may be read in church, stated as a prohibition:1

Let no private psalms nor any uncanonical books be read in the church, but only the Canonical Books of the New and Old Testament.

Canon 59 names no books. The list of canon 60 names them, but that list's genuineness is disputed (see below).

Canon 60 — the Old Testament list

The sixtieth canon supplies a catalogue. Its Old Testament counts twenty-two books:2

And these are the books of the Old Testament which ought to be read: 1, Genesis of the world; 2, The Exodus from Egypt; 3, Leviticus; 4, Numbers; 5, Deuteronomy; 6, Joshua, the son of Nun; 7, Judges, Ruth; 8, Esther; 9, Of the Kings, First and Second; 10, Of the Kings, Third and Fourth; 11, Chronicles, First and Second; 12, Esdras, First and Second; 13, The Book of Psalms; 14, The Proverbs of Solomon; 15, Ecclesiastes; 16, The Song of Songs; 17, Job; 18, The Twelve Prophets; 19, Isaiah; 20, Jeremiah, and Baruch, the Lamentations, and the Epistle; 21, Ezekiel; 22, Daniel.

Tobit, Judith, the Wisdom of Solomon, Sirach (Ecclesiasticus), and the two books of Maccabees are not in the list; the African councils (393–419) list them among the Scriptures (see their page). Item 20, "Jeremiah, and Baruch, the Lamentations, and the Epistle," includes Baruch and the Epistle of Jeremiah within the single entry "Jeremiah," as Origen, Cyril, and Athanasius count their Jeremiah (see their pages).

The genuineness of canon 60

Percival's edition heads canon 60 "[N.B.—This Canon is of most questionable genuineness]," noting that the book-list is absent from older canon-collections of Laodicea — the Latin version of Dionysius Exiguus and the collection of John of Antioch — and B. F. Westcott judged "the external evidence… decidedly against the authenticity" of the enumeration. On that view the list is a later addition spelling out the "canonical books" that canon 59 leaves unnamed.3 This page reports canon 60 as it stands in the received text, whether it is of c. 363 or of a later hand. Cyril (350) and Athanasius (367) also give twenty-two-book Old Testaments (see their pages). The doubts cited above concern the list of canon 60, not the rule of canon 59.

Reception and debate

  • Eastern lists of the period. Laodicea's list stands with the Eastern lists that do not count these books in the canon — Cyril, Athanasius, Gregory of Nazianzus, Amphilochius — while Basil quotes them as Scripture and the Western councils list them (see their pages). Its placing of Baruch and the Epistle of Jeremiah under "Jeremiah" matches Athanasius's list.
  • The same witness, two doctrines. This page treats Laodicea's Old-Testament list; the parallel New Testament canon page treats canon 60's New Testament — the present twenty-seven without the Apocalypse, which Cyril's list also omits. Both rest on the one disputed canon 60.
  • Confirmed with other canons in 692. The Council in Trullo (692), canon 2, confirmed the canons of Laodicea alongside those of Carthage and Athanasius, whose lists differ on the disputed books; it received the earlier canons together (see the Quinisext witness page). Whether those lists contradict one another is set out on an interpretation page.
