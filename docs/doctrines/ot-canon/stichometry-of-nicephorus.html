The Stichometry of Nicephorus on the Old Testament Canon

Dates: c. 810 AD (the Stichometry — a canon-list with line-counts — appended to the Chronographia [Chronographikon Syntomon] of Nicephorus, Patriarch of Constantinople c. 758–828; the appended catalogue's own origin is unsettled, and it may perhaps be located in Jerusalem) · Wikipedia
Witness: The witness is a document: a list that measures every book it names in stichoi (standard verse-lines) and sorts the books into named ranks. Its canonical Old Testament is the Hebrew-sized list (with Baruch attached to Jeremiah). It files the Maccabees, the Wisdom of Solomon, the Wisdom of Sirach, the Psalms and Odes of Solomon, Esther, Judith, Susanna, and Tobit by name in a lower rank, the Old-Testament antilegomena ("gainsaid… not recognized in the Church"), each with its line-count, above a third rank of apocrypha.

Claims & evidence

The canonical Old Testament — Hebrew-sized, with Baruch on Jeremiah

The catalogue's first rank, the books reckoned canonical, runs through the protocanonical Old Testament, the paired books (Kings, Chronicles, Esdras) collapsed and the single book of Jeremiah carrying Baruch immediately after it:1

Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numeri, Deuteronomium, Jesus, Judices et Ruth, Regnorum primus et secundus, Regnorum tertius et quartus, Paralipomenon primus et secundus, Esdrae primus et secundus, Liber Psalmorum, Paroemiae Salomonis, Ecclesiastes, Canticum canticorum, Job, Isaias, Jeremias, Baruch, Ezechiel, Daniel, Duodecim prophetae

The canonical rank does not include Tobit, Judith, Sirach, the Wisdom of Solomon, or the Maccabees. It names Baruch in its own place, immediately after Jeremiah; Athanasius, Epiphanius, and other Greek lists also carry Baruch with Jeremiah (see their pages). Its canonical rank is Hebrew-sized, like the list of John of Damascus a generation or more earlier; it differs from the Masoretic Jewish Bible in naming Baruch and in placing Esther in the second rank (below).

The second rank: the antilegomena, "gainsaid… not recognized in the Church"

Below the canon the list opens a named second rank for the Old Testament, the antilegomena — the "disputed" or "contradicted" books — and places the deuterocanonical books here, each title with its line-count:2

And the (writings) of the Old Testament which are gainsaid and are not recognized in the Church (canonized) are the following:

1. 3 Books of the Maccabees — 7300 lines
2. The Wisdom of Solomon — 1100 lines
3. The Wisdom of Jesus Sirach — 2800 lines
4. The Psalms and Odes of Solomon — 2100 lines
5. Esther — 350 lines
6. Judith — 1700 lines
7. Susanna — 500 lines
8. Tobith, also (called) Tobias — 700 lines

The list gives these books a named rank of their own, between the canonical books and the apocrypha, and measures each in stichoi. The Maccabees, Wisdom, Sirach, Judith, and Tobit are books that Latin councils on this timeline — the African councils, Florence, and Trent — list among the Scriptures; the Stichometry places them in the rank below its canon.

The third rank: the “apocrypha”

Beneath the antilegomena the list sets a third Old-Testament rank, the apocrypha proper — Enoch, the Testaments of the Patriarchs, the Prayer of Joseph, the Testament and the Assumption of Moses, Eldad and Modad, and the pseudepigraphic books of Baruch, Habakkuk, Ezekiel, and Daniel.2 The deuterocanonical books do not appear here. The list places the Maccabees, Wisdom, Sirach, Judith, and Tobit above the Enoch-and-Testaments group; Athanasius likewise distinguishes books "appointed by the Fathers to be read" from the apocrypha (see his page).

Reception and debate

  • A document, not a Father. The witness here is the canon-list itself, transmitted at the end of Nicephorus's short chronicle. The catalogue's origin "has not been clearly settled, but … may perhaps be located in Jerusalem," and whether it is "older than 850 (so Jülicher) remains open to question."2 The date used on the timeline, c. 810, is the chronicle's.
  • The stichoi. A stichometry is a working scribe's reckoning — the line-counts let a copyist price and check a manuscript. The list gives line-counts for the books of the antilegomena and the apocrypha as for the canonical books.
  • Esther among the disputed books, as in Athanasius. Like Athanasius, the list places Esther, a book of the Hebrew Bible, in the disputed rank alongside Judith and Tobit. Its canonical first rank thus lacks one Hebrew book (Esther) and names one Greek one (Baruch) with Jeremiah.
  • "Psalms and Odes of Solomon," not the canonical Psalter. The disputed item "Psalms and Odes of Solomon" is the pseudepigraphic Psalms of Solomon (with the Odes), not the Davidic Liber Psalmorum that stands in the canonical rank. It is listed among the antilegomena on its own account, a distinct disputed work, and is not part of the deuterocanon proper.
  • The New-Testament side is its own matter. The same catalogue omits the Revelation of John from its canonical New Testament and files it under the New-Testament antilegomena — a New-Testament-canon datum treated on the New Testament canon timeline; this page treats only the Old-Testament ranks.
