Josephus on the bronze sea's capacity

Dates: c. 93 (the Antiquities of the Jews) · Wikipedia
Summary: Retelling Solomon's reign at the end of the first century, Josephus describes the sea as a bronze hemisphere and follows the larger figure — three thousand baths — the same side he takes in the count of Solomon's stalls.

Claims & evidence

"Now this sea contained three thousand baths"

"[Solomon] also made a brazen sea, of the figure of a hemisphere… It was placed upon twelve oxen… Now this sea contained three thousand baths."

— Josephus, Antiquities of the Jews 8.79–80, trans. William Whiston. Josephus carries the "three thousand" of Chronicles, not the "two thousand" of Kings.

Reception and debate

  • The same pattern as Solomon's stalls. In the count of Solomon's stalls Josephus likewise followed the larger of the two biblical figures (forty thousand).
  • His place in the debate. Montgomery and Gehman read the "three thousand" as the Chronicler's own enlargement of Kings' figure, "followed by Josephus" (A Critical and Exegetical Commentary on the Books of Kings, ICC, 1951, p. 173). Those who explain the two figures as different measures or different fills, or as a copying error, are set out on the disputed-reading page.
