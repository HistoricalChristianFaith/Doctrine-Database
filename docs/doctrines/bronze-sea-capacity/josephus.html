Josephus on the bronze sea's capacity

Dates: c. 93 (the Antiquities of the Jews) · Wikipedia
Summary: Retelling Solomon's reign at the end of the first century, Josephus describes the sea as a bronze hemisphere and follows the larger figure — three thousand baths — the same side he takes in the count of Solomon's stalls.

Claims & evidence

"Now this sea contained three thousand baths"

"[Solomon] also made a brazen sea, of the figure of a hemisphere… It was placed upon twelve oxen… Now this sea contained three thousand baths."

— Josephus, Antiquities of the Jews 8.79–80, trans. William Whiston. Writing in Greek from a Hebrew source a thousand years before the Masoretic codices, Josephus carries the "three thousand" of Chronicles, not the "two thousand" of Kings. His testimony fixes the larger number as the received figure for the sea in the first century — old, not a medieval scribal peculiarity.

Notes

  • The same pattern as Solomon's stalls. In the count of Solomon's stalls Josephus likewise followed the larger of the two biblical figures (forty thousand). Whether that reflects the text he read or a storyteller's preference for the grander number, it means Josephus cannot be enlisted as an early witness for the smaller figure — here as there, he tells the other way.
  • Why it matters to the crux. The Ahaziah crux was settled partly because every ancient witness broke one way, against the odd Masoretic number. The bronze sea has no such convergence: each book's figure travels with it, and Josephus adds his weight to "three thousand." So the case for "two thousand" rests on the internal geometry of the vessel, not on an external line of witnesses — the evidential shape of Solomon's stalls, not of Ahaziah's age.
