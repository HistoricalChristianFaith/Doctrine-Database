Keil & Delitzsch on the bronze sea's capacity

Dates: 1872 (the Biblical Commentary on the Old Testament) · Wikipedia
Summary: The nineteenth-century Lutheran commentary judges the "three thousand" a copyist's error, proposing a confusion of numeral-letters and giving as its reason the capacity computed from the sea's own dimensions.

Claims & evidence

"The confusion of ג (3) with ב (2)"

"It held 2000 baths; according to the Chronicles, 3000 baths. The latter statement has arisen from the confusion of ג (3) with ב (2); since, according to the calculation of Thenius, the capacity of the vessel, from the dimensions given, could not exceed 2000 baths."

— Keil & Delitzsch, Biblical Commentary on the Old Testament, on 1 Kings 7:26. The commentators give two things: a mechanism, the numeral-letters (gimel, ג = 3, for beth, ב = 2), and a reason, Thenius's calculation that the vessel's volume, computed from the ten-cubit width and five-cubit depth, could not exceed two thousand baths.

The same judgement on the parallel verse

"[the differences] such as… 3000 baths instead of 2000, are probably the result of orthographical errors in the Chronicle."

— Keil & Delitzsch, on 2 Chronicles 4:5, where they also call the Chronicler's doubled verb "received and held" "pleonastic," with "no sufficient reason to strike out the words." Ellicott, Barnes, and the Cambridge Bible agree that a numeral has been corrupted; the Cambridge editors add that "whether the textual corruption is to be attributed to 1 Kin. or to Chron. cannot be determined."

Reception and debate

  • Other accounts of the slip. Ellicott's commentary proposes a slip within the spelled-out words (’alāphîm, "thousands," and ‘alpayim, "two thousand," being "written alike" in the unpointed text), and judges it "more likely" that "three" dropped out of Kings (Ellicott on 2 Chronicles 4:5). Curtis and Madsen, computing the capacity with a different bath, find that "Both estimates appear too large" (ICC Chronicles, 1910, pp. 230–231). These readings are set out on the disputed-reading page.
  • Their judgements on other Kings–Chronicles numbers. Keil & Delitzsch held Chronicles the corrupt witness in Ahaziah's age and here, and the original in the two-slayers crux.
  • "Pleonastic." They read the Chronicler's doubled verb as emphasis; Poole and Gill read the two verbs as naming two measures, the working fill and the brim-full capacity. Both readings are set out on the disputed-reading page.
