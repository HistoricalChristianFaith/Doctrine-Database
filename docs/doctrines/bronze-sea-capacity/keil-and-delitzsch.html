Keil & Delitzsch on the bronze sea's capacity

Dates: 1872 (the Biblical Commentary on the Old Testament) · Wikipedia
Summary: The nineteenth-century commentary that set the conservative-critical consensus judges the "three thousand" a copyist's error — and rests the verdict not on any theory of the letters but on the capacity computed from the sea's own dimensions.

Claims & evidence

"The confusion of ג (3) with ב (2)"

"It held 2000 baths; according to the Chronicles, 3000 baths. The latter statement has arisen from the confusion of ג (3) with ב (2); since, according to the calculation of Thenius, the capacity of the vessel, from the dimensions given, could not exceed 2000 baths."

— Keil & Delitzsch, Biblical Commentary on the Old Testament, on 1 Kings 7:26. The load-bearing move is the second clause: the vessel's volume, computed from the ten-cubit width and five-cubit depth, caps out near two thousand baths, so three thousand is a figure the basin could not physically hold. The numeral-letters (gimel, ג = 3, for beth, ב = 2) supply a plausible how; the geometry supplies the that.

The same verdict on the parallel verse

"[the differences] such as… 3000 baths instead of 2000, are probably the result of orthographical errors in the Chronicle."

— Keil & Delitzsch, on 2 Chronicles 4:5, where they also note that the Chronicler's doubled verb "received and held" is "pleonastic" — a stylistic emphasis, not a signal of two distinct measures. Ellicott, Barnes, and the Cambridge Bible agree that a numeral has been corrupted; the Cambridge editors add the honest caveat that "whether the textual corruption is to be attributed to 1 Kin. or to Chron. cannot be determined."

Notes

  • The mechanism is a reconstruction; the corruption is not. The received Hebrew spells the numbers in words, not letter-numerals, so the exact ג/ב confusion is a hypothesis about how the slip happened. What does not depend on it is the fact that three thousand overshoots the vessel's computed capacity — the point that carries the argument even if the letter-theory is set aside. This is weighed on the scribal-error page.
  • The same camp, case by case. Keil & Delitzsch held Chronicles the corrupt witness in Ahaziah's age and here, and the original in the two-slayers crux. The judgement follows the evidence of each passage, not a bias toward one book — here it is the geometry that decides.
  • "Pleonastic," against the harmonizers. Their reading of the doubled verb as mere emphasis is the direct counter to Poole and Gill's brim-full harmonization, which needs those two verbs to name two measures. See the working-fill page.
